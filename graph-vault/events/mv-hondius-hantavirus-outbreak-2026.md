# MV Hondius Hantavirus Outbreak (2026)

## Overview

The MV Hondius, a cruise ship operated by [[domains/health/entities/oceanwide-expeditions|Oceanwide Expeditions]], was the site of a hantavirus outbreak during its March-April 2026 voyage in the Southern Ocean / Antarctic region. The outbreak involved the Andes virus strain — the only hantavirus strain with documented human-to-human transmission capability — creating a unique risk profile for a cruise ship context with 1000+ passengers and crew from multiple nationalities.

At cutoff (May 21, 2026), there were 9 laboratory-confirmed cases among passengers and crew aboard the vessel. The international response included contact tracing across multiple countries (12 Dutch HCWs quarantined, contacts under monitoring in Scotland/Wales), WHO risk assessment as "low" with no sign of larger outbreak, and aggressive 42-day quarantine protocols for contacts.

## Forecasting Significance

### Key Risk Factors (Andes Strain on Cruise Ship)

1. **Andes virus H2H transmission**: The only hantavirus strain with confirmed human-to-human transmission (documented in 1996 El Bolsón and 2018-19 Epuyén outbreaks). This means secondary transmission off the vessel is possible, unlike a rodent-borne-only hantavirus outbreak.

2. **Multi-week incubation period**: Andes virus incubation is 2-4 weeks (up to 6 weeks for some hantavirus strains), meaning secondary cases from close contacts during the voyage could emerge weeks after disembarkation.

3. **Multinational dispersion**: 1000+ passengers and crew from ~10+ countries disembarked across multiple ports, creating a geographically dispersed contact network that challenges unified contact tracing.

4. **Closed environment amplification**: Cruise ships are enclosed environments with shared dining, entertainment, and cabin spaces — close/prolonged contact is common, the specific transmission vector for Andes virus H2H.

### Mitigating Factors

1. **Aggressive public health response**: 42-day quarantine protocols for close contacts, international notification systems, WHO coordination.
2. **H2H transmission is inefficient**: Even for Andes virus, H2H requires close/prolonged contact with an infected individual in the acute phase. Casual contact or brief exposure does not transmit.
3. **Historical precedent**: Even the largest Andes virus H2H outbreak (Epuyén 2018-19) peaked at ~30 cases, despite occurring in a small community with less robust public health infrastructure.
4. **No secondary off-ship cases confirmed at cutoff** (May 21, 2026) despite monitoring in multiple countries.
