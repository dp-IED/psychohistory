# US 2026 Midterm Elections — November 3, 2026

## Overview

The 2026 midterms are the most consequential US election between the 2024 and 2028 presidential cycles. They determine control of the House (currently R 218-213), the Senate (currently R 53-47), and 36 governorships — setting the legislative and oversight landscape for the final two years of Trump's second term.

## Structural Dynamics

### Midterm Penalty
The president's party has lost House seats in **17 of the last 20 midterms** (average loss: 26 seats). The Senate penalty averages 3-4 seats. Trump's second-term midterm is unusual — historically, second-term midterms are worse for the incumbent party (Bush 2006: -30 House/-6 Senate; Obama 2014: -13 House/-9 Senate).

### Key Modifiers
- **Trump's approval rating**: The single strongest predictor. If approval < 45%, expect R losses of 25+ House seats. If approval > 48%, losses narrow to single digits.
- **Economic conditions**: GDP growth, unemployment, and inflation in the 6 months before the election. The tariff-inflation-labor trilemma ([[domains/economics/concepts/monetary-policy-cycle-phases]]) will determine economic sentiment.
- **Tariff impact visibility**: If Liberation Day tariffs ([[domains/global/threads/us-trade-policy-tariffs/_thread]]) have caused visible consumer price increases by November, the midterm penalty amplifies.

### Referendum Dynamics
The [[domains/global/concepts/midterm-referendum-dynamics/_concept]] documents the pattern: midterms function as a referendum on the president, not on individual candidates. This means localized factors (incumbent quality, fundraising, district composition) are secondary to the national political environment.

## Forecasting Framework

For any congressional district or statewide race forecast:

1. **Apply midterm penalty baseline**: Start with presidential approval modifier. For every 1% approval below 50%, add ~1.2 House seats lost.
2. **Layer district PVI**: Cook PVI is the structural floor/ceiling. An R+5 district requires a D+5+ national environment to flip.
3. **Check incumbent quality**: Retiring incumbents reduce the structural advantage by 3-5 points.
4. **Apply Trump endorsement premium**: In Republican primaries, Trump endorsement adds 5-15 points. In general elections, the effect varies by district type (boosts turnout in R+ districts, may hurt in swing districts).
5. **Check the [[domains/global/concepts/midterm-legislative-bandwidth]] concept**: Legislative calendar compression in election years means fewer \"wins\" for incumbents to campaign on.
