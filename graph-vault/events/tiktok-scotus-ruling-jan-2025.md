# Supreme Court Upholds TikTok Divest-or-Ban Law (Jan 17, 2025)

## Event Summary

On January 17, 2025, the US Supreme Court unanimously (9-0) upheld the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, ruling that the divest-or-ban mandate targeting TikTok and its Chinese parent company ByteDance does not violate the First Amendment. The ruling affirmed Congress's authority to address national security risks posed by foreign-owned technology platforms.

## Significance

This ruling was the critical legal gate for the TikTok ban. Key implications:

1. **Legal precedent**: The unanimous decision established that Congress can ban Chinese-owned consumer applications by targeting ownership structure (rather than content or speech), setting a direct precedent for future forced divestiture legislation targeting any foreign-adversary-controlled technology platform.

2. **Procedural trajectory**: The Court's procedural choices were themselves the strongest forecast signal. The expedited trajectory — cert before judgment (Dec 18), compressed 3-week schedule to oral argument (Jan 10), no stay granted, and ruling within 7 days of argument (Jan 17) — perfectly predicted the unanimous outcome. Votes were locked before oral arguments.

3. **Implementation bifurcation**: The ruling did NOT settle the enforcement question. The ban legally took effect (TikTok went dark Jan 18-19), but Trump's Jan 20 executive order delayed enforcement. This confirmed the [[domains/global/concepts/executive-enforcement-delay/_concept]] pattern: legal effect and practical enforcement are distinct.

4. **Bipartisan validation**: The 9-0 vote across a 6-3 conservative-liberal split demonstrated that national security rationales for tech bans transcend ordinary ideological divisions. All justices — from Thomas and Alito to Sotomayor and Jackson — agreed on the constitutional framework.
