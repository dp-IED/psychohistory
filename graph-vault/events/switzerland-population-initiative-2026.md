# Switzerland: "No to Ten Million" Population Initiative (June 14, 2026)

## Overview

Switzerland's "No to ten million" popular initiative (German: "Keine 10-Millionen-Schweiz") was a constitutional referendum voted on June 14, 2026. The initiative proposed restricting Switzerland's permanent population to under 10 million through immigration limits.

## Structural Context

- **Swiss direct democracy**: Initiatives require both popular majority AND cantonal majority to pass
- **Historical pattern**: Restrictive immigration initiatives have a mixed track record:
  - 2014 "Against Mass Immigration" (popular YES 50.3%, cantonal YES 14.5/20.5 → passed after compromise implementation)
  - 2024 "Stabilization Act" (narrowly rejected)
- **Demographics**: Switzerland's population was ~9.1 million in 2026, with natural increase near zero — population growth is almost entirely immigration-driven
- **EU relations**: Switzerland's bilateral agreements with the EU include free movement of persons — a YES vote would require renegotiation or abrogation of these treaties

## Forecasting Relevance

- **Direct democracy dynamics**: Switzerland's initiative process provides a regular test of anti-immigration sentiment in a wealthy, direct-democracy context
- **EU-Swiss relations**: A YES outcome would trigger the "guillotine clause" in EU bilateral agreements, threatening Switzerland's access to the single market
- **European immigration trend**: Swiss initiatives serve as leading indicators for immigration restriction sentiment across Western Europe

## Resolution

The initiative was **rejected** on June 14, 2026. ~55% of voters voted against the proposal to cap the population at 10 million by cutting migration.
