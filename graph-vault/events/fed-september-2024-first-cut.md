# Fed September 2024 First Cut (50bp)

## Summary

On September 18, 2024, the Federal Reserve cut the federal funds rate by 50 basis points to 4.75-5.00%, marking the first rate cut of the easing cycle. The cut followed 14 months at the 5.25-5.50% terminal peak (July 2023 - September 2024).

The 50bp magnitude was larger than the standard 25bp cut and surprised most market participants, who had expected 25bp. Fed Chair Powell framed the larger cut as "insurance" against labor market softening rather than a panic response, but the magnitude signaled greater concern about economic weakening than the committee had previously communicated.

## Key Details

- **Vote**: 11-1 (Michelle Bowman dissented, favoring 25bp)
- **Dot plot**: Revised median projection showed 50bp more cuts by year-end 2024 (total 100bp in 2024)
- **SEP**: GDP growth revised down, unemployment revised up, inflation revised down
- **Market reaction**: Equities initially volatile then rallied; 2-year yield dropped ~10bp; USD weakened; Bitcoin rallied ~6% on the day

## Forecasting Significance

1. **First-move magnitude matters**: The 50bp vs 25bp choice revealed the Fed's asymmetric concern about labor market vs inflation — a signal that persisted through subsequent cuts.
2. **Cycle initiation**: The cut started a sequence of 200bp of cuts (Sept 2024: 50bp, Nov 2024: 25bp, Dec 2024: 25bp, Jan 2025: 25bp, Mar 2025: 25bp, May 2025: 25bp, Sep 2025: 25bp, Jan 2026: 25bp).
3. **Broad market impact**: Lower rates reduced discount rates on all risk assets, boosting equities, crypto, and real estate. The subsequent 6 months saw significant risk-on moves across asset classes.
4. **Political context**: The cut 7 weeks before the November 2024 presidential election drew criticism from Trump and some Republicans who argued the Fed was aiding the Biden/Harris administration.
