# German Federal Election 2025

## Date
February 23, 2025 (snap election, 7 months early)

## Result

| Party | Vote Share | Seats | Change from 2021 |
|-------|-----------|-------|-------------------|
| CDU/CSU | 28.5% | ~208 | +4.4pp |
| AfD | 20.8% | ~152 | +10.5pp |
| SPD | 16.4% | ~120 | -9.3pp |
| Greens | 12.0% | ~85 | -2.8pp |
| Left (Die Linke) | 9.0% | ~64 | +4.1pp |
| FDP | 4.3% | 0 | -7.2pp (lost all seats) |
| BSW | 4.98% | 0 | New (fell just below threshold) |
| Others | 4.8% | 1 (SSW) | — |

## Key Outcomes

1. **CDU/CSU+SPD grand coalition** formed (April 9, 2025 — 73-day negotiation)
2. **Friedrich Merz (CDU)** became Chancellor
3. **AfD became second-largest party** for the first time, doubled vote share but excluded from coalition (Brandmauer held)
4. **FDP lost all representation** — first time since 2013, precipitated by their own departure from government
5. **BSW narrowly missed threshold** by ~2,000 votes (4.98%) — dramatic near-miss that would have fundamentally altered coalition arithmetic
6. **Turnout**: 82.5% — highest since reunification
7. **Unrepresented voters**: 13.9% of voters cast ballots for parties that didn't clear 5% threshold — highest share in German history

## Structural Implications

- **Center-left coalition impossible**: SPD+Greens+Left = 37.4% — below majority. Any government must include CDU/CSU.
- **Brandmauer intact**: CDU/CSU+SPD grand coalition maintained the cordon sanitaire against the AfD, but the CDU/CSU's AfD-adjacent positioning on immigration and fiscal policy blurred the distinction.
- **Constitutional governance**: The outgoing Bundestag had to pass constitutional amendments before the new parliament convened (March 25, 2025), as the required supermajorities weren't available in the more fragmented new chamber.

## Forecasting Relevance

The 2025 election structurally determines German political trajectory through the end of the decade:
- The CDU/CSU+SPD coalition is stable for the full 4-year term (until March 2029)
- AfD's 20.8% serves as a ceiling test for far-right performance in European PR systems
- The FDP's extinction raises questions about liberal party survival in two-polarizing systems
- The next federal election is due on or before March 25, 2029
