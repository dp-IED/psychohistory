# Per-Question Reflection Cycle 13: Trump Trial Timeline

## What Happened

The question asked whether the DC federal election interference trial (US v. Trump, before Judge Tanya Chutkan) would fail to start before the November 2024 presidential election. The correct answer was YES — the trial did not start before November. The timeline:

1. **December 2023**: Judge Chutkan denies Trump's immunity motion; Trump files interlocutory appeal
2. **February 2024**: Supreme Court grants cert on the immunity question — effectively staying the case
3. **April 25, 2024**: SCOTUS oral arguments
4. **July 1, 2024**: SCOTUS issues Trump v. United States — broad presidential immunity, case remanded
5. **July-November 2024**: Immunity litigation on remand consumes remaining pre-election months
6. **November 5, 2024**: Trump wins election; federal case becomes dead per DOJ policy

I predicted YES (the trial would NOT start before November) and was correct.

## Diagnosis

### Why the prediction was correct

The reasoning chain was straightforward and based on general knowledge of the US legal system:

1. **SCOTUS timeline**: The Supreme Court takes 4-6 months from cert grant to decision for major cases. Cert was granted in February 2024, so the ruling would come no earlier than June-July 2024.
2. **Novel constitutional question**: Presidential immunity was a first-impression issue requiring deliberate, slow review.
3. **Remand proceedings**: Even after SCOTUS ruled, Judge Chutkan needed months to apply the new framework.
4. **Electoral mooting**: If the trial couldn't start by ~October 2024 (including jury selection), the pre-election window was lost. Post-win, the case would be dead.
5. **Sum of delays**: March (original date) to November (election) = ~8 months. With SCOTUS consuming February-July, there was no realistic path to trial.
