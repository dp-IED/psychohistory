# Forecast: Colombia 2026 Presidential Runoff (June 21)

**Election schedule**: First round May 31 — Runoff June 21 (if no candidate >50%)
**Active markets**: Polymarket $29.9M (largest political event), $6.1M on first-round winner

## First-Round Landscape

The first round (May 31) features a fragmented field. Ivan Cepeda Castro (Pacto Histórico, left) leads polling at ~35-38%, well below the 50% needed for a first-round outright win. The right-wing vote is split among multiple candidates: Abelardo de la Espriella (populist right) at ~18-22%, followed by several minor candidates dividing the remaining ~40%.

Historical first-round outright win rate in Colombia: ~10-15% in fragmented fields. The vault's existing forecast (p_yes=0.08 for first-round outright win) is consistent with this base rate.

## Runoff Dynamics

The runoff (June 21, three weeks after first round) is where the election is most likely decided. Key dynamics:

### 1. First-Round Right-Wing Consolidation

The critical variable: whether right-wing voters consolidate behind a single candidate between May 31 and June 21. In Colombia's two-round system, the first round acts as a party primary for the right:

- If de la Espriella finishes clear second (20%+), right-wing voters and establishment figures will coalesce around him
- If the right-wing vote is split 3+ ways in the first round, the weakened second-place candidate may not rally the full right-wing opposition
- Historical pattern: Colombian right-wing voters consolidate effectively in runoffs (2014 Zuluaga, 2018 Duque, 2022 Hernández)

### 2. Cepeda Ceiling in Runoff

Ivan Cepeda's polling ceiling at ~46-48% in head-to-head matchups reflects:

- **Structural left-wing ceiling**: Colombia's most left-wing presidential candidate would win a 30-year highwater mark for the left. No left-wing candidate has won since 1990 (César Gaviria, centrist Liberal)
- **Pacto Histórico brand**: Petro's unpopularity (approval ~35-40% leaving office) is a drag on the Pacto brand, even though Cepeda is less polarizing
- **Undecided voter behavior**: In polarized runoff environments, late-deciding voters break disproportionately toward the establishment/centrist option (Colombia 2022: Hernández gained 11pp from first round to runoff against Petro)

### 3. First-Round Margin Signal

The size of Cepeda's first-round lead is a leading indicator:

| First-Round Cepeda Margin | Runoff Cepeda Win Probability | Basis |
|--------------------------|------------------------------|-------|
| >50% outright | 1.0 | Election over |
| 15-20pp lead (45% vs 25-30%) | ~0.60 | Strong position, but well below majority |
| 10-15pp lead (40% vs 25-30%) | ~0.45 | Competitive; right-wing consolidation could flip |
| <10pp lead (38% vs 28-35%) | ~0.30 | Vulnerable; right-wing voter discipline wins |
| First-round loss | 0.0 | Cepeda loses |

### 4. Abstention Asymmetry

Colombian runoff turnouts are typically 10-15pp lower than first-round turnouts. Lower-turnout (more motivated) electorates tend to favor the more ideological/partisan candidate. For Cepeda (left):

- Higher turnout favors Cepeda (younger, urban voters more motivated)
- Lower turnout favors the right-wing candidate (older, rural voters more reliable)
- The net effect depends on which run-off base is more motivated — currently uncertain

### p_yes Estimates

**Cepeda wins runoff**: p ≈ 0.35-0.40
**Right-wing candidate wins runoff**: p ≈ 0.60-0.65

The right-wing advantage stems from:
- Historical left-wing ceiling in Colombia (~48% in runoff)
- Right-wing coalition formation capacity
- Petro administration unpopularity as a drag on Pacto brand
- Undecided voters' anti-incumbent lean in runoff

### Key Risk to Forecast

A first-round surge by Cepeda above 45% would change the calculus — suggesting his appeal transcends Pacto Histórico's base and reaches centrist voters. Conversely, a first-round right-wing consolidation beyond expectations (a single candidate at 30%+) would lock the right-wing advantage.
