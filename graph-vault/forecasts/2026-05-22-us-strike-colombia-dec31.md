# Forecast: US Strike on Colombia by December 31, 2026

**Polymarket YES:** 27.0% | **Volume:** $992,691 | **Deadline:** December 31, 2026

## Forecast

**Prediction: NO (p_yes = 0.12)**

## Reasoning

### Market Definition

The market resolves YES if "a US-initiated drone, missile, or air strike on the soil of Colombia is announced or credibly reported to have occurred by December 31, 2026." This is narrowly scoped to kinetic military action — not sanctions, not training missions, not counternarcotics advisory. It requires bombs/missiles on Colombian territory.

The earlier windows (January 31, March 31) both resolved NO, indicating this market has been tracking for 5+ months without an event. The residual probability for the Dec 31 window is 27%.

### Structural Analysis

**1. Historical base rate — near zero**

US direct military strikes on South American soil are essentially unprecedented in the post-Cold War era. The US has conducted:
- Joint counternarcotics operations in Colombia (aerial spraying, training) — not strikes
- Special operations advisory missions (Plan Colombia) — not strikes
- The 1989 Panama invasion was a full-scale intervention, not a "strike"
- US drone strikes are concentrated in the Middle East, Horn of Africa, and South Asia (Yemen, Somalia, Pakistan, Afghanistan) — zero strikes in Latin America

The base rate for a US drone/missile/air strike in Colombia over any 7-month window is <1%.

**2. Colombian sovereignty barrier**

Colombia is a US ally, not an adversary. Any US strike on Colombian soil would require:
- Colombian government consent (explicit or tacit), which is politically toxic for any Colombian president
- Petro's government (until June 21 runoff) has been cool toward the US. A new government may be more amenable to cooperation, but "consent to US bombing on our soil" is a red line for any Latin American leader
- The incoming president (likely Cepeda or de la Espriella) will be navigating a fragile mandate and cannot afford to be seen as a US puppet

**3. Trump administration's operational pattern**

Trump 2.0 foreign policy has been characterized by:
- Transactional deal-making (Alaska Summit, Gaza ceasefire, Armenia-Azerbaijan)
- Economic pressure (tariffs) as primary foreign policy tool
- Deterrence via posture and signaling, not new kinetic commitments
- Reducing military entanglements abroad (not expanding them)

A unilateral strike in Colombia would be a sharp departure from this pattern. It would require a specific, acute trigger — not just "cartels are bad."

**4. Casus belli assessment**

What could trigger a US strike on Colombia?
- **Cartel attack on US citizens/territory**: A cartel-directed mass casualty attack on US soil or against US diplomatic personnel could rapidly change the calculus. Status: no such event has occurred.
- **Venezuela spillover**: If the Venezuela situation escalated dramatically (Maduro attacking Colombian border regions, hosting Iranian/Hezbollah elements), US strikes targeting those elements on Colombian soil become conceivable. Status: Venezuela tensions are elevated but not at the "US bombs Latin America" level.
- **Hostage/rescue scenario**: A high-value American hostage held by ELN or cartels in Colombia could trigger a JSOC operation. But most cartels avoid targeting Americans precisely to avoid this trigger.
- **ELN/FARC dissident attack on US interests**: Possible but unlikely to reach the threshold for a unilateral US military response vs. Colombian-led operation with US support.

None of these triggers are active as of May 2026.

**5. Regional diplomatic costs**

Any US strike in Colombia would:
- Trigger condemnation from CELAC, OAS, and individual Latin American nations
- Damage US-Colombia trade relations ($40B+ bilateral trade)
- Alienate a key US ally in the region
- Provide propaganda material for China and Russia in Latin America
- Risk destabilizing the incoming Colombian government

The State Department and NSC would oppose this unless the trigger event was catastrophic.

**6. Military bandwidth**

US military forces as of mid-2026 are engaged in:
- European deterrence posture (NATO eastern flank)
- Middle East presence (Iran-related, post-ceasefire Gaza, Syria)
- Pacific pivot (China deterrence)
- Counterterrorism in Africa/Sahel

Opening a new kinetic front in South America would strain operational capacity and require diverting ISR (intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance) assets from higher-priority theaters.

### Counter-Signals

- **Cartel FTO designation**: Trump has discussed designating Mexican cartels as Foreign Terrorist Organizations. If extended to Colombian cartels, this creates a legal framework that could normalize strike operations. However, FTO designation ≠ strikes — it enables sanctions and prosecutions first.
- **Trump's unpredictability**: The Soleimani killing (2020) demonstrated Trump's willingness to use lethal force when presented with a specific, time-sensitive opportunity. A similar JSOC-targeted operation against a high-value cartel leader in Colombia could technically qualify as a "strike."
- **Colombia's incoming government**: If the new president (taking office ~August 2026) is strongly pro-US and requests/accepts US kinetic assistance against a specific target, a strike becomes more plausible.

### Final Estimate

Base rate (<1%) × scenario adjustment — I estimate ~12% probability. The 27% market price appears elevated, likely reflecting:
1. Recency bias from Trump's strike history (Soleimani)
2. Conflation of "US military operations in Colombia" (advisory, training) with "strikes" (kinetic)
3. General "Trump will do something crazy" sentiment

The market has had 5 months to observe that strikes DID NOT happen in the nearer-term windows (Jan, March). The residual 27% on the 7-month window prices in a constant per-month strike probability of ~4-5%, which is far too high for the structural barriers identified.

**p_yes = 0.12**
