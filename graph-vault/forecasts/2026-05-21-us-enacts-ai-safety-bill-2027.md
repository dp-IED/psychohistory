# Forecast: U.S. enacts AI safety bill before 2027?

**Polymarket YES:** 53.5% | **Volume:** $98,585

## Forecast

**Prediction: NO (p_yes = 0.38)**

## Reasoning

### Current State (May 2026)

The vault's [[domains/usa/threads/us-ai-safety-regulation-federal]] thread documents 3+ years of legislative failure on comprehensive AI regulation:

1. **2023**: Schumer SAFE Innovation Framework + Biden EO 14110 (executive action, not legislation)
2. **2024**: Multiple bills introduced (SAFE Innovation Act, AI Research & Accountability Act, CREATE AI Act) — none reached floor vote. California SB 1047 vetoed.
3. **2025**: Trump administration revoked Biden's AI EO. Deregulatory posture dominates. No comprehensive AI bill advanced.

### Key Structural Barriers

1. **Trump administration deregulatory stance**: The current administration prioritizes AI innovation over safety regulation. NIST AI Safety Institute is deprioritized. An AI safety bill would need to overcome an executive branch actively opposed to new tech regulation.

2. **Legislative bandwidth**: 2026 is a midterm election year. Congress has limited working days and competing priorities (tax/budget, defense, immigration). Comprehensive AI legislation has never been a priority bill that leadership clears for floor time.

3. **Partisan gridlock on liability frameworks**: Democrats favor mandatory safety testing + liability; Republicans oppose anything that could slow US AI competitiveness vs China. The same fault lines that killed 2024 bills persist.

4. **Industry opposition**: OpenAI, Meta, and most major AI companies oppose mandatory safety requirements. Only Anthropic supports regulation. Industry lobbying is effective at blocking bills.

### Counter-signals (why market is at 53.5%)

- The EU AI Act took effect in 2025, creating pressure for US to avoid being "behind"
- Midterm election dynamics could create a window for bipartisan messaging bills
- A narrow/symbolic bill (not comprehensive) could pass by rebranding as "AI competitiveness" rather than "AI safety"
- State-level regulation (California, Colorado) creates industry demand for federal preemption

### Resolution Criteria Risk

The market asks "AI safety bill" — this is broad. A narrowly-scoped bill (e.g., deepfake labeling, child safety, or election integrity) could qualify as "AI safety." However, the Trump administration's posture makes even narrow bills difficult unless they're framed as deregulatory (preempting state laws).

### Final Estimate

Base rate for comprehensive tech regulation passing under a deregulatory administration in a midterm year: ~15-20%. Adjusted upward for narrow-bill possibility (+10%) and EU pressure dynamics (+8%). Result: ~38%, substantially below the 53.5% market price.

The market appears to be pricing EU regulatory momentum + midterm window optimism rather than the concrete legislative barriers documented in the vault thread.
