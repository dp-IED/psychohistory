# Head-of-State ICC Arrest Dynamics

## Definition

A structural forecasting framework for predicting the likelihood and consequences of International Criminal Court arrest warrants against current or former heads of state. The pattern is defined by three dimensions: (1) whether the target is currently in power or post-exit, (2) whether the state of nationality cooperates with the ICC, and (3) whether the arrest has enforcement mechanisms (ICC does not have its own police force).

## Key Variables

### 1. In-Power vs Post-Exit Status
| Status | Historical Clean-Up Rate | Mechanism |
|--------|-------------------------|-----------|
| Sitting head of state | ~0% (zero enforced arrests while target in power) | UNSC referral states (Sudan, Libya) or state parties (Kenya, Ivory Coast) never enforced custody |
| Former head of state (recent exit) | ~15-20% | Depends on successor government cooperation |
| Former head of state (post-exit, regime change) | ~40-60% | New regime denies protection; Taylor, Gbagbo, Al Hassan cases |

**Forecasting rule**: An ICC arrest warrant for a sitting head of state has near-zero probability of enforced custody. Post-exit warrants are credible only when the successor government has incentive to cooperate.

### 2. State Cooperation Threshold
The critical variable is whether the state of nationality cooperates with ICC execution. Duterte (2025) represents a **voluntary cooperation** case where the successor government (Marcos) actively facilitated the arrest. Contrast with:
- **Non-cooperation**: Bashir (Sudan) — state refused to transfer despite UNSC resolution
- **Regime change cooperation**: Gbagbo (Ivory Coast) — new government transferred him post-arrest
- **Diplomatic pressure cooperation**: Taylor (Liberia) — Nigerian cooperation under US/UK pressure

### 3. Enforcement Mechanism
ICC lacks police powers; relies on state parties to execute warrants. Enforcement chain:
1. ICC Pre-Trial Chamber issues warrant → transmitted to all state parties
2. State party where target is present → domestic authorities execute
3. Target transferred to ICC detention center (The Hague)
4. Target appears before Pre-Trial Chamber → confirmation of charges hearing

The weakest link is step 2: states may refuse or delay execution.

## Forecasting Application

When a head-of-state ICC arrest forecast appears:

1. **Determine target status**: Sitting head of state? Post-exit? Recent exit or regime change?
2. **Assess successor incentives**: Does the current government benefit from the arrest (removes rival, legitimizes transition) or face costs (domestic backlash, base alienation)?
3. **Check enforcement path**: Is there a plausible travel scenario where the target enters ICC jurisdiction?
4. **Calculate custody probability**: For sitting heads: <1% without regime change. For post-exit with cooperating successor: 20-50%. For post-exit with non-cooperating successor: <5%.
