### Definition

Generational replacement (also known as "cohort replacement" or "demographic metabolism") refers to the process by which a population's aggregate characteristics change as older birth cohorts are replaced by younger ones through mortality and aging-in. Unlike period effects (shocks that affect all age groups simultaneously), generational replacement produces gradual, unidirectional, and highly predictable shifts in population-level attitudes and behaviors.

### Mechanism

1. **Formative imprinting**: Individuals develop core values, tastes, and political orientations during late adolescence and early adulthood (ages 16-25), a period of heightened neuroplasticity and identity formation. These imprints are largely stable thereafter.
2. **Cohort inertia**: A birth cohort carries its formative-era characteristics through its entire lifespan. The Silent Generation's formative experiences (Depression, WWII) differ from Boomers' (postwar prosperity, Vietnam, civil rights) which differ from Millennials' (9/11, Great Recession, smartphone ubiquity) which differ from Gen Z's (COVID-19, climate anxiety, TikTok-native communication).
3. **Gradual replacement**: Each year, ~1-1.5% of a national population is replaced — the oldest cohort dies off and the newest cohort reaches voting/viewing/consuming age. Over 20 years, this produces a 20-30% compositional shift in the adult population.
4. **Accelerated replacement in specific domains**: In sports viewership, media consumption, and social media adoption, the replacement rate is faster because younger cohorts dominate these activities disproportionately, and older cohorts age out of heavy engagement.

### Examples

- **Decline of organized religion in the West**: The rise of the "nones" (religiously unaffiliated) in the US from <10% in 1990 to ~30% in 2025 is primarily a generational replacement story — each successive cohort is less religious than the previous, and older religious cohorts are dying off.
- **Rising support for same-sex marriage**: The dramatic shift in US public opinion (from 27% support in 1996 to 71% in 2024) was driven 60-70% by generational replacement and 30-40% by period/within-cohort attitude change.
- **Women's sports viewership growth**: Gen Z's higher baseline interest in women's sports (vs. Millennials at the same age) will become an aggregate demand shift as Gen Z enters its peak sports-viewing years (25-45) over the next two decades.
- **Political polarization in the US**: The divergence in partisan identification between cohorts (younger cohorts leaning increasingly Democratic, older cohorts remaining Republican) follows a generational replacement pattern.
