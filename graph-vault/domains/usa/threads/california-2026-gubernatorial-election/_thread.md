# California 2026 Gubernatorial Election

## Summary

The 2026 California gubernatorial election is an open-seat race to succeed term-limited Governor Gavin Newsom (D). California uses a top-two primary system (June 2, 2026) followed by a general election (November 3, 2026). The race features a fractured field of 30+ candidates, with the top-two advancing to the general election regardless of party.

California has been a Democratic stronghold since 2006 (Schwarzenegger was the last Republican governor, 2003-2011). Newsom won re-election in 2022 with 59.2% against Republican Brian Dahle (40.8%).

### General Election Dynamics (Updated June 14)**

California's 2026 general election is between **Democrat Xavier Becerra** and **Republican Steve Hilton**:

1. **Democratic vote splitting**: With 15+ Democratic candidates, there is a non-trivial risk of two Republicans advancing if the Democratic vote is sufficiently fragmented. However, Becerra's frontrunner status and name recognition make this unlikely.

2. **Hilton as the Republican standard-bearer**: Steve Hilton (85% to advance from primary) is the clear Republican frontrunner. His Fox News platform gives him reach with the ~35% Republican base in California.

3. **Steyer's self-funding**: Tom Steyer's $250M+ self-funding capacity makes him competitive despite lower polling. California media markets are among the most expensive in the country (LA, SF, SD, Sacramento).

4. **Top-two scenarios**:
   - Becerra vs Hilton (most likely, ~50% probability): standard D vs R general
   - Becerra vs Steyer (~30%): two Democrats; Steyer's billionaire status becomes an issue
   - Hilton vs Steyer (~10%): Republican advances with a self-funder Democrat
   - Hilton vs Bianco (~5%): two Republicans — would be catastrophic for Democrats
   - Other (~5%): Porter, Padilla, or Harris enter and reshape the field

## General Election Dynamics

California's 2026 general election is strongly favored for Democrats regardless of the matchup:

- **Democratic registration advantage**: ~46% D, ~24% R, ~24% NPP (no party preference)
- **Presidential year turnout**: 2026 is a midterm year, which typically reduces Democratic turnout but California's shift to all-mail voting has mitigated this effect
- **Trump administration backlash**: Historical pattern of midterm backlash against the president's party could boost Democratic turnout in a state that voted against Trump by 20+ points in 2024

## Structural Uncertainties

1. **Katie Porter's decision**: If Porter runs and consolidates progressive support, she could outflank Becerra from the left in a top-two. Her 2024 Senate primary loss to Schiff suggests limited statewide appeal, but the gubernatorial primary has a different electorate.

2. **Kamala Harris entry**: Harris (former VP, former CA AG, former SF DA) would immediately become the frontrunner if she enters. Her 2024 presidential loss complicates her political future. She has not ruled out a run.

3. **Xavier Becerra's vulnerabilities**: Becerra served as HHS Secretary under Biden (2021-2025) and CA Attorney General (2017-2021). His HHS tenure during COVID-19 and his support for vaccine mandates could be attacked from both left and right. His CA AG record on criminal justice reform could be attacked by law enforcement-aligned candidates (Bianco).

4. **Independent/third-party dynamics**: Nicole Shanahan (RFK Jr's running mate) could draw independent votes, but California does not have a strong third-party tradition in gubernatorial races.
