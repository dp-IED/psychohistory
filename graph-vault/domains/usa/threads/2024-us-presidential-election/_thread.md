# 2024 US Presidential Election

## Overview

The 2024 US presidential election was a contest between Republican nominee Donald Trump and Democratic nominee Kamala Harris (after Joe Biden's unprecedented withdrawal from the race on July 21, 2024). Trump won the election with 312 electoral votes to Harris's 226, regaining the presidency after losing in 2020. The election was notable for: the first incumbent president to withdraw from a re-election campaign since LBJ (1968); the first female major-party nominee since Hillary Clinton (2016); and the first presidential election decided in the immediate aftermath of a sitting president's withdrawal.

## Key Phases

### Phase 1: Biden's Candidacy (April 2023 - July 2024)

- **April 25, 2023**: Biden announces re-election campaign. Harris remains as VP running mate.
- **2024 Democratic primary**: Biden faces minimal primary opposition (Dean Phillips, Marianne Williamson). Wins every state.
- **June 27, 2024**: First presidential debate (Biden vs Trump). Biden delivers a widely-panned performance with visible cognitive decline, triggering the withdrawal cascade.
- **July 10, 2024**: George Clooney publishes NYT op-ed "I Love Joe Biden but We Need a New Nominee" — inflection point in Democratic donor and elite opinion.
- **July 13, 2024**: Trump survives assassination attempt at Pennsylvania rally.
- **July 15, 2024**: Trump selects JD Vance as his running mate at RNC.

### Phase 2: Biden Withdrawal and Harris Nomination (July 21 - August 2024)

- **July 21, 2024**: Biden withdraws from the race in a public letter, endorses Kamala Harris. The withdrawal follows the [[domains/usa/concepts/incumbent-withdrawal-cascade]] pattern: public denial (June 8 statement declaring "I'm staying in the race"), party pressure cascade, then withdrawal.
- **July 22 - August 5, 2024**: Harris secures delegates, DNC holds virtual roll call, Harris certified as nominee.
- **August 6, 2024**: Harris selects Tim Walz as VP running mate.
- **August 19-22, 2024**: DNC convention in Chicago.

### Phase 3: General Election Campaign (August - November 2024)

- **August 23, 2024**: RFK Jr. suspends campaign, endorses Trump.
- **September 10, 2024**: Harris-Trump debate. Harris widely viewed as winning.
- **September - October 2024**: Campaign focuses on swing states (MI, WI, PA, AZ, GA, NV, NC).
- **October 2024**: Early voting begins in most states.
- **November 5, 2024**: Election Day. Trump wins 312-226 electoral votes.

### Phase 4: Post-Election Transition (November 2024 - January 2025)

- **November 6, 2024**: Trump declared winner. Harris concedes.
- **December 2024 - January 2025**: Lame-duck budget crisis and government shutdown.

## VP Selection Dynamics

### Democratic Veepstakes

- **Initial context (pre-withdrawal)**: As long as Biden remained the nominee, Kamala Harris (sitting VP) was the near-certain VP nominee — consistent with the [[domains/usa/concepts/incumbent-vp-renomination]] pattern. The "another woman" question was structurally NO because Harris was on the exclusion list and was the expected VP.
- **Post-withdrawal (Harris as nominee)**: Harris's VP selection followed the [[domains/usa/concepts/gender-balancing-ticket-composition/_concept]] pattern: a female nominee is structurally constrained to pick a male running mate. The finalist pool was all-male (Walz, Shapiro, Kelly, Beshear, Cooper).
- **Walz selection rationale**: Reinforcement model — Walz amplified Harris's Midwestern authenticity rather than expanding coalition demographics. See [[domains/usa/concepts/veepstakes-electoral-signal/_concept]] for the reinforcement vs. balancing framework.
- **Exclusion-list questions**: Both "another man" and "another woman" Polymarket questions resolved NO because the exclusion lists captured the entire viable candidate pool within each gender. The comprehensive-exclusion-list framework was the primary forecasting tool.

### Republican Veepstakes

- Trump selects JD Vance (male, populist-nationalist) on July 15, 2024.
- Other finalists included Doug Burgum, Marco Rubio, and Tim Scott.
- Vance selection followed the reinforcement model — amplifying Trump's populist base rather than expanding to moderates.

## Cross-Domain Interactions

1. **Biden withdrawal ↔ Harris nomination**: The compressed campaign timeline (105 days) constrained Harris's VP selection to well-vetted, non-controversial candidates. Walz's quick emergence as the pick (from dark horse to nominee in ~48 hours) was enabled by this time pressure.

2. **Trump immunity ruling (July 1, 2024) ↔ Campaign dynamics**: SCOTUS's Trump v. United States ruling granting broad presidential immunity affected Trump's legal strategy and campaign framing, though its direct electoral impact was debated.

3. **Assassination attempt (July 13) ↔ RNC unity**: The attempt unified the Republican convention around Trump, reinforcing his "persecution narrative" and potentially shifting swing-state perceptions.

## Forecasting Lessons

1. **Sitting VP renomination is the strongest baseline for any VP question**: The probability that a sitting VP is dropped from the ticket is <5% in modern US politics. Any VP question that doesn't account for this baseline will overestimate change.

2. **Gender-balancing dominates VP selection for female nominees**: Both major-party female nominees (Clinton 2016, Harris 2024) chose male running mates. This is structural, not coincidental — campaign strategists view same-gender tickets as demographically concentrated and risk-prone.

3. **Exclusion-list questions about VP picks are systematically biased toward NO**: The vetting process is transparent and media-covered. The plausible candidate pool is finite (10-20 names). Exclusion lists of 8+ names for single-gender questions capture most of the viable universe.

4. **The withdrawal cascade changes everything**: Questions phrased generically ("the 2024 Democratic VP nominee") that don't account for the possibility of a nominee change are structurally fragile. A two-path analysis (Biden stays vs. Harris becomes nominee) produces more robust estimates than a single-nominee analysis.
