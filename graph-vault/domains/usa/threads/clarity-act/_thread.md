# Clarity Act — Digital Asset Regulatory Framework

## Overview

The Clarity Act (officially titled "Clarity for Digital Tokens Act" or similar) is proposed US federal legislation that would establish a comprehensive regulatory framework for digital assets, including cryptocurrencies, stablecoins, and tokenized securities. The bill aims to address the long-standing regulatory ambiguity that has characterized US crypto policy.

## Purpose

- Provide clear statutory definitions for digital assets, distinguishing between commodities, securities, and payment stablecoins
- Assign regulatory jurisdiction between the SEC, CFTC, and potentially a new dedicated digital asset regulator
- Create registration pathways for crypto exchanges, custodians, and token issuers
- Establish consumer protection standards for digital asset platforms
- Define stablecoin issuance and reserve requirements

## Legislative Status (as of May 2026)

The Clarity Act is under consideration in the 119th Congress (2025-2026). Key milestones:

1. **House**: Passed the House Financial Services Committee. Awaiting floor vote.
2. **Senate**: Companion bill introduced in Senate Banking Committee. Bipartisan negotiations ongoing.
3. **Administration**: Trump administration has signaled general support for regulatory clarity but specific provisions remain contested.

## Key Political Dynamics

### Bipartisan Support
- Crypto regulation is one of the rare issues with genuine bipartisan interest in the 119th Congress
- Democrats want consumer/investor protections; Republicans want innovation-friendly framework
- The crypto industry has invested heavily in lobbying (Fairshake PAC spent $200M+ in 2024 elections)

### Opposition Points
- Progressive Democrats: Concern about insufficient consumer protections, climate impact of mining
- Some Senate Republicans: Preference for state-level regulation, opposition to new federal agencies
- SEC/Gensler-era resistance: Career SEC staff and institutional investors prefer securities treatment for most tokens
