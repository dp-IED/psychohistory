# US Government Shutdown Crises

## Overview

A thread tracking US federal government funding crises — shutdown events and near-shutdown episodes where Congress failed or nearly failed to pass appropriations or continuing resolutions before funding deadlines. The thread documents the structural pattern of CR-governance in the 118th-119th Congresses, where a narrow House Republican majority with a procedural veto-holding Freedom Caucus faction created repeated shutdown risk.

The canonical pattern established in this period: a bipartisan coalition (Democratic minority + moderate Republicans) becomes the governing majority for appropriations, but only after one or more failed partisan attempts, creating repeated near-deadline crises.

## Key Events

### 2023

- **2023-Sep**: FY2024 funding deadline (Sep 30). House Freedom Caucus blocks CRs. Speaker McCarthy passes bipartisan CR with Democratic votes (335-91). Representative Matt Gaetz files motion to vacate. McCarthy ousted (Oct 3), becoming the first Speaker removed by a floor vote.
- **2023-Oct-Nov**: Speaker-less for 22 days. House cannot pass legislation. Patrick McHenry serves as speaker pro tempore. Mike Johnson elected Speaker (Oct 25).
- **2023-Nov**: Second CR passed, extending funding to Jan 19/Feb 2, 2024.

### 2024

- **2024-Jan**: Third CR passed (Jan 18), extending to Mar 1/Mar 8.
- **2024-Mar**: Fourth CR passed (Feb 29), extending to Mar 8/Mar 22.
- **2024-Mar 6-23**: First and second minibus appropriations packages passed. Full FY2024 funding completed — no shutdown.
- **2024-Sep 9**: First FY2025 CR proposal (H.R. 9494) introduced with SAVE Act voting reform rider.
- **2024-Sep 18**: House REJECTS first proposal 202-220. 14 Republicans defect; Democrats unified in opposition.
- **2024-Sep 25**: Clean CR (Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2025) passes (341-82 House, 78-18 Senate). Funds government through Dec 20.
- **2024-Dec 17**: Bipartisan CR (Further Continuing Appropriations and Disaster Relief Supplemental Appropriations Act, 2025) released. 1,547 pages. Includes $110B disaster aid, farm bill extension, numerous riders.
- **2024-Dec 18-19**: Elon Musk posts 100+ times on X opposing the bill. Trump and Vance release statement opposing it. Johnson abandons the bipartisan deal — a critical inflection point where a non-officeholder (Musk) effectively killed a funding bill.
- **2024-Dec 19**: Second proposal (American Relief Act, 2025, version 1) — stripped of most riders but includes debt ceiling suspension through 2027. FAILS 174-235 with 38 Republicans defecting.
- **2024-Dec 20**: Third proposal (American Relief Act, 2025, version 2) — same as v1 but without debt ceiling suspension. PASSES 366-34 / 85-11. Biden signs. Funding extended through Mar 14, 2025. Technical funding lapse occurred (deadline passed before signing) but OMB did not activate shutdown procedures.

### 2025

- **2025-Mar**: Full-Year Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act passed 217-213 (House), 54-46 (Senate). 9 Senate Democrats provide decisive votes for cloture. Schumer's decision to advance the bill despite Democratic opposition prevents a shutdown.
- **2025-Oct 1 - Nov 12**: **Actual shutdown** (2025 United States federal government shutdown). 43-day shutdown — longest in US history. Triggered by Democratic opposition to a CR that did not extend expanded ACA subsidies expiring Nov 2025. Resolved when a bipartisan deal put the ACA extension to a standalone vote.

## Key Dynamics

### 1. Narrow House Majority + Procedural Veto

The Republican House majority (218-212 in 2023-2024, ~220-215 in 2025) gives individual factions outsized power. Freedom Caucus members on the Rules Committee (3 out of 9-13 members) can block any bill from reaching the floor. This means:

- **Partisan bills fail**: The majority cannot pass appropriations alone because the far-right faction opposes both Democratic compromises AND pure Republican bills as insufficiently conservative.
- **Bipartisan bills are necessary but costly**: Every successful funding bill requires Democratic votes, which requires Democratic concessions, which further inflames the Freedom Caucus.
- **Multiple attempts per deadline**: The pattern requires 2-3 failed partisan attempts before a bipartisan bill passes, often after the deadline has technically passed.

### 2. The Bipartisan Governing Coalition

Starting with the CR that caused McCarthy's ouster (Sep 2023), Democratic Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries became the de facto key decision-maker for government funding. The pattern:

- A partisan CR fails (Freedom Caucus blocks or defects).
- Johnson/Jeffries negotiate a bipartisan CR.
- Democrats provide the majority of votes for passage.
- Hardline Republicans vote against but cannot block because suspension-of-rules requires only 2/3 majority (bypassing Rules Committee).

This makes Jeffries the most powerful person in shutdown forecasting: if he supports a CR, it passes; if he opposes, it fails (unless the majority can pass it alone, which it cannot).

### 3. Musk-Trump Intervention (New Dynamic)

The December 2024 episode introduced a novel variable: **a non-officeholder (Elon Musk) killed a bipartisan funding deal through social media pressure, forcing the president-elect to oppose it.** This creates a new forecasting dimension:

- Musk's influence over Trump on budget matters is an emergent variable.
- Trump's post-election but pre-inauguration leverage over congressional Republicans is ambiguous.
- The episode demonstrates that CR negotiations are no longer purely congressional — they involve external actors with social media amplification.

### 4. Technical Funding Lapses vs. Formal Shutdowns

The Dec 2024 episode established an important distinction: **funding can lapse briefly without OMB activating shutdown procedures**. The OMB has discretion to continue operations when passage is imminent. This means:

- A "shutdown" for forecasting purposes depends on the resolution criteria.
- Polymarket questions may define shutdown differently from government practice.
- The distinction between a funding lapse (bill passed late) and an actual shutdown (OMB furloughs workers) matters for market resolution.

### 5. FTC Rule (ACA Subsidy Clock)

The 2025 43-day shutdown was triggered by a time-bound policy expiration (ACA subsidy cliff in Nov 2025). Time-bound expirations create hard deadlines that make compromise less likely because each party's leverage peaks at different points relative to the expiration date. This dynamic is distinct from standard CR negotiations and requires separate modeling.

## Forecasting Significance

This thread enables systematic analysis of future US government shutdown questions:

- **The structural baseline** is that funding will pass, but only after 1-2 failed attempts, often past the deadline.
- **The risk factors** are: (1) Freedom Caucus procedural veto, (2) Musk/Trump intervention, (3) time-bound policy expirations, (4) Democratic willingness to provide votes.
- **The key actors**: Mike Johnson (Speaker), Hakeem Jeffries (Minority Leader), House Freedom Caucus (procedural blockage), Elon Musk (shadow intervention), Donald Trump (influence over House Republicans).
- **The temporal pattern**: CR deadlines cluster around Sep 30, Dec 20, Mar 14. Know the calendar.
- **The distinction that matters**: Funding lapses vs. formal shutdowns. Check resolution criteria carefully.
