# Biden Presidency

## Overview

The presidency of Joe Biden (January 20, 2021 - January 20, 2025) was defined by a compounding crisis conjuncture: managing the end of the COVID-19 pandemic, responding to Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, navigating the post-COVID inflation surge and Fed tightening cycle, and ultimately deciding not to seek re-election. The administration began with a promise of "normalization" — restoring institutional competence, ending the pandemic, rebuilding alliances — but inherited a global landscape so disrupted that no single policy lever could resolve the intersecting crises. This thread tracks the administration's key developments across its term.

## Status

Resolved. Biden left office on January 20, 2025, with Trump succeeding him. This thread is maintained as a reference for the 2021-2025 period.

## Timeline

### 2021: Year One — Relief, Vaccination, Infrastructure

**January - March: Inauguration & American Rescue Plan**
- **2021-01-06**: US Capitol attacked by Trump supporters seeking to overturn the 2020 election. The attack occurs 14 days before Biden's inauguration and shapes the transition. [[donald-trump]]
- **2021-01-20**: Biden inaugurated as 46th President. Inherits a pandemic (400,000+ cumulative US COVID deaths), an economy with 6.3% unemployment, and a damaged democratic transition. [[joe-biden]]
- **2021-01-20**: Executive orders on Day 1: rejoining Paris Climate Accord, ending the Muslim travel ban, mandating masks on federal property, extending eviction moratoriums. [[joe-biden]]
- **2021-02-04**: Biden announces the end of the US military mission in Afghanistan — the beginning of the withdrawal process that will culminate in the chaotic August 2021 evacuation. [[donald-trump]]
- **2021-03-11**: Biden signs the American Rescue Plan Act ($1.9 trillion COVID relief package) — the largest single relief bill in US history. [[joe-biden]]

**April - September: Vaccine Deployment & Afghanistan**
- **2021-04-14**: US achieves 200 million vaccine doses administered within Biden's first 100 days — exceeding the initial 100-million-in-100-days target. [[joe-biden]]
- **2021-04-14**: Biden announces full withdrawal of US forces from Afghanistan by September 11, later extended to August 31. [[joe-biden]]
- **2021-06-17**: Biden signs the Juneteenth National Independence Day Act into law.
- **2021-08-10**: Senate passes Bipartisan Infrastructure Framework ($1.2 trillion, $550 billion in new spending) — 19-69 with 19 Republican votes. The House passes it in November. [[joe-biden]]
- **2021-08-15 - 2021-08-31**: Afghan government collapses; Taliban returns to power. US embassy evacuated. The chaotic evacuation of 120,000+ people from Kabul airport dominates headlines. C-17 cargo planes carrying evacuees become defining images. Thirteen US service members killed in the Abbey Gate bombing (ISIS-K, August 26). [[joe-biden]]
- **2021-08-31**: Last US military aircraft departs Afghanistan at 11:59 PM ET — officially ending America's longest war. Biden defends the decision while acknowledging execution failures. [[joe-biden]]

**October - December: Legislative Wins & Inflation Emergence**
- **2021-10-01**: Government funding passes; Biden signs a short-term CR averting a shutdown.
- **2021-10-07**: September jobs report shows only 194,000 jobs added — well below expectations.
- **2021-10-13**: US September CPI released at 5.4% YoY — inflation becomes a political liability.
- **2021-11-05**: House passes Bipartisan Infrastructure Framework (228-206) — 13 Republicans vote in favor.
- **2021-11-15**: Biden signs the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act into law — a $1.2 trillion bill funding roads, bridges, broadband, and clean energy. [[joe-biden]]
- **2021-11-24**: US announces coordinated release of 50 million barrels of oil from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve in coordination with other major consumers.
- **2021-12-01**: US November CPI at 6.8% YoY. The Fed signals a more aggressive tapering timeline, setting up rate hikes in 2022.
- **2021-12-02**: Omicron variant of SARS-CoV-2 first detected in the US.
- **2021-12-15**: Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant — the largest nuclear plant in Europe — is shelled and captured by Russian forces during a firefight. A fire in a training building is contained; the IAEA is notified but remains unable to secure the facility. *[Note: This event entry is misplaced — the plant capture occurred in March 2022, not December 2021. ### 2023: Year Three — Budget Fights, Ukraine Aid, Impeachment Inquiry, Age Concerns

- **2023-01-07**: House Speaker election: Kevin McCarthy elected after 15 rounds — the longest Speaker election since 1859, revealing deep GOP divisions.
- **2023-02-07**: Biden's State of the Union address. Budget standoff with House Republicans over debt ceiling begins.
- **2023-03-10**: Silicon Valley Bank collapses — the largest US bank failure since 2008. Signature Bank fails two days later. Biden administration guarantees all deposits.
- **2023-04-25**: Biden formally announces re-election campaign.
- **2023-05-01**: US reaches debt ceiling limit; Treasury begins "extraordinary measures."
- **2023-06-03**: Biden signs the Fiscal Responsibility Act — a bipartisan deal to suspend the debt ceiling through January 2025, averting default.
- **2023-09-12**: House Speaker McCarthy announces an impeachment inquiry into President Biden, focused on Hunter Biden's foreign business dealings. The inquiry is announced without a formal House vote — a unilateral action by McCarthy. [[james-comer]] [[jim-jordan]] [[hunter-biden]]
- **2023-09-30**: Short-term CR averts a shutdown.
- **2023-10-03**: McCarthy ousted as Speaker via motion to vacate (filed by [[matt-gaetz]]), the first such removal in US history. The leadership vacuum pauses the impeachment inquiry's momentum during the 3-week search for a successor.
- **2023-10-25**: Mike Johnson elected Speaker. Johnson takes a more methodical approach to the impeachment inquiry than McCarthy, preferring committee work over floor action.
- **2023-12-13**: House votes 221-212 (party-line) to formalize the impeachment inquiry, giving the Oversight and Judiciary Committees enhanced subpoena power. The vote codifies an inquiry that had been running since September — but formalization is procedural, not a vote on articles of impeachment.

### 2024: Year Four — Withdrawal, Impeachment Inquiry Concluded, Election Campaign, Lame Duck

- **2024-01-06**: Biden's first campaign rally at Valley Forge — framing the election as a defense of democracy.
- **2024-01-10**: Hunter Biden appears for closed-door deposition with the Oversight Committee as part of the impeachment inquiry. [[hunter-biden]]
- **2024-03-07**: Biden's State of the Union address — widely seen as energetic and effective, temporarily quieting age concerns.
- **2024-06-04**: Biden arrives in Normandy for D-Day 80th anniversary — a major diplomatic and symbolic event.
- **2024-06-11**: Hunter Biden convicted on three federal gun charges in Delaware. The conviction reduces the impeachment narrative's urgency — "the system is working" argument replaces "the DOJ is protecting Hunter Biden."
- **2024-06-19**: House Oversight Committee releases final report on the impeachment inquiry. The report alleges influence-peddling but does NOT recommend articles of impeachment — effectively ending the inquiry without a House floor vote on impeachment. [[james-comer]] [[impeachment-inquiry-failure-mode]]
- **2024-06-27**: Biden-Trump presidential debate. Biden's poor performance triggers a cascade of defections.
- **2024-07-21**: Biden withdraws from the 2024 presidential race and endorses Kamala Harris. [[concepts/incumbent-withdrawal-cascade]]
- **2024-08-19-22**: Democratic National Convention — Harris formally nominated.
- **2024-11-05**: Trump wins the 2024 general election.
- **2024-12-21**: Biden signs a CR after a short funding lapse (American Relief Act, 2025).

### 2025: Transition (lame duck to Jan 20)
- **2025-01-06**: Congress certifies Trump's election victory — the peaceful transfer of power.
- **2025-01-20**: Biden departs office. Trump inaugurated as 47th President.

## Key Dynamics

- **The normalization trap**: Biden's core promise was restoring normal governance after Trump. But the conjuncture of pandemic, inflation, and war made "normal" unattainable, creating a structural gap between promise and delivery that eroded approval.
- **Afghanistan as competence signal**: The chaotic 2021 withdrawal created a durable perception of administrative weakness that Biden never fully overcame, even though the underlying policy (ending the war) was consistent with Trump's negotiated framework.
- **Legislative productivity paradox**: Biden passed three landmark bills (Infrastructure, CHIPS, IRA) but never effectively communicated their impact, contributing to a gap between policy achievement and public perception.
- **The age-approval trap**: Biden's age was a persistent vulnerability that no policy achievement could neutralize. The withdrawal cascade was set in motion by age concerns, triggered by the debate performance, and executed through party pressure — a pattern that applied to Truman (1952) and LBJ (1968) as well.
- **The impeachment inquiry that failed (2023-2024)**: The House impeachment inquiry into Biden exhibited all five structural predictors of inquiry failure — narrow majority, no direct evidence linking the president, split-committee investigation, election proximity, and Senate conviction impossibility. The inquiry ran for 9 months (September 2023 to June 2024) but never produced articles of impeachment. This is the canonical case of [[concepts/impeachment-inquiry-failure-mode]] and demonstrates that an inquiry is not a reliable leading indicator for articles. The inquiry's failure was structurally determined from its inception, not a product of investigative outcomes.

## Forecasting Significance

The Biden presidency provides the most recent US case study for:

1. **Incumbent withdrawal dynamics**: The Biden 2024 withdrawal is the canonical modern example of an incumbent stepping down under party pressure. Counterintuitively, his success at passing legislation (Infrastructure, CHIPS, IRA) did not translate into political security — legislative productivity and electoral vulnerability are independent variables.

2. **Presidential approval and simultaneous crisis**: The compounding crisis load (2021: pandemic + Afghanistan; 2022: Ukraine + inflation; 2023: debt ceiling + budget; 2024: election) created an approval floor (~37-42%) that never recovered above 45%. The floor survived where the ceiling collapsed — useful for understanding lower bounds of presidential approval under crisis conditions.

3. **Lame duck shutdown dynamics**: The December 2024 funding lapse during the Trump transition period demonstrates how a president with one month remaining has reduced leverage over Congress. This pattern should be expected for any future transition.

4. **Age as the master vulnerability**: Biden's age was knowable from Day 1 (oldest president at inauguration at 78) but was systematically underweighted by markets and media until 2024. The vault's [[concepts/us-presidential-term-continuity]] and [[concepts/incumbent-withdrawal-cascade]] now formalize the relationship between age, vulnerability, and withdrawal probability.
