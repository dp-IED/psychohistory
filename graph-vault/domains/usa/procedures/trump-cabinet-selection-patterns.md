# Procedure: Trump Cabinet Nomination Prediction

## When to Use
Any question asking whether Donald Trump (as president-elect or president) will nominate a specific person for a cabinet-level position, or which person he will select for a specific role.

## Pre-Forecast Checklist

### Step 1: Identify the Cabinet Portfolio Type
Determine which category the position falls into:

| Category | Roles | Selection Dynamic |
|----------|-------|-------------------|
| High-credibility foreign policy | State, Defense, National Security Advisor, UN Ambassador | Establishment-credibility threshold applies. Trump picks someone who reassures allies and manages Senate confirmation smoothly. |
| High-credibility economic | Treasury, Commerce, USTR | Markets-and-trade signal. Prefers business figures or establishment-credible pols who can navigate markets. |
| Enforcement/loyalty | Attorney General, FBI Director, Homeland Security, CIA | Pure loyalty picks. No establishment credibility needed — these positions are enforcement tools. |
| Low-visibility domestic | Interior, Energy, Agriculture, HUD, Education, Transportation, Labor, HHS, VA | Mix — VP finalist placement, campaign loyalist reward, industry stakeholder. |

### Step 2: Identify the Personnel Pool
Check these sources in order:
1. **VP finalists not selected**: Rubio → State, Burgum → Interior, Scott → ??? Stefanik → UN Ambassador. These are the highest-probability cabinet candidates. Determine elimination reason (procedural vs. personal).
2. **Former Trump administration members**: People who served in Trump's first term but left before 2020.
3. **Campaign surrogates and loyalists**: People who defended Trump publicly during the 2020-2024 wilderness period.
4. **Congressional allies**: Sitting Senators and House members with committee jurisdiction over the relevant portfolio.
5. **Business allies**: Donors and business figures (rarely for high-visibility roles; more common for Commerce, Energy).

### Step 3: Apply the Rival-to-Ally Pipeline Lens
For any candidate with a history of criticizing Trump:
- **Stage 1 (Active Rivalry)**: Not yet a viable candidate — needs at least Stage 2+.
- **Stage 2 (Endorsement)**: Viable for lower-visibility roles but unlikely for high-credibility roles unless Stage 3 is advanced.
- **Stage 3 (Loyal Service, 2+ years)**: Viable for any role.
- **Stage 4 (Prior Appointment)**: Already received administration role once.

### Step 4: Assess Senate Confirmation Viability
- Is the nominee a sitting Senator? If yes → 90%+ confirmation probability (Senators rarely reject colleagues).
- Does the president's party have Senate majority? If yes → smooth path. If no → high-visibility roles face tougher scrutiny.
- Does the nominee have a scandal or vetting problem? Apply Step 2 elimination-reason analysis.

### Step 5: Cross-Check Timing
- **Post-election transition (Nov-Jan)**: All major cabinet nominations announced here. The first announced nomination is the establishment-signal pick.
- **First year of term (Jan-Dec)**: Remaining confirmations processed; some secondary nominations.
- **Mid-term (Year 2-3)**: Replacement nominations only (resignations, deaths).
- **Late term (Year 4)**: Low probability for new nominees unless mid-crisis.

### Step 6: Probability Calibration

| Scenario | Probability |
|----------|------------|
| VP finalist (procedural elimination) for high-credibility role | 60-80% |
| VP finalist (procedural elimination) for low-visibility role | 40-60% |
| Non-VP-finalist establishment figure for State/Treasury/Defense | 20-40% |
| Pure loyalist for enforcement role (AG, FBI) | 50-80% |
| Campaign surrogate for any role | 15-30% |
| Business ally/donor for Commerce/Energy | 25-40% |

## Recent Confirmed Patterns (Trump 2024-2025 Transition)

| Position | Nominee | VP Finalist? | Portfolio Type | Confirmed |
|----------|---------|-------------|----------------|-----------|
| VP | JD Vance | Yes (selected) | Campaign | Confirmed Jul 2024 |
| State | Marco Rubio | Yes (procedural elimination) | High-credibility foreign policy | Confirmed Jan 2025 |
| UN Ambassador | Elise Stefanik | Yes (procedural elimination) | High-credibility foreign policy | Confirmed Jan 2025 |
| Interior | Doug Burgum | Yes (procedural elimination) | Low-visibility domestic | Confirmed Jan 2025 |

**Pattern confirmation rate**: 3/4 VP finalists received administration positions (75%). The only VP finalist who did not (Tim Scott) remained in the Senate where his vote was more valuable than a cabinet position.
