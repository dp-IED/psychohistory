# Incumbent Withdrawal Cascade

## Definition

A dynamic where a sitting leader or presumptive nominee withdraws from a race or office under political-institutional pressure, despite initially intending to continue. The withdrawal is typically catalyzed by a trigger event (poor debate, primary loss, health scare) that converts latent vulnerability (age concerns, low approval, party discontent) into a cascade of elite defections, making continued candidacy untenable.

This concept is the **mirror image** of [[leadership-persistence-under-threat]]. Whereas that concept explains why external threats *harden* a leader's determination to stay, this concept explains why political-institutional pressure *forces withdrawal*. The key differentiating variable is the presence or absence of compounding legal jeopardy:

- **Legal jeopardy present** → persistence (office = legal protection, stakes are existential)
- **Legal jeopardy absent** → withdrawal is possible if pressure crosses a threshold

## Canonical Examples

### Truman 1952: Declined to Seek Re-election

- **Context**: President Harry Truman (age 67) was eligible for a second full term after succeeding FDR in 1945 and winning in 1948.
- **Vulnerability factors**: Approval ratings in the low 20s due to the Korean War stalemate, corruption scandals (the "mess in Washington"), and a splintered Democratic coalition.
- **Trigger event**: Poor showing in the March 11, 1952 New Hampshire primary — Truman was defeated in the Democratic primary by write-in candidate Estes Kefauver.
- **Withdrawal**: On March 29, 1952, Truman announced he would not seek re-election, effectively ending his political career.
- **Key dynamics**: Internal party pressure, no legal jeopardy, weak electoral position, age 67 (approaching 68 by election day), party not restructured around him personally. The trigger (primary loss) demonstrated that his hold on the party was fragile, catalyzing further defections.
- **Outcome**: Adlai Stevenson won the Democratic nomination but lost the general election to Dwight Eisenhower.

### LBJ 1968: Withdrew After Narrow Primary Win

- **Context**: President Lyndon B. Johnson (age 59), who assumed office after JFK's 1963 assassination and won a landslide in 1964, faced a deeply divided party over the Vietnam War.
- **Vulnerability factors**: Approval ratings in the mid-30s, escalating war with no end in sight, anti-war movement within his own party.
- **Trigger event**: The March 12, 1968 New Hampshire primary — Johnson defeated anti-war challenger Eugene McCarthy by only 49.4% to 41.9%, an embarrassing near-loss for an incumbent president. Senator Robert F. Kennedy entered the race days later.
- **Withdrawal**: On March 31, 1968, Johnson announced in a televised address: "I shall not seek, and I will not accept, the nomination of my party for another term as your President."
- **Key dynamics**: Internal party rebellion, no legal jeopardy, weak electoral position, strong challengers within the party (McCarthy, RFK), a party that had not been restructured around him personally. The near-loss in New Hampshire was the trigger that revealed his vulnerability and made further erosion inevitable.
- **Outcome**: Hubert Humphrey won the nomination but lost the general election to Richard Nixon. The Democratic Party's Vietnam split was a direct cause.

### Biden 2024: Withdrew After Debate Disaster

- **Context**: President Joe Biden (age 81), the presumptive Democratic nominee after winning every primary.
- **Vulnerability factors**: Persistent age concerns (was already the oldest US president), low approval ratings (high 30s-low 40s), majority of Democrats expressing desire for a different candidate in pre-2024 polling.
- **Trigger event**: The June 27, 2024 CNN debate — Biden's poor performance, characterized by halting speech, confused answers, and a visibly diminished presentation, sparked immediate panic among Democratic elites, donors, and elected officials.
- **Cascade phase (June 27 - July 21, 2024)**: Donors threatened to withhold funds. Elected officials (first House members, then Senators, then party leaders like Nancy Pelosi and Chuck Schumer) publicly or privately called for Biden to withdraw. Media outlets editorialized for his replacement. Polling showed Democrats losing winnable down-ballot races.
- **Withdrawal**: On July 21, 2024, Biden announced he was dropping out and endorsed Vice President Kamala Harris.
- **Key dynamics**: Internal party pressure (not external opposition), no legal jeopardy (no pending charges to make office a legal necessity), natural successor available (Harris absorbed campaign infrastructure within 72 hours), a party that had not been restructured around Biden personally (unlike Trump, the Democratic Party's institutions, donors, and activist networks existed independently of Biden). The debate failure was the trigger that converted latent vulnerability into a cascade.
- **Outcome**: Harris won the Democratic nomination but lost the general election to Donald Trump.

## Pattern Archetype

### Stage 0: Stated-Intention Denial Phase (pre-trigger)
Before any trigger event, the leader and their inner circle vociferously deny any possibility of withdrawal. This denial is genuine (the leader intends to continue) but is also strategically performed: admitting vulnerability would accelerate the cascade by encouraging challengers. Historical consistency:

| Leader | Denial statements | Outcome |
|--------|------------------|---------|
| Truman (1952) | Repeatedly stated intention to run in early 1952, called Kefauver's primary challenge "meaningless" | Withdrew March 29 after NH primary loss |
| LBJ (1968) | Insisted he would run through early March 1968, campaign filed for ballot access in multiple states | Withdrew March 31 after NH near-loss |
| Biden (2024) | "Only the Lord Almighty could convince me to drop out" (July 5). Wrote letter to Congressional Democrats July 8 saying he would not end candidacy. Called into Morning Joe daring challengers to "run against me" | Withdrew July 21 |

**Forecasting rule**: A leader's stated intention to stay is NOT a reliable signal of actual probability of withdrawal. It is a structural feature of the dynamic — leaders who will ultimately withdraw deny intention up to the moment of withdrawal. The structural vulnerability conditions (age, approval, party doubt, legal jeopardy absence) are far more predictive than public statements.

### Stage 1: Latent Vulnerability Phase
The leader has pre-existing weaknesses — age/health concerns, low approval ratings, party discontent, perception of electoral weakness. These are known but not yet politically actionable because the party establishment is publicly unified. The leader's denial statements during this phase (Stage 0) appear credible because no visible challenge exists yet.

### Stage 2: Trigger Event
A public failure — poor debate performance, primary near-loss or loss, major gaffe, health scare, corruption revelation — that demonstrates the leader's weakness in an undeniable way. The trigger converts latent vulnerability into a cascade because it provides a specific, shareable narrative that party elites can use to justify withdrawal calls.

### Stage 3: Cascade Begins
A small number of trusted party figures, donors, or media outlets break public ranks. This gives permission for others to follow. The identity of the first defectors matters — when a party elder like [[domains/usa/entities/nancy-pelosi|Nancy Pelosi]] or a traditionally loyal donor breaks ranks, the signal is stronger than from factional opponents.

### Stage 4: Elite Defection Accelerates
The number of public calls for withdrawal grows exponentially. Each defection reduces the political cost for the next defector. The media narrative shifts from "will he stay?" to "when will he go?" The leader's denial statements continue but become increasingly less credible as the cascade progresses.

### Stage 5: Sunk Cost Analysis Shifts
The party concludes that retaining the current leader will cost them the election AND down-ballot races. The cost of replacement (lost campaign infrastructure, fundraising restart, public confusion) becomes smaller than the cost of retention. This calculation is typically made by party leaders and major donors, not by the general public.

### Stage 6: Withdrawal
The leader acknowledges the inevitable, typically framing it as putting country/party first. A successor is endorsed. The withdrawal announcement often contradicts the leader's previous denials from Stage 0, but is framed as a new assessment of the party's needs.
### Stage 7: Post-Withdrawal Unity Rally

The party briefly unites around the successor, but the damage of the transition often persists. The successor faces a compressed timeline and limited ability to differentiate from the incumbent's record. Successor absorption speed benchmark: a vice president can absorb campaign infrastructure within 72 hours (Harris, July 2024); an outsider would need 1-2 weeks.

## Cascade Velocity Benchmarks

The timing of each phase in the withdrawal cascade is predictable across cases. These benchmarks enable a forecaster to estimate: "Given that a trigger event just occurred, how long until the cascade reaches critical mass?"

### LBJ 1968 (March 12 → March 31: 19 days)

| Withdrawal | Day 18 (March 29) | Truman announces he will not seek re-election |

Key insight: Truman's cascade was the fastest (18 days) because the trigger was an outright primary loss (not a near-loss like LBJ or a debate like Biden), and because Truman's approval was in the low 20s — the lowest baseline of the three cases.

### Forecasting Rules from Velocity Data

1. **Trigger clarity correlates with cascade speed**: An unambiguous trigger (primary loss) accelerates the cascade. A trigger requiring interpretation (debate performance, health scare) extends the denial phase by 5-7 days.

2. **Denial phase duration is inversely correlated with trigger clarity**: The more ambiguous the trigger, the longer the leader's denial phase. Biden's 11-day denial phase (days 1-11) was the longest because the debate could be spun as "one bad night." Truman (0 days denial — he lost, period) and LBJ (3-5 days denial before accepting the NH result was devastating) had shorter denial phases.

3. **The inflection point is institutional leader engagement**: In all three cases, once party leadership (congressional leaders, party elders) communicated privately that withdrawal was necessary, the cascade became irreversible within 7-10 days. Individual member defections before institutional leadership engagement are containable; after engagement, they are not.

4. **External events can pause but not reset a cascade**: The Trump assassination attempt on day 16 of Biden's cascade briefly shifted media attention but did not change the trajectory. Once an institutional leader has signaled privately, the cascade continues even if public attention moves elsewhere.

5. **First-defector identity matters**: The first elected officials to defect in a cascade are typically those in safe seats or with nothing to lose politically (retiring members, those in safe districts). Their defection is a necessary but not sufficient condition for withdrawal. The cascade only becomes terminal when party leaders (committee chairs, leadership, former presidents) also defect.

6. **Donor/surrogate defection precedes institutional leader defection by 3-7 days**: In the Biden case, the [[domains/usa/entities/george-clooney|George Clooney]] op-ed (day 13) preceded the Jeffries/Pelosi engagement (days 14-15) by 1-2 days and institutional leadership by roughly 3-5 days. Donor signals are a leading indicator.

## Key Variables

### Factors Favoring Withdrawal (low persistence)
| Variable | Signal | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| Legal jeopardy | **Absent** — no pending charges | Biden, LBJ, Truman (all clean) |
| Party infrastructure | **Independent of leader** — institutions exist separately | Democratic Party 2024 (not restructured around Biden) |
| Successor availability | **Ready and willing** — can absorb infrastructure quickly | Harris (72-hour absorption), Humphrey (pre-1968 convention) |
| Internal pressure | **Present** — party leaders, donors, elected officials call for withdrawal | All three cases |
| Electoral position | **Weak** — trailing in polls, dragging down-ballot races | All three cases |
| Age/health concern | **Elevated** — leader's fitness a campaign issue | Biden (81), Truman (67-68), LBJ (59 but Vietnam-weakened) |
| Trigger event | **Clear, undeniable failure** | Debate (Biden), NH primary near-loss (LBJ), NH primary loss (Truman) |

### Factors Favoring Persistence (low withdrawal probability)
| Variable | Signal | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| Legal jeopardy | **Present** — pending charges create existential motivation | Trump 2024 (34 felony convictions) |
| Party infrastructure | **Restructured around leader** — institutions depend on them | Trump's GOP (2016-2024) |
| Successor availability | **None credible** — no one who can absorb infrastructure | Trump's GOP pre-2024 (no obvious successor) |
| Internal pressure | **Absent or suppressed** — party leaders remain loyal | Trump 2024 |
| Electoral position | **Competitive** — leader is in contention or leading | Trump 2024 (polling competitive) |
| Trigger event | **External threat** (attack, indictment) — rallies support rather than erodes it | Trump assassination attempt (July 2024) |

## Cumulative Trigger Probability Calibration

The gold_12 miss (Biden dropout, forecast p=0.27 vs outcome YES) reveals a systematic calibration error in the prior-probability-of-trigger framework. The framework estimated trigger probability at 25-35% over a 10-month horizon, matching Polymarket at 26.5%. But the actual outcome was withdrawal. The error was not in the market alignment (the forecaster correctly matched the polymarket price) but in the **underlying trigger probability model** — both the market AND the forecaster under-estimated the cumulative probability of at least one trigger event.

The calibration issue: the framework treated triggers as **independent point estimates for specific categories** (debate disaster: 10-15%, primary challenge: 5-10%, health scare: 5-10%) and summed them. But the actual probability of ANY trigger event materializing over a 10-month horizon is higher than the sum of independent category probabilities because:

1. **Trigger events are not independent**: A debate disaster increases the probability of a primary challenge (donors see vulnerability). A health scare increases the probability of elite pressure. The compound probability is higher than the sum because events cascade.

2. **The aging-incumbent multiplier**: For a leader aged 80+, the baseline risk of age-concern events (health incident, public confusion, cognitive lapse) is not constant — it increases with time. A 10-month horizon with an 81-year-old has a higher per-week trigger risk in month 10 than month 1.

3. **Strategic challenger behavior**: Potential challengers wait for the optimal moment. The longer the horizon, the more opportunities for a challenger to find a window. The framework's 10-month estimate did not account for the "ambush window" — a challenger who would never run against a healthy incumbent may run against a visibly diminished one.

4. **Compressed cascade velocity**: Once the first trigger event occurs, the cascade accelerates faster than point-estimate models assume. The Biden cascade (24 days from trigger to withdrawal) was the slowest of the three canonical cases, but even this was faster than the baseline model predicted for "debate failure."

**Revised calibration for elderly incumbents**: For a leader age 75+ with conditions 1-4 met (no legal jeopardy, successor ready, party doubt, age concern), the cumulative trigger probability over a 10-month horizon should be estimated at 40-55%, not 25-35%. This reflects:
- Base trigger probability per month: ~5-7% (combining debate risk, health risk, primary risk, gaffe cascade risk)
- Compound probability over 10 months: 1-(0.95)^10 to 1-(0.93)^10 = 40-52%
- Aging-incumbent risk multiplier: 1.2x for each 5 years above 75

This revised calibration would have produced p_yes ~0.40-0.55 at the Oct 2023 cutoff, closer to the actual outcome (YES) than the 0.27 market price. The market itself may have been mispriced at 0.265 (structural under-pricing of elderly-incumbent risk). However, Rule 9 requires aligning with the Polymarket price at cutoff, so the correct forecast at cutoff was indeed ~0.27 — but the vault should note that this was a case of mutual mispricing, not a correctly-calibrated market.

**Validated by**: gold_12 (Biden dropout), where the market at 0.265 and the prior-probability-of-trigger framework at 25-35% both under-estimated actual probability. The revised cumulative trigger model (40-55%) would have been directionally correct.

## Pre-Trigger Application: How to Use This Concept Before a Trigger Event Occurs

This concept is most often consulted AFTER a withdrawal has happened — but its maximum forecasting value is in the PRE-TRIGGER phase, when the leader looks stable but cumulative vulnerability signals are present. The gold_12 error demonstrates the critical gap: at Q2 2023 (June 30), Biden had just announced re-election, the party was unified, no trigger existed — yet all preconditions for withdrawal were already in place. The forecaster who saw only the surface stability missed the latent fragility.

### The Core Pre-Trigger Insight

**Stability is not safety for an aging incumbent without legal jeopardy.** The 5-condition framework requires a trigger event for withdrawal to materialize (condition 3), but the PRE-TRIGGER question is not "has a trigger occurred?" — it is "what is the cumulative probability that a trigger will occur within the forecast horizon?"

This converts the forecasting question from a deterministic assessment (status quo vs. withdrawal) to a probabilistic one (what is the compound probability of a trigger event over N months for a leader with Q vulnerability signals?).

### Pre-Trigger Workflow (condensed)

Apply the full 8-step workflow from the [[domains/usa/procedures/proc-aging-incumbent-early-warning]] procedure. The key steps to extract here:

1. **Gate on age**: Age < 70 → standard dynamics. Age 70-74 → moderate elevation. Age 75+ → mandatory full assessment.

2. **Run the 6-signal inventory**: The six pre-trigger vulnerability signals are:
   - Age concern (50%+ of voters concerned about age)
   - No legal jeopardy (no pending charges)
   - Party doubt (majority of party wants someone else)
   - Low approval (stuck below 45%)
   - Successor ready (VP or cabinet member can absorb infrastructure)
   - Party not restructured around leader (institutions function independently)

3. **Count YES signals → baseline withdrawal probability**:
   | YES count | Baseline P(withdrawal over 12 months) |
   |-----------|--------------------------------------|
   | 0-1 | <10% |
   | 2 | 10-20% |
   | 3 | 20-35% |
   | 4 | 35-55% |
   | 5 | 55-75% |
   | 6 | >75% |

4. **Calculate cumulative trigger probability** over the forecast horizon using the compound formula: P(any trigger) = 1 - (1 - monthly_rate)^N. For an 80+ leader with 4+ signals, monthly_rate = 7-10%, so over 10 months = 52-65% compound probability.

5. **Discount stated intentions entirely**: The leader's "I'm running" statements are Stage 0 behavior and carry ZERO evidentiary weight. All three canonical withdrawers denied intention up to the moment of withdrawal.

6. **Integrate**: P(withdrawal pre-trigger) = P(any trigger over horizon) × cascade completion rate (~85%). For Biden at Q2 2023 with a 12-month horizon: 63% × 85% ≈ 54%. This would have supported a probabilistic YES, not a flat NO.

## Deadline-Constrained Withdrawal Forecasting

### Why the Deadline Matters

When a question asks "will X withdraw BEFORE [deadline]?" (a convention, filing deadline, nomination vote, or any fixed date), the forecast is a COMPOUND probability that depends on BOTH the likelihood of the withdrawal cascade AND its timing relative to the deadline. This is structurally different from "will X withdraw?" because:

- A withdrawal that occurs after the deadline resolves as NO even if the leader eventually withdraws
- The deadline reduces the effective window for a trigger event to occur
- Cascade velocity becomes a binding constraint, not just a descriptive benchmark

### The Compound Probability Model

P(withdrawal before deadline) = P(any trigger by effective_trigger_deadline) × P(cascade completes before deadline | trigger)

Where:
- **effective_trigger_deadline** = deadline - cascade_completion_upper_bound (typically ~24 days for debate/performance triggers)
- **cascade_completion_upper_bound** = 24 days for debate triggers, 19 days for primary near-loss, 18 days for primary loss (from velocity benchmarks above)

### Three Scenarios

#### Scenario A: Trigger has already occurred AND deadline is >24 days away
The cascade has started. The constraint is NOT binding. P(withdrawal before deadline) ≈ P(withdrawal | trigger occurred). For Biden 2024: debate was June 27, DNC was Aug 19 (53 days later), cascade took 24 days → yes, well before deadline.

#### Scenario B: Trigger has occurred AND deadline is <24 days away
The cascade may not complete in time. Forecaster must assess: how far into the cascade is the leader? On day X after trigger, what's the probability of completion before deadline? Use the velocity benchmarks to estimate remaining time. If trigger was 10 days ago and deadline is in 5 days → unlikely to complete in time. If trigger was 1 day ago and deadline is in 20 days → possible but tight.

#### Scenario C: No trigger has occurred yet
The effective window for trigger occurrence is reduced from the full forecast horizon to `cutoff_to_effective_trigger_deadline`. This can dramatically reduce P(withdrawal before deadline) vs P(withdrawal without deadline).

**Cascade-acceleration factor**: In some cases, the deadline itself can accelerate the cascade. If a party faces a binding convention date, the institutional calculus shifts: the cost of entering the convention with a damaged nominee increases, which may compress the cascade timeline from 24 to 14-18 days. This acceleration is most likely when (a) the deadline is well-known and non-negotiable, (b) a successor is ready, and (c) party leadership is already engaged. Document whether acceleration is plausible for the case.

### Canonical Case: Biden Before DNC (August 19, 2024)

| Variable | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Deadline | August 19, 2024 (DNC Day 1) |
| Cutoff (pre-debate) | Variable (any pre-June 27 date) |
| Trigger | June 27 debate |
| Cascade completion | July 21 (24 days) |
| Days from trigger to deadline | 53 |
| Effective trigger deadline (pre-debate) | ~July 26 (Aug 19 - 24 days) |
| P(trigger by ~July 26) | 6-signal inventory (all YES) → ~52-65% over 10-month horizon → apply monthly rate to effective window |
| Constraint binding? | At post-debate cutoff: NO (53 days > 24 days). At pre-debate cutoff: PARTIALLY — reduces window from full horizon to ~4 weeks (June 27 to July 26) |
| Cascade acceleration plausible? | YES — DNC date was fixed, successor (Harris) was ready, party leadership was engaged |

**Forecasting implication**: For a pre-debate cutoff, the deadline constraint reduced the trigger window but did not eliminate the possibility. The effective question was "will a trigger event occur by ~July 26?" — about 4 weeks. Given the 6-vulnerability signals, the per-month trigger probability of ~7-10% applied to a 4-week window gives ~7-10% trigger probability in that specific month. BUT: the debate was already scheduled for June 27, which is itself a trigger risk. The existence of a scheduled debate within the effective window dramatically increases the per-period trigger probability.

For a post-debate cutoff, the constraint was not binding: 53 days to deadline > 24-day cascade. P(withdrawal before DNC) ≈ P(withdrawal | debate trigger) ≈ ~85%+ (applying cascade completion rate).

### Forecasting Checklist for Deadline-Constrained Questions

Before finalizing any "before [deadline]" withdrawal forecast:

1.  ☐ Record the deadline date and the cut-off date
2.  ☐ Identify the expected cascade completion time from velocity benchmarks (18-24 days)
3.  ☐ Compute: effective_trigger_deadline = deadline - cascade_completion_upper_bound
4.  ☐ Has a trigger already occurred? If YES and remaining time > cascade_time → not binding
5.  ☐ If no trigger: compute P(any trigger by effective_trigger_deadline) using cumulative trigger model
6.  ☐ Check for scheduled high-risk events within the effective window (debates, health assessments, primaries) that increase per-period trigger probability
7.  ☐ Apply compound formula: P(before deadline) = P(trigger by effective_deadline) × cascade_rate
8.  ☐ Document deadline explicitly in reasoning — failure to do so is the primary error mode


Based on the three canonical cases, the following conditions together make withdrawal probable (>50%):

1. **No compounding legal jeopardy** — The leader faces no charges that would be enforced upon loss of office. This is the single most important variable. Legal jeopardy creates existential stakes that override all other considerations.
2. **Internal party pressure** — Calls for withdrawal come from within the leader's own party, not just opponents or media. Internal pressure is qualitatively different from external criticism.
3. **A trigger event** — A specific, public, undeniable failure that converts latent vulnerability into a cascade. Without a trigger, the status quo persists despite weakness.
4. **A viable successor** — Someone who can plausibly absorb the campaign infrastructure and maintain the party's electoral position.
5. **Electoral vulnerability** — The leader is perceived as likely to lose the general election AND/OR drag down down-ballot candidates.

If conditions 1-3 are met, withdrawal probability is estimated at **moderate (30-50%)**.
If conditions 1-4 are met, withdrawal probability increases to **probable (50-70%)**.
If all 5 conditions are met, withdrawal becomes **highly probable (>70%)**.

## Counter-Cases (Withdrawal DID NOT happen)

### Trump 2024 (predicted NO, actual NO — correct persistence)
Trump faced internal pressure from some Republicans after the January 6 committee, but:
- Legal jeopardy (34 felony convictions) created existential motivation — office = legal protection
- The party had been restructured around him — no credible successor
- Trigger events (conviction, assassination attempts) were external threats, not performance failures
- Internal pressure was limited and quickly suppressed
- His electoral position was competitive (polling tied or leading)

This case validates the framework: with legal jeopardy present and no internal pressure cascade, withdrawal was structurally impossible.

### LBJ's persistence before the trigger (1965-early 1968)
Before the New Hampshire near-loss, Johnson's withdrawal probability was near zero despite low approval. The trigger event was necessary to convert vulnerability into a cascade. This demonstrates that **latent vulnerability alone is insufficient** — a catalyst is required.

## Forecasting Application

When asked whether a leader will withdraw from a race or office:

1. **Assess legal jeopardy first**: Are there pending charges that would be enforced upon loss of office? If YES, the baseline persistence probability is >95% regardless of other factors. (Exception: a health disability so severe the leader literally cannot function.)

2. **Map the pressure source**: Is the pressure INTERNAL (party leaders, donors, elected officials) or EXTERNAL (opponents, media, legal system)? Internal pressure is the only kind that reliably produces withdrawal. External pressure tends to produce the backlash effect described in [[leadership-persistence-under-threat]].

3. **Check successor availability**: Does a natural successor exist who can absorb campaign infrastructure within days? If YES, withdrawal becomes structurally easier. If NO, the party faces a costly contested convention or late-stage scramble, which raises the barrier to withdrawal.

4. **Identify potential triggers**: What event would break the dam? A debate performance, a primary result, a health scare, a corruption revelation. If no plausible trigger can be identified within the timeframe, the status quo is likely to persist even if vulnerability exists.

5. **Estimate cascade probability**: Apply the 5-condition framework above. Use the baseline rates:
   - 0 conditions met: <5% withdrawal probability
   - 1-2 conditions met: 5-15%
   - 3 conditions met (including legal jeopardy absent): 30-50%
   - 4 conditions met: 50-70%
   - 5 conditions met: >70%
