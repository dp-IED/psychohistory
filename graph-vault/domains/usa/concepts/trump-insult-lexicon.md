# Trump Insult Lexicon

## Summary

Donald Trump's use of insults and pejorative nicknames is a defining feature of his communication style and a recurring subject of prediction markets (e.g., "Will Trump say [specific word] by [date]?"). This concept formalizes the structure of Trump's insult vocabulary, its frequency distribution, and the contextual triggers that activate different tiers of his lexicon. It is designed to support forecasts about whether Trump will use a specific word or phrase within a given time window.

## The Lexicon Hierarchy

Trump's insults can be categorized into tiers based on usage frequency:

### Tier 1: Daily Drivers
Used in nearly every rally and social media burst. These are his default political epithets.

- "Crooked" (Hillary, media, Dems)
- "Radical Left" (generic political opponents)
- "Fake News" (media)
- "Rigged" (system, elections)
- "Witch Hunt" (investigations)
- "Communist" / "Marxist" (Democratic opponents)
- "Fascist" (opponents who use legal system against him)

**Estimated frequency**: Multiple times per day in total; each individual word appears on average every 1-3 days across all platforms.

### Tier 2: Frequent Rotation
Deployed several times per week when the right target presents itself.

- "Lyin'" (Lyin' Ted, Lyin' Kamala, Lyin' media)
- "Sleepy" (Sleepy Joe — now retired; may attach to new targets)
- "Low Energy" (Jeb Bush — somewhat retired)
- "Birdbrain" (Nikki Haley)
- "Failed" (generic — "failed politician")
- "Corrupt" (generic — "corrupt establishment")
- "Unhinged" / "Deranged" / "Whacko" / "Nutjob" (opponents described as mentally unstable)
- "Sick" / "Disgusting" (behavior Trump condemns)
- "Lightweight" (opponents he dismisses as unserious)
- "Dumb" / "Stupid" (generic intelligence insult)
- "Lazy" (generic work-ethic insult)

**Estimated frequency**: Each word in this tier appears approximately 1-3 times per month.

### Tier 3: Occasional / Context-Dependent
Used when a specific target or event provides the right context.

- "Sleazebag" (used for James Comey 2018, Michael Cohen 2018)
- "Scumbag" (occasional)
- "Degenerate" (occasional)
- "Filthy" (occasional)
- "Lowlife" (occasional)
- "Scam" / "Con" / "Con Man" (occasional)
- "Pathetic" (occasional)
- "Disgrace" / "Disgraceful" (occasional)
- "Dummy" / "Moron" / "Idiot" (less common than "dumb")

**Estimated frequency**: Each word in this tier appears approximately 0-2 times per year. Historical data suggests approximately 3-6 uses per year across the entire Tier 3 set.

## Forecasting Methodology

When asked "Will Trump say [specific word] by [date]?" use the following framework:

### Step 1: Tier Classification
Classify the target word into Tier 1, 2, or 3 based on historical usage patterns. If the word has never been used by Trump, it goes into Tier 4 (unused / novel — essentially zero probability except in response to a specific trigger).

### Step 2: Base Rate Calculation
Multiply the tier-appropriate annualized frequency by the fraction of the year covered by the forecast window:

- **Tier 1**: ~120-365 uses/year → nearly deterministic for any window >48 hours
- **Tier 2**: ~12-36 uses/year → moderate probability (3-10% per month per word)
- **Tier 3**: ~0.5-2 uses/year per word → low probability (0.1-0.4% per month per word before adjustments)
- **Tier 4**: ~0 uses/year → extremely low (depends entirely on specific trigger)

### Step 3: Context Adjustment
Adjust the base rate based on four contextual factors:

1. **Communication volume multiplier**: Is Trump in a high-volume period (primary season, impeachment, war, crisis)?
   - Baseline: 1.0x
   - Midterm primary season: 1.3-1.5x
   - Impeachment/crisis: 1.5-2x
   - Post-election: 0.7x (lame duck)
   - Off-season/golfing: 0.5-0.7x

2. **Target salience trigger**: Is there a specific person or event in the news that naturally invites the target word?
   - If YES, probability increases by 3-10x depending on how tightly the word fits the target
   - If NO, rely on base rate with no upward adjustment

3. **Mimetic / media-echo effect**: If a prominent media figure or opponent uses the word about another target, Trump may adopt it.

4. **Recency effect**: If Trump has used the word in the last 90 days, it is more likely to recur (repetition priming). If not used in over a year, requires a strong trigger to retrieve.

### Step 4: Platform Consideration
"Say" in prediction market contexts typically includes spoken words (rallies, interviews, press conferences, debates) and written words (Truth Social, Twitter/X, written statements). If the word is unusual or complex, it is MORE likely to appear in written form (Truth Social) than spoken form.

## Key Insight: The Selection Bias Problem

When a prediction market exists for "Will Trump say [specific word]?" the very existence of the market introduces selection bias. The question was chosen by a market creator who believes the probability is non-trivial. Markets with volume > $1K suggest informed capital, and the market price itself is a signal. However, markets can also be created as novelty bets. Adjusting for selection bias is the hardest part of this forecasting domain.

**Rule of thumb**: If a Tier 3 word has a prediction market with >$5K volume, the market creator likely has a specific reason to believe the word might be said. The efficient market price should be taken seriously but not at face value — novelty markets tend to be overpriced relative to base-rate models.
