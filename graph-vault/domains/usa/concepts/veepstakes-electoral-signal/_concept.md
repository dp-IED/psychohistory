# Veepstakes Electoral Signal

## Concept

Vice presidential selection is one of the most observable strategic decisions a presidential campaign makes. The choice reveals the nominee's theory of the election: what problem they think needs solving. All VP selections fall into two categories:

### The Balancing Model

The nominee picks a running mate who compensates for a perceived weakness:
- **Geographic balancing**: Pick from a swing state or region where the nominee is weak
- **Ideological balancing**: Pick from a different party faction to unite coalition
- **Demographic balancing**: Pick a different gender, race, or age group
- **Experience balancing**: Pick someone with executive, foreign policy, or military credentials the nominee lacks

**Signal**: The nominee believes their main electoral problem is structural — the coalition has a hole that must be filled by the veep candidate themselves.

**Examples**: McCain/Palin (2008, gender-demographic + base-enthusiasm balancing), Biden/Harris (2020, demographic balancing + party unity)

### The Reinforcement Model

The nominee picks a running mate who amplifies their existing strengths:
- **Message amplification**: Pick someone who tells the same story more effectively
- **Demographic reinforcement**: Pick someone who shares the nominee's identity and authenticates their narrative
- **Style mirroring**: Pick someone with a similar communication style and appeal
- **Low-risk option**: Pick someone who won't create distracting controversy

**Signal**: The nominee believes their main electoral problem is persuasion — they need to communicate their message more effectively, not change coalition math.

**Examples**: Trump/Pence (2016, evangelical reinforcement), Trump/Vance (2024, populist reinforcement), Harris/Walz (2024, Midwestern authenticity reinforcement)

## Forecasting Application

### Step 1: Identify the Campaign's Core Problem
Before forecasting any VP pick, answer: What does the nominee's campaign need most?
- If significant coalition weakness (low base enthusiasm, faction defections) → expect balancing model
- If base is solid but swing voters unconvinced → expect reinforcement model

### Step 2: Predict Veep Traits, Not Names
The reinforcement vs. balancing distinction predicts the TYPE of pick, which narrows the plausible candidate pool:
- Balancing model → candidate with complementary demographic/geographic/ideological profile
- Reinforcement model → candidate who shares the nominee's strongest attributes

### Step 3: Assess the Exclusion-List Impact
When a Polymarket question lists N specific individuals, the question is not "who will be VP?" but "will someone NOT on this list be VP?" The reinforcement vs. balancing framework helps answer: if the pick will be a certain type (e.g., a woman for a male nominee), is the plausible pool of that type captured by the exclusion list?
- If yes → P(YES) < 10% (the universe is saturated)
- If no → P(YES) could be higher if the list missed a plausible candidate of the required type

## Canonical Cases

### 2024: Harris/Walz (Reinforcement)
- Harris's core problem: authenticity and name recognition
- Walz solved this: amplifies Midwestern working-class authenticity
- All finalists (Walz, Shapiro, Kelly, Beshear, Cooper) were male — consistent with gender-balancing constraint
- No woman was genuinely under consideration → "another woman" question structurally NO

### 2024: Trump/Vance (Reinforcement)
- Trump's core problem after assassination attempt: consolidating the base for maximum turnout
- Vance amplified populist-nationalist energy, added youth
- Burgum and Rubio (balancing picks for governing credibility) were passed over

### 2020: Biden/Harris (Balancing)
- Biden's core problem: unifying a party divided between moderate and progressive wings
- Harris balanced ideologically and demographically: Black woman, former prosecutor representing the party's diverse future
- Biden had pledged to pick a woman, narrowing the pool to ~5 contenders

### 2008: McCain/Palin (Balancing)
- McCain's core problem: base enthusiasm gap vs. Obama; age concerns
- Palin balanced: young, female, conservative movement figure, geographically from a non-contiguous state
- The pick energized the base but was a high-risk gamble on vetting quality
