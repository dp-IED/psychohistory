# Mass Sociogenic Event Forecasting

## Definition

A mass sociogenic event (also described as mass panic, mass hysteria, social contagion, or UFO/drone flap) is a recurring social phenomenon where widespread public concern and media attention cohere around perceived anomalous events — mysterious lights, drone swarms, strange sounds — that official investigations ultimately attribute to mundane, routine explanations (misidentified aircraft, celestial objects, authorized drones, atmospheric phenomena). The phenomenon is well-documented across US history and recurs in predictable cycles.

The forecasting relevance: questions about the nature or cause of such events typically resolve NO because official investigations almost never confirm the speculative or conspiratorial explanation. The resolution bar is systematically higher than public belief would suggest.

## Historical Recurrence in the US

The 2024-25 mystery drone wave (New Jersey, Northeast US) fits a documented pattern:

- **1896-97**: Mysterious airship sightings across the US — attributed to airships that didn't yet exist in large numbers. Later analyzed as mass misidentification and hoaxes.
- **World War I (1916)**: Unexplained lights over New Jersey blamed on German espionage. Sociologist Robert Bartholomew draws direct parallels to the 2024 drone wave.
- **1947**: The Kenneth Arnold sighting launches the modern UFO era. The subsequent "UFO flap" saw thousands of reports, most attributed to routine objects.
- **2019-20 Colorado/Nebraska drone sightings**: A wave of reported mystery drones that ultimately remained unexplained but did not result in official confirmation of foreign or malicious activity.
- **2024-25 Northeast US drone wave** (the canonical case for this concept): Thousands of reports over NJ, NY, PA, and other states over 4-6 weeks. Federal investigation (FBI, DHS, DoD, FAA) reviewed 5,000+ reports and concluded most were authorized drones, misidentified manned aircraft, and routine objects. The "nuclear material search" theory — that drones were searching for lost radioactive material — was explicitly denied by the NNSA and NJ DEP. No official confirmation of any anomalous cause was ever issued.

## Forecasting Implications

### Rule 1: The Resolution Bar is Structurally High

Questions that require official government **confirmation** of a speculative explanation (e.g., "searching for nuclear weapons," "foreign surveillance," "extraterrestrial") will systematically resolve NO. Governments have strong institutional incentives against:

- Confirming capabilities they lack (e.g., "we lost radioactive material and need drones to find it")
- Admitting vulnerability to foreign surveillance they cannot stop
- Confirming explanations that invite further scrutiny or litigation
- Confirming explanations that would require admitting prior error

The resolution criteria for these questions require not just that the theory exists or is plausible, but that a government agency explicitly states it as true. This is a high bar.

### Rule 2: The Longer Without Confirmation, the Lower the Probability

Mass sociogenic events follow a characteristic timeline:
- **Days 1-7**: Reports spike, media coverage intensifies, speculation proliferates
- **Days 8-21**: Official statements begin — agencies say "no evidence of threat" or "most sightings have mundane explanations"
- **Days 22-60**: Investigations conclude, joint statements issued, public interest wanes
- **Day 61+**: Residual regulatory changes (counter-drone legislation, flight restrictions) but the event itself fades as a news story

The probability of official confirmation DECLINES with each passing day after the initial report surge. If an event has been ongoing for 3+ weeks without official confirmation of the speculative explanation, the probability of eventual confirmation is near-zero.

### Rule 3: Social Media Intensity is Inversely Correlated with Official Confirmation Probability

Mass sociogenic events in the social media era follow a pattern where online attention — search interest, viral videos, AI-generated or deceptively edited content — peaks before the official investigation concludes. The social media frenzy creates an illusion of substance that is inversely correlated with the actual evidentiary basis. In the 2024 drone wave:
- TikTok hosted the most blatant AI-generated and mislabeled videos
- Viral clips were debunked as two-year-old footage from China, Star Wars props, or drone light shows
- Online search interest dropped from 100 to 2 points by January 12, 2025

High social media intensity is a leading indicator that the event is a mass sociogenic phenomenon, NOT that the speculative explanation has merit.

### Rule 4: Distinguish Civilian Reports from Verified Military Incursions

Mass sociogenic events can coexist with genuine security concerns. In the 2024 drone wave:
- Civilian reports (thousands over NJ suburbs, near power plants, over reservoirs) were almost all misidentified aircraft
- Verified military incursions (350 unauthorized drones over 100+ military bases in 2024) were a separate, genuine security concern
- The Foreign Policy Research Institute analyzed this as a potential "hide in the noise" strategy by foreign actors

Forecasters must treat these as two separate phenomena. A question about the civilian mystery drone wave should not be conflated with the military base incursion data.

## Application to the Canonical Case (Q44: Mystery Drones - Nuclear Search)

The correct NO forecast followed from applying these rules:
1. The resolution bar required government CONFIRMATION of the nuclear search theory
2. Multiple agencies explicitly denied it (NNSA: "no drones used"; DJ DEP: "drones not used to locate the object"; FBI/DHS/DoD joint statement: "nothing anomalous")
3. The lost radioactive object had already been recovered by mid-December without drones
4. The theory came from local officials (Belleville mayor, Congresswoman Malliotakis) — not from federal investigators
