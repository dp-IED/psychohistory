# US Third-Party Ceiling in Presidential Elections

**Redirect**: This concept's canonical home is at [[domains/usa/concepts/us-third-party-ceiling/_concept]], which applies the cross-domain third-party-ceiling-fptp framework to US presidential elections with US-specific structural barriers (Electoral College, ballot access variation, debate commission threshold, campaign finance asymmetry).

Key rule: No third-party candidate has won a single US state's popular vote since George Wallace in 1968. Even candidates with national vote share >18% (Perot 1992) win zero states because US support is distributed too evenly. For state-level questions about third-party victory, start with the state's partisan floor — the same structural feature that prevents a major-party flip also prevents a third-party breakthrough.
