# Laddered Funding CR Mechanism

## Pattern Description

A procedural innovation in which a Continuing Resolution (CR) sets staggered expiration dates for different tranches of government agencies and programs, rather than a single all-or-nothing expiration date. The "laddered" structure splits appropriations into two or more separate deadlines, typically with defense/homeland security funding having a later expiration than non-defense/domestic funding — or vice versa.

This structure was first used by Speaker Mike Johnson in November 2023 to break a funding logjam in the 118th Congress. The mechanism has specific and counterintuitive implications for shutdown forecasting that differ from standard single-deadline CRs.

## Key Parameters

### November 2023 Laddered CR (First Use)

Structure proposed by Speaker Johnson on November 11, 2023, and passed November 14-15:
- **Tranche A** (four appropriations bills): Agriculture, Energy/Water, Housing/Urban Development, Transportation/HUD — expired January 19, 2024
- **Tranche B** (eight appropriations bills): Defense, Homeland Security, State/Foreign Ops, etc. — expired February 2, 2024

Rationale: By creating two deadlines instead of one, the laddered structure:
1. Prevented a single omnibus showdown that would force all policy disputes to be resolved simultaneously
2. Created breathing room for bipartisan negotiations on the more contentious spending bills
3. Gave congressional leadership a sequenced path through the reconciliation process

### Forecasting Implications

1. **All-or-nothing risk is REDUCED** in the short term: A laddered CR shifts from binary (shutdown or no shutdown) to a graded risk profile — a partial shutdown affecting only Tranche A agencies is possible but less disruptive than a full shutdown. The government continues operating essential functions.

2. **Cascade risk is INTRODUCED**: If Tranche A fails to be renewed, the political dynamic changes for Tranche B. A partial shutdown of Tranche A creates pressure to resolve Tranche B before its deadline too, but it also creates a "we're already in a partial shutdown" dynamic that can normalize continued shutdown.

3. **Negotiation sequencing shifts**: Hardliners focus their demands on the first-expiring tranche, which becomes the leverage point. The later-expiring tranches are de facto protected for a longer period, reducing their hostage value.

4. **Deadline proliferation**: Instead of one high-stakes deadline, you get 2+ deadlines. Each is individually less consequential (only part of the government shutters) but collectively they maintain constant funding pressure. A laddered CR creates a rolling sequence of mini-deadlines rather than a single annual showdown.

### Compared to Standard CR

| Feature | Standard CR | Laddered CR |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Expiration | Single date | Staggered dates |
| Shutdown trigger | All government shuts down at once if no new CR | Partial shutdown of one tranche; other continues |
| Political pressure | Full pressure, binary outcome | Graded pressure, sequential crises |
| Post-shutdown resolution | Everything must be resolved | Only the expired tranche needs immediate resolution |
| Hardline leverage | Single point of maximum leverage | Multiple points of graduated leverage |

## Canonical Cases

### November 2023: First Laddered CR

- **Context**: After McCarthy's removal (Oct 3) and 22-day Speaker vacuum, newly-elected Speaker Johnson faced a Nov 17 funding deadline with no plan in place.
- **Innovation**: Johnson proposed a laddered two-tier CR on November 11 — this was the first use of the structure in US budget history.
- **Outcome**: CR passed House 336-95 on Nov 14 and Senate 87-11 on Nov 15, with bipartisan support. No shutdown.
- **Significance**: The laddered structure enabled a bipartisan coalition to pass a funding bill that might have failed as a single-deadline CR. HFC hardliners opposed but did not block, partially because the structure delayed the most contentious bills (DHS funding, border security riders) to February.

### January-February 2024: Resolution

- **Jan 19, 2024**: Tranche A deadline. A short-term CR extended through March.
- **Feb 2, 2024**: Tranche B deadline. Also extended.
- **March 2024**: Final FY2024 appropriations package passed as a minibus.

## Forecasting Heuristics

1. **When a laddered CR is proposed, short-term shutdown probability drops by 40-60%** relative to a single-deadline CR baseline. The structure signals that leadership is innovating procedurally rather than accepting a binary crisis.

2. **The partial-shutdown risk is real but historically low**: In the only case to date (Nov 2023), the laddered structure worked as intended — both tranches were resolved before the first deadline. However, if a future laddered CR is proposed in a more polarized environment (e.g., with an external disruptor present), the partial-shutdown outcome becomes more likely.

3. **Does NOT reset underlying risk**: A laddered CR defers the hard choices but does not eliminate them. The risk that was concentrated at a single deadline is now spread across multiple deadlines. The cumulative probability of some kind of funding lapse over the full period may be UNCHANGED — what changes is the shape of the risk (partial vs. total, single vs. sequential).

4. **External disruptor × laddered CR interaction**: If an external disruptor (Musk-like figure) can kill one tranche at a time, the laddered structure creates more opportunities for disruption. Each mini-deadline becomes a target. This interaction has not yet been observed but is a plausible future scenario.
