# First 100 Days Action Horizon

## Core Insight

The "first 100 days" metric is a persistent reference point in US political forecasting (since FDR set the precedent in 1933). However, it is systematically misunderstood as a window for _any_ action rather than for _specific types_ of action. The first 100 days strongly favors certain policy actions and disfavors others based on structural, not political, constraints.

## What Fits in 100 Days

### Executive orders on high-salience campaign issues
- Can be drafted before inauguration by the transition team
- Signed Day 1 or within first weeks
- Do not require congressional input
- Examples: border security directives, regulatory freeze, agency policy guidance

### Cabinet and subcabinet nominations
- Senate confirmation consumes significant floor time
- Most cabinet positions confirmed within 100 days; subcabinet often lags
- Confirmation battles can absorb White House attention and political capital

### Recess appointments and acting officials
- Faster path for install loyalists without Senate confirmation
- Subject to legal challenge (Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence v. FEC etc.)

### Agency guidance and policy memoranda
- No public comment required
- Can redirect enforcement priorities immediately
- Subject to litigation but not pre-publication review

### High-salience crisis response
- Natural disasters, national security incidents, economic emergencies
- Executive action receives broad deference during crisis
- "Shock and awe" strategy of rapid executive actions to demonstrate control

## What Does NOT Fit in 100 Days

### Legislative policy change (any nontrivial bill)
- Floor time is scarce and consumed by nominations and must-pass legislation (CRs, debt ceiling)
- New legislative proposals require committee hearings, markups, CBO scoring
- Most ambitious legislative agendas are not introduced until 100-200 days

### Major rulemaking requiring APA process
- Notice of Proposed Rulemaking → comment period → final rule → effective date
- Minimum 6-9 months for expedited; 12-18 months typical
- Even for priority rules, the NPRM cannot be published before inauguration (transition teams can draft but not publish)

### Statutory program elimination
- Requires legislation or a legally aggressive executive action
- Legislation cannot pass in 100 days unless already drafted, introduced, and pre-negotiated
- Executive action faces immediate litigation (see [[concepts/program-restriction-vs-elimination]])

### Complex interagency reorganization
- Executive branch reorganization requires planning, legal review, congressional notification
- Past efforts (e.g., creating Space Force, reorganizing DHS) took 12+ months

## Forecasting Heuristic: The 100-Day Budget Constraint

The first 100 days (Jan 20 ~ April 30) overlaps with the end of the first fiscal quarter and typically includes:
- A continuing resolution or full-year appropriations fight (March-April deadline)
- The first budget blueprint submission (February-March)
- Multiple must-pass deadlines (debt ceiling, CR, expiring authorizations)

These budget fights consume White House Legislative Affairs capacity, floor time, and political capital. **Any policy change that requires dedicated legislative floor time or significant OMB/agency staff hours is competing with appropriations for bandwidth.** This systematically biases the first 100 days toward actions that can be done via executive order, agency memo, or existing authority — and against anything requiring legislation or major rulemaking.

## Empirical Track Record

| President | Major legislative wins in first 100 days? | Major regulatory eliminations? |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Trump 2017 | One (ACA repeal attempt failed; tax reform took ~10 months) | DACA rescission announced (later blocked); no program eliminated |
| Biden 2021 | American Rescue Plan (March, day ~60) — pre-existing COVID crisis, budget reconciliation | No program eliminated; reversed Trump regulatory actions |
| Obama 2009 | ARRA stimulus (Feb), Lilly Ledbetter Act (Jan) — limited to pre-crisis/pre-negotiated | No program eliminated |
| FDR 1933 | 15 major bills in "First Hundred Days" — unique crisis context | Multiple programs created, none eliminated |

**Note**: Even FDR (the origin of the 100-days metric) was creating programs, not eliminating them. Elimination is systematically harder than creation in this window because creation needs executive/congressional action once; elimination needs action plus overcoming the constituency that benefits.

## Application: H-1B Elimination Question

For a "will Trump eliminate H-1B in first 100 days" question:

| Factor | Assessment | Bar |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| Statutory basis | INA codified by Congress | Very high — elimination needs legislation or dubious EO |
| APA timeline | Rulemaking cannot be completed in 100 days | Very high |
| Congressional bandwidth | Nominations + CR fight + debt ceiling | Very high |
| Coalition constraints | Tech donors would oppose | High |
| Alternative (restriction) | Available via EO/guidance | Low — makes restriction vastly more likely |
| Crisis trigger | No H-1B-linked national security crisis | Absent |

**Combined**: The probability of H-1B elimination in the first 100 days is structurally near-zero regardless of campaign rhetoric.
