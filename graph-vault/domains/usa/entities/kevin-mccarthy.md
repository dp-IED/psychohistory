# Kevin McCarthy

## Summary

Kevin McCarthy served as the 55th Speaker of the US House of Representatives from January 7, 2023 to October 3, 2023 — the shortest speakership since 1875 and the first terminated by a motion to vacate in US history. His removal by a single-member floor vote (216-210, with 8 Republicans and 208 Democrats voting to vacate) was a watershed event in US budget politics: it demonstrated that a hardline faction of 8 members could depose a Speaker of their own party who relied on Democratic votes to pass funding bills.

The immediate trigger for McCarthy's removal was his passage of a 45-day continuing resolution (CR) on September 30, 2023, which averted a government shutdown by funding the government through November 17. The CR passed with more Democratic than Republican votes, infuriating the House Freedom Caucus. Representative Matt Gaetz (R-FL) filed the motion to vacate on October 2, and the House voted on October 3 to remove McCarthy.

## Significance for Forecasting

- **First-mover precedent**: McCarthy's removal established that the motion to vacate rule (single member can force a vote) is a credible threat, not a theoretical procedure. Every subsequent Speaker (Johnson, 118th and 119th Congress) governs under this constraint. The baseline probability of a Speaker being removed during a narrow-majority Congress is now structurally elevated — any Speaker who passes bipartisan funding deals faces existential risk from a small defection group.

- **Double-edged effect on shutdown probability**: McCarthy's ouster had contradictory implications for near-term shutdown risk:
  1. **Short-term risk REDUCTION**: A newly-elected Speaker has maximum incentive to demonstrate governing competence by avoiding a shutdown on their first funding test (see [[speaker-crisis-paradox]]). Johnson passed his first CR (the laddered November 2023 CR) with bipartisan support, proving he could govern.
  2. **Medium-term risk ELEVATION**: The precedent that a Speaker can be removed for passing a bipartisan CR creates a permanent structural vulnerability. Subsequent funding deadlines (Dec 2024, March 2025) operate under the shadow of another potential removal.

- **Hardline faction satiation**: After achieving their primary tactical objective (removing McCarthy), the HFC temporarily reduced brinkmanship intensity. The act of deposing a Speaker satiated the faction's need to demonstrate power, creating a brief window (weeks to months) where shutdown risk from HFC-originated demands was lower than the baseline.

- **Speaker succession as a forecasting variable**: Any change in Speakership (removal, resignation, death) during a funding crisis changes the probability distribution for shutdown outcomes. The default assumption should be: new Speaker + first funding test = SHORT-TERM SHUTDOWN RISK DECREASED (prove-competence incentive dominates). After the first CR is passed (30-90 days), return to baseline.

## Key Events

- **Jan 7, 2023**: Elected Speaker after 15 rounds of voting — the longest Speaker election since 1859. The protracted election signaled the depth of GOP factional divisions.
- **May 31, 2023**: Negotiated the Fiscal Responsibility Act (FRA) with President Biden to raise the debt ceiling, trading spending caps for a debt limit suspension through January 2025. 71 House Republicans voted against the FRA.
- **Sept 30, 2023**: Passed a clean 45-day CR with Democratic votes to avert a government shutdown. HFC hardliners denounced the deal.
- **Oct 2, 2023**: Rep. Matt Gaetz filed a motion to vacate.
- **Oct 3, 2023**: House voted 216-210 to remove McCarthy — 8 Republicans and all 208 Democrats voting present. First successful removal of a Speaker in US history.
