# US Department of the Treasury

## Role in Debt Ceiling Episodes

The Treasury Department is the operational actor in every debt ceiling crisis:

- **Administers extraordinary measures**: When the debt ceiling binds or reinstates, Treasury can use accounting maneuvers (G-Fund suspension, CSRDF suspension, SLGS suspension) to continue borrowing and paying obligations without new Congressional authorization.
- **Communicates the X-date**: Treasury sends periodic letters to Congress estimating when extraordinary measures will be exhausted. These letters are the canonical reference for debt ceiling timing.
- **Manages the cash balance**: Treasury's daily cash management determines how long extraordinary measures can sustain operations. A larger cash balance at reinstatement provides more runway.
- **Prioritizes payments (if applicable)**: Treasury must decide which obligations to pay if it reaches the X-date without Congressional action — bondholders vs. Social Security vs. military pay. This prioritization authority is legally contested.

## Key Forecasting Role

Treasury's X-date estimate is the single most important data point for forecasting debt ceiling resolution timing. The estimate comes from internal Treasury models and is updated in letters to Congressional leadership (typically the Speaker and Minority Leader, with copies to the Budget and Ways & Means committees).

## Historical Context

Treasury Secretaries under each modern debt ceiling episode:
- 2011: Timothy Geithner (Obama)
- 2013: Jacob Lew (Obama)
- 2017-2021: Steven Mnuchin (Trump)
- 2021-2023: Janet Yellen (Biden) — FRA 2023
- 2023-2025: Janet Yellen (Biden) — lame duck reinstatement
- 2025+: Scott Bessent (Trump)
