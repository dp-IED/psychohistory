## Summary

The New York State Unified Court System is the judicial branch of New York State government. It has a unique naming convention that differs from most US states: the trial-level court of general jurisdiction is called the **New York Supreme Court** (local nomenclature: "Supreme Court, New York County" for Manhattan), not a "Superior Court" or "District Court" as in other states. This naming convention causes frequent confusion in forecasting contexts: a "New York Supreme Court Justice" is a trial-level judge, not an appellate justice.

### Court Structure Relevant to Forecasting

| Court Level | NY Name | Federal Equivalent | Notes |
|-------------|---------|-------------------|-------|
| Trial (general jurisdiction) | Supreme Court | US District Court | Handles felonies, civil cases above $25K |
| Trial (limited jurisdiction) | County Court / City Court | N/A | Misdemeanors, smaller civil cases |
| Intermediate appeals | Supreme Court, Appellate Division | US Circuit Courts of Appeal | Divided into four Judicial Departments (1st = Manhattan/Bronx, 2nd = Brooklyn/Queens/Staten Island/Long Island, 3rd = Albany/Upstate, 4th = Buffalo/Western NY) |
| Highest court | Court of Appeals | US Supreme Court | New York's court of last resort. Despite its name, it is the HIGHEST court, not a trial court. |

### Key Naming Confusion for Forecasters

- **"New York Supreme Court" = trial court.** When Donald Trump was sentenced by "New York Supreme Court Justice Juan Merchan," this was a trial-level proceeding, not an appellate proceeding.
- **"New York Court of Appeals" = highest court.** If a case is appealed past the Appellate Division, it goes to the Court of Appeals.
- **New York County Supreme Court** specifically refers to the Manhattan branch of the Supreme Court (New York County = Manhattan).

### Significance for Forecasting

Understanding the NY court system structure is essential for questions involving:
- **SCOTUS review of NY proceedings**: Because SCOTUS's jurisdiction over state courts is limited (see [[concepts/adequate-independent-state-grounds]]), the SCOTUS-NY court relationship operates differently from SCOTUS-federal circuit court relationships.
- **Appellate timeline estimation**: NY has a three-tier appellate system (Supreme Court → Appellate Division → Court of Appeals), and each tier adds 6-18 months to the timeline.
- **Elective vs. appointed judiciary**: New York State Supreme Court justices are elected (though often through a convention/primary process dominated by party leaders), which influences sentencing behavior in high-profile cases.
- **Sentencing authority**: The trial-level Supreme Court has broad sentencing discretion for felonies, subject to statutory ranges. The sentencing judge's discretion is New York state law, not federal law.

## Key Cases

- **People v. Donald Trump (Indictment No. 71543-2023)**: New York County Supreme Court, Justice Juan Merchan presiding. Trump convicted May 30, 2024 on 34 counts of falsifying business records (Class E felonies). Sentenced to unconditional discharge January 10, 2025.
