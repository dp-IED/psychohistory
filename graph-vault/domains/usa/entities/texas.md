# Texas

## Summary

Texas is the second-largest US state by population and a critical battleground in American politics. Its 38 US House delegation (post-2024: 25R-13D) makes it the second-largest House delegation. Texas has become increasingly competitive at the presidential level — though still Republican-leaning — with suburban districts around Dallas-Fort Worth, Houston, Austin, and San Antonio trending toward Democrats. The 2026 midterm cycle has identified TX-15, TX-28, and TX-34 as competitive Democratic-held seats, while deep-red districts like TX-13 (Cook PVI R+26, represented by Ronny Jackson) remain safely Republican. Texas also serves as a critical node for US energy infrastructure, border security, and tech industry relocation (Austin, Dallas).

## Significance for Forecasting

- **House majority leverage**: Texas's 38 House seats make it a critical lever for control of the US House. The state's competitive districts (TX-15, TX-28, TX-34) are among the most likely Democratic pick-up targets or Republican holds that could determine majority control in 2026.
- **Political realignment bellwether**: Texas's rightward shift in 2024 (Trump outperformed polling) and suburban trends are a key indicator for broader national political realignment patterns. The state tests whether Hispanic voter shifts toward Republicans are durable or specific to the 2024 cycle.
- **Energy and climate forecasting**: Texas's position as the leading US oil and gas producer, combined with its vulnerability to extreme weather (Winter Storm Uri 2021, Hurricane Harvey 2017, heat waves), creates a dual significance for both energy market and climate resilience forecasting.
- **Border and immigration**: Texas's 1,254-mile border with Mexico makes it central to any US immigration policy forecasting. State-level initiatives (Operation Lone Star) and federal border policy intersect directly in Texas.
