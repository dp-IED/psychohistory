# Peggy Flanagan

## Summary

Peggy Flanagan is the Lieutenant Governor of Minnesota (since 2023, serving under Governor Tim Walz). She is a member of the Minnesota Democratic-Farmer-Labor Party (DFL) and a citizen of the White Earth Nation. As of June 2026, she is the frontrunner for the DFL nomination for US Senate in the 2026 election to succeed retiring Senator Tina Smith, trading at 73.5% YES on Polymarket (~$9.9K volume).

## Current Position (June 2026)

- **Role**: Lieutenant Governor of Minnesota (since Jan 2023)
- **Party**: Democratic-Farmer-Labor (DFL)
- **Senate race**: Leading candidate for DFL nomination to succeed retiring Senator Tina Smith
- **Market probability**: 73.5% YES for DFL Senate nomination

## Background

- **LG tenure**: Elected 2022 alongside Governor Tim Walz. Focused on education, tribal relations, and children's issues.
- **State Legislature**: MN House of Representatives (2015-2023), representing District 46A (St. Louis Park, Golden Valley).
- **Progressive profile**: Strong labor ties, Native American representation, universal school meals program champion.
- **Walz succession**: If Walz runs for President (2028), Flanagan would be positioned to run for Governor — the Senate race may be strategic positioning.

## Forecasting Significance

- **Frontrunner dynamics**: At 73.5%, Flanagan is the clear frontrunner but not prohibitive. The 26.5% residual reflects: (a) other DFL candidates (Rep. Angie Craig, AG Keith Ellison, Mayor Melvin Carter) could enter, (b) primary dynamics (Minnesota has open primaries), (c) Walz may not endorse early.
- **Open seat advantages**: No incumbent — Flanagan's LG position gives her the highest name recognition and institutional machine support among potential candidates.
- **General election**: Minnesota is D+1, trending bluer (Walz won by 7.7% in 2022). DFL nominee will be favored in general election but not safe.
- **Key risk**: If Gov. Walz endorses a different candidate (or remains neutral), Flanagan's LG advantage is diminished. Minnesota DFL primaries can be competitive (2018 AG primary: Ellison won with 49.8%).
