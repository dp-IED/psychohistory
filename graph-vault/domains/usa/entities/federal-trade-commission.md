# Federal Trade Commission (FTC)

The FTC is an independent federal agency with dual antitrust and consumer protection jurisdiction. Alongside the DOJ Antitrust Division, it is one of the two primary US antitrust enforcers.

## Antitrust Enforcement Structure
- Enforces Section 5 of the FTC Act (unfair methods of competition) AND Sections 1 & 2 of the Sherman Act (alongside DOJ)
- Both agencies share jurisdiction under Hart-Scott-Rodino merger review — FTC reviews certain sectors (pharma, healthcare, consumer goods, tech) while DOJ reviews others
- FTC can bring administrative complaints before its own administrative law judges (ALJ) as well as in federal court
- FTC lacks criminal enforcement authority (that is DOJ's domain)

## Key Antitrust Actions in Tech (2020-2026)
- **FTC v. Meta (2020-present)**: Monopolization case seeking divestiture of Instagram and WhatsApp
- **FTC v. Amazon (2023-present)**: Monopolization case targeting Amazon marketplace practices
- **Meta/Within challenge (2022-2023)**: Blocked Meta's acquisition of VR fitness app developer — FTC won preliminary injunction in federal court; Meta abandoned the deal
- **Microsoft/Activision challenge (2022-2023)**: Sought to block Microsoft's $69B acquisition; lost in federal court; appealed and lost; deal closed
- **Illumina/Grail challenge (2021-2024)**: Challenged vertical merger in cancer screening; lost in multiple courts

## Forecasting Relevance
The FTC is the sole plaintiff in FTC v. Meta — the agency's enforcement priorities directly determine remedy posture. Key considerations:
- Agency leadership changes with presidential administration (Commission composition shifts)
- Career staff have institutional momentum (cases continue regardless of leadership)
- The FTC's win/loss record in Big Tech cases is mixed (won Meta/Within; lost Microsoft/Activision; Meta and Amazon cases ongoing)
