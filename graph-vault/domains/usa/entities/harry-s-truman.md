## Summary

33rd President of the United States, assuming office after the death of Franklin D. Roosevelt (April 12, 1945). Presided over the end of World War II, the decision to use atomic weapons, the Marshall Plan, the Berlin Airlift, the Korean War, and the recognition of Israel. Won an upset re-election in 1948 against Thomas Dewey.

## Forecasting Relevance: The 1952 Withdrawal

Truman's decision not to seek re-election in 1952 is a canonical example of the [[concepts/incumbent-withdrawal-cascade]] pattern:

- **Vulnerability**: Approval ratings in the low 20s due to Korean War stalemate, corruption scandals ("the mess in Washington"), splintered Democratic coalition.
- **Trigger**: Loss in the March 11, 1952 New Hampshire Democratic primary to write-in candidate Estes Kefauver.
- **Withdrawal**: March 29, 1952 announcement that he would not seek re-election.
- **Key factors**: No legal jeopardy (no pending charges), age 67, internal party pressure, weak electoral position.

Truman's case is the earliest modern example of an incumbent withdrawing under political-institutional pressure rather than legal threat. The pattern — low approval + internal party doubt + absence of legal jeopardy + trigger event — is directly analogous to LBJ 1968 and Biden 2024.
