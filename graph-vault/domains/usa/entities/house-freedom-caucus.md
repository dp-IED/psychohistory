# House Freedom Caucus

## Summary

The House Freedom Caucus (HFC) is a congressional caucus of conservative and far-right Republican House members, founded in January 2015 by a group of Tea Party movement members. The caucus has ~35-45 members in the 118th-119th Congresses. Its structural power far exceeds its numerical strength because of its Rules Committee seats (3 out of 9-13 members) and the one-member motion-to-vacate threshold that makes the Speaker vulnerable to a single disaffected HFC member.

## Significance for Forecasting

The Freedom Caucus is the primary structural cause of US government shutdown risk in the 2023-2026 period:

1. **Procedural blockage**: With 3 seats on the Rules Committee, the HFC can block any bill from reaching the House floor by voting with Democrats to create a 7-7 tie (under the 7R-6D or similar split). This means even bills supported by a majority of Republicans can be blocked.

2. **Motion-to-vacate trigger**: A single member can force a vote to remove the Speaker. This was used to oust McCarthy (Oct 2023). The threat constrains Johnson from compromising with Democrats.

3. **CR opposition pattern**: HFC members consistently vote against continuing resolutions — both partisan and bipartisan ones. In Sep 2023, 21 HFC members voted against the CR. In Sep 2024, 14 Republicans (mostly HFC) voted against the clean CR. In Dec 2024, 38 Republicans (mostly HFC) voted against the second CR.

4. **Internal cohesion**: The HFC is not monolithic. Some members are more pragmatic (willing to accept CRs under protest), while the "no compromise" faction (10-15 members) will oppose any CR on principle. The split within the HFC between pragmatists and purists is a key forecasting variable.

## Forecasting-Relevant Dynamics

- **CR tolerance range**: ~10-15 HFC members will oppose any CR regardless of content. A further 10-15 will oppose CRs that do not include specific conservative policy riders (border security, spending cuts, abortion restrictions). Only 5-10 HFC members can be persuaded to vote for CRs under suspension-of-rules.
- **Total "no" votes available**: For shutdown forecasting, assume 20-30 Republican "no" votes on any CR. The Speaker needs Democratic votes to make up the difference.
- **Motion-to-vacate threshold**: After McCarthy's ouster, the threshold was effectively demonstrated. Johnson survives because HFC members are reluctant to repeat the 22-day speaker-less period. But a single member filing a motion to vacate forces a floor vote — even if it fails, it consumes legislative time and weakens the Speaker.
- **Multiplier effect**: The HFC's influence extends through primary threats. Members who reliably vote against CRs face no credible primary challenge; members who vote for CRs face HFC-backed primary opponents.

## Key Members (Forecasting-Relevant)

- **Jim Jordan (OH-4)**: HFC co-founder, former chair. Judiciary Committee chair. Broker between HFC and leadership.
- **Chip Roy (TX-21)**: Rules Committee member. Key procedural blocker. Vocal CR opponent.
- **Ralph Norman (SC-5)**: Rules Committee member. Pragmatic HFC member who can sometimes be persuaded.
- **Bob Good (VA-5)**: Former HFC chair (2024). Hardline. Known for "don't fear a shutdown" comment.
- **Matt Gaetz (FL-1)**: Filed motion to vacate McCarthy. Unpredictable — sometimes supports CRs, sometimes opposes.

## Timeline

- **2015**: Founded by Tea Party members. Objective: push Republican leadership rightward.
- **2015-2022**: Used Rules Committee seats and procedural tactics to influence legislation. Key role in 2018 shutdown and multiple debt ceiling fights.
- **2023-Jan**: Key role in McCarthy's 15-ballot Speaker election. Secured Rules Committee seats and one-member motion-to-vacate threshold.
- **2023-Sep**: 21 members vote against CR. After McCarthy passes it with Democratic votes, Gaetz files motion to vacate. McCarthy ousted.
- **2023-Oct**: Blocked Scalise and Emmer from Speaker nomination. Supported Johnson.
- **2024-Sep**: 14 HFC members vote against clean CR.
- **2024-Dec**: 38 Republicans (many HFC) vote against second CR proposal.
- **2025-Mar**: HFC split on full-year CR — some support, some oppose. But lack of unified opposition allows passage.
- **2025-Oct**: Role in 43-day shutdown. Maintained unified opposition to CR without ACA extension.
