## Summary

36th President of the United States, assuming office after the assassination of John F. Kennedy (November 22, 1963) and winning a landslide in 1964. Served as Senate Majority Leader before the vice presidency. His presidency was defined by the Great Society domestic programs (Civil Rights Act 1964, Voting Rights Act 1965, Medicare/Medicaid) and the escalation of the Vietnam War.

## Forecasting Relevance: The 1968 Withdrawal

Johnson's withdrawal from the 1968 presidential race is a canonical example of the [[concepts/incumbent-withdrawal-cascade]] pattern:

- **Vulnerability**: Low approval (mid-30s), party split over Vietnam War, anti-war movement within the Democratic Party.
- **Trigger**: Narrow win in the March 12, 1968 New Hampshire primary (49.4% vs. Eugene McCarthy's 41.9%), followed by Robert F. Kennedy entering the race.
- **Withdrawal**: March 31, 1968 televised address: "I shall not seek, and I will not accept, the nomination of my party for another term as your President."
- **Key factors**: No legal jeopardy (no pending charges), internal party rebellion, viable successors (Humphrey, McCarthy, RFK), weak electoral position.

The 1968 case demonstrates that internal party pressure plus a trigger event can force an incumbent to withdraw even when they had every intention of running. This pattern recurred with Biden in 2024.
