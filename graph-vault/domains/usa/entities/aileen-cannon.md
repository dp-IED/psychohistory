# Aileen Cannon

## Summary

Aileen Mercedes Cannon is a United States District Judge for the Southern District of Florida, appointed by President Trump in 2020. She presided over the Trump classified documents case (Case No. 23-cr-80101), which was widely criticized for its slow timeline and unusual procedural rulings that consistently favored the defendant.

Cannon's handling of the documents case became a central example of the "judicial-timing-political-deadline" dynamic: through a series of decisions that delayed pre-trial proceedings, she effectively ensured the case could not reach trial before the November 2024 election. After Trump's election victory, she dismissed the case entirely, ruling that Special Counsel Jack Smith's appointment was unconstitutional — a controversial ruling that the DOJ criticized but which had practical effect given the DOJ's policy against prosecuting a sitting president.

## Significance for Forecasting

Cannon's case demonstrates how a single judge's scheduling discretion can determine the outcome of a politically charged prosecution. In cases where the defendant has strong delay incentives and the judge is sympathetic (or unusually responsive to defense arguments), the case timeline is effectively controlled by the judge, not the prosecution. For forecasts about legal timelines, identifying the presiding judge's past behavior is as important as the legal merits.

## Timeline

- **2020**: Appointed to US District Court (SD Florida) by President Trump
- **June 2023**: Assigned the Trump classified documents case
- **2023-2024**: Series of procedural rulings slow-walked the case (unusual discovery schedule, sealed hearings, granting defense requests for extensions)
- **July 15, 2024**: Judge Cannon dismisses the documents case — later reversed on appeal; case proceeds with reassignment prospect
- **January 2025**: After Trump's election victory, dismisses case on grounds Smith's appointment was unconstitutional
