## Summary

The Democratic National Committee (DNC) is the formal governing body of the Democratic Party of the United States. It coordinates party strategy, supports candidates at all levels, and manages the presidential nominating process — including the delegate selection rules, the primary and caucus calendar, the virtual roll call process, and the quadrennial Democratic National Convention.

## Institutional Role in Forecasting

The DNC is most relevant to forecasting questions about:

1. **Presidential nomination mechanics**: The DNC sets the delegate allocation rules, primary schedule, and convention procedures. These rules determine how delegates are allocated, whether virtual roll calls can replace in-person conventions, and what flexibility exists for late-stage nominee replacement.

2. **Convention procedures**: The Democratic National Convention is the party's supreme nominating body, but its function is **ratification** — it certifies the candidate chosen through the primary process and confirmed by delegate votes. The DNC cannot unilaterally "reinstate" a candidate who has withdrawn, because the convention operates on the candidate slate submitted by the party after the primary process.

3. **Virtual roll call authority**: In 2024, the DNC Rules Committee authorized a virtual roll call (August 5, 2024) before the in-person convention (August 19-22), to ensure the Democratic nominee was certified before Ohio's ballot access deadline. This mechanism was procedurally novel and had implications for the procedural lock-in of Harris's nomination — once the virtual roll call had occurred, there was no procedural path to reinstate Biden.

4. **Primary calendar and delegate rules**: The DNC's Rules and Bylaws Committee determines which states vote when, how delegates are allocated, and what penalties states face for violating scheduling rules. These decisions affect the pace of the nomination race and which candidates benefit from early momentum.

5. **Party governance between conventions**: Between national conventions, the DNC manages party affairs, including fundraising (joint fundraising committees with the presidential campaign), party messaging, and state party coordination. The DNC chair is a consequential figure for party strategy during election cycles.

## Key Figures

- **DNC Chair**: The party's top operational official ([[jaime-harrison]], chair during 2021-2025 cycle). The chair manages the convention, coordinates with the presidential campaign, and represents the party institutionally.
- **DNC Rules and Bylaws Committee**: Sets primary calendar rules and delegate allocation. Chaired by [[minyon-moore]] during the 2024 cycle.

## Key Dates

- **August 2023**: DNC sets 2024 primary calendar with South Carolina first; rules for delegate allocation finalized
- **May 2024**: DNC authorizes virtual roll call to ensure ballot access compliance with Ohio's deadline
- **July 22-24, 2024**: DNC coordinates delegate endorsement cascade after Biden's withdrawal, confirming Harris as successor
- **August 5, 2024**: Virtual roll call formally nominates Kamala Harris
- **August 19-22, 2024**: Democratic National Convention in Chicago; formal ratification of Harris-Walz ticket
