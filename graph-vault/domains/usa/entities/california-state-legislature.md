# California State Legislature

## Summary

The California State Legislature is the state's bicameral legislative body, composed of the 80-member Assembly (2-year terms, no term limits) and the 40-member Senate (4-year terms, max 2 terms). It is a part-time, citizen legislature — members are in session roughly 8 months per year (January through August), with a hard end-of-session deadline of August 31 in the second year of each two-year session. California uses a two-year session cycle: bills introduced in the first year can carry over to the second year, but all unpassed bills die at the end of the second year's August 31 deadline.

## Significance for Forecasting

The Legislature's structural features are critical inputs for any California legislative forecast:

1. **Part-time structure**: The Legislature's part-time nature means members have outside jobs and limited staff. Complex bills require significant external expertise. This creates dependency on advocacy groups and the executive branch for technical drafting.

2. **Legislative deadlines**: The two-year session structure creates predictable binary deadlines:
   - **August 31 of even-numbered years**: Hard deadline for all bills to pass both chambers. Any bill that has not passed by this date dies, regardless of support level. This is the most important temporal constraint.
   - **January**: Session convenes. Bills from the first year carry over.
   - **September 30**: Governor's deadline to sign or veto bills passed before August 31. Bills that are not signed or vetoed automatically become law.

3. **Supermajority override**: California requires a 2/3 vote in both chambers to override a gubernatorial veto. Given the Democratic supermajority (but not 2/3 for all issues), override is practically impossible for controversial bills — making the governor's signature dispositive.

4. **Democratic supermajority**: As of the 2020s, Democrats hold supermajorities in both chambers (roughly 60-62 of 80 in Assembly, 30-32 of 40 in Senate). However, the Democratic caucus is ideologically diverse — ranging from progressive San Francisco Bay Area members to moderate Central Valley and Southern California members. This diversity is most visible on tech regulation, where the party splits between pro-safety/pro-consumer and pro-innovation camps.

5. **Committee gatekeeping**: Each bill must pass through policy and fiscal committees before reaching the floor. Committee chairs have significant power to advance or kill bills. For tech regulation, the Assembly Judiciary Committee and Senate Governmental Organization Committee are key gatekeeping committees.

6. **Gut-and-amend practice**: The California Legislature has a practice where the contents of one bill are removed and replaced with entirely different text (often a controversial bill that couldn't advance through the normal committee process). This bypasses committee review and can accelerate bills at the last minute.
