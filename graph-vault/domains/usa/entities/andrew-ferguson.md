# Andrew Ferguson

Andrew Ferguson was appointed Chair of the US Federal Trade Commission by President Trump in January 2025. He represents the shift from enforcement-first (Khan era) to a more permissive approach to Big Tech antitrust.

## Background
- Previously served as a commissioner on the FTC (appointed by Biden in 2024)
- Prior to the FTC, served as counsel to Senate Majority Leader Mitch McConnell and as a law clerk to Supreme Court Justice Clarence Thomas
- More conventional antitrust philosophy than Khan — closer to the consumer welfare standard

## Policy Approach
- More skeptical of aggressive monopolization cases against US tech companies
- Maintained ongoing litigation (FTC v. Meta, FTC v. Amazon) but less likely to pursue maximum structural remedies
- More favorable to merger activity and innovation arguments
- Focused enforcement on consumer protection, fraud, and traditional antitrust violations rather than novel monopolization theories

## Relevance to Forecasting
Ferguson's FTC is unlikely to voluntarily dismiss the Meta case (enormous institutional investment), but less likely to push for the most aggressive remedy (divestiture). The agency's posture in remedy proceedings will be more permissive than Khan's FTC.
