# Russell Vought

## Role

Russell Vought has served as Director of the Office of Management and Budget in two non-consecutive periods: under President Trump (July 2020 - January 2021, confirmed 51-45) and again under the second Trump administration (January 2025 - present, confirmed 53-47). He is a key architect of the Trump administration's executive power theory regarding budget execution.

## Forecasting Relevance

Vought's forecasting significance arises from his role as OMB Director and his distinctive interpretation of the Impoundment Control Act:

### 1. Shutdown Declaration Discretion

As OMB Director, Vought controls whether a funding lapse triggers formal shutdown procedures. His known positions suggest he may be less likely than Shalanda Young to delay shutdown activation for the sake of congressional convenience. Specifically:

- Vought was acting OMB Director during the 2018-2019 shutdown (the longest in US history at 35 days). He would have been involved in shutdown planning.
- He authored the administration's interpretation of the Impoundment Control Act (ICA) that argues the President has constitutional authority to withhold appropriated funds even when Congress has mandated spending — a position at odds with the ICA's text and the 1974 Congressional Budget Act.
- This impoundment theory could affect shutdown dynamics: if the administration believes it can legally ignore spending mandates, the incentive to avoid a shutdown is reduced (since the administration can simply choose to continue operations it deems essential regardless of appropriations law).

### 2. Impoundment Control Act Controversy

Vought is the leading intellectual architect of the "Unitary Executive" theory applied to budget execution. His 2020 OMB memorandum argued that the President has constitutional authority to impound (withhold) appropriated funds despite the Impoundment Control Act of 1974. This position:

- Has been rejected by courts (including the Supreme Court in Train v. City of New York, 1975)
- Would, if implemented, fundamentally change the budget bargaining dynamic between Congress and the President
- Creates legal uncertainty around shutdown mechanics — if the administration claims it can continue operating without appropriations, the traditional shutdown lever loses force

### 3. Debt Ceiling and CR Negotiations

Vought participated in the 2023 debt ceiling negotiations (as a private-sector advisor) and the 2025 debt ceiling strategy. His known positions:

- Favors using the debt ceiling as leverage for spending cuts
- Views CRs as a symptom of congressional dysfunction and prefers reconciliation-driven budget processes
- Has deep working relationships with House Freedom Caucus members dating to his first OMB tenure (2020) and the subsequent Article I project think tank work

## Historical Context

- **First OMB tenure (2020-2021)**: Confirmed narrowly (51-45). Focused on regulatory reform and the executive budget process. Authored the impoundment memo.
- **Intervening period (2021-2025)**: Founded the Article I Project think tank, which advanced the impoundment theory and unitary executive budget interpretation. Advised House Republicans on budget strategy.
- **Second OMB tenure (2025-present)**: Confirmed 53-47. Implementing the impoundment strategy through OMB guidance.
