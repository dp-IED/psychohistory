## Summary

The United States Court of International Trade (CIT) is a federal Article III court with nationwide jurisdiction over civil actions arising out of US customs and trade laws. The CIT has exclusive jurisdiction over challenges to tariff actions brought under 28 U.S.C. § 1581(i). Its judges are appointed for life by the President. The CIT became a central actor in Trump-era trade policy when it ruled in *Learning Resources v. Trump* (May 28, 2025) that President Trump's Liberation Day tariffs exceeded his IEEPA authority.

## Key Rulings

- **May 28, 2025** — *Learning Resources v. Trump*: CIT vacates Liberation Day IEEPA tariffs, ruling the president overstepped emergency economic powers. The decision is immediately appealed. [[timeline/2025-Q2]]

## Forecasting Significance

The CIT is the primary judicial forum for trade policy challenges. Its rulings can create temporary freezes on tariff actions (as with the Liberation Day vacatur). Key questions:
- Does the CIT ruling survive appeal? If affirmed, the administration must use Section 232 or Section 301 (which require investigations) rather than IEEPA — slowing the pace of future tariff actions
- Do CIT judges appointed under different administrations pattern their rulings along partisan lines? Early evidence suggests some ideological division but less than in other courts
- Can the administration circumvent CIT through legislative authority (Congress passes tariff law)?
