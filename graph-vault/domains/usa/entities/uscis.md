# US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS)

## Summary

USCIS is the agency within the Department of Homeland Security responsible for administering the nation's lawful immigration system, including visa adjudication, green card processing, naturalization, asylum adjudications, and humanitarian programs. It is the operational arm of immigration policy — changes to any visa program (including H-1B) are implemented by USCIS through policy memoranda, adjudication guidance, and regulatory changes.

## Role in H-1B

- Adjudicates both individual H-1B petitions (Form I-129) and the annual H-1B lottery (electronic registration system adopted 2020)
- Issues policy guidance defining "specialty occupation" eligibility criteria
- Conducts site visits through the Administrative Site Visit and Verification Program
- Has used adjudication discretion to effectively restrict H-1B in the past: raising RFE (Request for Evidence) rates, narrowing interpretations of specialty occupation, increasing denials for entry-level programming positions

## Key Forecasting-Relevant Facts

- USCIS is a fee-funded agency (~96% of budget from filing fees). H-1B filing fees are a significant revenue source ($460 base filing fee + $500 fraud prevention fee + $1,500/$750 training fee per petition).
- The agency's policy direction is determined by the USCIS Director (presidential appointee, Senate-confirmed), career leadership, and DHS political leadership
- Policy changes at USCIS can be immediate (policy memoranda, adjudicator guidance) or take months to years (formal rulemaking)
- USCIS historically operates with significant backlogs (6-12 months for H-1B processing, years for green cards). This backlog functions as a de facto restriction mechanism regardless of policy intent.
