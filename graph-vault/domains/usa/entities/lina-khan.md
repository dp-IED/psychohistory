# Lina Khan

Lina Khan, born 1989, was Chair of the US Federal Trade Commission from June 2021 to January 2025, appointed by President Biden at age 32 — the youngest FTC Chair in history. She is the central figure in the 2021-2025 enforcement-first era of US antitrust.

## Background
- Gained prominence with her 2017 Yale Law Journal article "Amazon's Antitrust Paradox" which argued that the consumer welfare standard was inadequate for addressing the market power of digital platforms
- Served as counsel to the House Judiciary Subcommittee on Antitrust (2019-2020), contributing to the landmark bipartisan antitrust report
- Law clerk to Judge Brett Kavanaugh (DC Circuit) and Justice Sonia Sotomayor (Supreme Court)

## Key Actions
- **Major enforcement**: FTC brought monopolization cases against Meta (refiled 2021) and Amazon (2023), challenged over a dozen mergers including Meta/Within, and issued a ban on non-compete agreements (later struck down by courts)
- **Philosophical shift**: Rejected the Chicago School consumer welfare standard, arguing that antitrust should consider harm to workers, suppliers, and innovation — not just consumer prices
- **Multi-front enforcement**: Pursued enforcement against pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), hospital consolidation, AI partnerships (Microsoft/Inflection AI), and Big Tech data practices
- **Criticism**: Accused of overreach; lost several high-profile merger challenges in court; drew criticism from both business groups (too aggressive) and progressives (not aggressive enough on structural remedies)

## Relevance to Forecasting
Khan's tenure defined the enforcement-first era (2021-2024). Her departure in January 2025 (with the change in administration) represents a structural shift in enforcement posture. Cases filed under Khan continue through the courts but with less aggressive pursuit of maximum remedies.
