# Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)

## Summary

The US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is the federal agency responsible for enforcing federal securities laws, regulating securities markets, and protecting investors. Established in 1934 after the stock market crash of 1929, the SEC has broad authority over public companies, broker-dealers, investment advisers, exchanges, and securities offerings. Under Chair Gary Gensler (2021-2025), the SEC became notably aggressive in regulating the cryptocurrency industry, pursuing enforcement actions against major exchanges (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken) and taking a restrictive stance on crypto ETF approvals until compelled by court rulings to approve them.

## Significance for Forecasting

The SEC is a central actor in multiple forecasting domains: crypto regulation, ETF approvals, corporate disclosure rules, and securities enforcement. SEC decisions directly affect prediction market questions about cryptocurrency product availability, tech regulation timelines, and financial market structure. The SEC's regulatory posture shifts with presidential administrations, making appointment timing a forecasting variable.

## Timeline

- **1934**: Established by the Securities Exchange Act
- **2021-04**: Gary Gensler becomes SEC Chair under Biden
- **2023-06**: SEC sues Binance and Coinbase for securities law violations
- **2023-08**: DC Circuit rules in Grayscale v. SEC that denial of Bitcoin ETF was arbitrary and capricious
- **2024-01-10**: SEC approves 11 spot Bitcoin ETFs, reversing a decade of denials
- **2024-01**: SEC Chair Gensler issues statement emphasizing Bitcoin ETF approval is not an endorsement of crypto
- **2024-05-23**: SEC approves 19b-4 forms for 8 spot Ethereum ETFs
- **2024-07-22/23**: SEC Division of Corporation Finance approves S-1 registration statements; spot Ethereum ETFs begin trading on NASDAQ, NYSE Arca, and CBOE BZX
- **2025-01**: Gensler resigns as SEC Chair following Trump inauguration
