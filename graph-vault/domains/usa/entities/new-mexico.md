# New Mexico

## Summary

New Mexico is a state in the Southwestern United States with 5 electoral votes (as of 2024). It has been a reliably Democratic-leaning state in presidential elections since 2004, when it was won by George W. Bush by a narrow 0.8% margin (the only Republican to win it since 1988). Since 2008, New Mexico has voted Democratic by margins ranging from 5-15 points. The state's Democratic lean is driven by a large Hispanic/Latino population (47.7%), urban centers (Albuquerque, Santa Fe, Las Cruces), and significant Native American voting blocs.

Note: The 2000 and 2004 elections were exceptionally close in NM. From 2008 onward, the state has been consistently Democratic at the presidential level, with margins of 8-15 points. Even in 2024 — which shifted right nationally and in neighboring Arizona — New Mexico remained solidly Democratic.

## 2024 Election Specifics

- **Harris (D)**: 50.8% (484,260 votes)
- **Trump (R)**: 45.0% (429,369 votes)  
- **Margin**: D+5.7 (55,891 votes)
- **Electoral votes**: 5 for Harris

New Mexico's 2024 margin was closer than 2020 (D+10.8) due to national rightward shift, but still a comfortable Democratic win — the state was never considered a battleground or competitive at any point in the cycle.

## Demographic Drivers of Electoral Behavior

1. **Hispanic/Latino majority-minority state**: 47.7% Hispanic — the highest share of any US state. Democratic-leaning, though Hispanic voters shifted somewhat rightward in 2024 nationally, the impact in NM was muted by the state's established Hispanic Democratic identity and the absence of a border-crisis messaging focus (NM shares a shorter border segment with Mexico than Texas or Arizona).

2. **Urban-rural divide**: Bernalillo County (Albuquerque) and Santa Fe County account for ~45% of the vote and vote strongly Democratic. The eastern plains (Lea, Eddy counties) and southern tier are Republican. The Democratic margin in urban counties offsets rural Republican votes.

3. **Native American vote**: Significant populations in the northwest (Navajo Nation) and central pueblos vote heavily Democratic and are a critical part of the Democratic coalition.

4. **Oil and gas**: Southeastern NM (Permian Basin) is a major oil-producing region. This creates a Republican-leaning rural economic zone but does not swing the state due to the smaller population share compared to urban centers.

## Significance for Forecasting

New Mexico serves as a test case for "safe-but-narrowing" Democratic states: states where Democratic presidential margins are structurally secure (5-15 points) but could narrow under specific national conditions (Hispanic voter shift, turnout changes). The state is NOT competitive absent a national Republican landslide (>8-point popular vote margin). Any question about a Republican winning New Mexico should be treated as requiring a near-mythical national environment — the state's baseline D+8 PVI makes it structurally difficult for Republicans.
