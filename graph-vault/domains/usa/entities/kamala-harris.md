## Summary

49th Vice President of the United States, serving from January 20, 2021 to January 20, 2025 under President Joe Biden. Previously served as US Senator from California (2017-2021) and Attorney General of California (2011-2017). 

After Biden withdrew from the 2024 presidential race on July 21, 2024, Harris secured the Democratic nomination within days, inheriting the campaign infrastructure, fundraising apparatus, and staff. She selected Minnesota Governor Tim Walz as her running mate on August 6, 2024. Harris lost the general election to Donald Trump on November 5, 2024, winning 226 electoral votes to Trump's 312.

## Significance for Forecasting

### Successor Absorption Benchmark
Harris's 72-hour absorption of Biden's campaign infrastructure after his withdrawal establishes the benchmark for how quickly a vice president can succeed a withdrawing incumbent. This case is critical for any forecast involving late-stage nominee replacement:
- Campaign staff and infrastructure transferred within 48-72 hours
- Fundraising apparatus switched from Biden to Harris accounts immediately
- Delegate commitment transferred via endorsement cascade
- Ballot access in all 50 states maintained without interruption
- Total campaign duration after replacement: 107 days

This proved that a VP successor can absorb campaign machinery faster than any outsider could, but the compressed timeline limited the successor's ability to differentiate from the incumbent's record.

### The "Bridge Candidate" Dynamic
Biden had described himself during the 2020 campaign as a "bridge candidate" to a new generation of Democratic leaders. This framing, combined with his age and Harris's position as VP, created an expectation structure that made withdrawal more politically feasible — Harris was the natural inheritor of the "bridge" framing.

- **January 20, 2021**: Inaugurated as Vice President

- **August 19-22, 2024**: Democratic National Convention nominates Harris-Walz ticket

- **January 20, 2025**: Leaves office
