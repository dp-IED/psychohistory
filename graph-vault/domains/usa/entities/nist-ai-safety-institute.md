# NIST AI Safety Institute (AISI)

Established by President Biden's Executive Order 14110 (Oct 30, 2023), the AISI was designed to create technical standards for evaluating frontier AI models — including mandatory safety testing reports from companies training models above a compute threshold (10^25 FLOP). The AISI's creation was the most concrete institutional outcome of the Biden administration's AI policy.

President Trump's January 2025 executive order revoked Biden's EO, removing the AISI's mandatory reporting authority and reducing it to an advisory body. Companies that had submitted models for voluntary evaluation could continue to do so, but the testing requirement — and the AISI's enforcement teeth — was eliminated.

The AISI's rise and fall demonstrates the vulnerability of executive-created AI governance institutions to partisan turnover. Any federal AI safety regulation that relies on executive authority rather than legislation is inherently reversible.

## Forecasting Significance

- The AISI may be revived by a future Democratic administration, but its institutional memory and technical capacity have been degraded
- International analogs (UK AISI, EU AI Office) are not reversible by US presidential action, making them more durable governance structures
- Questions about AI safety testing requirements should distinguish between legislated requirements (hard to pass, hard to reverse) and executive-created ones (easy to create, easy to reverse)
