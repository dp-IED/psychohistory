# Judge James Boasberg

Judge James E. Boasberg is a US District Judge for the District of Columbia, presiding over the FTC v. Meta antitrust case (Case 1:20-cv-03590-JEB).

## Background
- Appointed by President Obama in 2011
- Previously served as an Associate Judge on the DC Superior Court (2002-2011)
- Chief Judge of the FISA Court (2019-2022)
- Known for thorough, procedurally rigorous opinions

## Role in FTC v. Meta
- **June 28, 2021**: Granted Meta's motion to dismiss the FTC's original complaint, finding the FTC failed to adequately define the "personal social networking" market and failed to plead sufficient facts showing Meta maintained monopoly power
- **August 2021**: FTC filed amended complaint with more detailed market definition and factual allegations
- **January 11, 2022**: Denied Meta's motion to dismiss the amended complaint, finding the FTC had sufficiently pled its claims to proceed to discovery
- **2022-2025**: Presiding over discovery phase
- **2025-2026**: Managing summary judgment briefing

## Judicial Philosophy
Boasberg's opinions show:
- High pleading standards (dismissed original complaint for insufficient factual allegations)
- Willingness to let cases proceed when properly pled (allowed amended complaint to survive)
- No clear ideological tilt on antitrust; decided based on procedural rigor
- Careful management of complex litigation

## Forecasting Relevance
Boasberg's rulings in the summary judgment phase and potential trial will determine whether the case proceeds to remedy. Key considerations:
- He has already demonstrated willingness to dismiss poorly pled claims
- The amended complaint survived his scrutiny, suggesting the case has sufficient merit to proceed
- His procedural rigor means any liability finding will be well-grounded in evidence
- On appeal, his decisions face high scrutiny (DC Circuit is one of the most influential appeals courts)
