## Summary

Tanya S. Chutkan is a US District Judge for the District of Columbia, presiding over US v. Trump (Case No. 23-cr-00257-TSC), the federal election interference case against Donald Trump regarding efforts to overturn the 2020 election. Nominated by President Barack Obama in 2013, Chutkan was confirmed 95-0 by the Senate. Prior to her judgeship, she served as a public defender in Washington DC for over a decade.

Chutkan's handling of the Trump case was central to the question of whether a trial could begin before the November 2024 election. Initially setting a March 4, 2024 trial date — well before the election — Chutkan signaled a preference for an expedited timeline. However, Trump's appeal of the presidential immunity question (rejected by Chutkan in December 2023) automatically stayed proceedings once the Supreme Court agreed to hear it in February 2024. Chutkan had no control over the Supreme Court's timeline, and the case was effectively paused for months. After the Supreme Court's July 1, 2024 immunity ruling, the case returned to Chutkan for proceedings to determine which conduct was official (immune) vs. private (potentially prosecutable), a process that consumed the remaining months before the election.

Chutkan's pre-appointment schedule (March 4 trial date) and post-remand schedule (allowing time for immunity briefing) illustrate a key dynamic: trial judges may wish to proceed quickly but are constrained by appellate timelines and the complexity of novel constitutional questions. The judge's scheduling preference is only one variable among many in trial-timing questions.

## Significance for Forecasting

Chutkan's role demonstrates that in high-profile political cases, the trial judge's scheduling preferences are often overridden by appellate proceedings. The "speed" of a case is determined more by the complexity of appeals available to the defendant than by the trial judge's calendar. When forecasting trial timing, check: (a) what appeals are available to the defendant, (b) whether those appeals carry an automatic stay, and (c) how long the appellate court typically takes to decide such matters.

## Timeline

- **2013**: Confirmed as US District Judge for DC (95-0)
- **August 2023**: Assigned to US v. Trump (election interference case)
- **August 28, 2023**: Sets trial date for March 4, 2024
- **December 1, 2023**: Denies Trump's motion to dismiss based on presidential immunity
- **December 2023**: Trump files interlocutory appeal of immunity ruling; case is stayed
- **February 2024**: Supreme Court agrees to hear immunity appeal; stay continues
- **July 1, 2024**: Supreme Court issues Trump v. United States; case returns to Chutkan
- **August 27, 2024**: Smith files superseding indictment; immunity litigation resumes
