# Office of Management and Budget

## Role

The Office of Management and Budget (OMB) is the largest office within the Executive Office of the President. It prepares the President's budget proposal, oversees agency performance, coordinates legislative proposals, and — critically for forecasting — **determines whether a funding lapse triggers an actual government shutdown**.

## Shutdown Determination Authority

OMB holds de facto discretionary authority over whether a funding lapse becomes a formal government shutdown:

1. **When a CR expires**, agencies technically lose appropriations authority. However, OMB can direct agencies to continue operations if passage of a new CR is imminent, effectively treating the funding gap as a technical lapse rather than a shutdown.

2. **The Antideficiency Act** prohibits agencies from spending money without appropriation. OMB issues guidance on how agencies should interpret the Act during lapses — including whether to furlough employees or continue operations.

3. **OMB Director's discretion** is the binding variable: the Director can instruct agencies to:
   - **Activate shutdown procedures** (furlough non-excepted employees, cease non-excepted activities)
   - **Maintain operations** with guidance that passage is imminent (no furloughs, continued normal operations)
   - **Partial activation** (some agencies continue, others furlough)

4. **Precedent**: In December 2024, OMB Director Shalanda Young chose NOT to activate formal shutdown procedures despite the funding technically lapsing on Dec 21, because passage of the replacement CR was imminent (signed by Biden hours later). This decision defined the event as a "technical funding lapse" rather than a "formal shutdown" under standard government definitions — though Polymarket's broad resolution definition still treated it as a shutdown.

## Forecasting Significance

OMB's shutdown determination is a critical variable when:

- **The resolution text defines "shutdown" broadly vs. narrowly**: If the question says "government shutdown" without specifying OMB activation, a technical funding lapse at OMB's discretion may still count. If the question requires OMB activation (furloughs, formal procedures), the OMB Director's decision is dispositive.

- **The funding deadline passes by hours rather than days**: When a replacement bill is passed but signed after the deadline, OMB typically does not activate shutdown procedures. This is the canonical "near-shutdown" pattern of 2023-2025.

- **The OMB Director's political alignment differs from the President-elect**: During transition periods, the outgoing President's OMB Director may have different incentives than the incoming administration regarding shutdown declarations.
