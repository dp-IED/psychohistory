# 2027 French Presidential Election

## Overview

The 2027 French presidential election is scheduled for April 2027 (first round) with a runoff in May 2027. Emmanuel Macron is term-limited and cannot run again, making this the first open presidential race since 2017. The election will determine Macron's successor and potentially reshape French and European politics.

## Key Dynamics

### The Post-Macron Landscape
- Macron's centrist coalition (Renaissance) lacks a clear successor. Potential candidates include Édouard Philippe (Horizons), Gabriel Attal (Renaissance), and Bruno Le Maire
- The center-right Republicans (LR) are fractured between alliance with Macron and independent presidential ambitions
- The left remains fragmented: Jean-Luc Mélenchon (LFI), the Socialist Party, Greens, and Communists have failed to unify behind a single candidate

### National Rally (RN) Position
- The RN is positioned as the leading opposition party after winning 31.4% in the 2024 European elections and ~37% in the 2024 legislative elections
- Marine Le Pen (b. 1968) has been the RN's presidential candidate in 2012, 2017, and 2022, reaching the runoff in both 2017 (33.9%) and 2022 (41.5%)
- Jordan Bardella (b. 1995) has served as RN party president since November 2022 and is widely seen as the RN's 2027 candidate — but has NOT formally announced as of May 2026
- Le Pen's legal eligibility for 2027 is uncertain due to ongoing judicial proceedings regarding EU parliamentary assistant funds

### Candidate Announcement Timing
- French presidential candidates typically announce 6-18 months before the election
- The formal campaign period begins ~6 weeks before the first round
- Bardella's announcement timing is a strategic decision contingent on Le Pen's legal situation and party polling
