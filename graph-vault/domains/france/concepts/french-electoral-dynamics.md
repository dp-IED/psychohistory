# French Electoral Dynamics

## Overview

French presidential elections operate under the Fifth Republic's two-round system, which creates unique forecasting dynamics distinct from single-round plurality systems. This concept documents the structural features that any French presidential election forecast must account for.

## Two-Round System Mechanics

### First Round
- All candidates compete; typically 10-16 candidates
- If no candidate receives >50%, the top two advance to a runoff
- Historically, no first round has produced a >50% winner since de Gaulle in 1965 (the first direct election)
- **Forecasting implication**: First round results are a fragmentation signal, not a winner signal. The first-round vote share distribution determines who reaches the runoff.

### Second Round (Runoff)
- Held 14 days after the first round
- Top two candidates compete head-to-head
- Winner by simple majority (50%+1)
- **Forecasting implication**: Runoff dynamics are about coalition formation — how voters from eliminated candidates redistribute in the 14-day gap between rounds.

### The Cordon Sanitaire (Republican Front)

A historical norm whereby mainstream parties coalesce behind the non-RN candidate in a runoff to block the far-right from power. This has been applied in every presidential runoff involving the FN/RN (2002, 2017, 2022):

- **2002**: Chirac (82%) vs Le Pen (18%) — maximum republican front
- **2017**: Macron (66%) vs Le Pen (34%) — diminished but still decisive
- **2022**: Macron (58.5%) vs Le Pen (41.5%) — further erosion

**Forecasting implication**: The cordon sanitaire erodes over time but has not yet collapsed. A 2027 RN-in-runoff scenario would likely still face a diminished republican front (~55-60% vs ~40-45% for centrist candidate). However, if both runoff candidates are anti-establishment (e.g., RN vs LFI), the dynamics are unpredictable.

## The 5th Republic Constitutional Structure

### Semi-Presidential System
- The President is the head of state (foreign policy, defense, EU affairs, constitutional powers — Article 16 emergency powers)
- The Prime Minister is the head of government (domestic policy)
- The President appoints the PM, but the National Assembly can dismiss the PM via no-confidence

### Cohabitation Risk
- When the President's party does not hold a National Assembly majority, the President must appoint a PM from the opposition majority
- Cohabitation occurred three times under the 5th Republic: Mitterrand-Chirac (1986-88), Mitterrand-Balladur (1993-95), Chirac-Jospin (1997-2002)
- **Forecasting implication**: A 2027 election that produces a President from one bloc and a legislative majority from the opposition would create the first cohabitation since 2002. This affects policy forecasting (domestic legislation stalls) and foreign policy forecasting (President retains control of foreign/defense but needs PM cooperation on EU fiscal matters).

### Legislative Synchronization
- Presidential elections are followed ~4-6 weeks later by legislative elections
- The "presidential coattail" effect means the newly elected President usually wins a legislative majority in the immediate election
- **Forecasting implication**: A candidate who wins the presidency in 2027 is highly likely (>80%) to also win a legislative majority unless the legislative election is delayed or the victory margin is extremely narrow (<3%).

## Key Historical Patterns

### RN Ceiling
The RN's electoral ceiling has risen across consecutive elections:
- 2002: Le Pen (J-M) 16.9% → 17.8% runoff → eliminated in first round thereafter until 2017
- 2012: Le Pen (M) 17.9% (3rd place)
- 2017: Le Pen (M) 21.3% → 33.9% runoff
- 2022: Le Pen (M) 23.1% → 41.5% runoff
- 2024 legislative: RN ~37% (largest single bloc)

Each cycle, the RN gains ~3-5 percentage points in the first round and ~7-8 points in the runoff. **At current trend rates, an RN candidate in 2027 would project to 26-28% first round and 48-52% runoff** — i.e., within striking distance of the presidency.

### Left Fragmentation
The French left has been structurally fragmented since the collapse of the two-party system in 2017:
- **LFI (Mélenchon)**: 22% in 2022 first round — far-left populist, anti-NATO, anti-EU
- **Socialist Party**: <5% in 2022 — reduced from 25%+ in 2012
- **Greens (EELV)**: ~5% in 2022 — ecologist
- **Communists (PCF)**: <3% — marginal

In 2022, a unified left candidate (NUPES alliance, Mélenchon first round) got 22% — close to reaching the runoff (Le Pen 23.1%). If the left unifies behind a single candidate in 2027, that candidate could reach 18-22% and potentially make the runoff in a fragmented centrist field.

**Forecasting implication**: Left unification is the single biggest structural variable that could change the runoff composition. A unified left candidate at 20+% could reach the runoff if centrist candidates fragment.

### Centrist Fragmentation
Macron's 2017 coalition was a broad tent that included center-left, center, and center-right voters. By 2027, that coalition has fractured into:
- **Renaissance** (Macron loyalists): Attal, Le Maire, Darmanin
- **Horizons** (Édouard Philippe): center-right Macron-aligned
- **MoDem** (François Bayrou): centrist, historically associated with Macron

**Forecasting implication**: The center is likely to field 2-3 candidates in the first round, none exceeding 15-18% individually, making a centrist runoff appearance less likely than in 2017 or 2022.

## Forecasting Checklist

Before forecasting any French presidential election question:

1. **Check the front-runner coalition**: Is there a clear favorite (polls >30%) or is the field fragmented?
2. **Assess the RN vs anti-RN structure**: Is the cordon sanitaire holding? Are there defections?
3. **Evaluate centrist unity**: How many centrist candidates are running? Can they coalesce?
4. **Check left unity**: Are the left parties united behind one candidate?
5. **Apply the cohabitation filter**: If the leading candidate is from a party without legislative majority, what are cohabitation odds?
6. **Cross-reference with entity files**: Le Pen eligibility, Bardella positioning, Philippe strategy, left candidate dynamics
