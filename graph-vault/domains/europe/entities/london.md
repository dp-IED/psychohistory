# London

## Summary

London is the capital and largest city of the United Kingdom, with a population of ~8.98M (city proper) and ~14.9M (metropolitan area). Located in southeastern England on the River Thames, London is a global financial center, a major transport hub, and the seat of the UK government. Its climate is temperate oceanic (Köppen: Cfb), with mild winters, warm summers, and fairly evenly distributed precipitation (~600mm annually). February average highs reach ~9-10°C, with record highs around 21.2°C (Kew Gardens, 2019). London's weather is dominated by Atlantic westerlies, with occasional continental blocking patterns bringing cold easterlies in winter or warm southerlies in spring.

## Significance for Forecasting

- **Weather prediction markets**: London daily temperature forecasts appear on Polymarket (e.g., February 2026 series). The urban heat island effect contributes 1-3°C above surrounding rural areas on calm, clear nights. Official observation stations at Heathrow, St James Park, and Northolt are the key resolution sources.
- **Political forecasting**: As the UK capital, London anchors major political events — general elections, changes of government, and policy announcements. The July 2024 Labour landslide (Keir Starmer becoming PM) is a recent example.
- **Microclimate factors**: Spring temperature forecasts in London are particularly challenging due to the Atlantic-synoptic duality — mild maritime airmasses from the west and cold continental blocks from the east create bimodal outcomes.
- **Economic indicator**: London's financial sector performance and property market are leading indicators for UK economic health and are frequently the subject of prediction market questions.
