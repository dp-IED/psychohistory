# Labour Party (UK)

The Labour Party sits at the centre-left of British politics. Following its 2024 landslide victory, the party holds approximately 400+ seats in the House of Commons. The party encompasses a broad coalition ranging from soft-left to centrist positions.

## Key Internal Factions

- **Starmerite centrists**: The leadership faction emphasizing fiscal responsibility, pro-business policy, and EU rapprochement
- **Soft left / Open Left**: More progressive on fiscal policy, public ownership, and green investment
- **Hard left / Momentum-adjacent**: Remnant of the Corbyn era, focused on nationalization and wealth taxes
- **Trade union wing**: Pragmatic, focused on workers' rights and employment law

## Current Leadership

- Leader: Keir Starmer (PM)
- Deputy Leader: Angela Rayner
- Chancellor: Rachel Reeves
- Foreign Secretary: David Lammy

## Forecasting Relevance — Leadership Contest Rules

Labour's leadership contest rules are critical for the Andy Burnham UK PM forecast (vault p_yes=0.35 vs market 95.3%):

1. **Nomination threshold**: 20% of Parliamentary Labour Party (~80 of ~400 MPs) must nominate a candidate. Burnham is not an MP — he must first win a by-election to gain a Commons seat.
2. **MP elimination rounds**: Candidates with fewest MP nominations are eliminated sequentially until two remain
3. **OMOV final ballot**: The final two go to a postal ballot of all Labour members. Members are more left-wing than MPs, favoring candidates with strong internal party support
4. **Non-MP barrier**: No non-MP has ever won the Labour leadership in the party's 100+ year history. Burnham would need to be the first
5. **Timeline constraint**: Full contest (by-election → nomination → elimination → ballot) takes 8-12 weeks minimum
