# Met Office

## Summary

The UK Met Office (officially the Meteorological Office) is the United Kingdom's national weather service, headquartered in Exeter, Devon. It operates the Unified Model — one of the world's leading global and regional weather prediction systems — and provides operational forecasts, severe weather warnings through the National Severe Weather Warning Service (NSWWS), climate projections, and specialized services for aviation, defense, and emergency response. The Met Office maintains the UK's official weather observation network, including stations at Heathrow, St James Park, Northolt, and Kew Gardens in London, which serve as authoritative data sources for weather-related prediction market resolution.

## Significance for Forecasting

- **Prediction market data authority**: Met Office observations are the authoritative source for UK weather prediction market resolution. Daily temperature records from Heathrow, St James Park, and Northolt are the primary resolution sources for Polymarket-style questions about London temperatures.
- **Probabilistic forecasting capability**: The MOGREPS ensemble provides probabilistic temperature guidance at 2km resolution for the UK, making it one of the most advanced national weather services. Its skill in forecasting extreme events (heatwaves, storms, freezing conditions) directly affects the accuracy of weather predictions that underlie related prediction markets.
- **Climate policy relevance**: Met Office climate projections inform UK government policy on net-zero targets, flood defense investment, and infrastructure resilience — topics that appear in UK domestic policy forecasting.
- **Aviation weather authority**: Met Office aviation services provide METAR/TAF reports for London Heathrow, Gatwick, Stansted, Luton, and City Airport, affecting aviation disruption forecasting.
