## Timeline

### 2024-07-05: Labour Landslide Victory
Labour wins ~410 seats, largest Labour majority since 1997. Conservatives reduced to ~120 seats.

### 2024-09 to 2025-03: First Year Challenges
- Budget delivers tax increases and spending cuts
- NHS waiting lists begin to decline but slowly
- Small boat crossings remain contentious
- Growth remains sluggish (~1-1.5%)

### 2025-04 to 2025-09: Mid-Term Pressures
- Local elections show Conservative recovery
- Internal Labour dissent over welfare cuts and two-child benefit cap

### 2025-10 to 2026-05: Current Period
- Economic indicators mixed — inflation near target, growth tepid
- NHS improvements modest but tangible
- Immigration remains politically salient
- Starmer's personal ratings: net negative but above government ratings
- No serious leadership challenge yet materialized

- Confidence vote petitions: threshold is 15% of Labour MPs (~60 signatures)

## Voting Mechanics

Under the Fixed-term Parliaments Act (repealed by the Dissolution and Calling of Parliament Act 2022):
- The PM can call an election at any time
- No fixed-term anymore — maximum 5 years
- A no-confidence vote requires simple majority of those voting
- If no-confidence passes, 14 days for a new government to form, or an election is called
- Labour party leadership challenges require 20% of Labour MPs (80+ signatures) for a leadership contest

## Forecasting Significance

The question of whether Starmer is "out" by June 30, 2026 is about:
1. **Resignation**: Voluntary departure (unlikely for a PM with a large majority)
2. **Party coup**: Labour MPs trigger a leadership contest and vote him out
3. **General election loss** followed by resignation — extremely unlikely given fixed 5-year maximum
4. **Health or scandal** — unpredictable but low baseline probability
