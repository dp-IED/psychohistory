# Sweden 2026 General Election — Tracking Thread

## Overview

Sweden's fixed-term general election is scheduled for **September 13, 2026**. The election determines the composition of the 349-seat Riksdag, which in turn elects the Prime Minister through coalition negotiations. Prime Minister **Ulf Kristersson** (Moderate Party) seeks re-election with his fragile centre-right coalition.

### The SD Kingmaker Paradox

The defining structural feature of Swedish politics in 2026:

1. **SD is the largest right-bloc party** (~20% in polls) but is excluded from cabinet due to the centre-right's cordon sanitaire — the "anyone but SD" norm among M, KD, L
2. SD provides confidence-and-supply to Kristersson's government without formal coalition participation
3. If SD gains seats at the expense of M (Moderates), the right-bloc's parliamentary math gets *harder* — Kristersson needs SD support while maintaining L and KD participation
4. L (Liberals) and KD (Christian Democrats) are **near the 4% threshold** — if either falls out of parliament, the right-bloc loses those seats entirely, making a parliamentary majority nearly impossible even with SD support
5. The paradox: SD's success is inversely correlated with the right-bloc's ability to govern

## Electoral Mechanics

- **System**: Proportional representation with 29 multi-member constituencies plus 39 adjustment seats
- **Threshold**: 4% national (or 12% in a single constituency)
- **Government formation**: The Speaker nominates a PM candidate; the Riksdag must vote NO to a candidate by absolute majority to block them — abstentions count as support. This means even a minority PM can govern if no alternative majority coalesces against them
- **Fixed term**: 4-year terms, snap elections are possible but rare (last: 1958)

### Key Uncertainties

1. **Will a right-bloc party fall below 4%?** If L or KD drops out of Riksdag, right-bloc path to government effectively shuts
2. **Does SD's cordon sanitaire hold?** Any signal of M opening coalition talks with SD would transform the race — but also risks losing L support
3. **Can Andersson form a stable left government?** The left bloc's advantage is narrow; V (Left Party) participation would be controversial for C (Centre)
4. **Incumbency drag or incumbency rebound?** Kristersson's government has faced headwinds on crime, energy prices, NATO costs. But no single crisis has defined the term. The question is whether voters punish the incumbent or default to the status quo
