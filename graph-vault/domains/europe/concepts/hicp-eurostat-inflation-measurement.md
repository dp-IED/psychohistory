# HICP / Eurostat Inflation Measurement

## Definition

The Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP) is the official measure of consumer price inflation across the European Union, produced by Eurostat in coordination with national statistical institutes. It is the metric used for the ECB's inflation target ("below but close to 2%"), for EU fiscal surveillance (the Maastricht inflation criterion for euro adoption), and for indexation mechanisms across member states.

## Structure

HICP differs from CPI measures in other major economies in several structural respects:

### Published Releases
- **Flash estimate**: Published ~last week of the month for the current month (e.g., Oct 29 for October data). The most market-moving data release in eurozone economics.
- **Final release**: Published ~2 weeks after flash, with detail by component and member state.
- **Coverage**: EU-wide (27 members) and euro area (20 members, EA20). The EA20 figure is the primary ECB target metric.

### Basket Composition (Eurozone average)
| Component | Approx. Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Energy | ~10% | Higher than US CPI (~7%); makes eurozone HICP more volatile |
| Food, alcohol & tobacco | ~20% | Higher than US; less processed food share |
| Non-energy industrial goods | ~26% | Includes durable goods, clothing, autos |
| Services | ~44% | Lower than US CPI; less healthcare/education due to public provision |

### Key Differences from US CPI
1. **Owner-occupied housing (OOH) excluded**: US CPI includes owners' equivalent rent (OER) — ~25% of the index. Eurozone HICP excludes OOH entirely, though Eurostat produces a supplementary OOH index. This makes HICP structurally less sensitive to housing costs and more sensitive to energy and goods prices.
2. **Health insurance treatment**: Different accounting for health insurance costs.
3. **Weights updated annually**: Eurozone HICP weights are updated each year (vs US CPI biennial pre-2022, now annual).
4. **Geographic coverage**: Eurozone average masks wide dispersion — national HICP rates in Oct 2021 ranged from ~3% (Malta) to ~7% (Lithuania). The ECB sets a single rate for heterogenous economies.

### Energy Weight Sensitivity
The higher energy weight in HICP (~10% vs ~7% US CPI) means that a given percentage change in oil or gas prices produces a larger direct contribution to headline HICP than to US CPI. Combined with Europe's greater gas dependence (gas-powered marginal electricity pricing), this explains why the eurozone's headline inflation peaked at 10.6% (Oct 2022) vs the US CPI peak of 9.1% (Jun 2022) — the shock was structurally larger for Europe even before accounting for the Russian supply cutoff.

## Forecasting Significance

- **Flash estimate timing matters**: ECB rate decisions are based on the HICP flash, published ~2 weeks before each Governing Council meeting. The flash is the strongest leading indicator for the rate decision.
- **Disaggregation is essential**: Energy-driven HICP has different policy implications than services-driven HICP. The ECB is more tolerant of energy-driven overshoots (supply shock, expected to reverse) than services-driven overshoots (demand-driven, likely persistent).
- **Country dispersion is a political variable**: When German HICP (3.1% in Oct 2021) differs from Baltic HICP (~6-7% in Oct 2021), the German and Estonian central bank governors bring different inflation experiences to the Governing Council table. This hawk-dove spread is a structural input to ECB decision-making.
- **Cross-country comparison is informative**: Comparing HICP to US CPI reveals the structural factors driving inflation differentials — energy dependence, housing treatment, services share — and thus informs EUR/USD and rate-path forecasts.
