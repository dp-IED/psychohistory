# Protracted War Stalemate

## Definition

A condition in an inter-state conventional conflict where: (a) neither side can achieve decisive military victory, (b) neither side is suffering enough to accept unfavorable terms, and (c) both sides can continue fighting through external sustainment. In this condition, ceasefires are structurally unlikely regardless of diplomatic activity.

## Causal Mechanism

The protracted war stalemate operates through five mutually reinforcing factors:

### Factor 1: Territorial Incompatibility
The parties' territorial demands are mutually exclusive and irreconcilable without one side's political suicide. The war aims cannot be fudged, split, or compromised. In the Russia-Ukraine case (2022-2024): Russia demanded recognition of annexed territories; Ukraine demanded restoration of pre-2014 borders. These positions could not co-exist.

**Forecasting trigger**: When peace terms require one party to repudiate a core constitutional/legal commitment (annexation, territorial integrity), the probability of a ceasefire within 12 months is P < 0.10 absent a decisive battlefield outcome.

### Factor 2: Military Balance Parity
The military balance produces positional attrition — both sides can defend but neither can achieve operational breakthroughs. Key indicators:
- Front-line movement measured in kilometers per month, not tens of kilometers
- Attackers suffer disproportionate casualties relative to territorial gains (3:1 or higher kill ratios for defenders)
- Neither side has air superiority that enables decisive ground maneuver
- Electronic warfare and air defense systems degrade the other side's precision-strike capability
- The front forms a continuous line of contact (no major salients or gaps)

**Forecasting trigger**: When front-line movement has been <5km/month for 6+ consecutive months on the primary axis, the military situation is a stalemate. Stalemates do not produce ceasefires — they produce either (a) frozen conflicts, (b) slow attrition until one side breaks, or (c) external mediation that changes both sides' incentive structures.

### Factor 3: No Mutually Hurting Stalemate
A "mutually hurting stalemate" (Zartman's concept) exists when both parties perceive they are paying unacceptable costs and there's a way out. In a protracted war stalemate, the costs may be high but they are not unbearable for either side because:
- External patrons subsidize the weaker side's continued resistance
- The stronger side's regime can absorb casualties through authoritarian controls
- Wartime economies adapt to sustain the conflict indefinitely
- Elite audiences benefit from war-related economic activity

**Forecasting trigger**: A ceasefire requires a mutually hurting stalemate. Check: is either side's regime at risk of collapse from war costs? Are external patrons cutting off supplies? Are domestic publics demanding withdrawal? If no to all three, the hurting condition is absent and ceasefire probability is near-zero.

### Factor 4: No Credible Mediator
A ceasefire requires an external actor with sufficient leverage over both parties to either: (a) enforce a settlement, (b) provide security guarantees that replace the military conflict, or (c) make the costs of refusal unbearable. Absent this actor, the parties have no mechanism to coordinate on a ceasefire even if they wanted one.

Characteristics of a credible mediator for inter-state war:
- Military or economic leverage over BOTH parties
- Willingness to use that leverage (not just offer good offices)
- Trust from both sides (or sufficient power to overcome distrust)
- Capacity to enforce or guaranty terms
- Strategic interest in ending the conflict

**Forecasting trigger**: No credible mediator → no ceasefire. The Trump administration (Jan 2025+) filled this role for Russia-Ukraine via aid leverage over Ukraine and sanctions leverage over Russia. Before Trump, no actor had this combination of attributes.

### Factor 5: External Sustainment
Both sides continue fighting because external patrons provide the material capacity to continue. This is distinct from escalation (Factor 3) — the costs may be high but outside supplies prevent collapse. Key indicators:
- Arms shipments continue to the weaker party
- The stronger party has domestic production sufficient to sustain combat
- Economic sanctions against the aggressor are porous or insufficient
- Energy/commodity export revenues fund the war effort

**Forecasting trigger**: When external sustainment is stable and not threatened, the war continues. Ceasefire probability only increases when (a) a patron threatens to withdraw support, or (b) a patron signals reduced willingness to resupply.

## Applicability Check for Forecasting

Before predicting a ceasefire in an ongoing inter-state war, run this checklist:

1. Are the territorial demands mutually exclusive? If YES, ceasefire unlikely.
2. Is the front line moving <5km/month? If YES, military stalemate.
3. Is either side's regime at risk from war costs? If NO, no mutually hurting stalemate.
4. Does a credible mediator exist? If NO, no diplomatic mechanism.
5. Are both sides receiving sufficient external supplies to continue? If YES, continuation is feasible.
6. Is there a political deadline (election, inauguration, transition) that could force a breakthrough? (Apply [[domains/global/concepts/political-deadline-ceasefire]])

**If 4+ factors point toward continuation, the base rate for a ceasefire within the next 12 months is P < 0.15.** This framework correctly predicted NO for "Russia x Ukraine Ceasefire in 2024?" — all five factors favored continuation.

## Historical Cases
- **Russia-Ukraine 2022-2024**: The canonical contemporary case. All five factors present. No ceasefire.
- **Iran-Iraq War 1980-1988**: Protracted conventional war with territorial incompatibility (Shatt al-Arab), military parity after 1982, no credible mediator until UN Security Council Resolution 598 (1987), external sustainment (both sides supplied by patrons). Ceasefire occurred only when both sides were exhausted and Iraq was near collapse.
- **Western Front WWI 1914-1918**: Territorial incompatibility (Alsace-Lorraine), military parity (trench warfare), no mutually hurting stalemate until 1917 (Russian collapse, US entry), no credible mediator. Ceasefire only came with decisive military outcome (German army collapse).
