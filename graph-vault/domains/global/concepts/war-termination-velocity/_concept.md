# War Termination Velocity

## Definition

A measure of how quickly active military hostilities transition to a formal peace framework. The US-Iran permanent peace deal (June 14, 2026) established the fastest enemy-to-peace transition in modern history: **~3.5 months** from the February 28, 2026 Operation Epic Fury to the Pakistan-mediated permanent peace declaration.

This concept formalizes the observed pattern that decapitation strikes + regime-change-level threats can create unusually rapid negotiating windows — and provides base rates for forecasting the speed of future war-to-peace transitions.

## Velocity Scale (Empirical)

| Category | Duration | Examples | Conditions |
|----------|----------|----------|------------|
| **Hyper-velocity** | <6 months | US-Iran 2026 (~3.5mo) | Decapitation strike + existential regime threat + credible mediator + aligned incentives |
| **Fast** | 6-18 months | US-Iraq surge 2007-2008 (~15mo); Colombia-FARC 2015-2016 (~18mo) | Military deadlock shifted by escalation; external mediator |
| **Standard** | 2-5 years | US-Japan 1942-1945 (3yr); Yemen Hudaydah 2018-2022 (4yr) | Conventional war with clear front lines; peace when one side exhausts capacity |
| **Slow** | 5-15 years | US-Vietnam 1968-1973 (5yr); Iran-Iraq 1980-1988 (8yr) | Asymmetric/proxy warfare; no clear battlefield resolution |
| **Inter-generational** | 15+ years | US-China normalization 1949-1979 (30yr); Israel-Palestine Oslo 1993-ongoing (30yr+) | Identity conflicts, territorial disputes, unresolved grievances |

## US-Iran 2026: The Hyper-Velocity Case

### Preconditions for 3.5-month pace

1. **Decapitation strike on command structure**: Khamenei assassinated, IRGC leadership decimated — the negotiating counterparty had no capacity to resist
2. **Existential regime threat**: Iran's nuclear program and conventional military were both degraded; continuation of hostilities risked regime collapse
3. **Credible mediator already in place**: Pakistan had been warming ties with both Washington and Tehran; ready-made diplomatic channel
4. **Both sides had aligned incentives**: Trump wanted a foreign policy capstone pre-midterms; Iran's weakened leadership wanted to lock in de-escalation before Khamenei's succession created chaos
5. **Low bar resolution criteria**: The Polymarket question resolved on a "permanent end to military hostilities" declaration — not a formal treaty requiring ratification
6. **Executive agreement mechanism**: The US could bypass Congressional treaty approval via executive agreement, dramatically shortening the negotiation-to-signature path

### Comparison to other post-war transitions

| Conflict | Hostilities End | Formal Peace | Duration | Ratio vs US-Iran |
|----------|----------------|--------------|----------|-------------------|
| US-Iran war 2026 | Feb 28, 2026 | Jun 14, 2026 | **3.5 months** | 1x (baseline) |
| US-Iran Twelve-Day War | Jun 24, 2025 | Ceasefire only | ~48 hours to ceasefire | War was 12 days, but no permanent peace until 2026 |
| 1991 Gulf War | Feb 28, 1991 | Formal acceptance Apr 11, 1991 | **1.5 months** | 0.4x (UNSCR 687 imposed terms, not negotiation) |
| 2003 Iraq invasion | May 1, 2003 | Sovereignty Jun 28, 2004 | **14 months** | 4x (occupation phase complicated transition) |
| Colombia-FARC | 2015 (ceasefire) | 2016 (agreement) | **~18 months** | 5x (negotiated settlement, no decapitation) |

**Key insight**: The US-Iran hyper-velocity case is most comparable to the 1991 Gulf War (imposed terms after decisive defeat), not to negotiated settlements. The critical variable is whether one side can dictate terms, not bargain them.

## Forecasting Applications

### When to expect hyper-velocity termination

The following conditions predict <6-month war-to-peace transitions:

1. **Decapitation strike succeeds** (leadership killed/captured)
2. **Regime survival is credibly threatened** (not just military defeat)
3. **A credible mediator is already on standby** (not sourced post-conflict)
4. **Executive agreement is available** (no treaty ratification required)
5. **Both sides have time-pressure reasons to move fast** (electoral deadlines, succession windows)

### Base rates for question calibration

When forecasting a "permanent peace deal by [date]" question on a newly started war:

- If 4+ conditions met: median completion within 6 months (anchor at ~25-30% for next-6-months, up to 60-70% for next-12)
- If 2-3 conditions met: median completion 1-2 years (anchor below 30% for next-year)
- If 0-1 conditions met: median completion 5+ years or never (anchor below 10% for next-year)

### The "peace deal by [date]" deadline cascade pattern

Polymarket markets with escalating deadlines (May 26 → May 31 → Jun 15 → Jun 30 → Jul 31) show a characteristic probability accumulation curve:
- Very near deadlines (<30 days from hostilities): <10%
- Near deadlines (30-90 days): 25-35%
- Medium deadlines (3-6 months): 40-55%
- Far deadlines (6-12 months): 60-80%

The curve is not linear — it shows a steep rise between the 3-month and 6-month marks, indicating the market prices the negotiation window as opening 3-6 months post-hostilities.

## Key Uncertainties

1. **Small-n problem**: The hyper-velocity category has only 2 modern cases (Gulf War 1991, US-Iran 2026). Generalization risk is high.
2. **Decapitation prevalence**: Decapitation strikes are rare. When they don't occur, expect standard or slow velocity.
3. **Mediator availability**: Pakistan's ready-made channel was exceptional. Most conflicts don't have a trusted bilateral interlocutor.
