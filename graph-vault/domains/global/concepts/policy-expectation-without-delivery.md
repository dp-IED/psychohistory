# Policy Expectation Without Delivery

## Definition

A recurring pattern in political-financial forecasting: a politician or administration makes a high-profile policy promise that creates measurable market or public expectations, but the promise is not delivered within the expected timeframe. The gap between expectation and delivery generates systematic forecastable effects — most commonly a drawdown in asset prices, a decay in approval ratings, or a credibility discount on subsequent promises.

## Pattern Archetype

The pattern follows a predictable 4-phase sequence:

### Phase 1: Promise → Hype
- Policy announcement during campaign or early in term
- Market prices in delivery with high probability (often >80%)
- Asset prices rally, polling improves, affected sectors respond

### Phase 2: Expectation Lock-In
- Market adapts to the promise-as-default assumption
- No explicit delivery deadline, creating ambiguity about timing
- Supporting narratives emerge (advisors confirm, leaks suggest imminent action)
- Prices sustain at elevated levels despite no delivery

### Phase 3: Delay Without Communication
- No delivery at expected milestone (first 100 days, first year, first relevant deadline)
- Administration makes no public statement explaining the delay
- Uncertainty compounds: market begins discounting delivery probability
- Initial drawdown begins, often attributed to other factors

### Phase 4: Disappointment Cascade
- Cumulative non-delivery crosses a threshold (~3-6 months beyond expectation window)
- Drawdown accelerates as leveraged positions unwind
- Adjacent policy promises also lose credibility (contagion effect)
- Asset settles at pre-promise baseline or below

## Canonical Cases

### Strategic Bitcoin Reserve (2024-2026)
- **Promise**: Trump's campaign pledge to establish a US Strategic Bitcoin Reserve
- **Peak expectation**: Bitcoin at ~$109K ATH post-election (pricing in >80% delivery probability)
- **Drawdown**: ~38% from $97K to $60K by Feb 2026 as no EO materialized
- **Primary mechanism**: Campaign promise → market expectation → delay without communication → drawdown
- **Detailed case**: [[domains/economics/concepts/strategic-bitcoin-reserve]]

### Tariff Policy (Trump 2nd Term)
- **Promise**: Aggressive tariff increases on China and other trading partners
- **Expectation**: Markets priced in rapid tariff escalation; affected sectors hedged accordingly
- **Delay pattern**: Implementation timeline ambiguity created repeated "tariff whiplash" cycles
- **Detail**: [[domains/global/concepts/trump-tariff-escalation-bargaining/_concept]]

### H-1B Program Elimination (2025-2026)
- **Promise**: Trump campaign vow to eliminate H-1B program
- **Reality**: Executive action limited to restriction, not elimination
- **Mechanism**: Legal/administrative barriers prevented elimination; program-restriction-vs-elimination gap
- **Detail**: [[domains/usa/concepts/program-restriction-vs-elimination/_concept]], [[domains/usa/concepts/first-100-days-action-horizon/_concept]]

## Forecasting Application

### Recognition Checklist
When a question involves a policy promise with a market-observable effect:

1. Has the promise been made by an executive actor (president, governor, mayor)?
2. Is there a measurable market/asset that would react to delivery?
3. What is the "expectation window" — when was delivery plausibly expected?
4. Has any delivery milestone passed without action?
5. Has the administration communicated about the delay?

### Probability Calibration

| Time Since Expectation Window | Monthly Drawdown Risk | Notes |
|------------------------------|----------------------|-------|
| 0-30 days | 5-10% | Normal implementation lag |
| 30-90 days | 15-25% | First material concern |
| 90-180 days | 30-50% | Drawdown likely underway |
| 180-365 days | 50-70% | Expectation largely abandoned |

### Compounding Factors (Increases Drawdown Magnitude)

- **Leverage in the asset class**: Crypto markets have high leverage → drawdowns are faster and deeper
- **Narrow catalyst dependence**: Assets whose entire thesis relies on one policy promise are most vulnerable
- **Silent administration**: Public communication about delay reduces drawdown by ~50%; silence amplifies it
- **Unrelated macro headwinds**: When delay coincides with macro deterioration (tariff uncertainty, rate hikes), the combined effect is multiplicative not additive

### Mitigating Factors (Decreases Drawdown Magnitude)

- **Gradual implementation**: If the policy can be delivered incrementally, expectation decay is slower
- **Legislative path available**: Even if executive action is delayed, a legislative path keeps expectations alive
- **Multiple delivery mechanisms**: Multiple routes to delivery (executive order, legislation, agency action) reduce probability-all-or-nothing
