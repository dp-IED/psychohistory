# Peace-Broker Diplomatic Playbook

## Definition

A consistent foreign policy pattern observed in Donald Trump's second term (2025-2026): a bilateral summit with a major power establishes a "peace broker" brand, followed by sequential conflict resolution engagements that are progressively audacious, culminating in a capstone deal. The pattern converts transactional deal-making into a recognizable diplomatic methodology.

## The Playbook Sequence

### Phase 1: Establish the Brand — Bilateral Summit with Adversary

A high-profile, bilateral summit with a major US adversary demonstrates that the Trump administration can negotiate directly with rivals. The summit creates media spectacle, establishes personal rapport, and signals to other conflict parties that the US is open for deal-making.

| Summit | Date | Adversary | Outcome |
|--------|------|-----------|---------|
| **Alaska Summit** | Aug 15, 2025 | Putin (Russia) | Ceasefire framework on Ukraine; established Trump-as-peace-broker narrative |

### Phase 2: Quick Wins — Low-Complexity Conflicts

The administration wades into lower-complexity conflicts where the US has leverage and the parties have aligned interests to settle. These quick wins build a track record and demonstrate credibility to harder cases.

| Conflict | Resolution | Timeframe | Complexity |
|----------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **Armenia-Azerbaijan** | Post-2023 Nagorno-Karabakh normalization | 2025 | Low (post-war status quo acceptable) |
| **Gaza ceasefire (Oct 2025)** | Phase 2 of 20-point peace plan | 2025 | Medium (Hamas weakened, PA cooperative) |

### Phase 3: The Capstone — High-Stakes Conflict Resolution

The administration tackles the hardest conflict, leveraging credibility built from Phases 1-2. This is the defining foreign policy achievement — the bet is that accumulated goodwill, demonstrated capability, and aligned incentives create the conditions for the most improbable deal.

| Conflict | Resolution | Timeframe | Complexity |
|----------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **US-Iran permanent peace deal** | Jun 14, 2026 | ~3.5 months from Feb 28 war | Very high (direct state-on-state war → peace) |

### Phase 4 (hypothetical): What Comes Next

Having resolved the hardest case, two paths are possible:
- **Consolidation**: Admin pivots to implementing existing agreements (Gaza reconstruction, Ukraine freeze line, Iran sanctions relief sequencing)
- **Extension**: Admin seeks another capstone — Ukraine-Russia permanent settlement or US-China framework agreement would be logical next targets

### The Diplomatic Bandwidth Cascade

A critical sub-pattern of the playbook: **each completed deal frees diplomatic attention for the next conflict.** This creates a cascading effect where resolution velocity accelerates as the sequence progresses:

| Step | Deal | Duration before catalyst | Attention freed for |
|------|------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | Alaska Summit (Aug 2025) | — (established brand) | Armenia-Azerbaijan, Gaza |
| 2 | Armenia-Azerbaijan | ~4 months post-summit | Gaza consolidation |
| 3 | Gaza ceasefire (Oct 2025) | ~2 months | Iran negotiation ramp-up |
| 4 | US-Iran peace deal (Jun 2026) | ~8 months (including war) | Ukraine ceasefire push |

**Bandwidth mechanics**:
- The White House National Security Council has finite capacity for simultaneous high-stakes negotiations
- Iran peaked at full bandwidth consumption (war + negotiation) from Feb-Jun 2026
- With Iran resolved (Jun 14, 2026), freed capacity allows Ukraine to move from "framework discussion" to "active ceasefire push"
- The Q3 2026 window (Jul-Sep) is the natural next slot in the cascade

**Forecasting implication**: When the playbook is active and a high-bandwidth conflict resolves, expect rapid pivot to the next unresolved major conflict. The Russia-Ukraine ceasefire forecast (vault p_yes=0.48 vs market 0.325, run #48) explicitly uses this pattern — the Iran deal completion is the catalyst that raises Ukraine ceasefire probability above generic base rates. The market at 32.5% appears underpriced because it does not fully discount the bandwidth cascade effect.

**Contrast with alternative**: Without the cascade pattern, each conflict is independent — Iran deal completion has no bearing on Ukraine probability. The cascade pattern makes them dependent serial events. This is a testable prediction: if Trump does NOT pivot to Ukraine in Q3 2026, the bandwidth cascade model is falsified for this administration.

## Structural Conditions That Enable the Playbook

1. **Executive agreement authority**: The US can commit to deals without Senate ratification when they don't constitute formal treaties. This is critical for speed.
2. **Adversary's weakened position**: In each case (Iran post-decapitation, Hamas post-assassination, Putin under sanctions), the counterparty needed the deal more than the US did.
3. **Political deadline alignment**: Midterm elections (Nov 2026) create a pre-November window for foreign policy wins — the playbook accelerates toward this deadline.
4. **Narrative consistency**: Each deal reinforces the "Trump ends wars" 2024 campaign promise. The foreign policy serves domestic political branding.

## Forecasting Applications

### Identifying when the playbook is in use

Look for these signals:
- A surprise summit with a major power adversary announced on short notice
- The president's social media teases "big things happening" without specifics
- Multiple deadlines cascade on Polymarket for the same underlying event
- Conflicts that were stalemated for years suddenly show movement

### Question types where this concept applies

1. "Will the US and [X] reach a peace deal by [date]?" — The playbook suggests specific conflict targets but wide timing uncertainty
2. "Will [conflict] be resolved during Trump's term?" — The playbook makes resolution more likely for conflicts in the Middle East and Eastern Europe, less so for East Asia
3. "Will Trump hold a summit with [adversary leader]?" — The playbook suggests summitry is the entry condition; if no summit by mid-term, the playbook isn't running

### Playbook state tracking

## Comparison to Alternative Models

| Model | Approach | Predicts | Doesn't Predict |
|-------|----------|----------|-----------------|
| **Peace-Broker Playbook** | Summit→sequential→capstone | Speed, sequencing, brand-building | Why specific conflicts are chosen |
| **Transactional Diplomacy** | Each deal on its merits | Deal speed, no ideological constraint | Sequencing pattern |
| **Isolationist Retrenchment** | Reduce global footprint | Disengagement from long wars | Why new conflicts are entered (Iran) |

The peace-broker playbook and transactional diplomacy are complementary: the playbook explains *the order*; transactionalism explains *the deals themselves*. Together they explain the full second-term foreign policy timeline better than either alone.
