# Great Power Multilateral Intervention

## Summary

The pattern in which multiple great powers cooperate to intervene militarily in a weaker state's internal affairs, typically justified by protection of civilian lives or international order. The [[Eight-Nation Alliance]]'s intervention in the [[Boxer Rebellion]] is a canonical example.

## Patterns

1. **Trigger**: A crisis within a weak state threatens foreign lives or property
2. **Coordination**: Powers form an ad hoc coalition despite underlying rivalries
3. **Escalation**: Limited intervention grows into full military occupation
4. **Post-intervention competition**: Allied powers compete for influence and concessions

## Historical Examples

- [[Eight-Nation Alliance]] (1900) — [[Boxer Rebellion]]
- Previous: [[Opium Wars]] (1839-1842, 1856-1860) — fewer powers, same pattern

## Forecasting Utility

Identify unstable regimes where multiple great powers have commercial or strategic interests. The probability of multilateral intervention increases when: (a) foreign nationals are concentrated in an urban area under threat, (b) major powers share a commercial stake (trade routes, railways), and (c) no single power has hegemonic control over the target state.
