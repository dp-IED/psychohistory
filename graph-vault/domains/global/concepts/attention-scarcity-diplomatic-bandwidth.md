# Attention Scarcity and Diplomatic Bandwidth Competition

## Definition

A structural constraint on diplomatic capacity: when state-on-state war erupts, it crowds out diplomatic bandwidth for asymmetric conflicts (insurgencies, civil wars, terrorist campaigns), and vice versa. The effect is asymmetric — interstate conflict **dominates** diplomatic attention because it directly threatens global stability, major power interests, and financial markets, while asymmetric conflicts can persist indefinitely below the threshold of major-power crisis priority.

## Core Mechanism

The mechanism operates through four channels:

1. **Leadership attention**: Presidents, PMs, and foreign ministers have finite time. A state-on-state crisis demands NSC/COBRA/crisis cabinet meetings, direct leader-to-leader calls, and continuous intelligence briefings. This leaves no capacity for secondary diplomatic tracks.

2. **Mediator bandwidth**: Third-party mediators (US, Qatar, Egypt, Turkey, UN) have finite negotiation teams. When their diplomatic corps is absorbed by a state-on-state crisis, asymmetric conflict mediation stalls.

3. **Domestic political capital**: Governments can only sustain a limited number of high-cost foreign policy initiatives simultaneously. A war in one theater consumes political capital that could have been used to push through controversial ceasefire terms in another.

4. **Intelligence and military resources**: Drone coverage, SIGINT intercepts, and special forces are finite. Re-tasking them to one theater degrades coverage elsewhere, reducing the flow of actionable intelligence needed for ceasefire enforcement.

## Historical Patterns

### Pattern A: Interstate Conflict Crowds Out Asymmetric (Observed in Israel-Iran-Gaza, 2024–2025)

| Phase | Interstate (Iran-Israel) | Asymmetric (Gaza) | Effect |
|-------|------------------------|-------------------|--------|
| Pre-war (pre-Jun 2024) | Proxy conflict, low direct risk | Active ceasefire negotiations | Bandwidth shared |
| State-on-state war (Jun 2024) | **Full crisis** | Negotiations frozen | Asymmetric crowded out |
| Ceasefire (Jun 2024) | De-escalating | Bandwidth reopens (Jul 2025) | Asymmetric resumed |

The June 2024 Iran-Israel direct exchange froze Gaza ceasefire talks for approximately 12-14 months. Diplomatic attention only returned to Gaza after the Iran-Israel ceasefire was secured (June 24, 2024) and the damage assessment completed. The first resumed Gaza talks occurred around mid-2025 — consistent with a ~12-month diplomatic re-engagement delay.

### Pattern B: Multi-Theater Overload (US Foreign Policy)

The US simultaneously managing Ukraine, Israel-Iran, Gaza, and Taiwan contingencies creates structural bandwidth scarcity. Each crisis reduces available diplomatic capital for the others. This pattern was a key factor in the slow US response to the Iran-Israel escalation in June 2024.

## Relationship to the Peace-Broker Bandwidth Cascade

This concept and the [[domains/global/concepts/peace-broker-diplomatic-playbook/_concept|diplomatic bandwidth cascade]] are complementary inverses:

| Dimension | Attention-Scarcity (this concept) | Bandwidth Cascade (peace-broker) |
|-----------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Direction** | Crises consume bandwidth, crowding out other tracks | Completed deals free bandwidth, accelerating next track |
| **Mechanism** | Finite NSC/mediator capacity; competition between active conflicts | Serial resolution frees sequential capacity |
| **Forecasting use** | When assessing whether parallel negotiations are feasible | When assessing velocity of next conflict resolution after a deal |
| **Canonical case** | Iran-Israel war (Jun 2024) froze Gaza talks for 12-14 months | Iran peace deal (Jun 2026) freed capacity for Ukraine ceasefire push |

Together they form a complete model: crises consume bandwidth (attention-scarcity); deal completions free bandwidth (bandwidth cascade). The Russia-Ukraine ceasefire forecast ([[runs/20260618-russia-ukraine-ceasefire]], p_yes=0.48) sits at the intersection — it depends on the bandwidth cascade thesis while the attention-scarcity pattern explains why Ukraine was deprioritized during the Iran peak (Feb-Jun 2026).

## Forecasting Applications

When forecasting a ceasefire or political settlement in an asymmetric conflict:

1. **Check for active state-on-state conflicts** involving at least one party's patron (e.g., US, Iran, Russia). If an interstate crisis is active, asymmetric ceasefire negotiations are structurally constrained.

2. **Estimate the re-engagement lag**: After a state-on-state ceasefire, it takes approximately **7-10 weeks minimum** for diplomatic teams to be reassigned and new frameworks negotiated for the asymmetric track. In severe cases (where the state-on-state war directly involved the mediator as a co-belligerent), the lag extends to **12-14 months**.

3. **Monitor the inverse**: When an asymmetric conflict flares (e.g., Gaza war Oct 2023), it increases the probability of state-on-state escalation through axis solidarity mechanisms (Houthi Red Sea attacks, Hezbollah border strikes, Iran proxy activation). The asymmetric conflict becomes a **trigger-density multiplier** for interstate escalation.

4. **Avoid the symmetric fallacy**: Do not assume that bandwidth competition is symmetrical. State-on-state conflict dominates asymmetric conflict in the competition for diplomatic attention. A minor interstate dispute will crowd out a major asymmetric conflict, not the other way around.
