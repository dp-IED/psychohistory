# Wartime Blame-Shifting

## Definition

A recurring pattern where a belligerent regime, typically authoritarian, falsely attributes a domestic terrorist attack or security crisis to its external adversary during an active war. The regime uses the attack to advance multiple propaganda objectives simultaneously despite clear evidence pointing to a different perpetrator (usually a non-state jihadist group).

This pattern is distinct from generic disinformation because it occurs in a specific context — during an active war that the regime is losing or struggling in — and serves specific wartime functions that peacetime denial-of-responsibility does not.

---

## Core Mechanism

The pattern follows a consistent sequence:

### Trigger
A terrorist attack, mass-casualty event, or security breach occurs within the regime's territory. The attack causes significant casualties and public shock.

### Stage 1: Immediate Attribution to the Adversary
Within hours or days of the attack, the regime (leader, foreign ministry, or state media) suggests or explicitly claims the external adversary was involved — before any credible investigation. This attribution often:
- Lacks specific evidence (no named perpetrators, no operational details)
- Contradicts the actual perpetrator's claim of responsibility
- Relies on vague language ("Ukraine's fingerprints are on this," "the traces lead to our enemies")

### Stage 2: Investigation Capture
The official investigation is directed toward validating the regime's predetermined narrative. Evidence pointing to the actual perpetrator is minimized, suppressed, or reframed as part of the adversary's "deception operation." Independent journalists and human rights organizations are denied access or labeled as foreign agents.

### Stage 3: Policy Justification
The regime uses the attack to justify:
- **Escalation of the war** (increased bombing, new mobilization, expanded target lists)
- **Domestic crackdown** (expanded surveillance, restrictions on civil liberties, crackdown on opposition as "enemy collaborators")
- **Rejection of negotiations** (framing talks with the adversary as "negotiating with terrorists")
- **New legal framework** (retroactive terrorism designations, expanded security powers)

### Stage 4: Narrative Lock-In
Even after the actual perpetrator is definitively identified (through forensic evidence, intelligence sharing from allies, or further claims of responsibility), the regime continues to insist on the adversary's involvement. The cost of admitting error — acknowledging the security failure, losing the propaganda benefit, or being seen as lying to the public — exceeds the cost of maintaining the false narrative.

---

## Canonical Case: Russia's Response to Crocus City Hall (March 2024)

The Crocus City Hall attack perfectly instantiates the pattern:

| Stage | Event | Timeline |
|-------|-------|----------|
| **Trigger** | ISIS-K attack at Crocus City Hall kills 145 in Moscow | March 22, 2024 |
| **Stage 1** | Putin acknowledges "radical Islamists" but says perpetrators were "attempting to escape to Ukraine" and suggests "window" was "prepared on the Ukrainian side" | March 23-25, 2024 |
| **Stage 2** | FSB investigation focuses on Ukrainian links; suspects are shown on state TV claiming Ukrainian coordination (under apparent coercion); US intelligence confirming ISIS-K responsibility is dismissed as "unreliable" | March 25-April 2024 |
| **Stage 3** | Putin uses the attack to justify increased strikes on Ukrainian energy infrastructure and frame Ukraine as a "terrorist state" — the rhetorical groundwork for expanded targeting | April 2024 |
| **Stage 4** | Russian officials continue to reference Ukrainian involvement in the Crocus attack into 2025-2026 despite universal independent confirmation of ISIS-K responsibility | Ongoing |

## Historical Parallels

### Assad Regime and Chemical Weapons
The Assad regime in Syria (2013-2018) repeatedly blamed opposition forces or external actors (US, Turkey) for chemical weapons attacks that UN investigators and OPCW found were conducted by Syrian government forces. The mechanism is structurally parallel: a regime under military pressure uses a shocking event to frame the adversary as the illegitimate aggressor.

### Nazi Germany and the Gleiwitz Incident (1939)
The Gleiwitz radio station attack — a false-flag operation where SS forces dressed as Polish soldiers attacked a German radio station — was used to justify the invasion of Poland. This is the historical archetype of wartime blame-shifting, though the Crocus pattern differs in that Russia did not perpetrate the attack itself but opportunistically redirected attribution.

### Saddam Hussein and the Gulf War Narrative
Saddam's regime blamed US/Israeli involvement for various attacks and uprisings during the Iran-Iraq War and post-Gulf War period, using external blaming to delegitimize domestic opposition.

## Forecasting Indicators

When a question involves a terrorist attack or security crisis in a belligerent state during an active war:

1. **Check the regime's narrative within 72 hours**: The speed and specificity of the adversary attribution is itself a signal. Rapid attribution without evidence (hours to days) suggests blame-shifting is active. Delayed, cautious, evidence-based attribution (1-2 weeks) suggests a more honest investigation.

2. **Look for evidence of pre-attack warnings**: Did the attacked regime receive specific warnings from intelligence-sharing partners? If warned, the regime's failure to prevent the attack creates a domestic vulnerability that blame-shifting helps manage. The Crocus case is canonical: the US warned Russia specifically about ISIS-K targeting Moscow gatherings.

3. **Assess the regime's propaganda needs at the time of the attack**: A regime that is losing the war, facing domestic unrest, or struggling to recruit soldiers has the highest incentive for blame-shifting. A regime that is winning or stable has lower incentive.

4. **Monitor the actual perpetrator's response**: If the group that claimed responsibility provides detailed operational information (target selection, weapons procurement, logistics) that matches forensic evidence, the false-flag/opportunistic attribution claim is weaker. If the group does not claim responsibility (e.g., a false-flag attack conducted by the regime itself), the evidentiary burden shifts.

5. **Track international consensus on attribution**: The number of states and international organizations that endorse the regime's attribution vs. the alternative attribution is a useful proxy for narrative success. In the Crocus case, no major state or organization endorsed Russia's Ukrainian attribution — the diplomatic consensus was unanimous that ISIS-K was responsible.

## Distinction from Related Concepts

- **False-flag attack** (perpetrated by the accuser): The regime itself carries out the attack to frame the adversary. The Crocus case is NOT a false-flag (ISIS-K genuinely conducted the attack) — it is **opportunistic blame-shifting**: the regime capitalizes on a real attack by redirecting public attribution.

- **Generic wartime propaganda**: All belligerents engage in propaganda. Blame-shifting is a specific subset where an ATTACK on the propagandist's own territory is blamed on the adversary. The emotional charge — the public's fear, grief, and desire for retribution — makes this a uniquely powerful propaganda tool.

- **Conspiracy theory promotion**: Unlike genuine conspiracy theories (which may be believed by the promoters), blame-shifting is a deliberate instrumental act — the regime knows ISIS-K was responsible but makes a strategic calculation to blame Ukraine anyway for short-term war benefits.

## Forecasting Applications

When forecasting questions about:
- **Terrorist attack attribution** during a war: Default to NO on questions asking whether the adversary was responsible, unless specific evidence emerges that contradicts the initial non-state actor claim.
- **Russian escalation triggers**: The blame-shifting pattern suggests Russia may use future domestic attacks (whether terrorist or accidental) to justify escalation, frame Ukraine as a "terrorist state," or increase domestic mobilization.
- **War termination dynamics**: A regime invested in blame-shifting has structurally reduced ability to de-escalate, because it has committed to the narrative that the adversary is a "terrorist state" — making negotiated settlement harder.
- **Domestic stability**: If a regime resorts to blame-shifting after a terrorist attack, it signals insecurity about its ability to maintain the "strong leader" narrative in the face of a security failure.
