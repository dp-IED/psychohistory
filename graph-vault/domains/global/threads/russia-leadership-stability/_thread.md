# Russia Leadership Stability

## Thread Summary

Tracks the probability of Vladimir Putin being removed from power as President of Russia before end of 2026. Covers formal constitutional removal mechanisms, elite faction dynamics, health indicators, and war-related triggers.

**Polymarket reference:** "Putin out as President of Russia by December 31, 2026?" — Price TBD (as of 2026-05-20)

## Key Dynamics

### 1. Formal Removal Mechanisms
- **Impeachment by Federation Council** — requires State Duma charge (treason or grave crime) + Constitutional Court confirmation + 2/3 Federation Council vote; never used
- **Resignation** — presidential prerogative; would indicate elite pressure
- **Incapacity determination** — constitutional mechanism if president cannot fulfill duties (requires medical commission + government confirmation)

### 2. Elite & Factional Dynamics
- **Siloviki vs. Technocrats** — security service faction (FSB, National Guard, Chekists) vs. economic managers (Central Bank, Ministry of Finance)
- **Military faction** — Wagner/Prighozin legacy, Shoigu-Gerasimov tension, Ukraine war performance
- **Succession planning** — any visible grooming of successors (Mishustin, Patrushev, Medvedev, Sobyanin)
- **Patronage networks** — sanctions pressure on elite families may erode loyalty

### 3. War-Related Triggers
- **Ukraine war culminating event** — defeat in key battle, mass mobilization protests, NATO escalation
- **Elite war fatigue** — cost of war exceeding benefits for key factions
- **Military coup risk** — historical pattern in authoritarian states after failed military campaigns

### 5. Domestic Security Crises
- **Terrorist attacks on Russian soil** create a dual challenge for the regime: (a) the FSB's failure to prevent the attack despite warnings undermines the "strong state" narrative, (b) the regime must simultaneously retaliate against the actual perpetrator while managing public anger
- **Crocus City Hall attack (March 22, 2024)**: 145 killed by ISIS-K. Putin employed [[domains/global/concepts/wartime-blame-shifting]] — blaming Ukraine despite clear ISIS-K responsibility — to (a) redirect public anger from FSB incompetence to the external enemy, (b) justify increased strikes on Ukraine, (c) avoid acknowledging that ISIS-K, which Russia claimed to have defeated in Syria, was still operationally capable
- **FSB intelligence failure signal**: The US warned Russia specifically about ISIS-K targeting Moscow "large gatherings" in early March 2024. Russia dismissed the warnings as "unfounded." The FSB's failure is either (a) political (could not act because acknowledging the threat would mean conceding ISIS-K was active after Russia claimed ISIS defeated in Syria), (b) operational (incompetence), or (c) both. Any explanation damages the regime's security legitimacy
- **Future terrorist attacks** on Russian soil will trigger the same blame-shifting dynamic. The next attack should be anticipated to produce: immediate Ukrainian/Ukrainian-adversary attribution → investigation directed accordingly → escalation justification. Forecasters should treat Russian attribution of domestic attacks to Ukraine as the default regime response pattern, not as evidence of actual Ukrainian involvement.

### 4. Health Indicators
- Putin's age (born 1952, age 73 in 2026)
- Speculation about Parkinson's/gait issues (public appearances monitored)
- COVID/isolation protocols observed
