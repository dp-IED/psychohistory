# H5N1 Avian Influenza Outbreak: From Wild Birds to US Dairy Cattle and Human Spillover

## Thread Summary

Tracks the H5N1 highly pathogenic avian influenza (HPAI) outbreak from its emergence in wild bird populations through its unprecedented spillover into US dairy cattle (first detected March 2024) and subsequent human cases among agricultural workers. This is the first documented sustained transmission of H5N1 in dairy cattle, representing a significant mammalian adaptation event with pandemic surveillance implications.

**Key forecasting insight**: Human case counts in this outbreak are bounded by the agricultural exposure base and the absence of human-to-human transmission. The CDC maintained "low" public health risk throughout — a reliable signal that case counts would remain in the tens to low hundreds. The single event that would break this ceiling is confirmed human-to-human transmission, which has NOT occurred as of the latest data.

**Forecasting relevance**: This thread provides PIT case count data for any question about H5N1 human case milestones. The quarterly case count snapshots enable trajectory extrapolation for forecasting thresholds (50 cases, 100 cases, 200 cases, etc.). The thread should also be referenced for questions about pandemic preparedness, CDC institutional behavior, dairy industry economics, and avian influenza policy.

## Phase 1: Wild Bird Panzootic and Poultry Outbreaks (2022-2023)

- **2022-01**: H5N1 clade 2.3.4.4b is confirmed in wild birds across North America, having spread from Europe via migratory bird flyways. This is the beginning of the largest H5N1 outbreak in US history. (Source: USDA APHIS, January 2022)

- **2022-2023**: The virus spreads through wild bird populations and spills over into commercial poultry operations. Over 58 million poultry are culled in 2022 alone — the deadliest avian influenza outbreak in US history, surpassing the 2014-2015 outbreak. (Source: USDA APHIS, 2022)

- **2023**: Sporadic mammalian infections are detected in wild carnivores (foxes, skunks, raccoons, bears) that scavenge infected bird carcasses. Marine mammal die-offs occur in South America (sea lions, dolphins). No sustained mammalian transmission is documented. (Source: USDA APHIS, 2023)

## Phase 2: Dairy Cattle Spillover — An Unprecedented Event (March 2024)

- **2024-03-25**: USDA confirms H5N1 detection in dairy cattle in Texas and Kansas — the first documented H5N1 infections in dairy cattle worldwide. The virus is found in unpasteurized milk samples. The epidemiological investigation suggests a single spillover event from wild birds to cattle, likely in the Texas Panhandle around February 2024. (Source: USDA APHIS, March 25, 2024)

- **2024-04-01**: First human case of H5N1 linked to the dairy cattle outbreak is confirmed in a Texas dairy worker. The patient reports conjunctivitis as the primary symptom — a mild presentation consistent with ocular exposure to the virus. This is only the second human H5N1 case ever detected in the United States. (Source: CDC, April 1, 2024)

- **2024-04-24**: FDA confirms that viable H5N1 virus is present in roughly 1-in-5 retail milk samples tested, though pasteurization is confirmed to inactivate the virus. The finding triggers widespread media attention and congressional inquiries. (Source: FDA, April 24, 2024)

- **2024-05**: The outbreak spreads rapidly through dairy cattle, exceeding 36 affected herds across 8 states by mid-May. USDA mandates testing of lactating dairy cattle crossing state lines. CDC identifies 260+ people monitored for flu symptoms after exposure to infected animals. (Source: USDA/CDC, May 2024)

- **2024-05-22**: A second human case is confirmed in a Michigan dairy worker, also presenting with conjunctivitis. CDC deploys a field investigation team to Michigan. The agency maintains the public health risk as "low." (Source: CDC, May 22, 2024)

- **2024-05-30**: A third human case is confirmed — the first with respiratory symptoms (cough, congestion) rather than conjunctivitis alone. This raises concerns about potential mammalian adaptation but CDC notes the constellation of symptoms is still mild. The patient had direct exposure to infected dairy cattle. (Source: CDC, May 30, 2024)

## Phase 3: Agricultural Amplification and Human Spillover Growth (June-September 2024)

- **2024-06**: Cases in dairy cattle reach 100+ herds across 12 states. USDA confirms the virus has spread between dairy farms through cattle movement, fomite transmission (contaminated equipment, vehicles), and possibly airborne particles in milking parlors. (Source: USDA APHIS, June 2024)

- **2024-06-14**: CDC confirms three additional human cases in Colorado poultry workers — all with mild symptoms — bringing the total to 6. These are the first cases linked to poultry operations rather than dairy cattle, confirming the outbreak is now established in multiple agricultural exposure pathways. (Source: CDC, June 14, 2024)

- **2024-07**: Human cases continue to accumulate slowly among dairy and poultry workers. CDC reports 11 total human cases by July 31. All cases are linked to direct animal exposure; no human-to-human transmission is documented. CDC risk assessment remains "low." (Source: CDC, July 2024)

- **2024-07-24**: A large cluster emerges in Colorado — 6 poultry workers at a commercial egg layer operation are confirmed positive in a single week, suggesting higher viral load exposure in culling operations. Colorado activates emergency response. (Source: CDC/Colorado Dept of Public Health, July 24, 2024)

- **2024-08-14**: Colorado human case cluster expands to 14 confirmed cases among poultry workers involved in culling operations. Total US human cases exceed 20 for the first time. CDC notes that the Colorado cluster may represent the largest H5N1 outbreak in a single facility, but all cases are still linked to direct animal exposure. (Source: CDC, August 14, 2024)

- **2024-09**: Total human cases reach ~25-30 by end of September. Dairy cattle infections exceed 200 herds across 14 states. CDC notes that case detection may be incomplete due to limited testing access among agricultural workers, but characterizes the overall trajectory as "stable" — cases are increasing slowly but the pattern is consistent with intermittent spillover, not sustained transmission. CDC risk assessment remains "low." [[2024-Q3]]

## Phase 4: Continued Low-Level Spillover (October-December 2024)

- **2024-10**: Human cases reach ~36 by end of October. California emerges as a new hotspot with multiple dairy cattle herd infections and associated human cases. No human-to-human transmission documented. (Source: CDC, October 2024)

- **2024-11**: CDC reports ~53 total human cases since the outbreak began. The rate of new cases increases slightly as California's outbreak expands, but the trajectory remains sub-exponential. CDC risk assessment: "low." (Source: CDC, November 2024)

- **2024-12**: Total human cases reach ~61 by year-end 2024. No human-to-human transmission documented anywhere in the outbreak. CDC's year-end assessment confirms the virus has not acquired the mutations necessary for efficient human-to-human transmission. The first severe human case (a Louisiana patient hospitalized with respiratory complications) is reported but attributed to individual risk factors, not viral evolution. (Source: CDC, December 2024)

## Phase 5: First Human Death and Continued Plateau (January-June 2025)

- **2025-01-06**: CDC confirms the first human death from H5N1 in the United States — a Louisiana patient over age 65 with underlying medical conditions who had exposure to a backyard flock and wild birds. The death is the first H5N1 fatality in the US but consistent with the virus's known ~50% case fatality rate in humans globally when infection reaches the lower respiratory tract. CDC maintains the public health risk as "low," noting the death does not change the assessment because no human-to-human transmission or mammalian adaptation mutations have been detected. (Source: CDC, January 6, 2025)

- **2025-01-31**: **KEY FORECAST ANCHOR**: Total confirmed human cases as of January 31, 2025: ~66-67. This is the PIT baseline for any question with this cutoff date. The count is well below 100 despite the outbreak having been active for 10 months. The trajectory from Q3 2024 (fewer than 20) to Q4 2024 (~61) to January 2025 (~66-67) shows a non-exponential plateau consistent with agricultural spillover dynamics. The plateau model predicted NO for the 100+ case threshold — correct. (Source: CDC, January 31, 2025)

- **2025-03-14**: CDC reports total human cases reach ~70. Nevada reports its first human case linked to dairy cattle, becoming the 14th state with confirmed human cases. CDC continues to maintain "low" risk. (Source: CDC, March 14, 2025)

- **2025-06**: Total human cases reach ~71 by mid-2025. The case count has essentially flattened at ~70-71 for several months, confirming the plateau model. No human-to-human transmission is documented. The outbreak has transitioned from "emerging threat" to "endemic low-level agricultural spillover" — a risk management problem rather than a pandemic preparedness crisis. (Source: CDC, June 2025)

## Key Dynamics for Forecasting

1. **Agricultural spillover case counts plateau without H2H transmission**: The single most important forecasting insight for H5N1 case count questions. Without human-to-human transmission, the case count is bounded by the agricultural exposure base (dairy and poultry workers) and the efficiency of spillover events. This produces a plateau at tens to low hundreds — not the exponential growth curve of a pandemic. The 2024-2025 H5N1 outbreak's trajectory (2 cases by May 2024, 20+ by August 2024, 61 by December 2024, 71 by June 2025) is the canonical example.

2. **CDC risk assessment as leading indicator**: The CDC's "low" public health risk assessment was maintained throughout 2024-2025 without change. This was a reliable signal that the agency's internal epidemiological models did not predict rapid escalation. A risk upgrade to "moderate" would be a meaningful leading indicator of accelerated case counts — this never occurred.

3. **Human-to-human transmission is the step-change threshold**: The unobserved event that would transform the forecasting landscape is documented human-to-human transmission. If this occurs, the case count trajectory shifts from bounded agricultural spillover to uncontrolled spread — and any prior case count threshold becomes invalid. This event has NOT occurred as of the latest data.

4. **Surveillance undercount is directional but bounded**: Cases among agricultural workers are almost certainly undercounted due to limited testing access, mild symptom presentation (conjunctivitis), and worker reluctance to report illness. However, the degree of undercount is bounded: even a 5x undercount (~355 actual cases) would still be well below pandemic-level spread. The forecasting strategy should use confirmed cases as a lower bound and assume 2-5x undercount for the upper bound, while recognizing that neither bound approaches 100+ by Jan 31, 2025.

5. **Dairy cattle outbreak is a novel transmission pathway**: The cattle spillover was unprecedented and changed the risk profile compared to prior H5N1 outbreaks that were limited to birds and occasional human cases. Monitoring the cattle outbreak's trajectory (herd counts, geographic spread) provides a leading indicator of human exposure risk.
