## Overview

This thread tracks changes in nuclear weapons posture, doctrine, and deployment by nuclear-armed states — distinct from the broader Russia-Ukraine war or Iran nuclear program threads. Nuclear posture changes (alert levels, treaty withdrawals, deployment announcements, doctrinal shifts) are the primary leading indicators for assessing whether the structural barriers against nuclear weapon use are weakening. 

**Key distinction**: Most nuclear posture events are political signaling (deterrence, negotiation leverage, domestic audience management) rather than preparation for use. The thread's analytical value is in distinguishing saber-rattling from actual preparation — a distinction that is critical for nuclear detonation forecasting.

### 2023

- **Feb 21** — Putin announces Russia is suspending participation in the New START nuclear arms reduction treaty, the last remaining bilateral arms control agreement between the US and Russia. Suspension means Russia stops providing data on its nuclear forces and halts on-site inspections. The treaty remains in effect legally (Russia did not withdraw entirely) but verification mechanisms are frozen. [[timeline/2023-Q1]]
  - **Forecasting significance**: New START suspension is a nuclear **posture signaling** event, not a use preparation event. The suspension affects the long-term arms control regime, not short-term detonation probability.
  
- **May 25** — Russia and Belarus sign an agreement allowing the stationing of Russian tactical nuclear weapons on Belarusian territory. The deal puts Russian nuclear weapons on Belarusian soil for the first time since the end of the Cold War. [[timeline/2023-Q2]]
  - **Forecasting significance**: This is a forward-deployment event — Russia moves nuclear warheads closer to NATO territory. While this represents an escalation in the Russia-NATO confrontation, it does not indicate preparation for use. Tactical nuclear weapons stationed in Belarus serve a deterrent/threat-signaling function. The deployment increases the risk of escalation in a future Russia-NATO crisis but does not change the immediate base rate of nuclear detonation.

## Key Dynamics

### 1. Nuclear Rhetoric ⇔ Battlefield Position Correlation

Across all nuclear-armed states, nuclear references in official communications correlate inversely with conventional military success. When a nuclear-armed state is losing a conventional war (or facing a serious security challenge), nuclear rhetoric increases. This pattern holds for:
- Russia in Ukraine (2022-ongoing): nuclear rhetoric spikes after Kherson loss, Kharkiv counteroffensive, Kursk incursion
- US during the Cold War: nuclear threats during the Korean War stalemate (1950-53), Dien Bien Phu (1954)
- Pakistan: nuclear references during crises with India (2001-02, 2008)

**Forecasting rule**: Nuclear rhetoric that follows a conventional military reversal is **deterrent signaling**, not preparation for use. The probability of actual nuclear use is not elevated by such rhetoric alone. Use-only probabilities rise only when rhetoric is accompanied by operational preparation (alert level changes, weapon dispersal, command and control modification).

### 2. Arms Control Treaty Degradation

The New START suspension (Feb 2023) is part of a broader degradation of the arms control architecture:
- INF Treaty (1987) → US withdrew 2019 citing Russian violations
- Open Skies Treaty (1992) → US withdrew 2020, Russia withdrew 2021
- New START (2010) → Russia suspended participation 2023
- No remaining bilateral arms control agreement with verification mechanisms

This degradation increases long-term strategic uncertainty but does NOT increase short-term nuclear detonation probability. The arms control architecture's erosion primarily affects force planning, transparency, and crisis stability — not the immediate likelihood of use.

### 3. Forward Deployment and Nuclear Sharing

The Russia-Belarus tactical nuclear deployment (2023) mirrors NATO's nuclear sharing arrangements (US weapons in Belgium, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, Turkey) but in a post-Cold War escalation context. Key distinction:
- NATO's nuclear sharing has existed since the 1950s with established command and control
- Russia's Belarus deployment is new, with less tested C2 procedures, increasing the risk of unauthorized use or loss of control during crisis scenarios

### 4. Nuclear Modernization Programs

All P5 states are actively modernizing their nuclear arsenals:
- US: Sentinel ICBM, B-21 Raider, Columbia-class SSBN, W87-1 warhead — $1.5T program over 30 years
- Russia: Avangard hypersonic glide vehicle, Burevestnik nuclear-powered cruise missile (questionable reliability), Sarmat heavy ICBM, Poseidon nuclear torpedo
- China: Rapid expansion of silo-based and mobile ICBM force — estimated 700+ warheads by 2027, potentially 1,000+ by 2030
- UK: Dreadnought-class SSBN program
- France: Third-generation SSBN (SNLE-3G) program

**Forecasting significance**: Modernization programs are long-cycle structural developments. They do not change short-term detonation probability. Their forecasting value is in assessing long-term crisis stability (e.g., China's ICBM expansion reduces US first-strike confidence over the 2025-2035 horizon).

## Forecasting Significance

This thread provides the **structural context for any nuclear detonation forecast**:

1. **Establishes the baseline**: Nuclear detonation is an extremely low base-rate event. The thread documents why — through the nuclear taboo, deterrence structure, CTBT monitoring, and weapons safety record.

2. **Distinguishes saber-rattling from use preparation**: Most nuclear posture events (treaty suspensions, deployment announcements, doctrinal statements) are political signaling, not use preparation. The thread maintains this distinction to prevent over-reaction to nuclear news.

3. **Tracks the erosion of crisis stability**: Arms control degradation, new forward deployments, and modernization programs increase long-term strategic risk. While these do not change short-term (6-month) detonation probability, they increase the probability over 5-10 year horizons.

4. **Connects to the nuclear-use-base-rates concept**: Every nuclear posture event should be assessed against the base-rate framework — does this event actually increase the probability of a nuclear detonation within the forecasting window, or is it a routine (if alarming) political signal?
