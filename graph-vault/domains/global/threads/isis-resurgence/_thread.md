# ISIS Resurgence: Operational Capability and Global Threat Trajectory

## Thread Summary

Tracks the Islamic State's reconstitution and operational trajectory since its territorial defeat in 2019, with primary focus on ISIS-Khorasan (ISIS-K) as the most globally active affiliate. The thread spans the Abbey Gate bombing (August 2021), the Kerman bombings (January 2024), and the Crocus City Hall attack (March 2024), connecting these events into a single narrative of ISIS's post-caliphate evolution.

**Key driver**: The Taliban's takeover of Afghanistan in August 2021 created both opportunity (access to US weapons, sanctuary in Taliban-contested areas) and motive (competition with Taliban for jihadist legitimacy) for ISIS-K's resurgence.

**Forecasting relevance**: ISIS-K's demonstrated capability to strike Russia and Iran — both states that, in different ways, claimed to have defeated ISIS — has implications for future terrorism forecasts, including: probability of ISIS-K attacks in Europe or the US; whether Russia's ISIS threat will affect its war effort in Ukraine; and whether Iran becomes a sustained ISIS-K target.

---

## Phase 1: Post-Caliphate Reconstitution (2019-2021)

- **2019-10-27**: Death of Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi in US Delta Force raid. Abu Ibrahim al-Qurayshi becomes new caliph. The Islamic State is territorially defeated but retains underground networks, sleeper cells, and ideological reach.

- **2020**: COVID-19 pandemic disrupts counter-terrorism operations globally, providing operational space for ISIS reconstitution. ISIS-K in Afghanistan intensifies attacks on the Afghan government and NATO forces.

- **2021-01**: US intelligence assessments note ISIS-K is the most active ISIS affiliate with an "ambitious external operations agenda" (source: UN Security Council monitoring team reports, Jan 2021).

---

## Phase 2: The Afghanistan Opportunity (Aug 2021 - 2023)

- **2021-08-15**: Taliban captures Kabul as US-backed Afghan government collapses.

- **2021-08-26**: **Abbey Gate bombing** at Kabul airport — ISIS-K suicide bomber kills 13 US service members and ~170 Afghan civilians. The deadliest attack on US forces in Afghanistan since 2011. Demonstrates ISIS-K's operational capability even as the Taliban takes power. [[2021-Q3]]

- **2021-09 - 2023**: ISIS-K wages an insurgency against the Taliban regime, attacking Taliban checkpoints, Hazara civilians (particularly Shia mosques and schools), and foreign diplomatic targets (Chinese-owned hotel in Kabul, Russian embassy near-miss). The Taliban's counter-ISIS operations are limited by capacity and divert resources from governance.

- **2023**: ISIS-K conducts sustained operations targeting:
  - Taliban patrols and officials (particularly in Nangarhar, Kunar, Kabul)
  - Hazara Shia civilians (multiple bombings of mosques, schools)
  - Chinese economic interests in Afghanistan (Longan hotel attack, December 2022)
  - Pakistan Taliban (TTP) safe havens, contributing to TTP-Pakistan conflict

**Key dynamic**: The Taliban and ISIS-K are locked in a competition for jihadist legitimacy — the Taliban claims it brought peace and Islamic governance; ISIS-K argues the Taliban abandoned true jihad by negotiating with the US and failing to impose sharia. This rivalry drives operational tempo on both sides.

## Phase 3: Global Strike Capability Demonstrated (2024)

### Kerman Bombings (January 3, 2024)
- **Event**: Twin bombings at the memorial ceremony for Qasem Soleimani in Kerman, Iran. ~100 killed, ~280 injured. Deadliest terrorist attack on Iranian soil since the 1970s.
- **Attribution**: ISIS-K claims responsibility. The attack demonstrates ISIS-K's ability to project force into Iran, bypassing Iranian intelligence services.
- **Significance**: Iran had previously claimed it was unaffected by ISIS (having fought ISIS in Iraq/Syria). The attack shatters this narrative and forces Iran to confront the ISIS-K threat from Afghanistan.
- **Cutoff note**: This event occurred before the Crocus City Hall attack and was part of the same ISIS-K operational campaign. However, a February 2024 Iranian retaliatory strike inside Pakistan (directed at what Iran claimed was an ISIS-K training camp) indicates the attack triggered a security response from Iran. [[2024-Q1]]

### Crocus City Hall Attack (March 22, 2024)
- **Event**: Four gunmen open fire on concertgoers and set fire to the Crocus City Hall in Krasnogorsk, Moscow. 145 killed, 551 injured. Deadliest terrorist attack in Russia since the Beslan school siege (2004).
- **Attribution**: ISIS-K claims responsibility via Amaq news agency within hours. US intelligence confirms ISIS-K responsibility — US officials had warned Russia about an imminent ISIS-K attack targeting large gatherings in Moscow in early March 2024.
- **Russian response**: Putin acknowledges the attack was carried out by "radical Islamists" but simultaneously suggests Ukrainian involvement — an unsupported claim that fits the [[concepts/wartime-blame-shifting]] pattern. No evidence of Ukrainian involvement is ever produced.
- **Security failure**: The attack represents a major failure of Russian intelligence/security services (FSB). Russia had received multiple warnings from the US (via the "duty to warn" intelligence-sharing protocol) about imminent ISIS-K attack plans targeting Moscow.

**Propaganda value for ISIS**: The Crocus City Hall attack is ISIS-K's most high-profile external operation, demonstrating the ability to strike a nuclear-armed P5 state in its capital. The attack benefits ISIS recruiting by: (1) embarrassing a regime that claimed to have defeated ISIS in Syria, (2) exposing Russian vulnerability despite the security state apparatus, (3) exploiting Russia's Central Asian migrant population for operational cover.

## Phase 4: Post-Crocus Trajectory (2024-2026)

- **2024**: ISIS-K plots disrupted in Germany (planned attacks on Christmas markets, Cologne Cathedral), Austria, and the UK. European counter-terrorism services increase alert levels.
- **2025-2026**: The trajectory of ISIS-K's external operations depends on:
  - Taliban capacity to suppress ISIS-K in Afghanistan (limited)
  - Western intelligence cooperation with Taliban (politically constrained)
  - Russian/Pakistani/Iranian counter-ISIS operations (uncoordinated, varying effectiveness)
  - Recruitment flows from Central Asia, the Caucasus, and South Asia

## Forecasting Implications

1. **ISIS-K as a multi-state threat**: Unlike Al-Qaeda, which focused on the "far enemy" (US), ISIS-K treats both the "far enemy" (Western states) and "near enemies" (Russia, Iran, China, Taliban) as legitimate targets. This broad targeting makes ISIS-K a threat to more states simultaneously.

2. **Russian security vulnerability**: The FSB's failure to prevent the Crocus attack despite explicit US warnings suggests deep systemic weaknesses in Russian domestic intelligence. This has implications for:
   - Security of the 2025-2026 World Cup / major Russian events
   - Potential ISIS-K attacks on other Russian soft targets
   - Russian ability to divert security forces to Ukraine (unlikely to be materially affected, but political vulnerability matters)
   - Domestic political consequences: the security failure erodes the "strong state" narrative

3. **Iran as an ongoing ISIS-K target**: The Kerman bombing and Iranian retaliation inside Pakistan suggest an Iran-ISIS-K dynamic that could escalate further. Iran's desire to avenge Soleimani's death at the Kerman anniversary bombing creates ongoing risk.

4. **Cascade forecasting**: If ISIS-K can strike Russia and Iran with impunity, the probability of a successful ISIS-K attack in Europe or North America in the 2026-2028 period should be revised upward from pre-Crocus baselines, particularly for venues with large Central Asian diaspora populations.
