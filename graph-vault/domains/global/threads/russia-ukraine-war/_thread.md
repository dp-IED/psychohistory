# Russia-Ukraine War

## Overview

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine began on February 24, 2022 — the largest conventional military conflict in Europe since 1945. As of the 2026 perspective, the war has gone through six distinct phases: the initial Russian offensive and Kyiv withdrawal (Feb-Apr 2022), the Donbas artillery war (May-Sep 2022), the Ukrainian counteroffensives (Sep-Nov 2022), the protracted winter-spring positional warfare (Dec 2022-May 2023), the failed Ukrainian 2023 counteroffensive (Jun-Nov 2023), and the period of Russian initiative and Western aid uncertainty (2024-present).

This thread is the primary forecasting reference for: (1) ceasefire timing and feasibility, (2) territorial outcomes, (3) war termination dynamics, (4) NATO-Russia escalation, and (5) post-war settlement architecture. The active Russia-Ukraine ceasefire forecast ([[runs/20260618-russia-ukraine-ceasefire]], vault p_yes=0.48 vs market 0.325, Dec 31 deadline) is the thread's primary live prediction — divergence case driven by the peace-broker bandwidth cascade thesis. It is organized around the structural barriers to ceasefire — factors that make negotiated settlement structurally unlikely regardless of battlefield developments.

### Phase 4: Winter Stalemate and Bakhmut (Dec 2022 - May 2023)

At the close of 2022, both sides faced the first winter of the war. Russia had mobilized 300,000 reservists (Sep 2022) and was constructing extensive defensive lines in occupied territories. Ukraine had liberated Kherson (Nov 11) and Kharkiv Oblast (Sep) but lacked the force ratios to sustain offensive operations through winter.

- **Dec 2022-Feb 2023**: Russian forces under General Sergei Surovikin (appointed Oct 2022) constructed an extensive defensive network — "Surovikin Line" — across occupied southern and eastern Ukraine. Multi-layered minefields, dragon's teeth anti-tank barriers, and trench systems. This defensive architecture was specifically designed to absorb a Ukrainian counteroffensive in 2023.
- **Jan 2023**: Russia captures Soledar (Jan 16) after months of Wagner Group assaults. Casualty rate extreme — Wagner lost thousands of convict-recruits.
- **Feb-Mar 2023**: Battle of Bakhmut intensifies. Wagner Group leads human-wave assaults, suffering 20,000+ casualties (estimated 10,000 killed) for marginal territorial gains. Ukraine holds Bakhmut through deliberate defense-in-depth, trading space for Russian attrition.
- **May 20-21, 2023**: Russia claims capture of Bakhmut after 10 months of fighting. Ukraine confirms withdrawal to western outskirts. The battle cost Russia an estimated 30,000+ casualties (20,000 Wagner alone) and consumed Russia's prisoner-recruit pool. Ukraine also suffered heavy losses but at a lower kill ratio than Russian assaults produced.
- **May 2023**: Ukraine receives first Leopard 2 and Challenger 2 main battle tanks from allies. Training and equipping of new brigades for planned counteroffensive continues through spring.

**Forecasting significance**: Bakhmut demonstrated Russia's willingness to accept extreme casualty rates for symbolic territorial gains. The Wagner Group's leading role revealed Russian military dependence on irregular forces. Ukraine's strategy of attrition-defense, exchanging space for Russian lives, set the pattern for 2023-2024.

### Phase 5: Ukrainian 2023 Counteroffensive (Jun-Nov 2023)

The eagerly-anticipated Ukrainian counteroffensive began in June 2023 with multiple axes of advance in southern Ukraine (Zaporizhzhia Oblast) and eastern Ukraine (Bakhmut flanks).

- **Jun 4-8, 2023**: Ukraine launches offensive operations in southern Ukraine along three axes — towards Tokmak, Berdiansk, and the flanks of Bakhmut. Initial progress slow against prepared Russian defenses.
- **Jun-Jul 2023**: Ukraine's Western-trained brigades encounter the full depth of Russian defensive preparations — dense minefields, pre-registered artillery zones, and effective Russian electronic warfare (EW) systems that degrade Ukrainian drone reconnaissance and communication. Western combined-arms doctrine (leapfrogging through gaps) fails against a mined, pre-sighted defense with no gaps to exploit.
- **Jul 2023**: The US supplies cluster munitions (DPICM) to Ukraine to address artillery shell shortages. UK and France supply Storm Shadow/Scalp long-range cruise missiles — Ukraine begins systematic strikes on Russian logistics nodes, command posts, and naval targets.
- **Aug 2023**: Ukraine achieves the liberation of Robotyne (village in Zaporizhzhia Oblast, Aug 28), its most significant territorial gain of the counteroffensive. Total advance: ~10km at deepest penetration, far short of the strategic objective (Tokmak and the Azov Sea land bridge).
- **Sep-Oct 2023**: Ukraine's offensive momentum stalls against Russian defensive depth. Russia activates its own offensive operation at Avdiivka (Oct 10) — a grinding assault that pins Ukrainian forces and prevents redeployment to other sectors.
- **Nov 2023**: Ukrainian commanders (General Valeriy Zaluzhnyi) publicly acknowledge that the counteroffensive has reached a positional stalemate. Zaluzhnyi's November 2023 Economist essay — "it is not a deep and beautiful breakthrough" — marks the definitive end of expectations for a decisive Ukrainian victory. The war enters a new phase: protracted attrition.

**FORECASTING SIGNIFICANCE — CRITICAL**: The failure of the 2023 counteroffensive was the single most consequential structural development for 2024 ceasefire probability. Before this failure, a plausible pathway to negotiations existed: decisive Ukrainian territorial gains could have forced Russia to the table. After the failure, the options narrowed to: (a) indefinite attrition warfare, (b) Russian territorial gains, or (c) external mediation (which did not materialize in 2024). The counteroffensive's failure meant neither side could achieve a battlefield victory, and neither side had an incentive to accept a frozen conflict — the exact structural preconditions for NO ceasefire in 2024.

### Phase 6: Russian Initiative and US Aid Crisis (Dec 2023 - Dec 2024)

- **Dec 2023**: EU proposes €50B Ukraine aid package; Hungary vetoes. US Congress enters debate over $61.4B Ukraine supplemental — House Republicans demand border security linkage. Ukraine faces acute ammunition shortages as US supplies dwindle.
- **Jan-Feb 2024**: Russia captures Avdiivka (Feb 17) — the first major Russian territorial gain since Bakhmut. Avdiivka's fall was enabled directly by Ukraine's artillery shell shortage: Russia was firing 5-10x more shells per day. Ukraine withdraws from positions north and east of Avdiivka.
- **Feb 2024**: EU adopts €50B Ukraine Facility (Feb 1) after Hungary drops objection. US aid remains stuck in House. Ukraine's ammunition situation critical — Russian artillery fires 10,000+ rounds/day vs Ukraine's ~2,000.
- **Apr 20, 2024**: US House passes $61B Ukraine aid package after months of delay. The six-month gap (Oct 2023-Apr 2024) had the direct effect of enabling Russian territorial gains, depleting Ukrainian air defense interceptors, and signaling to Moscow that Western support was fragile.
- **May-Jun 2024**: Russian forces open new front in northern Kharkiv Oblast (May 10), advancing toward Vovchansk. The offensive spreads Ukrainian forces thin across an extended front. Russia makes slow but steady gains in Donetsk Oblast — Chasiv Yar, Ocheretyne, and other settlements fall.
- **Jun 2024**: EU formally opens accession negotiations with Ukraine and Moldova (Jun 25). Russia demands withdrawal of Ukrainian troops from Donetsk and Luhansk as precondition for talks — rejected by Ukraine.
- **Jul 2024**: Biden withdraws from presidential race (Jul 21). Trump's rising election odds introduce major uncertainty about future US aid to Ukraine. Zelenskyy signals openness to negotiations but conditions remain non-negotiable (no territorial concessions, NATO membership).
- **Aug 6, 2024**: Ukraine launches surprise incursion into Kursk Oblast, Russia — first occupation of Russian territory since WWII. Ukraine controls ~1,000 sq km at peak. Operation designed to:
  1. Force Russia to redeploy troops from Donbas front
  2. Demonstrate Ukraine's continued offensive capability
  3. Create a bargaining chip for future negotiations
  4. Boost morale and show Western donors Ukraine can still act
  Russia does NOT substantively redeploy from Donbas — continues grinding advance toward Pokrovsk.
- **Sep-Oct 2024**: Russia continues Donbas offensive — captures Vuhledar (Oct 1), advances on Pokrovsk and Kurakhove. The Kursk incursion, while tactically surprising, fails to achieve its primary strategic objective (relieving Donbas pressure).
- **Nov 2024**: Trump wins US presidential election (Nov 5). Introduces major uncertainty: Trump had claimed he could "end the war in 24 hours" but provided no details. Ukraine fears being forced into unfavorable settlement. Russia calculates that Trump will reduce US aid, improving Russia's long-term position.
- **Dec 2024**: No ceasefire or peace settlement achieved. Russia continues slow advance in Donbas. Ukraine holds Kursk salient but Russian counterattacks begin reducing it. Neither side makes serious diplomatic overtures.

**Forecasting significance — US aid as structural variable**: The six-month US aid gap (Oct 2023-Apr 2024) is the canonical case of "aid delay as Russian assurance" — the delay itself signaled to Moscow that Ukraine's external support was fragile, reducing any incentive Russia had to negotiate. A ceasefire in 2024 would have required either (a) a decisive Ukrainian victory (precluded by the 2023 counteroffensive failure and ammunition shortage), (b) a decisive Russian victory (precluded by Ukrainian resistance and eventual Western aid resumption), or (c) credible mediation (none existed — China was pro-Russia, India/Brazil/Gulf states lacked leverage, and the US under Biden refused to pressure Ukraine). The question resolved NO because none of these three pathways were viable.

## Structural Barriers to Ceasefire (2024-specific)

### 1. Territorial Incompatibility
Russia demanded recognition of annexed territories (Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson, Crimea). Ukraine demanded full restoration of pre-2014 borders. These positions were irreconcilable in 2024 — neither side could accept the other's terms without political suicide.

### 2. No Mutually Hurting Stalemate
The 2023 counteroffensive failure created positional warfare where both sides could continue fighting indefinitely. Russia could absorb manpower losses and trade territory for time. Ukraine could defend static positions with Western material. Neither side was suffering enough to accept unfavorable terms.

### 3. No Credible Mediator
In 2024, no external actor had sufficient leverage over both parties. China was perceived as pro-Russia (and offered a peace plan that would freeze Russian gains). The US under Biden refused to pressure Ukraine. The EU lacked unified foreign policy. The UN had no enforcement mechanism. Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and the UAE offered mediation venues but had no leverage to enforce terms. The absence of a Trump-like mediator (who would pressure both sides) meant no negotiating dynamic existed.

### 4. Domestic Political Constraints
**Ukraine**: Zelenskyy had decreed (Sep 2022) that negotiations with Putin were illegal. Ukrainian public opinion (80%+ opposition to territorial concessions) made any compromise politically toxic. **Russia**: Putin had staked regime legitimacy on the war and the annexation of four oblasts. Accepting anything less than de facto control of annexed territories would have been a political defeat.

### 5. External Sustainment
Ukraine continued to receive Western military aid (after the Apr 2024 US package). Europe's defense industries began ramping up production. Russia's economy was on a war footing. Both sides had the material capacity to continue fighting through 2024. The war's continuation did not impose unbearable costs on either patron (Western publics were war-weary but not revolting; Russian society was mobilized but not collapsing).

## Ceasefire Forecasting Framework

For any future "Russia x Ukraine ceasefire by [date]" question, apply this checklist:

1. **Territorial precondition**: Has either side made a major concession on territorial demands? (If no, ceasefire unlikely)
2. **Military trajectory**: Is either side achieving decisive battlefield victories? (If no — positional warfare — ceasefire unlikely mid-fighting; if yes, check if the victor has incentive to stop before total victory)
3. **Mediator capacity**: Does a credible mediator exist with leverage over both sides? (In 2024, no. The Trump administration, starting Jan 2025, would fill this role — see [[domains/global/threads/us-russia-diplomatic-relations]])
4. **External sustainment**: Can both sides continue fighting without unacceptable patron costs? (In 2024, yes — the US aid package passed and Europe was adapting)
5. **Domestic political space**: Do either side's leaders have the political room to make concessions? (In 2024, no)
6. **Patron signaling**: Has the primary patron (US) signaled intent to reduce or condition aid? (In 2024, Biden was firm; Trump's victory in Nov introduced future uncertainty but did not affect 2024 timeline)

When all six factors point toward continuation, the base rate for a ceasefire resolution within the current year is near-zero (P < 0.10).
