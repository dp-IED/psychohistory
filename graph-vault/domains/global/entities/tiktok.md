# TikTok

## Summary

TikTok is a short-form video-sharing social networking service owned by Chinese company ByteDance. Launched internationally in September 2017 (as a successor to musical.ly, which was acquired in 2017), TikTok became one of the world's most downloaded apps, surpassing 1 billion monthly active users by 2021. Its algorithm-driven content recommendation system proved highly engaging and culturally influential.

## Significance for Forecasting

TikTok became a central flashpoint in US-China technology competition, demonstrating how national security concerns about Chinese-owned technology platforms can lead to forced divestiture legislation with bipartisan support. The TikTok case established a legal and political precedent for banning Chinese-owned consumer applications in Western markets.

## Timeline

- **2016**: ByteDance launches Douyin (Chinese version)
- **2017**: ByteDance launches TikTok internationally; acquires musical.ly
- **2019**: CFIUS reviews ByteDance's acquisition of musical.ly
- **2020**: Trump issues executive orders attempting to ban TikTok; blocked by courts
- **2021**: Biden revokes Trump orders, orders CFIUS review
- **2023**: Montana passes first state-level TikTok ban (later blocked)
- **2024-03-13**: House passes Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act (352-65)
- **2024-04-24**: Biden signs bill into law, giving ByteDance 9 months to divest
- **2024-05-07**: TikTok files lawsuit challenging the law
- **2024-12-06**: DC Circuit Court upholds the law
- **2024-12-18**: Supreme Court agrees to hear expedited appeal
- **2025-01-10**: Oral arguments at Supreme Court
- **2025-01-17**: Supreme Court unanimously upholds the law
- **2025-01-18 to 01-19**: TikTok goes dark in the US, removed from app stores
- **2025-01-20**: Trump issues executive order delaying enforcement for 75 days; service restored
