## Summary

Prime Minister of Israel (1996-1999, 2009-2021, 2022-). The longest-serving Israeli PM, Netanyahu has shaped Israel's security posture for over a decade. During the 2023-2025 period, he led Israel through the October 7 Hamas attack, the subsequent 15-month Gaza war, escalation with Hezbollah, and the June 2025 Twelve-Day War with Iran. His decision to authorize Operation Rising Lion (June 13, 2025) — ordering the largest Israeli military operation against Iran — was the culmination of a long preemptive strike doctrine.

## Decision-Making Patterns

### Preemptive Strike Doctrine (Begin Doctrine Connection)
Netanyahu is a strong adherent of the Begin Doctrine — Israel's policy of preemptive strikes to prevent adversary states from acquiring weapons of mass destruction. He explicitly invoked this doctrine regarding Iran's nuclear program throughout his 2010s tenure, notably in his 2012 UN General Assembly speech where he drew a "red line" on Iran's enrichment capacity. The June 2025 strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities were the doctrine's most extensive application.

**Forecasting implication**: When Netanyahu is in power and Iran approaches nuclear latency, P(Israeli preemptive strike) is structurally elevated. The threshold for action is lower under Netanyahu than under a more cautious Israeli PM.

### Risk Calculus in the Shadow War Phase (Oct 2023 - Mar 2024)
During this period, Netanyahu faced multiple constraints that made a direct strike on Iran improbable:
- **Military bandwidth**: IDF fully committed to Gaza ground operations
- **Domestic legitimacy crisis**: Post-Oct 7 failure was an existential political threat; a strike on Iran risked expanding the war beyond Israel's capacity
- **US containment pressure**: Biden administration actively worked to prevent Israel-Iran escalation
- **No trigger event**: No major IRGC commander killed on Netanyahu's direct order in this period

Netanyahu's revealed preference during this phase: escalate through proxies (Hezbollah commanders, Iranian targets in Syria), not directly. This suggests a threshold-based decision rule — he will not strike Iran directly unless a specific trigger event occurs (senior IRGC killed, nuclear threshold crossed, or US green light).

### Escalation Thresholds (Retrospective Analysis)
Once the April 1, 2024 Damascus consulate strike occurred (authorized by Netanyahu), the escalation was rapid:
- April 13: Iran retaliates
- April 19: Israel strikes Isfahan (first direct Israeli strike on Iranian soil)
- July 2024: Haniyeh assassination in Tehran
- October 2024: Ballistic missile exchanges
- June 2025: Full-scale war

This pattern reveals that Netanyahu's threshold for direct action against Iran is crossed when: (1) Iran retaliates for an Israeli strike, making further Israeli action politically defensible as "response," or (2) Iran approaches a nuclear capability milestone.
