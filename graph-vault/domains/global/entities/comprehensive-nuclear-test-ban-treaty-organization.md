## Summary

The Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty Organization (CTBTO) is the international organization established to implement the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty (CTBT), which opened for signature on September 24, 1996. The CTBTO is headquartered in Vienna, Austria.

The CTBTO's primary operational component is the **International Monitoring System (IMS)** — a global network of 337 planned facilities (300+ operational as of 2023) using four verification technologies:
- **Seismic**: 50 primary and 120 auxiliary stations detect underground explosions
- **Hydroacoustic**: 11 stations in oceans detect underwater explosions
- **Infrasound**: 60 stations detect atmospheric explosions
- **Radionuclide**: 80 stations detect radioactive particles and noble gases from nuclear explosions

The IMS can detect any nuclear explosion over 1 kiloton anywhere on Earth. The detection and attribution capability makes clandestine nuclear testing by any state **effectively impossible** without detection.

## Significance for Forecasting

The CTBTO's IMS capability directly determines the feasibility of a state conducting an undetected nuclear test. Because the IMS makes clandestine testing impossible:
- Any nuclear test by a P5 state would require **advance political decision** to withdraw from or violate the CTBT
- Any nuclear test by a non-signatory (North Korea, India, Pakistan, Israel) IS detectable and IS publicly attributed
- The **absence of any IMS-detected nuclear explosion** in a given time window is strong negative evidence supporting a NO forecast for "will a nuclear weapon detonate?"

This entity is the verification backbone for the [[concepts/nuclear-use-base-rates]] concept.

## Timeline

- **1996-09-24**: CTBT opens for signature
- **1996-11-19**: CTBTO Preparatory Commission established
- **2000**: IMS design finalized; construction begins
- **2006**: North Korea conducts first nuclear test — detected immediately by IMS
- **2009, 2013, 2016 (x2), 2017**: All North Korean tests detected and attributed by IMS
- **As of 2023**: 300+ of 337 IMS stations operational
- **Not yet in force**: Requires ratification by 8 Annex 2 states (China, Egypt, India, Iran, Israel, North Korea, Pakistan, US) — several have not ratified
