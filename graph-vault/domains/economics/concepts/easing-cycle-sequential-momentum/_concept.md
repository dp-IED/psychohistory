# Easing Cycle Sequential Momentum

## Definition

Once an advanced-economy central bank has confirmed the first cut of an easing cycle, the **default expectation** for the next 2-3 meetings is continued cuts at the same pace (typically 25bp), absent a material data surprise or explicit change in forward guidance. This is the "sequential momentum" property of easing cycles: cuts cluster in sequences, not isolated events.

## Why This Happens

1. **Institutional inertia**: Central banks that are structurally cautious about initiating a new phase will not reverse direction immediately. A first cut represents the culmination of months of internal debate and data accumulation. The Governing Council/FOMC must be sufficiently convinced inflation is under control to approve the first cut — that conviction does not evaporate in 6 weeks.

2. **Forward guidance lock-in**: The first cut is always telegraphed 1-2 meetings in advance. Once delivered, the forward guidance for the next meeting reflects the same disinflation conviction — unless data materially worsens (inflation re-accelerates) or guidance explicitly shifts.

3. **Data continuity**: The data between two meetings that are 6-8 weeks apart is rarely transformative enough to reverse a newly established policy direction. The same conditions that justified the first cut (declining inflation, weakening growth) are still present at the next meeting — they may even have strengthened.

4. **Market expectations lock-in**: Markets price sequential cuts once a cycle begins. Deviating from the priced path (holding when a cut was expected) would tighten financial conditions and signal panic or confusion — which central banks avoid unless data forces them.

5. **Political economy of graduated moves**: Central banks prefer to signal that policy is on a predictable path. A single isolated cut followed by a hold creates uncertainty about the policy trajectory. Consecutive cuts at a steady pace communicate confidence in the disinflation narrative.

## Structural Boundaries

The sequential momentum property holds for **2-3 meetings** after the first cut (not indefinitely). By the 3rd-4th cut, one of three scenarios typically intervenes:

| Scenario | What Happens | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Reaching neutral** | The policy rate approaches the estimated neutral rate. The central bank slows or pauses to assess. | ECB after 4th cut (Dec 2024) — approach to neutral triggered a hold in Jan 2025 |
| **Data re-acceleration** | Inflation or growth data surprises to the upside, justifying a pause. | Fed H1 2025 — tariff-induced inflation concerns forced a 7-meeting mid-cycle pause |
| **External shock** | Geopolitical, financial, or fiscal event forces reassessment of the policy path. | N/A for this cycle |

## Calibration (Heuristic)

For forward guidance telegraphing a cut:
- **If the first cut has been delivered AND guidance is unchanged**: P(cut at next meeting) = 0.75-0.85
- **If the first cut was delivered AND the next meeting is 6-8 weeks later AND no major data has surprised**: P(cut at next meeting) = 0.70-0.80
- **If 2+ cuts have been delivered AND guidance remains dovish**: P(cut at next meeting) = 0.80-0.90
- **If the first cut was larger than 25bp (50bp+)**: P(subsequent 25bp cut) = 0.85-0.95 within 2 meetings
- **If the central bank explicitly guides for a "data-dependent" pause after the first cut**: P(hold at next meeting) = 0.60-0.70

## Application: ECB October 2024 Cut

At the time of the ECB October 2024 meeting (cutoff before Oct 17):

1. The ECB had delivered two cuts (June 6: 25bp to 3.75%; Sep 12: 25bp to 3.50%) — the easing cycle was confirmed and established
2. Inflation was at ~1.7% (below 2% target)
3. Growth was deteriorating (German recession, weak PMIs)
4. Lagarde's press conference on Sep 12 emphasized that disinflation was "well on track" and growth risks were "tilted to the downside"
5. No hawkish pushback had emerged; the Governing Council was unified in the cutting direction

All five conditions satisfied the sequential momentum framework: P(Oct cut) = ~0.80, making YES the clear baseline.

## Cross-Central-Bank Applicability

This concept applies to any advanced-economy central bank with:
- A structured forward guidance apparatus (Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ, RBA, RBNZ, BoC)
- A well-telegraphed first cut
- A 4-8 week meeting cadence
- An inflation-targeting mandate

It does NOT apply to:
- EM central banks under political constraints (see [[domains/mena/concepts/em-central-bank-credibility-normalization]] for those)
- Central banks in crisis management mode (emergency cuts between meetings — different regime entirely)
- Central banks operating under fixed exchange rate regimes (e.g., HKMA)
