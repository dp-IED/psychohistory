# Monetary Policy Cycle Phases

## Definition

Central bank monetary policy follows a structural cycle of identifiable phases: **tightening** (hiking), **plateau** (holding at peak), **easing** (cutting), and **extended hold during easing** (pause). Each phase has a **default next move** — the action most likely absent a major data surprise. The phase is the structural baseline; forward guidance refines the probability for specific meetings.

This concept exists because forward guidance analysis alone is insufficient: even when the Fed's language is ambiguous (e.g., "data-dependent" without directional bias), the structural phase constrains what is plausible. A late-plateau Fed cannot hike without re-establishing an entirely new tightening cycle — and it has never done so in the modern era.

## Phase Categories

### Phase 1: Active Tightening Cycle
- **Description**: The Fed is raising rates sequentially to combat above-target inflation.
- **Default next move**: Another hike (typically 25bp in the mature phase, 50-75bp in the early phase).
- **Signal**: FOMC statement uses "further" or "additional" to describe policy firming.
- **Hike termination conditions**: Inflation data showing sustained decline, or labor market deterioration.
- **Can the Fed pause or cut?**: Yes, it can pause (data-dependent), but cutting during a tightening cycle is extremely rare — signals an emergency abort.
- **Canonical example**: March 2022 – July 2023 (525bp of cumulative hikes).

### Phase 2: Early Plateau (0-6 months at peak)
- **Description**: The Fed has stopped hiking but is uncertain whether the tightening cycle is complete. Inflation remains above target; labor market is tight.
- **Default next move**: Hold. The Fed is waiting for data to confirm the cycle is done.
- **Signal**: FOMC statement uses "patient" or "data-dependent" without directional bias; dot plot shows rates at or near peak with potential for an additional hike.
- **Hike possible?**: Yes, if inflation re-accelerates or proves sticky above target. The probability is low (~10-20%) but non-zero.
- **Cut possible?**: Unlikely (5-10%) in the first 3 months. Possible after 3-6 months if data softens sharply.
- **Canonical example**: August 2023 – January 2024. The Fed held at 5.25-5.50% while markets debated whether the July 2023 hike was the last.

### Phase 3: Late Plateau (6+ months at peak, or plateau + declining inflation signal)
- **Description**: The economy has absorbed peak rates, inflation is clearly declining, and the labor market is cooling. The question has shifted from "are we done hiking?" to "when do we cut?"
- **Default next move**: Hold, with growing probability of a cut at the next live meeting.
- **Signal**: FOMC statement shifts language from "inflation remains elevated" toward "inflation has moderated" or "making progress"; dot plot shows cuts in the projection. Powell's press conference begins to acknowledge the conditions necessary for a cut.
- **Hike possible?**: Near-zero. The Fed would need to re-establish an entirely new tightening cycle with fresh forward guidance — a multi-month process. The probability of a hike at any specific meeting in a late plateau is <0.5%. The Fed has NEVER resumed hiking after 6+ months at a peak without first entering an easing cycle and then restarting (which is a different regime entirely).
- **Cut possible?**: Probable (60-70%+ at a live meeting if data cooperates). The first cut may be larger than 25bp (see the "first cut magnitude" pattern below).
- **Canonical example**: February 2024 – August 2024. The Fed held at 5.25-5.50% from February through July while inflation slowly declined and the labor market cooled. The June 2024 dot plot shifted from 3 expected cuts (March) to 1 expected cut (June). The first cut came in September 2024 — at 50bp, not 25bp.

  **This phase is the answer to Q28**: The question "Fed increases interest rates by 25+ bps after July 2024 meeting?" was structurally impossible to resolve YES because the Fed was in a late plateau with declining inflation. A hike would have required the Fed to reverse direction from a 12-month plateau without any new tightening-cycle signals — something that has never happened in the Fed's modern history.

### Phase 4: Active Easing Cycle
- **Description**: The Fed is cutting rates sequentially to normalize toward neutral or to support a softening economy.
- **Default next move**: Another cut (typically 25bp once the cycle is established).
- **Signal**: FOMC statement uses language about "supporting the economy" or "normalizing policy"; dot plot shows continued cuts.
- **Cut magnitude**: Typically 25bp, but the FIRST cut of the cycle tends to be larger (50bp in September 2024; 75bp in June 2022 for the hiking cycle equivalent). Subsequent cuts standardize to 25bp.
- **Can the Fed pause or hike?**: Yes, it can pause (uncertainty-driven), but hiking during an easing cycle is extremely rare — would require a major inflation re-acceleration.
- **Canonical example**: September 2024 – April 2026. The Fed cut from 5.50% to 3.25-3.50% over 6 meetings (with a 7-meeting H1 2025 pause driven by tariff uncertainty).

### Phase 5: Extended Hold During Easing (6+ months pause mid-cycle)
- **Description**: The Fed has paused mid-easing cycle due to an exogenous shock (tariffs, fiscal policy uncertainty, financial instability). The easing cycle is not complete — the Fed plans to resume cutting — but near-term uncertainty prevents action.
- **Default next move**: Hold. The Fed is "data-dependent" and waiting for clarity.
- **Signal**: Forward guidance is removed entirely from FOMC statements; Powell emphasizes "unusual uncertainty" and the need to assess policy developments.
- **Hike possible?**: Very low (<5%) — would require inflation to re-accelerate above target simultaneously with the uncertainty resolution.
- **Cut possible?**: Deferred — will resume when uncertainty clears, not before.
- **Canonical example**: January – June 2025. The Fed held for 7 consecutive meetings (January through June) after the Liberation Day tariffs created simultaneous inflation risk and growth risk. This was the longest pause within an easing cycle in modern Fed history.

## Key Principles

### 1. The plateau phase is irreversible in direction

Once the Fed has been at a peak rate for 6+ months with declining inflation (Phase 3), a hike is structurally impossible without:
- A new tightening cycle initiated by the FOMC (requiring 2-3 meetings of new forward guidance)
- Fresh data showing inflation decisively re-accelerating above the previous peak
- A complete reversal of the Fed's own dot plot projections

No single FOMC meeting can produce a hike out of a late plateau. The forward guidance apparatus would need to be rebuilt from scratch. This means that for any question asking "Will the Fed hike at [meeting]" where the Fed is in a late plateau, the answer is effectively NO for the next 2-3 meetings regardless of intermediate data.

### 2. The first move of a new phase is systematically larger

Both the first hike of the tightening cycle (June 2022: 75bp vs 50bp guided) and the first cut of the easing cycle (September 2024: 50bp vs 25bp guided) were larger than forward guidance indicated. This is a structural feature, not an accident: the Fed uses the first move of a new phase to recalibrate the rate level in one decisive step rather than making two smaller moves. This means:

- For a question about the first move after a plateau: if the specified magnitude is 25bp, the probability that the specified magnitude matches actual is lower than the probability that a move occurs at all.
- For a question about subsequent moves in an established cycle: 25bp is the norm, and magnitude-specific questions are more reliable.

See [[central-bank-forward-guidance]] Step 8 for the full magnitude-specific calibration framework.

### 3. The "negative space" of forward guidance

Phase transitions are often telegraphed through what the Fed REMOVES from its statement rather than what it adds. The progression from "further" → "additional" → "data-dependent" → "patient" → removing directional language → adding easing conditions is itself a chain of signals. The removal of "further hikes" from the FOMC statement signals that the peak is near. The removal of directional language entirely (as in H1 2025) signals a hold. This pattern is codified in [[central-bank-forward-guidance]].

## Relationship to Forward Guidance

The monetary-policy-cycle-phases concept provides the **structural baseline** — the default next move given the cycle position. The [[central-bank-forward-guidance]] concept provides the **meeting-level refinement** — the specific signals that confirm or override the baseline.

- **Phase alone cannot predict precise meeting timing**: Even in a late plateau with a probable first cut, the exact meeting depends on data releases and forward guidance.
- **Forward guidance alone cannot override phase constraints**: Even if the Fed's language were suddenly hawkish (which it would not be in a late plateau), a late-plateau Fed cannot hike without initiating a new tightening cycle.
- **When phase and guidance agree → high confidence forecast**: In Q28 (late plateau + no forward guidance for hikes), the probability of a hike was near-zero — both frameworks pointed the same direction.
- **When phase and guidance disagree → the phase dominates timing, but guidance dominates direction**: If the Fed were in a late plateau but somehow signaled a hike (hypothetical contradiction), the phase constraint makes the hike unlikely at the next meeting — but the guidance signals a forthcoming cycle change.

## ECB-Specific Calibrations

The monetary-policy-cycle-phases framework applies to the ECB as well as the Fed, but with structural calibrations:

| Feature | Fed | ECB |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Tightening cycle length | 16 months (Mar 2022 - Jul 2023) | 14 months (Jul 2022 - Sep 2023) |
| Total tightening | 525bp | 450bp |
| Plateau length | 13 months (Aug 2023 - Aug 2024) | 9 months (Sep 2023 - May 2024) |
| First cut timing | Sep 2024 (7 months after peak) | Jun 2024 (9 months after peak) |
| First cut magnitude | 50bp | 25bp |
| Meeting cadence | 8/year (fixed schedule) | ~8/year (every 6 weeks) |
| Forward guidance style | Explicit (dot plot + press conference) | Implicit (consensus language) |

Key ECB divergence from the framework's canonical examples:
- **First cut magnitude is NOT systematically larger**: The ECB's first cut was 25bp, same as subsequent cuts. The "first cut is larger" pattern (observed for the Fed) does not generalize to the ECB. This is likely because the ECB is structurally more cautious (20-nation consensus) and because eurozone growth was already weaker, so a 50bp cut would have signaled alarm.
- **Easing cycle cadence is less consistent**: The ECB held in July 2024 (between first and second cuts) but then cut consecutively from Sep-Dec 2024. The Fed cut twice consecutively (Sep, Nov, Dec 2024) and then faced a longer pause. Both show variable cadence, but the ECB's was driven by Governing Council deliberation rather than exogenous shocks.
