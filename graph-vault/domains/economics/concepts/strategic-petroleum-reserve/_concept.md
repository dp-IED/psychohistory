# Strategic Petroleum Reserve as Price Stabilizer

## Definition

The use of government-held emergency oil stockpiles (strategic petroleum reserves, or SPR) to suppress or stabilize crude oil prices during supply disruptions. SPR releases function as a temporary supply augmentation — injecting stored oil into the market to compensate for disrupted production or imports. The mechanism works through two channels: (1) the actual physical delivery of oil onto the market, and (2) the signaling effect that governments are willing to intervene, which reduces the fear premium. The second channel is often more powerful in the near term.

## Key Mechanisms

### US Strategic Petroleum Reserve
- Largest single-country strategic reserve (~600M barrels at peak capacity)
- Located in four underground salt caverns on the Louisiana and Texas Gulf Coasts
- Maximum drawdown rate: ~4.4M barrels per day (in practice, ~1M bpd is sustained over months)
- Established by the Energy Policy and Conservation Act of 1975 after the 1973 oil embargo
- **2022 precedent**: Biden authorized the largest release in history — 1M bpd for 6 months (180M barrels total), announced March 31, 2022. Combined with IEA coordinated releases, total government stock releases in 2022 exceeded 365M barrels.

### IEA Coordinated Release Mechanism
- Required: all 31 IEA members hold stocks equivalent to at least 90 days of net oil imports
- Trigger: a supply disruption threatening the members' economies
- Historical activations: 1991 (Gulf War), 2005 (Hurricane Katrina), 2011 (Libya), 2022 (Russia-Ukraine)
- 2022 activation: largest in history (60M + 120M barrels in two tranches)

## Forecasting Significance

For questions about oil prices during supply disruptions:

1. **SPR releases are telegraphed**: Governments preview SPR decisions through media leaks, official statements, and diplomatic signals. Markets price the anticipated release before the formal announcement. The suppression effect begins during the anticipation phase.

2. **Scale matters**: The 2022 release of ~365M barrels (US + IEA combined) was unprecedented and suppressed prices by an estimated $10-20/barrel relative to the no-intervention counterfactual.

3. **Diminishing returns**: The first SPR release is the most effective. Subsequent releases from a depleted reserve have less signaling impact. By late 2022, the US SPR had been drawn to ~375M barrels — the lowest since 1984 — reducing its credibility as a future stabilizer.

4. **Replenishment creates a price floor**: The commitment to replenish the SPR creates a price floor — the government becomes a buyer in the market during low-price periods. This floor was demonstrated when DOE announced buyback programs in 2023-2024.

5. **Institutional credibility**: The market's belief in the SPR's remaining capacity determines the effectiveness of subsequent releases. A reserve drawn below 50% of capacity loses signaling power for future crises.

## Historical Effectiveness

| Crisis | Release | Price Suppression |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| 1991 Gulf War | IEA 33M barrels | ~$3-5/barrel, 2-3 weeks |
| 2005 Hurricane Katrina | IEA 60M barrels | ~$5-8/barrel, 2-4 weeks |
| 2011 Libya | IEA 60M barrels | ~$2-4/barrel, brief |
| 2022 Russia-Ukraine | US 180M + IEA 60M + IEA 120M | ~$10-20/barrel, sustained months |
