# Christine Lagarde

## Summary

President of the European Central Bank (2019–present), succeeding Mario Draghi. A French lawyer and politician by background, Lagarde served as French Minister of Economy and Finance (2007–2011) and as Managing Director of the International Monetary Fund (2011–2019). She is the first female ECB President.

Lagarde's tenure at the ECB has been defined by three phases: managing monetary policy through the COVID-19 pandemic (2020–2021, maintaining ultra-loose policy through PEPP and TLTROs), a delayed response to the post-pandemic inflation surge (late 2021–mid 2022), and the most aggressive rate hiking cycle in ECB history (July 2022–September 2023, totalling 450bp of tightening). Her communication style differs notably from Draghi's — less inclined toward dramatic ad-hoc interventions ("whatever it takes"), more focused on data-dependent forward guidance and Governing Council consensus-building.

## Significance

Lagarde's most consequential decision was the ECB's July 2022 rate hike — the first in 11 years — and the subsequent series of 75bp and 50bp hikes that brought the deposit facility rate from −0.50% to 4.00%. The key analytical question about her tenure is whether the ECB's late start on hiking (the lag between when inflation first exceeded target in mid-2021 and when the ECB actually began raising rates in July 2022) reflected a genuine analytical error about the persistence of inflation, a structural constraint from the Governing Council's institutional caution, or Lagarde's own legal/political background (not a trained economist like Draghi or Trichet).

## Timeline

- **2007-06-19**: Becomes French Minister of Economy and Finance
- **2011-07-05**: Becomes IMF Managing Director
- **2019-11-01**: Becomes ECB President
- **2020-03-18**: Launches PEPP in response to COVID-19
- **2021-07-08**: Unveils new 2% symmetric inflation target
- **2021-09-09**: Announces modest reduction in PEPP pace
- **2021-12-16**: States "very unlikely" that ECB will raise rates in 2022
- **2022-03-10**: Concludes PEPP net purchases
- **2022-07-21**: Leads first ECB rate hike in 11 years (+50bp)
- **2022-09-08**: +75bp hike — largest in ECB history
|- **2023-09-14**: Final hike to 4.00% DFR; hiking cycle peaks
|- **2024-06-06**: First cut of easing cycle (−25bp to 3.75% DFR)
|- **2024-09-12**: Second cut (−25bp to 3.50% DFR) — confirms easing cycle momentum
|- **2024-10-17**: Third cut (−25bp to 3.25% DFR) — consecutive cut as growth deteriorates and inflation falls below target
|- **2024-12-12**: Fourth cut (−25bp to 3.00% DFR)
