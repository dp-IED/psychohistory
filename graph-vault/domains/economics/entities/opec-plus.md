# OPEC+ (Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries Plus)

## Summary

OPEC+ is an expanded alliance of oil-producing nations formed in December 2016, combining the traditional 13 OPEC members (led by Saudi Arabia) with 10 non-OPEC producers (led by Russia). The alliance controls approximately 40-45% of global oil production and holds the majority of global spare production capacity (estimated at 3-5 million barrels per day, predominantly in Saudi Arabia and the UAE). OPEC+'s production decisions are the single most important variable in global oil supply, as the alliance explicitly manages production levels to influence prices — a unique institutional arrangement in global commodities.

## Significance for Forecasting

- **Supply-side price control**: OPEC+ production cuts directly raise prices by removing supply from the market. The October 2022 cut of 2M bpd added ~$10-15/barrel to Brent prices. The alliance controls the marginal barrel that determines global price levels.
- **Spare capacity as strategic variable**: The existence of spare capacity means that during supply shocks, OPEC+ can choose to increase production to stabilize prices (as it did partially in 2022) or maintain discipline to benefit from higher prices. Saudi Arabia's willingness to use spare capacity is a key political variable.
- **Russia-Saudi coordination axis**: The Russia-Saudi relationship within OPEC+ determines the alliance's cohesion. In 2020, a Russia-Saudi price war caused Brent to briefly trade below $20. In 2022-2023, the alliance maintained coordinated cuts despite the Ukraine war.
- **Decision lag**: OPEC+ meetings occur every 1-2 months. Production changes take 1-2 months to fully impact physical markets. Forecasts must account for this implementation lag.
- **Compliance monitoring**: Not all members adhere to production quotas. Iraq, Nigeria, and others frequently overproduce. Compliance is typically 80-100% during disciplined periods, 50-80% during less cohesive periods.
