# Jerome Powell

## Summary

Chair of the Federal Reserve Board of Governors, first appointed to the Board in 2012 by President Barack Obama, elevated to Chair by President Donald Trump in 2018, and reappointed Chair by President Joe Biden (confirmed by the Senate May 22, 2022). A lawyer and investment banker by background, Powell served as an undersecretary in the Treasury Department under President George H.W. Bush and worked at the Carlyle Group and Dillon Read.

Powell presided over the most aggressive Federal Reserve rate hiking cycle since the early 1980s (2022–2023: 525bp of hikes from 0-0.25% to 5.25-5.50%), then the easing cycle beginning September 2024. By early 2026 the Fed had completed three cuts to 3.25-3.50% and was paused, with inflation slowly converging toward 2%.

## Significance

Powell's communications at FOMC press conferences are the single most important policy signal in global financial markets. His commitment to the 2% inflation target defines the macroeconomic environment for all asset classes. The post-2024 easing cycle and data-dependent pause position Powell as the steward of the "soft landing" narrative — unemployment near 4%, GDP ~2%, inflation converging. The key forecasting question for 2026-27 is whether Trump administration tariff policy will rekindle inflation and force a policy reversal, or whether the Fed can remain on hold.

Under the second Trump administration (2025-), Powell's independence is under structural pressure. Trump publicly clashed with Powell during his first term. While Powell's Fed Chair term runs through May 2026 (Governor term through 2028), the political environment creates unusual uncertainty around the Fed's institutional autonomy, particularly on trade-policy inflation pass-through.

## Timeline

- **2012-05-25:** Took office as Fed Governor
- **2018-02-05:** Began first term as Chair (nominated by Trump)
- **2020-03:** Emergency rate cuts to zero during COVID
- **2022-05-22:** Reconfirmed for second term as Chair (nominated by Biden)
- **2022-07-27 – 2023-07-26:** Hiking cycle: 525bp total, peak at 5.25-5.50%
- **2024-09-18:** First rate cut (50bp) — larger than standard 25bp, "recalibration" framing
- **2024-11-07:** Second cut (25bp)
- **2024-12-18:** Third cut (25bp) — SEP shows median expectation of 2 more cuts in 2025
- **2025:** Paused through most of year as inflation progress stalls; "data-dependent" posture
- **2026-01-28:** Fourth cut (25bp) to 3.25-3.50% (final cut in easing cycle as of May 2026)
- **2026-05:** Paused at 3.25-3.50%; tariff uncertainty creating conflicting inflation signals
