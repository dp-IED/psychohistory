# WTI Crude Oil

## Summary

West Texas Intermediate (WTI) is the benchmark crude oil futures contract traded on the NYMEX (CME Group), representing light, sweet crude oil delivered to Cushing, Oklahoma. It is one of the two major global oil benchmarks, alongside Brent Crude. WTI is primarily used as a pricing reference for US-produced crude oil and is sensitive to US supply-demand dynamics, refinery capacity, pipeline infrastructure, and energy policy.

## Significance for Forecasting

- **Geopolitical risk premium**: WTI prices are highly sensitive to Middle East tensions, Iran conflict, Russia-Ukraine war, and OPEC+ supply decisions. The June 2025 Twelve-Day War (Iran-Israel with US involvement) caused a significant spike.
- **US energy policy**: Trump administration energy policy (deregulation, "drill baby drill" rhetoric, pipeline approvals, strategic petroleum reserve policy) directly impacts WTI supply expectations.
- **Macroeconomic driver**: Oil prices are both a leading indicator of inflation and a lagging indicator of global economic activity. $100+ oil is typically contractionary for the global economy.
- **Seasonal patterns**: May is typically a month of increasing gasoline demand (US driving season starts Memorial Day), which supports crude oil prices.
- **Price levels**: $100+ WTI is a psychological threshold that historically triggers demand destruction and political pressure for SPR releases. $115 would be a ~15% move from the $100 level.
