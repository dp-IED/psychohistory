# Michelle Bowman

## Summary

Member of the Federal Reserve Board of Governors since November 2018, appointed by President Donald Trump to fill an unexpired term and reappointed by President Joe Biden in January 2020 to a full 14-year term (expiring 2034). Bowman previously served as the Kansas State Bank Commissioner (2017-2018) and as a senior official at the U.S. Department of Homeland Security. She is the first woman with state banking regulatory experience to serve on the Fed Board.

## Significance for Forecasting

Bowman is the most important dissenter on the current FOMC. Her dissenting vote at the September 18, 2024 FOMC meeting — favoring a 25bp cut over the actual 50bp cut — was **the first governor dissent since 2005**. (Governor dissents are rarer than regional bank president dissents because governors are Board members appointed by the President with 14-year terms, giving them greater independence to dissent without career risk.)

Bowman represents a structurally hawkish faction on the Board. Her voting pattern signals:
- **Caution on inflation**: She consistently preferred slower easing than the majority, suggesting she saw inflation risks as higher than her colleagues
- **First-move magnitude skepticism**: She voted for 25bp when the majority chose 50bp — the "first cut is larger" pattern has internal opposition
- **Independent streak**: Her willingness to be the sole dissenter against the Chair in a public meeting demonstrates institutional independence

For any future question about FOMC split votes, dissent patterns, or hawkish internal opposition to the easing cycle, Bowman is the key actor to track. Her reappointment in 2026+ under a second Trump administration may be relevant to Fed independence dynamics.
