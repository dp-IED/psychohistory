# Federal Reserve System

## Summary

The central banking system of the United States, established by the Federal Reserve Act of 1913 in response to a series of financial panics, particularly the Panic of 1907. The Fed operates as a quasi-independent government entity. Its dual mandate from Congress is maximum employment and stable prices (2% inflation target, formalized in 2012). The Fed's policy decisions — particularly changes to the federal funds rate — are the single most consequential force in global financial markets.

The Fed conducts monetary policy through the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC), which meets 8 times per year. The Chair of the Board of Governors is the most visible figure, testifying to Congress semi-annually and holding press conferences after each FOMC meeting.

## Significance for Forecasting

The Federal Reserve is the single most important institution for short-term macroeconomic and financial market forecasting. Every question about interest rates, inflation, recession, or credit conditions touches Fed policy. The Fed's forward guidance apparatus (statement language, dot plot, press conference, minutes) provides a structured, predictable signal of future policy moves — making rate decisions unusually forecastable compared to most geopolitical events.

The post-COVID period (2022-2026) saw the Fed's most aggressive hiking cycle in 40 years (0% → 5.25-5.50% in 16 months), followed by a plateau and then the beginning of an easing cycle in September 2024.

## Timeline

- **1913-12-23**: Federal Reserve Act signed by President Woodrow Wilson
- **1951**: Treasury-Federal Reserve Accord establishes Fed independence from Treasury
- **1977**: Dual mandate formalized by Congress
- **1979-1987**: Paul Volcker chairmanship — inflation broken through aggressive rate hikes (fed funds rate peaked at 20%)
- **1987-2006**: Alan Greenspan chairmanship — Great Moderation
- **2006-2014**: Ben Bernanke chairmanship — 2008 financial crisis, ZIRP, quantitative easing
- **2014-2018**: Janet Yellen chairmanship — first woman to serve as Chair
- **2018-2022**: Jerome Powell chairmanship — pre-COVID tightening, COVID emergency easing
- **2022-2023**: Most aggressive hiking cycle in 40 years — fed funds from ~0% to 5.25-5.50%
- **2024-H1**: Rates held at 5.25-5.50%
- **2024-09-18**: First rate cut in over four years — 50bp to 4.75-5.00%
- **2024-11-07**: Cut 25bp to 4.50-4.75%
- **2024-12-18**: Cut 25bp to 4.25-4.50%
- **H1 2025**: Multiple holds at 4.25-4.50% amid tariff uncertainty
- **2025-09-17**: Cut 25bp to 4.00-4.25% (first 2025 cut)
- **2025-10-29**: Cut 25bp to 3.75-4.00%
- **2025-12-10**: Cut 25bp to 3.50-3.75%
