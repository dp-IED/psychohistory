# Silver

## Summary

Silver is a precious metal traded globally as both an industrial commodity and a monetary asset. Unlike gold, silver has significant industrial applications (solar panels, electronics, medical devices) that create dual demand drivers: investment/hedge demand and industrial consumption.

## Significance for Forecasting

Silver prices are driven by distinct factors from both gold and base metals:

1. **Industrial demand growth**: Solar photovoltaic manufacturing consumes ~20% of annual silver supply. The green energy transition creates structural demand growth, with silver used in electrical contacts, solders, and solar cells.
2. **Monetary/hedge demand**: Silver benefits from the same safe-haven flows as gold during monetary uncertainty, inflation hedging, and geopolitical risk, but with higher volatility due to smaller market size.
3. **Supply constraints**: Silver mine production has been flat-to-declining since 2016. Primary silver mines are rare; most silver is produced as a byproduct of copper, lead, and zinc mining, making supply unresponsive to silver prices alone.
4. **Gold-silver ratio**: The ratio (currently ~80-90:1) is historically elevated above the long-term mean (~60:1), suggesting potential mean reversion that would benefit silver.
5. **Macro sensitivity**: Silver is highly sensitive to real interest rates, USD strength, and industrial cycle. A Fed cutting cycle is generally bullish for silver.
