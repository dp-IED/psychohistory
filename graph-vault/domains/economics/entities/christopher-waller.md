# Christopher Waller

## Summary

Member of the Federal Reserve Board of Governors since December 2020, appointed by President Donald Trump. Before joining the Board, Waller served as Executive Vice President and Director of Research at the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. He holds a PhD in economics from Washington State University.

## Significance for Forecasting

Waller is one of the most consequential FOMC members for forecasting because his public speeches frequently move market expectations. He was initially considered hawkish (given his St. Louis Fed background) but has shown pragmatic flexibility:

- **2023-2024**: Waller was among the first FOMC members to publicly acknowledge that progress on inflation was sufficient to consider rate cuts. His November 2023 speech explicitly stating that the policy rate was "well into restrictive territory" was a pivotal dovish signal that shifted market pricing toward rate cuts.
- **March 2025**: Dissented on the balance sheet reduction pace (voting for slowing the runoff), not on the rate decision itself — indicating nuanced internal positions distinct from the hawk/dove binary.
- **2025 tariff period**: Remained with the majority in supporting holds despite internal divisions.

Waller's voting record through the 2022-2026 cycle makes him a reliable indicator of the FOMC center of gravity — he tends to vote with the majority but is willing to dissent on operational details (balance sheet) rather than rate direction. His speeches are closely watched by markets for shifts in the median FOMC member's thinking.

## Timeline

- **2020-12-18**: Takes office as Fed Governor
- **2023-11**: Landmark dovish speech — states Fed's policy rate is "well into restrictive territory" and inflation is falling, opening the door to rate cuts in 2024
- **2024**: Votes with majority on all holds and the September/November/December cuts
- **2024-12-18**: Votes with majority on 25bp cut (no dissent)
- **2025-03-19**: **Dissents** — votes for slower balance sheet runoff pace, not on rate (11-1 vote)
- **2025-H1**: Votes with majority on all holds amid tariff uncertainty
- **2025-H2**: Votes with majority on resumed cuts
