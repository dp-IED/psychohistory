# ECB Monetary Policy Cycle (2022-current)

## Purpose

Track the European Central Bank's monetary policy through the post-COVID cycle — from the hiking phase (2022-2023) through the plateau (late 2023-H1 2024) and the easing cycle (H2 2024-ongoing). This thread is the eurozone parallel to [[domains/economics/threads/us-monetary-policy-cycle-2022-2026]] and covers the same structural dynamics: the hiking cycle, the plateau, the first cut and the subsequent cutting cycle.

## Why a Separate Thread (Not Merged with US)

The ECB's monetary policy cycle differs structurally from the Fed's in ways that matter for forecasting:

1. **Slower start, later peak**: The ECB began hiking in July 2022 (vs. Fed's March 2022) and peaked at 4.00% DFR (vs. Fed's 5.50%) — both later and lower than the Fed.

2. **Different meeting cadence**: The ECB Governing Council meets approximately every 6 weeks (8 meetings/year), not on the Fed's fixed 8-meeting calendar. Non-meeting decisions are possible (emergency PEPP/TPI) but rate changes only occur at scheduled meetings.

3. **Single mandate vs dual**: The ECB's primary mandate is price stability only — growth is a secondary consideration. This makes the ECB structurally more hawkish in theory, though in practice the weaker eurozone growth picture has limited rate increases.

4. **Governing Council fragmentation**: 20 national central bank governors with divergent national inflation experiences produce a persistent hawk-dove split that shapes both the pace and the forward guidance. This makes ECB forward guidance less precise than the Fed's — the consensus-building process produces compromise language rather than clear directional signals.

5. **Energy dependence**: Eurozone inflation is structurally more energy-sensitive than US inflation (higher energy weight in HICP basket, greater manufacturing base exposure to gas prices). This makes ECB policy more reactive to TTF gas prices and geopolitical energy shocks.

6. **No fiscal counterparty**: The ECB operates without a eurozone-wide fiscal authority, making it more cautious about aggressive moves that could fragment sovereign bond markets. The Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI) exists to manage this risk, but its very existence constrains the pace of policy change.

## ECB Meeting Calendar

The ECB Governing Council holds monetary policy meetings approximately every 6 weeks, announced in advance for the full year. Rate decisions are announced at 14:15 CET on the meeting day, followed by Lagarde's press conference at 14:45 CET.

**2024 meetings**: Jan 25, Mar 7, Apr 11, Jun 6, Jul 18, Sep 12, Oct 17, Dec 12

## Cycle Narrative

### Phase 1: Tightening Cycle (July 2022 – September 2023)

Total tightening: 450bp (deposit rate from -0.50% to 4.00%)

| Date | Move | DFR After | Notes |
|------|------|-----------|-------|
| 2022-07-21 | +50bp | 0.00% | First hike in 11 years; larger than the 25bp some expected |
| 2022-09-08 | +75bp | 0.75% | Largest single hike in ECB history |
| 2022-10-27 | +75bp | 1.50% | Continued aggressive tightening |
| 2022-12-15 | +50bp | 2.00% | Downshift from 75bp pace |
| 2023-02-02 | +50bp | 2.50% | Coordinated with BoE |
| 2023-03-16 | +50bp | 3.00% | Amid banking turmoil (SVB/Credit Suisse) — ECB proceeded with hike |
| 2023-05-04 | +25bp | 3.25% | Downshift to 25bp pace |
| 2023-06-15 | +25bp | 3.50% | Continued |
| 2023-07-27 | +25bp | 3.75% | Hints at possible pause |
| 2023-09-14 | +25bp | 4.00% | Final hike — signal that rates are at level that will make substantial contribution to returning inflation to target |

### Phase 2: Plateau (September 2023 – May 2024)

- **Oct 26, 2023**: First hold after hiking cycle. Statement notes inflation declining but still elevated.
- **Dec 14, 2023**: Hold. ECB staff projections lower growth and inflation forecasts. Lagarde explicitly discusses rate cuts in press conference for the first time — markets price in cuts starting spring 2024.
- **Jan 25, 2024**: Hold. Lagarde says it's "premature to discuss rate cuts." Markets scale back early-cut expectations.
- **Mar 7, 2024**: Hold. ECB staff project inflation at 2.3% for 2024, 2.0% for 2025, 1.9% for 2026. Wage growth remains a concern.
- **Apr 11, 2024**: Hold. Lagarde signals a June cut is "likely" if incoming data confirms the disinflation path. This is the formal forward guidance for the first cut.

### Phase 3: Easing Cycle (June 2024 – ongoing)

| Date | Move | DFR After | Notes |
|------|------|-----------|-------|
| 2024-06-06 | -25bp | 3.75% | First cut of easing cycle. Telegraphed at April meeting. |
| 2024-07-18 | Hold | 3.75% | Hold to digest first cut, assess data |
| **2024-09-12** | **-25bp** | **3.50%** | **Second cut. Inflation declining toward target, growth weakening.** |
| **2024-10-17** | **-25bp** | **3.25%** | **Third cut. Consecutive cut confirming easing cycle momentum. Eurozone growth falling; German recession deepening.** |
| 2024-12-12 | -25bp | 3.00% | Fourth cut |

**Key causal chain**: The October 2024 cut was predictable because:
1. The ECB was in Phase 3 (Active Easing Cycle) — once a cutting cycle starts, the default expectation for the next meeting is another cut
2. Eurozone HICP inflation had fallen from 10.6% peak to ~1.7% by September 2024 — below the 2% target
3. Eurozone growth was deteriorating: German GDP contracted, manufacturing PMIs remained below 50, French political uncertainty weighed on investment
4. The September 12 cut confirmed the easing trajectory with no dissenting hawkish voices
5. ECB forward guidance at the September meeting signaled continued loosening
6. The Fed's 50bp cut on Sep 18 provided cover for synchronized global easing

## Forward Guidance Tracking

### Key Signal Events

- **2024-04-11**: Lagarde signals June cut is "likely" — first formal forward guidance for a cut
- **2024-06-06**: Lagarde says rate path is "data-dependent" but inflation outlook improved — signals more cuts conditional on data
- **2024-09-12**: Lagarde confirms disinflation process is "well on track," growth risks are "tilted to the downside" — strong signal for continued cuts
- **2024-10-17**: Cut delivered; Lagarde highlights "inflation has fallen faster than expected," growth remains weak — confirms continued easing bias

### Governing Council Configuration

President: Christine Lagarde (centrist, consensus-builder)
Chief Economist: Philip Lane (dovish, data-dependent)
Key hawks: Joachim Nagel (Bundesbank), Robert Holzmann (OeNB), Klaas Knot (DNB)
Key doves: Fabio Panetta (Banca d'Italia, until Nov 2023), Piero Cipollone (ECB Board, dovish)

The hawk-dove balance in 2024 shifted toward dovish as inflation fell below target and growth deteriorated, making consecutive cuts politically feasible within the Governing Council.
