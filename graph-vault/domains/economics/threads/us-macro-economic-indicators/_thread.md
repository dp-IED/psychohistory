# US Macroeconomic Indicators

## Overview

This thread tracks key US macroeconomic indicators across quarters:
- Real GDP growth (quarterly annualized rate, advance/second/third estimates)
- CPI and PCE inflation (headline and core)
- Federal Reserve policy rate (fed funds target range)
- Employment (nonfarm payrolls, unemployment rate)
- Trade and tariff policy impacts
- Key conjunctural variables for forecasting

The thread serves as the primary reference for any US macro-economic forecasting question.

## Quarterly Indicator Tracking

### 2024

#### Q4 2024
- **GDP**: +2.4% annualized (third estimate) — continued expansion above trend
- **CPI**: Headline ~2.7% YoY (Dec), core ~3.2% — sticky services inflation
- **Fed**: Three consecutive cuts (Sep: -50bp to 4.75-5.00%, Nov: -25bp to 4.50-4.75%, Dec: -25bp to 4.25-4.50%) — the easing cycle
- **Employment**: NFP +256K (Dec), unemployment 4.1% — labor market resilient but softening from 2023 peaks
- **Key dynamic**: The Trump victory in Nov 2024 created uncertainty about tariff policy and fiscal trajectory, but the economy entered 2025 with strong momentum from the Q3-Q4 2024 growth surge

### 2025

#### Q1 2025
- **GDP (Advance Estimate, Apr 30)**: Approximately -0.6% annualized — slight contraction, the first negative quarter since Q2 2022. The contraction was driven by a sharp import surge (businesses front-loaded imports ahead of expected tariff increases) and a moderation in consumer spending. The -0.6% was far above the -2% threshold — a 1.4 percentage point gap meaning the "-2%" threshold was never genuinely threatened.
- **CPI**: Headline ~2.8% YoY (Mar) — tariff pass-through beginning to appear in goods prices
- **Fed**: Held at 4.25-4.50% (Jan 29 and Mar 19 meetings). The pause reflected tariff uncertainty creating a stagflationary risk profile. Market expectations shifted from 6 cuts in 2025 (Sep 2024 pricing) to zero or one cut by end of Q1.
- **Employment**: NFP growth moderated to ~150-200K/month — labor market cooling but not collapsing
- **Trade**: Trump's tariff regime began in earnest (steel/aluminum 25%, Canada/Mexico tariffs, China tariff escalation). Effective US tariff rate rose from 2.5% to ~27%.
- **Key dynamic**: The economy experienced a "tariff-induced technical contraction" — the GDP decline was largely driven by import front-loading (a statistical subtraction in GDP accounting) rather than a collapse in domestic demand. This is a critical distinction for GDP threshold forecasting: tariff-driven GDP contractions are typically shallower than financial-crisis or pandemic contractions because the underlying demand remains intact.

#### Q2 2025
- **GDP (Advance Estimate, Jul 30)**: >+2.0% annualized — strong rebound from Q1's tariff-driven technical contraction
- **Key dynamic**: The rebound was driven primarily by **import normalization** — the mechanical reversal of Q1's import front-loading. Businesses had pulled forward imports into Q1 ahead of the April 2 Liberation Day tariffs; with the tariffs in place, Q2 imports normalized toward trend, adding ~1.5-2.5pp to GDP via the net exports component. This mechanical rebound dominated the quarter's GDP reading. Consumer spending and business investment held up better than consensus expected, despite the stock market crash (post-April 2) and Iran-Israel war uncertainty (June 13-24). The advance estimate confirmed that Q1's contraction was a **Type N (noise)** statistical artifact (see [[domains/economics/concepts/gdp-contraction-signal-vs-noise/_concept]]), with the subsequent mechanical rebound pushing growth above the 2% threshold that would have seemed improbable based on the negative Q1 reading alone.
- **Forecasting lesson**: Q1 GDP of -0.6% did NOT predict Q2 weakness because the contraction was not demand-driven. The signal-vs-noise framework predicted the rebound. Key insight: financial market stress (April stock crash) and geopolitical shocks (June war) have 2-4 quarter lags to real GDP — they do not immediately cancel mechanical rebounds from import normalization.
- **Headwinds that were present but insufficient**: Liberation Day tariffs (beginning April 2) created supply chain costs and uncertainty, the stock market crash (April 3-9) produced negative wealth effects, and the Iran-Israel war (June 13-24) disrupted Middle East trade routes. However, none of these was large enough to cancel the ~2pp mechanical rebound from import normalization. The tariff damage to GDP appeared more in Q3-Q4 2025 (cumulative effect) than in Q2.

#### Q3 2025
- **GDP**: ~+2.3% annualized (FRED data) — moderate growth resumed
- **Key dynamic**: Tariff regime becoming embedded; some sectors (manufacturing, agriculture) showing strain

#### Q4 2025
- **GDP**: +0.5% annualized (BEA data, per Apr 30, 2026 release) — sharp slowdown from Q3
- **Key dynamic**: Cumulative effect of tariffs, policy uncertainty, and high rates suppressing growth

### 2026

#### Q1 2026
- **GDP (Advance Estimate, Apr 30, 2026)**: +2.0% annualized — recovery driven by investment, exports, consumer spending, and government spending
- **Key dynamic**: Economy stabilized after the 2025 tariff shock digestion

## Historical Reference: GDP Contractions and the -2% Threshold

| Quarter | GDP (SAAR) | Description | Trigger |
|---------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| 2020 Q2 | -28.0% | COVID pandemic lockdowns | Global health emergency |
| 2008 Q4 | -8.5% | Global financial crisis peak | Lehman collapse, credit freeze |
| 2009 Q1 | -5.1% | GFC aftermath | Systemic financial stress |
| 2008 Q3 | -2.1% | GFC onset | Housing crash, TARP debate |
| 2020 Q1 | -4.9% | COVID onset | Initial lockdowns |
| 2022 Q1 | -1.4% | Post-surge inventory adjustment | Fed tightening begin |
| 2022 Q2 | -0.6% | Continued inventory adjustment | Energy price shock |
| 2025 Q1 | -0.6% | Tariff-driven import surge | Trump tariff escalation |

**Key insight**: -2% annualized quarterly GDP decline is an unusually severe threshold. It has been exceeded only during the GFC (2008 Q3: -2.1%, 2008 Q4: -8.5%, 2009 Q1: -5.1%) and COVID (2020 Q1: -4.9%, 2020 Q2: -28.0%). Even the "soft landing" inventory corrections of 2022 never breached -2%. Forecasting a GDP reading below -2% requires identifying a catalyst of comparable severity to the GFC or COVID — trade disputes alone have never produced this magnitude of contraction in US history.

## Key Forecasting Variables

1. **Current GDP trajectory**: The starting point — is the economy growing above or below trend entering the forecast period?
2. **Identified shock catalysts**: What could produce a -2%+ contraction? Major candidates: financial crisis, pandemic, war on US soil, severe energy supply disruption, extreme policy error. Tariff escalation alone has never breached -2%.
3. **Transmission mechanism**: Even with a catalyst, does the transmission mechanism exist? Financial contagion requires high leverage and interconnectedness; pandemic requires public health restrictions; energy shock requires supply dependence.
4. **Advance estimate vs revised**: The advance estimate is the first read (subject to revision). It can miss late-quarter activity. But even the advance estimate has never confused a shallow contraction with a -2%+ collapse.
5. **Statistical artifacts**: Some GDP movements are statistical illusions — import front-loading (Q1 2025), inventory swings (2022), or one-off factors. These should be discounted relative to genuine demand contractions.
