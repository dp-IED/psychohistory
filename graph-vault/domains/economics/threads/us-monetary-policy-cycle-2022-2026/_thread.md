# US Monetary Policy Cycle: Post-COVID Inflation and Rate Normalization

## Overview

The 2021-2026 period represents the most aggressive US monetary policy tightening cycle in 40 years, followed by a plateau and the beginning of an easing cycle. This thread tracks the Fed's response to post-COVID inflation — from the initial "transitory" framing (2021), through the emergency hiking cycle (2022-2023), the extended hold (2024-H1), the first cut (September 2024), and the ongoing normalization.

This thread is critical for any forecast about interest rates, inflation, recession probability, credit conditions, housing markets, corporate debt, or yield curve dynamics. The Fed's path — which it telegraphs through forward guidance — is the most important single variable in global macro forecasting.

## Key Events

### Phase 1: Inflation Emerges and the "Transitory" Debate (2021)

- **March 2021**: Biden signs the $1.9 trillion American Rescue Plan, adding to already-elevated household savings from prior COVID relief
- **April-June 2021**: CPI begins rising above 2%, Fed Chair Powell characterizes as "transitory"
- **H2 2021**: Supply chain disruptions, energy price spikes, and labor shortages push CPI toward 6-7% by year-end. Powell retires "transitory" language in November
- **December 2021**: Fed signals accelerated taper of QE, opening the door to rate hikes in 2022

### Phase 2: Emergency Hiking Cycle (2022-2023)

- **January 26, 2022**: FOMC held rates at 0.00-0.25% but signaled a rate hike was "warranted soon." Chair Powell stated the labor market was "very strong" and inflation was "well above 2%." The statement telegraphed the March hike and signaled balance sheet reduction later in 2022. [[2022-Q1]]
- **March 17, 2022**: First rate hike — 25bp to 0.25-0.50%
- **May 5, 2022**: 50bp hike to 0.75-1.00%. Powell states 75bp hike is "not actively considered" — a forward guidance that would prove costly to credibility within weeks.
- **June 16, 2022**: 75bp hike to 1.50-1.75% (largest since 1994). Triggered by May CPI (June 10 print) accelerating to 8.6% YoY — a surprise to the upside that forced the Fed to abandon its pre-communicated 50bp plan. The gap between guidance and actual outcome damaged Fed communications credibility.
- **July 27, 2022**: 75bp hike to 2.25-2.50%
- **September 21, 2022**: 75bp hike to 3.00-3.25%
- **November 2, 2022**: 75bp hike to 3.75-4.00%
- **December 14, 2022**: 50bp hike to 4.25-4.50% (downshift from 75bp telegraphed)
- **February 1, 2023**: 25bp hike to 4.50-4.75%
- **March 22, 2023**: 25bp hike to 4.75-5.00% (proceeded despite SVB failure)
- **May 3, 2023**: 25bp hike to 5.00-5.25%
- **July 26, 2023**: 25bp hike to 5.25-5.50% (final hike of the cycle)
- Total: 525bp in 16 months — the fastest tightening cycle since 1982-1983

### Phase 3: The Plateau (August 2023 - August 2024)

- **September 2023 - June 2024**: Fed holds at 5.25-5.50%. Dot plot shifts from "higher for longer" to signaling eventual cuts
- **H1 2024**: CPI declines gradually but remains above 3% (core PCE ~2.7%). Fed emphasizes "data dependence" and need for "greater confidence" before cutting
- **June 2024 SEP**: Dot plot shifts from 3 expected 2024 cuts (March SEP) to 1 expected 2024 cut. Markets interpret this as September being the live meeting
- **July 2024 FOMC**: Hold at 5.25-5.50%. Powell signals September cut possible if data cooperates [[2024-Q3]]

### Phase 4: First Cut and Easing Cycle Begins (September 2024 - )

- **September 18, 2024**: Fed cuts 50bp to 4.75-5.00% — larger than the 25bp widely expected. Powell characterizes as "recalibration" not panic. Governor Michelle Bowman dissents (favored 25bp), first governor dissent since 2005 [[2024-Q3]]
- **October-December 2024**: Additional cuts expected at remaining 2024 meetings (November, December)

### Phase 5: Continued Easing and Tariff-Led Pause (Q4 2024 - H1 2025)

- **November 7, 2024**: Fed cuts 25bp to 4.50-4.75%. Unanimous 12-0 vote. The post-election meeting proceeds normally — no political interference despite Trump's victory [[2024-Q4]]
- **December 18, 2024**: Fed cuts 25bp to 4.25-4.50%. Cleveland Fed President Beth M. Hammack dissents (preferred to maintain rate). The dot plot signals a slower pace of cuts in 2025 [[2024-Q4]]
- **January 29, 2025**: Fed holds at 4.25-4.50%. First post-Trump-inauguration FOMC meeting. Statement drops "progress on inflation" language. Powell emphasizes patience to assess new administration's policies [[2025-Q1]]
- **March 19, 2025**: Fed holds at 4.25-4.50% (11-1). Christopher Waller dissents on balance sheet reduction pace, not the rate decision [[2025-Q1]]
- **May 7, 2025**: Fed holds at 4.25-4.50% (12-0). First meeting after Liberation Day tariffs. Powell acknowledges "unusual uncertainty" from tariff policy. Forward guidance removed [[2025-Q2]]
- **June 18, 2025**: Fed holds at 4.25-4.50% (12-0). Tariffs partially vacated by CIT but on appeal. Fed waiting for legal and economic clarity [[2025-Q2]]

The Q4 2024 - H1 2025 period marks a dramatic shift in the monetary policy narrative. The September-December 2024 cuts (100bp total) were followed by a seven-meeting hold from January through June 2025 — the longest pause since the 2023-2024 plateau. The Trump administration's Liberation Day tariffs (April 2, 2025) introduced a stagflationary dynamic (tariffs = higher prices + lower growth) that gave the Fed no clear path to continue cutting. Market expectations swung from six cuts in 2025 (September 2024 pricing) to zero or one cut by mid-2025.

### Phase 6: Easing Resumes and Tariff-Adjusted Caution (H2 2025 - Q1 2026)

- **September 17, 2025**: Fed cuts 25bp to 4.00-4.25% — first cut since the H1 2025 pause. Tariff uncertainty remained elevated but the labor market cooling and inflation convergence toward target provided cover for resuming the easing cycle [[2025-Q3]]
- **October 29, 2025**: Fed cuts 25bp to 3.75-4.00% (10-2 vote). Miran dissented favoring 50bp; Schmid dissented favoring hold. The two-dissent vote reflected growing internal division — doves wanted faster easing, hawks wanted more inflation data [[2025-Q4]]
- **December 10, 2025**: Fed cuts 25bp to 3.50-3.75% (9-3 vote). Broadest dissent coalition of the cycle — Miran (favored 50bp), Schmid (favored hold), Goolsbee (favored hold). The dot plot released at this SEP meeting signaled 2-3 additional cuts in 2026, with the median projecting rates at 3.00-3.25% by end-2026 [[2025-Q4]]
- **January 28, 2026**: Fed cuts 25bp to 3.25-3.50% (10-1 vote). Miran dissented favoring 50bp. Chair Powell's press conference emphasized continued data-dependence and noted that tariff policy uncertainty was making the economic outlook unusually hard to parse. This cut brought the cumulative easing to 225bp from the September 2024 peak. The post-meeting statement maintained the assessment that inflation was moving toward 2% but acknowledged the labor market was cooling gradually [[2026-Q1]]

The H2 2025 - Q1 2026 period represents the resumption of the easing cycle after the tariff-induced H1 2025 pause, with 100bp of additional cuts (September 2025 through January 2026). The cumulative rate path from the cycle peak: 5.50% → 3.25-3.50% (225bp total cuts). The easing cycle faced persistent headwinds from tariff uncertainty that limited the pace and created growing internal FOMC divisions between doves favoring faster easing and hawks concerned about tariff passthrough to inflation.

### Phase 7: The Cut-Then-Hold Zigzag (Q1-Q2 2026)

- **March 18, 2026**: Fed held at 3.25-3.50% (11-0 vote). After three consecutive cuts (September 2025, October 2025, December 2025, January 2026), the Fed paused. The dot plot showed internal division: some members projected one more 2026 cut, others projected the rate would hold through year-end. Chair Powell cited sticky services inflation and tariff passthrough uncertainty as reasons for caution. [[2026-Q1]]
- **May 6, 2026**: Fed held at 3.25-3.50% (11-0). Statement noted that \"tariff-related price increases in selected goods categories\" were creating upward pressure on inflation measures, but the Committee expected these to be transitory.
- **June 17, 2026**: Fed held at 3.25-3.50% (10-1 vote). Governor Miran dissented favoring a 25bp cut, arguing the labor market needed support. The dot plot revised downward the expected 2026 cuts — from 2-3 (December 2025 SEP) to 1-2. [[2026-Q2]]

The Q1-Q2 2026 period is the canonical **cut-then-hold** pattern: the Fed cut in January (the fourth consecutive meeting with a cut), then held for three consecutive meetings (March, May, June). This pattern — a sequence of cuts followed by an extended pause — is distinct from both "hold then cut" (H1 2025) and "continuous cutting" (H2 2025). It represents the third distinct phase of the easing cycle and reflects the asymmetric response function: the Fed cuts when inflation data is favorable, but holds at the first sign of inflation stickiness, creating a "downward staircase" pattern rather than a smooth glide path.

**Forecasting implication**: The cut-then-hold pattern is the dominant rate-path signal for the remainder of 2026. Forecasters should expect the Fed to hold through mid-2026, with the next cut conditional on either (a) clear evidence that tariff passthrough is a one-time price level shift (transitory), or (b) sufficient labor market deterioration to make tariff inflation a secondary concern. The net signal is \"easing paused, not abandoned\" — the terminal rate of the cycle is likely 2.75-3.00%, but the path to get there will be slow and interrupted by holds.

## Key Dynamics

1. **Forward Guidance Dominance**: The Fed telegraphs rate moves 1-2 meetings in advance through the dot plot, Chair press conferences, and FOMC statements. Surprise moves are extremely rare. See [[central-bank-forward-guidance]].

2. **Asymmetric Mispricing Risk**: The September 2024 50bp cut shows that while direction is telegraphed, magnitude may not be. Markets systematically underestimate the size of the first cut in a new cycle.

3. **Data Dependency**: The Fed's tight coupling to CPI/PCE data creates a forecasting chain: inflation data forecasts → labor market data forecasts → Fed rate decision forecasts. Each link introduces uncertainty but follows a known lag structure (~6-12 months from data to policy response).

4. **Election-Year Constraints**: 2024 being an election year created additional caution for the Fed. The first cut came in September (post-election cycle beginning) rather than earlier in the year when political pressure was highest. The Fed avoided both seeming politically motivated and acting before the economic data justified it.

5. **Tariff-Induced Policy Paralysis**: The H1 2025 experience demonstrated a novel dynamic: trade policy uncertainty can paralyze the Fed. The Liberation Day tariffs created simultaneous inflation risk (tariff passthrough) and growth risk (trade disruption), giving the Fed no clear direction. When the policy instrument (tariffs) is controlled by the executive branch and the economic effects are uncertain, the Fed's default response is to pause until clarity emerges. This dynamic is likely to recur in any future trade-war scenario — the Fed will hold rather than act into uncertainty.

## Forecasting Significance

This thread is the primary reference for any question about:
- Fed rate decisions at specific FOMC meetings
- The path of interest rates over a given horizon
- The timing and sequence of the easing cycle
- Market reactions to Fed policy (yield curve, equity, FX)
- The interaction between Fed policy and political developments (election, fiscal policy)

To forecast a specific FOMC decision:
1. Identify the meeting date
2. Read the most recent FOMC statement and Chair press conference transcript
3. Check the most recent dot plot (quarterly SEP meetings only)
4. Check market pricing (CME FedWatch)
5. Apply the [[central-bank-forward-guidance]] concept
