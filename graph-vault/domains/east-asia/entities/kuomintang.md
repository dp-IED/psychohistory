# Kuomintang (KMT)

## Summary

The Kuomintang (KMT, Chinese Nationalist Party) is one of Taiwan's two major political parties. Founded in mainland China in 1919, it ruled the Republic of China as a one-party state until the 1980s. In contemporary Taiwan, the KMT advocates for closer cross-strait economic ties and a more conciliatory approach toward China, positioning itself to the right of the DPP on sovereignty issues while maintaining that it does not support unification under current conditions.

In the 2024 presidential election, KMT candidate Hou Yu-ih received 33.49% of the vote — second place behind Lai Ching-te (DPP, 40.05%) but well ahead of Ko Wen-je (TPP, 26.46%). The KMT's failure to coordinate with the TPP (which shares the anti-DPP electorate) was the decisive structural factor in the election: the combined KMT+TPP vote (59.95%) exceeded Lai's total by nearly 20 points.

## Significance for Forecasting

The KMT's electoral path to the presidency depends on either: (1) absorbing the TPP through a pre-election alliance, or (2) the TPP collapsing as a viable third-party alternative. The 2000 and 2024 elections both demonstrate that KMT-TPP division guarantees DPP victory in a single-round plurality system. Any forecast about a future Taiwan presidential election must assess KMT-TPP coordination prospects as the dominant structural variable.

## Timeline

- **1919**: Founded in mainland China
- **1949**: Lost Chinese Civil War, relocated to Taiwan
- **1986-2000**: Gradually democratized, lost presidency in 2000
- **2008-2016**: Returned to power (Ma Ying-jeou)
- **2024**: Hou Yu-ih loses to Lai Ching-te with 33.49%
