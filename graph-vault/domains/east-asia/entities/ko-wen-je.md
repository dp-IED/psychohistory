# Ko Wen-je (柯文哲)

## Summary

Ko Wen-je is a Taiwanese politician and physician, founder of the Taiwan People's Party (TPP), and Mayor of Taipei (2014-2022). He ran as the TPP presidential candidate in the 2024 election, receiving 26.46% of the vote and finishing third. While his personal vote share was substantial, his candidacy split the anti-DPP vote between the KMT and TPP, effectively handing Lai Ching-te the presidency.

Ko's political positioning is pragmatic centrism: critical of both major parties, appealing to younger urban voters who want competent governance without the ideological baggage of either the DPP's sovereignty emphasis or the KMT's China orientation. His career trajectory — from medicine to independent mayoralty to third-party presidential campaign — mirrors the rise of third-way politics in Taiwan.

## Pre-Election Polling Trajectory (2023)

Ko's polling trajectory reveals the structural ceiling effect for third-party candidates in FPTP systems:

| Period | Ko (TPP) | Hou (KMT) | Lai (DPP) | Context |
|--------|----------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Early 2023 | 20-25% | 30-35% | 35-40% | KMT primary still unfolding; Ko consolidating anti-establishment vote |
| Mid 2023 | 25-30% | 25-30% | 35-38% | Ko's peak zone; KMT-TPP alliance discussions begin; Ko pulls even with Hou |
| Oct 2023 | ~30% (peak) | ~25% | ~35% | Alliance talks collapse; Ko briefly surpasses Hou in some polls |
| Nov 2023 | 22-27% | 28-32% | 36-40% | After alliance failure, KMT consolidates base; Ko's ceiling reasserts |
| Jan 2024 (final) | 26.46% | 33.49% | 40.05% | Strategic voting against TPP; KMT base returns to Hou; Ko settles at ceiling |

**Key insight**: Ko peaked at ~30% in October when the KMT-TPP alliance collapse made him the sole anti-DPP "protest" option, but he could not hold that support. As election day approached, the KMT's organizational base reasserted itself, anti-DPP voters strategically consolidated behind Hou (the viable alternative), and Ko's vote settled at 26.46% — well within the historical third-party ceiling range (see [[domains/east-asia/concepts/third-party-ceiling-fptp/_concept]]).

## Strategic Analysis: The Prisoner's Dilemma

Ko faced a structural prisoner's dilemma in the 2024 election:

- **Cooperate with KMT**: Form a joint opposition ticket. Ko would have to be the vice-presidential nominee (the KMT would never accept a TPP-led ticket). This might defeat Lai but at the cost of TPP's independent identity.
- **Defect (stay in race)**: Ko preserves TPP independence and maximizes his personal brand, but guarantees Lai's victory by splitting the anti-DPP vote.

Ko chose defection. The calculus: preserving TPP as a viable future force was more valuable than winning the 2024 election as a KMT junior partner. This choice confirms the structural analysis — Ko's goal was TPP long-term viability, not 2024 victory — and aligns with the [[domains/east-asia/concepts/third-party-ceiling-fptp/_concept]] prediction that third-party candidates in FPTP cannot win and therefore act as spoilers by strategic choice, not by accident.

## Significance for Forecasting

Ko Wen-je and the TPP are the most consequential structural variable in Taiwan elections. The TPP draws voters from both DPP and KMT constituencies. If the TPP continues as an independent force in 2028, it will split the opposition vote again, reinforcing the divided-opposition pattern. If the TPP collapses or merges with the KMT, the 2028 race becomes structurally different. Future Taiwan election forecasts must assess Ko's future role and TPP viability.
