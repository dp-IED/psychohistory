# Democratic Progressive Party (DPP)

## Summary

The Democratic Progressive Party (DPP) is one of Taiwan's two major political parties, founded in 1986 during martial law. It historically advocated for Taiwanese independence (since moderated to emphasize preserving the status quo and defending Taiwan's sovereignty), distinguishing it from the Kuomintang's unification-leaning orientation. The DPP has held the presidency since 2016 (Tsai Ing-wen 2016-2024, Lai Ching-te 2024-2028).

The DPP's electoral strategy relies on: (1) mobilizing the anti-China/pro-sovereignty base, (2) framing KMT-TPP opposition splits to win pluralities in Taiwan's single-round plurality system, and (3) economic governance credibility. In the 2024 presidential election, DPP candidate Lai Ching-te won with 40.05% — a divided-opposition plurality win enabled by the KMT-TPP split.

## Significance for Forecasting

The DPP's electoral fortunes are structurally linked to opposition fragmentation. If the KMT and TPP coordinate on a joint candidate in a future election, the DPP's path to victory narrows significantly. Future Taiwan election forecasts must assess: (1) whether DPP incumbency provides institutional advantages, (2) opposition coordination status, and (3) cross-strait tension levels as a mobilization driver.

## Timeline

- **1986**: Founded, breaking KMT's one-party system
- **2000-2008**: First period in presidency (Chen Shui-bian)
- **2016-present**: Current presidency period (Tsai → Lai)
- **2024-01-13**: Lai Ching-te wins with 40.05% — third DPP presidential win
