# Taiwan People's Party (TPP)

## Summary

The Taiwan People's Party (TPP) is a centrist political party founded by Ko Wen-je in 2019. Positioning itself as a pragmatic alternative to both the DPP and KMT, the TPP appeals to voters dissatisfied with the two-party system, particularly younger urban professionals. Its policy platform is vaguely centrist: it advocates for efficient governance, fiscal responsibility, and maintaining the status quo on cross-strait relations without embracing either the DPP's sovereignty emphasis or the KMT's pro-China orientation.

In the 2024 presidential election, party founder Ko Wen-je received 26.46% of the vote — a strong third-place showing that denied both major parties a majority by splitting the anti-DPP vote. The TPP holds about 8 seats in the Legislative Yuan, making it a potential kingmaker in coalition-building.

The TPP's role as a "vote-splitting" third party is its most consequential feature for forecasting: by drawing voters from both KMT and DPP constituencies, it can tilt elections toward either major party depending on which side it draws more voters from. In 2024, the TPP drew more from the KMT (anti-DPP but not pro-China voters), effectively handing Lai the presidency.
