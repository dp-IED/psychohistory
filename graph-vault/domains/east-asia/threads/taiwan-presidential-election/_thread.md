# Taiwan Presidential Election Thread

## Overview

Tracks Taiwan's presidential elections, campaign dynamics, opposition coordination, and electoral outcomes. Taiwan uses a single-round plurality (first-past-the-post) system for its presidential elections — the candidate with the most votes wins outright, regardless of whether they reach 50%. This electoral rule makes opposition fragmentation the dominant structural variable in every election.

## Key Dynamics

1. **Opposition fragmentation as the decisive variable**: In a two-party race (DPP vs KMT), the election is competitive and within 5 points. In a three-way race (DPP vs KMT vs TPP), the DPP wins with a low plurality (35-45%) because the anti-DPP vote is split. Taiwan's 2000 and 2024 elections both followed this pattern.

2. **The TPP as structural kingmaker**: The Taiwan People's Party draws voters from both DPP and KMT constituencies. Its mere existence tilts elections toward the DPP by splitting the anti-DPP vote. Future elections depend on whether the TPP runs its own candidate, negotiates a joint ticket with the KMT, or collapses.

3. **Cross-strait tension as mobilization driver**: Periods of heightened China military activity around Taiwan (increased incursions, large-scale exercises, gray-zone operations) tend to boost DPP support by reinforcing sovereignty concerns and the "China threat" narrative.

4. **Incumbency advantage in a divided landscape**: A sitting DPP president (Lai Ching-te 2024-2028) has institutional mobilization, administrative resources, and the ability to set the campaign narrative without needing majority support — only relative advantage over a fragmented opposition.

## Timeline

### 2023 Pre-Election Period

- **2023 Q1-Q2**: Speculation intensifies about whether Ko Wen-je (TPP) will run. The KMT primary process narrows to Hou Yu-ih as the consensus candidate. Terry Gou (independent billionaire) enters the race, complicating the field.
- **2023 May**: KMT formally nominates Hou Yu-ih as presidential candidate.
- **2023 Q3-Q4**: Polling consistently shows Lai Ching-te (DPP) at 35-40%, with Hou (KMT) at 25-30% and Ko (TPP) at 20-25%. The KMT and TPP explore an opposition alliance but fail to agree on terms for a joint ticket.

#### The KMT-TPP Alliance Failure (Critical Juncture, July-November 2023)

The failure of opposition coordination between the KMT and TPP is the decisive structural moment of the 2024 election. Understanding why it failed is essential for forecasting future Taiwan elections and similar dynamics in other FPTP systems.

**Timeline of negotiations:**

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|-------------|
| July 2023 | KMT and TPP begin informal alliance discussions | Both sides recognize that a split opposition guarantees DPP victory |
| September 2023 | KMT proposes "blue-white alliance" framework | KMT assumes it leads the ticket; Ko would be VP nominee |
| October 2023 | Ko rejects KMT's terms; proposes alternative formats | Ko demands an open primary or poll-based selection to determine the ticket leader |
| November 2023 | Last-minute talks at presidential level fail | Both Hou and Ko confirm they will remain in the race |
| November 24, 2023 | Candidate registration deadline passes | Three-way race is locked in structurally |

**Why negotiations failed:**

1. **Who leads the ticket**: The KMT, as the larger and more established party, assumed it would provide the presidential candidate with the TPP providing the VP. Ko refused to accept VP status, believing it would destroy the TPP's independent identity and his personal brand. Neither side could agree on a primary or poll-based selection mechanism that both trusted.

2. **Mutual distrust**: The KMT viewed the TPP as an upstart spoiler. The TPP viewed the KMT as a declining party clinging to an anachronistic China policy. Neither believed the other would honor a post-election power-sharing agreement.

3. **Ko's personal ambition**: After building the TPP from scratch in four years and serving as Taipei mayor for eight, Ko was unwilling to subordinate himself to Hou, a less nationally prominent figure.

4. **Timing pressure**: The negotiations stretched into November, leaving no time to build a joint campaign infrastructure even if an agreement had been reached.

**Forecasting significance**: The KMT-TPP alliance failure reveals that opposition coordination in Taiwan is structurally unlikely unless:
- One party is clearly dominant in polling (was not the case in 2023)
- A formal coalition mechanism exists pre-election (no precedent in Taiwan)
- The smaller party faces existential collapse (TPP was growing, not shrinking)
- Candidate registration deadlines create a "gun to the head" moment (deadlines passed without coordination, confirming the failure)

Any forecast about a future Taiwan presidential election must assess whether these conditions have changed.

#### The Terry Gou Factor (Q3 2023)

Terry Gou, Foxconn founder and billionaire, declared an independent presidential run in August 2023 and qualified for the ballot with 900,000+ signatures. His entry initially created a potential four-way race, drawing votes primarily from the KMT base. However, Gou withdrew on the registration deadline (November 24, 2023) after the KMT-TPP alliance talks collapsed, recognizing that a four-way race would further fragment the anti-DPP vote without any path to victory. His withdrawal simplified the race back to a three-way contest.

**Gou's significance for forecasting**: Independent billionaire candidates in Taiwan are transitory spoilers, not structural forces. Without a party organization, they lack the infrastructure to sustain a campaign through election day. Future independent candidates in Taiwan should be assessed as brief polling perturbations, not game-changers.

- **2023 November**: Last-minute speculation about opposition coordination fades as both Hou and Ko confirm they will remain in the race, and Gou withdraws. The outcome is structurally determined: opposition fragmentation guarantees Lai's victory in the three-way race.

### 2024 Election

- **2024-01-13**: Lai Ching-te (DPP) wins with 40.05% of the vote. Hou Yu-ih (KMT) receives 33.49%, Ko Wen-je (TPP) receives 26.46%. Turnout is 71.86%. The DPP also wins 51 of 113 legislative seats, losing its majority but remaining the largest party.
- **2024-05-20**: Lai Ching-te inaugurated as President.

### 2024-2028 Post-Election Period

- The TPP holds 8 legislative seats, retaining kingmaker potential for the 2028 election.
- Lai's presidency faces: (1) a divided legislature (DPP short of majority), (2) sustained Chinese military pressure (gray-zone operations, increased incursions), and (3) the structural question of whether the KMT and TPP will coordinate for 2028.

## Forecasting Significance

Every future question about a Taiwan presidential election must assess:

1. **Candidate count**: Is this a 2-way or 3-way race? If 3-way, apply the [[domains/east-asia/concepts/divided-opposition-plurality-win/_concept]] (for the front-runner's path) and the [[domains/east-asia/concepts/third-party-ceiling-fptp/_concept]] (for why any third-party candidate cannot win).
2. **Opposition coordination status**: Are KMT-TPP alliance negotiations active? Have registration deadlines passed? Use the KMT-TPP Alliance Failure analysis above as a template for assessing conditions.
3. **Third-party candidate viability**: If the question asks about Ko Wen-je or another third-party/independent candidate, apply the [[domains/east-asia/concepts/third-party-ceiling-fptp/_concept]] immediately. Third-party candidates in Taiwan's FPTP system are structurally incapable of winning the presidency.
4. **Incumbency**: Does the DPP hold the presidency? If yes, institutional advantage applies.
5. **Cross-strait tension level**: Has Chinese military activity increased? This typically benefits the DPP.
6. **TPP trajectory**: Is the TPP growing, stable, or collapsing? Its trajectory determines whether the 2028 race is 2-way or 3-way.
