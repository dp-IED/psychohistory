# Xi Jinping Leadership Stability

## Thread Summary

Tracks the probability of Xi Jinping being removed from power as CCP General Secretary, whether through resignation, dismissal, detention, or disqualification. Covers formal institutional mechanisms and informal factional dynamics.

**Polymarket reference:** "Xi Jinping out before 2027?" — 6.5% Yes, $9.5M volume (as of 2026-05-20)

## Key Dynamics

### 1. Institutional Removal Mechanisms
- **CCP Central Committee vote** — requires majority of Central Committee to remove General Secretary (never invoked in PRC history)
- **Politburo Standing Committee decision** — internal party mechanism, opaque
- **Standing Committee of the National People's Congress** — constitutional mechanism for removing President/Chairman
- **Anti-graft investigation** — informal removal through corruption charges (used against Bo Xilai, Zhou Yongkang, Sun Lifeng, Li Zhanshu's associates)

### 2. Factional Dynamics
- **Princelings vs. Populists** — Xi's anti-corruption campaign consolidated power but created resentment
- **Military loyalty** — PLA leadership reshuffled extensively under Xi; CMC vice-chair before 2027 succession planning
- **Elite consensus** — the "two sessions" and Central Committee plenums as venues for elite negotiation
- **Succession planning** — likely successors in the 2027 20th Party Congress; any signs of premature succession pressure

### 3. Health & Capacity Indicators
- Xi's age (born 1953, age 73 in 2026)
- Public appearances frequency and duration
- Voice quality, physical stamina in public events
- Hospital visits or unexplained absences

### 4. Economic & Performance Triggers
- **Economic crisis threshold** — GDP growth below 3%, banking crisis, or debt default could trigger elite unrest
- **Policy failure blame** — property sector collapse, local government debt crisis, youth unemployment
- **Taiwan escalation failure** — a failed or stalemated Taiwan operation
- **COVID/health crisis mishandling** — re-emergence of pandemic as legitimacy challenge

### 5. Historical Precedents
- No sitting CCP General Secretary has been removed since Mao (1976 — Hua Guofeng was challenged but not formally removed until Deng consolidated power)
- Jiang Zemin and Hu Jintao served fixed terms and retired at 18th/19th Party Congresses
- Xi's third term (2022–2027) broke the two-term norm, suggesting longer tenure
- The 2027 20th Party Congress is the natural succession point

## Probability Assessment

The market prices 6.5% by end of 2026. Key factors:

**Arguments for departure:**
- Age 73 and observable health decline
- Elite dissatisfaction with economic management (property crisis, deflation)
- Factional pressure from military if Taiwan posture fails
- Break with two-term norm creates succession uncertainty

**Arguments against departure:**
- Xi has comprehensively consolidated power (anti-corruption, military loyalty, think-tank control)
- No viable successor identified or permitted to build power base
- CCP's institutional norm against removing incumbent General Secretary
- 2027 Party Congress is the natural transition point — any removal before would be unprecedented
- Economic difficulties blamed on external factors (tariffs, demographic decline)
