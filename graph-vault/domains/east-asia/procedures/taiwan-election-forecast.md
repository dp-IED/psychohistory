# Taiwan Presidential Election Forecast Procedure

## When to Use

Apply this procedure when forecasting any Taiwan presidential election outcome or any question about whether a specific candidate will win the Taiwan presidency.

## ⚠ Reasoning Trap Warning (Read First)

**The Two-Race Fallacy**: The single most common error in Taiwan election forecasting is treating a three-candidate race under FPTP as a two-candidate popularity contest. In Taiwan's single-round plurality system, a front-runner polling at 35-40% with the opposition split is structurally DOMINANT, not weak. Do not apply incumbency-fatigue or "needs 50%" logic. The structural variables (candidate count, opposition coordination status, electoral system) dominate performance variables (approval, platform, scandals).

See [[domains/global/concepts/plurality-race-reasoning-trap/_concept]] for the full reasoning trap documentation.

Key diagnostics for detecting the trap in your own reasoning:
- If you find yourself saying "Lai is only at 38% — the DPP has been in power 8 years" → STOP. You are applying two-race analysis. Re-check the candidate count and coordination status.
- If you say "the combined opposition vote exceeds the front-runner's" → This is the structural precondition for a divided-opposition win, not evidence of opposition strength. The opposition CANNOT combine.
- If you cite historical precedent → Good. Now verify that the precedent is from the SAME electoral system under the SAME candidate count condition. Taiwan 2000 (3-way, 39.3%) is relevant. US 2020 (2-way) is not.

**If you are predicting NO for a front-runner at 30-45% in a 3-way race, you must explicitly answer**: "How will the opposition coordinate despite the failure of alliance negotiations and the locked-in three-candidate field?" If the answer is unclear, your NO prediction is likely driven by the two-race fallacy.

## Pre-Forecast Audit

### Step 1: Determine the Candidate Count
How many credible candidates (polling >10%) are in the race?

- **2 candidates** (DPP vs KMT or DPP vs joint opposition ticket): Normal competitive election. Apply standard polling analysis.
- **3 candidates** (DPP vs KMT vs TPP): Apply the [[domains/east-asia/concepts/divided-opposition-plurality-win/_concept]]. Front-runner at 35-45% with opposition split is guaranteed a win at 85-95% confidence.
- **1 dominant candidate**: Auto-win if opposition fails to field a credible challenger.

### Step 2: Check Electoral System
Taiwan uses single-round plurality for presidential elections. Confirm this has not changed. If a runoff or two-round system has been adopted, the fragmented-opposition dynamic only applies to the first round.

### Step 3: Assess Opposition Coordination Status
- Are KMT-TPP alliance negotiations active? If yes, monitor.
- Have candidate registration deadlines passed? If yes, fragmentation is locked in.
- Has any candidate agreed to withdraw? Last-minute withdrawal is rare but would reshuffle the race.

### Step 4: Check Historical Precedent
- 2000: DPP won with 39.3% (3-way race, KMT split)
- 2004: DPP won with 50.1% (2-way race after opposition united)
- 2008: KMT won with 58.4% (2-way race, DPP weakened by corruption scandals)
- 2012: KMT won with 51.6% (2-way race)
- 2016: DPP won with 56.1% (2-way race, KMT collapse)
- 2020: DPP won with 57.1% (2-way race)
- 2024: DPP won with 40.05% (3-way race, opposition fragmented)

**Key insight**: The DPP has never won a 2-way race with less than 50%. The 40.05% win in 2024 was entirely a product of opposition fragmentation, not DPP strength.

### Step 5: Apply the Fragmentation Multiplier
In a 3-way race where the DPP front-runner polls at 35-45%:

| Condition | P(DPP win) |
|-----------|-----------|
| KMT and TPP both running, no alliance | 85-95% |
| One opposition candidate withdraws (late) | 60-75% (depends on where their support goes) |
| KMT-TPP joint ticket formed | 40-50% (structural race) |
| TPP collapses or endorses KMT | 45-55% (back to 2-way race) |

### Step 6: Assess Incumbency
- **Incumbent DPP president running**: +5-10% structural advantage (mobilization, name recognition, admin resources)
- **No incumbent (open seat)**: Standard race dynamics

### Step 7: Check Cross-Strait Tension Level
- **Elevated tension** (Chinese military exercises, diplomatic pressure increasing): Typically +2-5% for DPP (sovereignty mobilization)
- **Low tension**: Neutral effect

### Step 8: Document the Structural Rationale
Explicitly state the electoral system, candidate count, opposition coordination status, and what the fragmentation implies. Example:

> Taiwan uses single-round plurality. Three candidates are running: Lai Ching-te (DPP, 38% in polls), Hou Yu-ih (KMT, 28%), Ko Wen-je (TPP, 22%). Opposition alliance talks failed. The KMT+TPP combined vote exceeds Lai's total, but they cannot coordinate. In this structural configuration, Lai is guaranteed to win with 37-43% — the divided-opposition pattern. P(YES) = 90%.

## Post-Forecast Reflection

After outcome known, verify:
- Did the candidate count match pre-election assessments?
- Did any last-minute withdrawal or endorsement change the race?
- Was the fragmentation effect stronger or weaker than anticipated?
