# Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)

## Type
US federal public health agency under HHS; headquartered in Atlanta, GA.

## Mandate
The CDC is the primary US authority for infectious disease surveillance,
outbreak investigation, and public health risk assessment. Its
respiratory disease portfolio is the definitive data source for domestic
forecasting questions on influenza, RSV, and COVID-19.

## Key Surveillance Systems for Respiratory Forecasting

- **FluView**: Weekly influenza surveillance report. Tracks ILI
  (influenza-like illness) outpatient visit percentages, lab-confirmed
  hospitalizations, pediatric deaths, and virus subtyping. Published
  Fridays during flu season (Week 40–Week 20).
- **NREVSS** (National Respiratory and Enteric Virus Surveillance
  System): Lab-based surveillance tracking RSV, seasonal coronaviruses,
  parainfluenza, adenovirus, and human metapneumovirus via PCR
  detections.
- **ILINet**: ~3,400 outpatient providers reporting weekly ILI visit
  counts. Feeds into the national ILI baseline — currently 1.9% for
  the 2025-26 season.
- **NHSN** (shared with CMS): Hospital capacity and respiratory
  admissions data reported from ~6,000 hospitals.

## Structural Position
The CDC sits at the federal-state boundary. State health departments
report notifiable disease data to CDC; CDC aggregates, analyzes, and
publishes. CDC CANNOT mandate testing or reporting from private
providers — compliance is voluntary for most surveillance networks.
This creates a persistent 1-2 week lag in official case counts vs.
clinical reality.

## Director
Mandy Cohen, MD (confirmed June 2023). Internal medicine physician,
former North Carolina HHS Secretary.

## Recent Developments (as of May 2026)
- CDC maintains the 1.9% ILINet national baseline for 2025-26.
- Post-COVID institutional restructuring has increased emphasis on
  wastewater surveillance and genomic sequencing through the Pathogen
  Genomics Centers of Excellence network.
- The agency's credibility recovery post-COVID continues; watch for
  risk-assessment conservatism as a bias in escalation signals.

## Hantavirus Surveillance Context

The CDC is the primary US authority for domestic hantavirus surveillance
and outbreak investigation. For the MV Hondius outbreak (May 2026):

- **Notifiable disease**: Hantavirus infection is nationally notifiable
  in the US — all confirmed cases must be reported to CDC.
- **Contact tracing**: CDC coordinated monitoring of US-based contacts
  from the MV Hondius voyage (passengers returning to the US).
- **Andes virus expertise**: CDC's Special Pathogens Branch maintains
  expertise on the Andes virus strain (the only hantavirus with
  confirmed H2H transmission), relevant for risk assessment.
- **Hantavirus Epidemiology**: CDC tracks Sin Nombre virus cases in the
  US (Four Corners region, Yosemite, etc.) with annual case counts
  typically 20-40 nationwide. This provides a US-specific baseline for
  hantavirus outbreak forecasting.
- **Cruise ship outbreak protocols**: CDC's Vessel Sanitation Program
  — originally designed for gastrointestinal outbreaks on cruise ships
  — may coordinate with international partners for non-GI outbreaks
  like hantavirus.
