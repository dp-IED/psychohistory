# World Health Organization (WHO)

## Type
United Nations specialized agency for global public health; headquartered
in Geneva, Switzerland.

## Mandate
The WHO coordinates international disease surveillance, issues
epidemic/pandemic declarations, and provides technical guidance to
member states. Under the International Health Regulations (IHR, 2005),
196 countries are obligated to report certain disease outbreaks.

## Global Influenza Program

The WHO's Global Influenza Surveillance and Response System (GISRS) is
the backbone of seasonal influenza strain selection:

- **GISRS network**: 152 institutions in 127 countries conducting
  year-round virological surveillance. Labs submit specimens to 7 WHO
  Collaborating Centres for antigenic and genetic characterization.
- **Vaccine composition meetings**: Held twice annually (Feb/Mar for
  Northern Hemisphere, Sep/Oct for Southern Hemisphere). WHO recommends
  the specific influenza strains for inclusion in seasonal vaccines
  based on GISRS data.
- **Pandemic Influenza Preparedness (PIP) Framework**: Governs
  sharing of influenza viruses with pandemic potential and access to
  vaccines.

## Structural Position
The WHO can declare a Public Health Emergency of International Concern
(PHEIC) — the highest alarm under IHR. A PHEIC does NOT auto-trigger
travel bans or funding; it is a political/diplomatic signal that
accelerates member-state response. A pandemic declaration (Phase 6 on
the pandemic alert scale) requires sustained community transmission in
at least two WHO regions.

## Forecasting Significance
- WHO PHEIC declarations are discrete binary events that often appear
  as Metaculus tournament questions.
- GISRS strain selection accuracy directly determines [[domains/health/concepts/vaccine-effectiveness/_concept]].
- WHO situation reports (SITREPs) are the authoritative source for
  global case counts and transmission classification, useful as PIT
  cutoffs.
- The pandemic alert phases provide a clear escalation framework —
  see [[domains/health/concepts/outbreak-escalation/_concept]].

## Hantavirus Surveillance Role

The WHO coordinated the international response to the MV Hondius
hantavirus outbreak (May 2026) through the International Health
Regulations mechanism, issuing a risk assessment of "no sign of larger
outbreak" at cutoff. Key responsibilities relevant to forecasting:

- **IHR notification**: 196 states parties must report hantavirus
  cases — WHO aggregates and publishes situation reports.
- **Risk assessment**: WHO's formal risk level ("low" for MV Hondius)
  is the authoritative PIT signal for forecasting secondary spread.
- **Multi-country coordination**: For transport-vector outbreaks,
  WHO bridges surveillance between affected countries with different
  reporting standards and capacities.
- **Andes virus tracking**: WHO's GISRS network does not cover
  hantaviruses, but the IHR event-based surveillance system does —
  a distinction that affects detection lags.
