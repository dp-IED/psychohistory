# Pope Leo XIV (b. 1955)

Pope Leo XIV (born Robert Francis Prevost, 14 September 1955) is head of the Catholic Church and sovereign of Vatican City, elected on 8 May 2025. He is the first pope born in the United States (Chicago, Illinois), the first with US and Peruvian citizenship, the first from the Order of Saint Augustine, and the second pope from the Americas (after Francis).

## Career Path
- **1977**: Entered the Order of Saint Augustine (Augustinians)
- **1982**: Ordained as a priest
- **1987**: Doctor of Canon Law, Pontifical University of St. Thomas Aquinas (Angelicum), Rome
- **1980s-1990s**: Missionary work in Peru — parish pastor, diocesan official, seminary teacher, administrator
- **2001-2013**: Prior General of the Order of Saint Augustine, based in Rome
- **2015-2023**: Bishop of Chiclayo, Peru
- **2023**: Appointed Prefect of the Dicastery for Bishops and President of the Pontifical Commission for Latin America by Pope Francis; made a cardinal
- **2025 (May 8)**: Elected Pope Leo XIV on 4th ballot of the 2025 papal conclave

## Election Context
- **Dark horse candidate**: His election was unexpected by observers — Vatican insiders believed a US pope was unrealistic given US superpower status
- **Papal name**: Chose Leo XIV, echoing Leo XIII's concern for workers amid the Industrial Revolution — positioned as a response to AI and the new industrial revolution
- **Age at election**: 69 (relatively young by recent papal standards — Francis was 76, Benedict 78, John Paul II 58)
- **Citizenship**: Dual US-Peruvian; speaks English, Spanish, Italian, and Latin

## Areas of Emphasis (Early Papacy)
- AI and technology ethics
- Migrant rights
- Climate action (continuing Francis's emphasis)
- Arms control and opposition to nationalism
- Synodality and Vatican II implementation
- Social teaching continuity on environment, marriage, and sexuality

## Forecasting Significance
- Age 69 at election suggests a potentially long reign (15-25 years)
- First pope from the United States — a geopolitical milestone that affects US-Vatican relations
- Dual US-Peruvian identity bridges North and South America
- Augustinian background (rather than Jesuit or diocesan) represents a new institutional tradition
