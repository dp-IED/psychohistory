# Pope Francis (1936-2025)

Pope Francis (born Jorge Mario Bergoglio, 17 December 1936 – 21 April 2025) was head of the Catholic Church and sovereign of Vatican City from 13 March 2013 until his death in 2025. He was the first Jesuit pope, the first from the Americas, and the first pope born or raised outside Europe since the 8th-century Syrian pope Gregory III.

## Key Health Risk Factors
- **Age**: 88 at time of death — well above male life expectancy for both Argentina (73) and Italy (81)
- **Respiratory vulnerability**: Recurrent bronchial infections; had part of one lung removed in his youth (1957)
- **Mobility impairment**: Severe knee osteoarthritis requiring wheelchair from ~2020 onward — reduced mobility is a mortality risk multiplier in elderly patients
- **Surgical history**: Two major abdominal surgeries (2021, 2023) in his 80s
- **Papal workload**: Despite age and health, maintained an active travel schedule through late 2024

## Papacy Notes
- 12 years, 39 days as pope (2013-2025) — longer than average papal reign (~7.5 years for modern popes)
- Known for Jesuit austerity, emphasis on mercy, climate action, migrant rights, interfaith dialogue, and synodality
- His health decline was gradual but accelerated in 2024-2025, with increasing absences from public events

## Signal Importance for Forecasting
The Pope Francis mortality case is a template for assessing **elderly leader mortality risk**: an 88-year-old with documented respiratory vulnerability, reduced mobility, and recent surgical history has a materially elevated 1-year mortality risk. The base-rate annual mortality for an 88-year-old male in good health is ~10-12%; with Francis's comorbidities, the true risk was likely 20-35% per year.
