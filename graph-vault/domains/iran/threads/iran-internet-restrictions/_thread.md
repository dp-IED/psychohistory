# Iranian Internet Restrictions and State Censorship

## Overview

This thread tracks Iran's systematic use of internet blackouts and restrictions as a tool of state control. Iran has one of the most sophisticated internet censorship systems in the world (the "National Internet" or "Halal Internet" initiative), deploying DPI (Deep Packet Inspection), protocol blocking, and complete shutdowns during periods of political unrest or military conflict.

## Key Patterns

### Trigger Events for Blackouts

1. **Election-related protests** (2009, 2017-2018, 2019-2020): Targeted restrictions on social media + messaging apps
2. **Fuel price protests** (2019 November): Near-total shutdown lasting ~7 days — the most severe internet blackout in Iran until 2026
3. **Military conflict** (2026 February): Nationwide blackout began February 28, 2026 during Iran-US-Israel military engagement
4. **Religious/national holidays** with protest risk: Usually partial throttling, not full shutdown

### Restoration Patterns

- Internet blackouts in Iran typically last 5-14 days, then restore to a restricted state (social media still blocked, international bandwidth limited)
- Full restoration (removing all restrictions) is rare — even after the 2019 blackout, restrictions on Instagram and WhatsApp remained
- The post-conflict restoration typically involves a "phased restoration": government/essential services first, then limited international access, then social media (if at all)

## 2026 Context

- On February 28, 2026, a nationwide internet blackout began in Iran amid military engagement with the United States and Israel
- This was the most severe and complete blackout in Iranian history (total shutdown, not just targeted restrictions)
- As of May 2026 (~80+ days), internet access remains severely restricted
- The "Internet Access restored in Iran by June 30, 2026?" market at 21% Yes ($99.5K volume) implies the market sees limited probability of even partial restoration by end of June
- Key variables: US and EU diplomatic pressure, Iran's internal economic needs (business depends on internet access), the state of post-war regime stability

## Key Questions

- Is the 2026 blackout different because it's war-related (vs protest-related)? War-related blackouts may last longer because the regime frames it as a national security measure rather than political suppression
- What international mechanisms exist to pressure restoration? UN, EU, US sanctions leverage
- Does partial restoration (government services + limited commercial) count as "restored" for market resolution? The resolution criteria may be ambiguous
