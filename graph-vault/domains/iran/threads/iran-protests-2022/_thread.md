# Iran Protests (Mahsa Amini)

## Overview

The death of 22-year-old Mahsa Amini on September 16, 2022, while in the custody of Iran's morality police (Gasht-e Ershad) for allegedly violating mandatory hijab laws, triggered the most sustained and widespread protest movement in Iran since the 2009 Green Movement. What began as localized demonstrations in Tehran and Amini's home province of Kurdistan rapidly spread to over 80 cities across Iran within days. The protests — initially focused on mandatory hijab and police brutality — broadened into a wider challenge to the Islamic Republic's political, economic, and social system. The regime's response was swift and violent, involving mass arrests, internet shutdowns, live-fire against protesters, and a brutal security crackdown that would claim hundreds of lives by end of year.

## Key Events (This Quarter)

- **2022-09-16**: Mahsa Amini dies in Tehran hospital after three days in a coma. She had been arrested by the morality police on September 13 for improper hijab. [[2022-Q3]]
- **2022-09-17**: Protests erupt in Tehran, Karaj, and Amini's hometown of Saqqez in Kurdistan province. Videos of women removing and waving their hijabs spread rapidly on social media despite government-imposed internet restrictions.
- **2022-09-19**: Supreme Leader Khamenei breaks silence, expressing "sadness" over Amini's death but blaming the US and Israel for the protests — framing that escalates regime rhetoric.
- **2022-09-20-30**: Protests continue to spread. The regime deploys security forces heavily, imposes internet blackouts in Kurdistan and other provinces, and arrests hundreds. The death toll of protesters begins to mount.

## Forecasting Significance

The Iran protests represent a structural challenge to the Islamic Republic's political stability. Multiple factors make this movement distinct from previous uprisings (2009, 2019): (a) women are the visible leaders, (b) the protest transcends class and ethnic divides, (c) the regime's economic failures (inflation above 50%, currency collapse, water shortages) compound political grievances, and (d) social media enables organization despite censorship. The trajectory of the protest movement — whether it escalates, is crushed, or produces political concessions — is a central geopolitical forecasting variable for Middle East stability, oil markets, and the regional balance of power.
