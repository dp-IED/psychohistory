## Summary

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC, Sepah) is a branch of Iran's armed forces established after the 1979 Islamic Revolution to defend the regime rather than the nation's borders. It operates parallel to Iran's regular military (Artesh) and controls Iran's missile program, nuclear security, asymmetric warfare capabilities (Quds Force), and the Basij militia. The IRGC is also deeply embedded in Iran's economy (construction, oil, banking, telecommunications) through its conglomerate, Khatam al-Anbiya. The IRGC-Quds Force, under Qasem Soleimani (until 2020) and Esmail Qaani (2020-), directs Iran's proxy network across the Middle East: Hezbollah (Lebanon), Hamas/PIJ (Gaza), Houthis (Yemen), and Shia militias (Iraq, Syria).

## Significance for Forecasting

- **Key escalation actor**: The IRGC is the primary Iranian entity that would execute or authorize a retaliatory strike against Israel or the US. Its commanders (IRGC Aerospace Force for missiles, IRGC Navy for Persian Gulf operations, Quds Force for proxy operations) have their own decision-making calculus partially independent of the elected civilian government.

- **Use it or lose it dynamic**: The IRGC's missile and nuclear infrastructure are the organization's most valuable assets. When these are threatened (proxy network degraded, nuclear facilities targeted), the IRGC leadership has institutional incentives to escalate rather than accept degradation — the organization's raison d'etre is "resistance."

- **Informational asymmetry**: Unlike Western militaries, the IRGC does not publish detailed doctrine or strategy. Its decision-making is opaque, concentrated in a small leadership circle (Supreme Leader + IRGC commanders + Expediency Council). Forecasting IRGC actions relies on revealed preferences: past retaliation patterns (calibrated, proportional, with face-saving messaging), red lines (assassination of senior commanders triggers response), and operational constraints (precision strike capability limited compared to Israel/US).

- **Internal factionalism**: The IRGC is not a monolith. Hardliners (those favoring maximal confrontation with Israel/US) and pragmatists (those prioritizing IRGC economic interests and regime survival) compete for influence. Khamenei arbitrates between factions. Forecasting IRGC retaliation requires assessing which faction's posture is dominant at the time.

## Forecasting Patterns: IRGC Retaliation History

| Trigger | IRGC Response | Lag | Escalation Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| US killing of Qasem Soleimani (Jan 2020) | Ballistic missile strike on US bases in Iraq (Ain al-Asad) | 5 days | Significant — first direct Iranian strike on US forces |
| Israeli killing of IRGC commanders in Damascus (Apr 2024) | Drone/missile attack on Israel (Operation True Promise I) | 12 days | Major — first direct Iranian attack on Israel |
| Haniyeh assassination in Tehran (Jul 2024) | No immediate IRGC military response; vowed revenge | Ongoing | Deferred — suggests threshold-based decision making |
| Nasrallah assassination (Sep 2024) | Ballistic missile attack on Israel (Operation True Promise II) | Days | Major — IRGC Aerospace Force assets used |
| Israeli strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities (Jun 2025) | Ballistic missiles + drones on Israeli cities + US bases in Qatar/Iraq | Hours | Full-scale retaliation |

**Pattern**: IRGC retaliation is (a) proportional to the trigger's severity, (b) delayed by 0-12 days depending on whether operational plans exist, (c) always framed as "legal and legitimate defense," and (d) modulated by Khamenei's risk appetite at the moment.
