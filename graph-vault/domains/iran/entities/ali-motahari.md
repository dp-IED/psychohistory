# Ali Motahari

## Summary

Ali Motahari is an Iranian conservative politician, former member of the Islamic Consultative Assembly (Majlis), and son of the influential philosopher Ayatollah Morteza Motahari. He is listed as a candidate in Polymarket's "Iran leader end of 2026?" contract at **0.15% YES** ($99K volume), reflecting a near-zero probability of becoming Iran's head of state.

## Political Background

- **Majlis Tenure**: Served multiple terms representing Tehran; known for occasional criticism of government policies
- **Ideological Positioning**: Conservative-reformist straddler — critical of Ahmadinejad-era populism, supportive of nuclear negotiations under Rouhani, but firmly within the Islamic Republic's constitutional framework
- **Principle-ist Faction**: Affiliated with the traditional conservative (principle-ist) camp, not reformists; represents the old-guard clerical-political establishment rather than the IRGC-security apparatus
- **2024 Election**: Barred from running for the Assembly of Experts by the Guardian Council, limiting his institutional path to power

## Why Near-Zero Probability (0.15%)

Motahari has no viable path to becoming Iran's head of state:

1. **Wrong institutional path**: Iran's head of state is the Supreme Leader, selected by the Assembly of Experts from senior clerics. Motahari has been BARRED from the Assembly of Experts itself.
2. **Lack of clerical seniority**: While son of a prominent ayatollah, Motahari himself is not a senior cleric (marja') — the informal prerequisite for Supreme Leader candidacy
3. **IRGC opposition**: The IRGC-security apparatus that controls succession dynamics views Motahari's independent streak as disqualifying
4. **No presidential path either**: He has not contested the presidency (the elected head of government, not head of state)
5. **Polymarket category error**: The market may list him because he's a recognizable political name, but he's not a serious candidate for Supreme Leader succession
