# Presidential Tenure Risk Assessment

## When to Use

Apply this procedure when a forecasting question asks whether a specific president/leader will leave office before a specific date. This covers questions of the form:

- "Will [Leader] cease to be president in [Year]?"
- "Will [Leader] still be in power on [Date]?"
- "Will [Leader] resign/impeached/removed before [Event]?"

## Procedure

### Step 1: Identify the Leader and Country

Record:
- Leader, title, country
- Constitutional term length and scheduled end date
- Current year in office (first year, midterm, lame duck)
- Previous political experience (first-time president, former minister, etc.)

### Step 2: Assess All Removal Pathways

For each of the four pathways, answer:

| Pathway | Key Question | Data Needed |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Impeachment** | Does the opposition have the required supermajority? | Legislative seat counts, party discipline record, impeachment precedent in country |
| **Resignation** | Is there a credible threat of near-certain removal if the leader doesn't resign? | Approval trajectory, protest intensity, elite defection signals, scandal severity |
| **Death/incapacity** | Does the leader have known health issues or a high-assassination-risk environment? | Age, known conditions, security posture, country's assassination history |
| **Coup/unconstitutional** | Is the military willing to act against civilian authority? | Civilian control of military history, coup precedent, international reaction pattern |

Scoring rule: if NO pathway's threshold is met, predict continued tenure. Multiple pathways at the margin increase risk, but even combined marginal risks rarely exceed ~20%.

### Step 3: Check Economic Trajectory

Economic direction is the single most important structural variable for leader tenure:

| Economic Signal | Protective (low removal risk) | Warning (elevated removal risk) |
|----------------|------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Inflation** | Falling from high levels, or stable at moderate levels | Rising rapidly; hyperinflation returning |
| **GDP growth** | Positive and/or recovering from recession | Negative; recession deepening |
| **Real wages** | Rising or stable | Falling sharply for extended period |
| **Poverty** | Declining | Rising |
| **Scarcity** | No acute shortages | Widespread shortages (food, fuel, medicine) |

A leader with 3+ protective signals AND 0-1 warning signals has very low removal risk regardless of scandal damage.

### Step 4: Assess Opposition Cohesion

| Opposition State | Removal Risk |
|-----------------|-------------|
| Unified single party/coalition that could govern | Elevated — coordinated impeachment/removal possible |
| Fragmented across 3+ parties with incompatible platforms | Low — no single alternative government |
| Weak/no legislative representation | Very low — no institutional mechanism |

### Step 5: Check International Support

External actors can either protect or undermine a leader:

| Actor | Protective | Destabilizing |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| IMF / IFIs | Current program, positive relationship, loans | Program suspended, negative reports |
| US / EU | Ally status, economic/military support | Sanctions, public criticism |
| Regional bodies (OAS, UNASUR) | Recognition of legitimacy | Condemnation, election non-recognition |
| Key bilateral partners | Trade deals, diplomatic backing | Withdrawal of support |

### Step 6: Check Institutional Resilience

Country-specific institutional factors:

- **Democratic history**: Years of continuous democracy since last coup/unconstitutional removal
- **Civilian control of military**: Has the military ever successfully intervened? When? Is there a pattern?
- **Constitutional removal difficulty**: Supermajority required? Single-chamber or bicameral?
- **Independent judiciary**: Has the judiciary ever acted against the executive in a politically significant case?
- **Impeachment precedent**: Have impeachments historically succeeded? Failed? What was the context?

### Step 7: Synthesize Using the Reference Class

Build a reference class of similar leaders (populist, radical reformer, first-term president, same region) and compare:

| Factor | Favors Survival | Favors Removal |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| Quick win (inflation reduction, etc.) | Yes | No |
| Party organization | Strong | Weak/none |
| Opposition unity | Fragmented | Unified |
| Constitutional threshold met? | No | Yes |
| Scandals controllable? | Yes (owes oversight) | No (independent investigation) |
| International support | Strong | Weak/hostile |

If 4+ of 6 factors favor survival → NO (survival probability >85%)
If 4+ of 6 factors favor removal → YES (removal probability >50%)
Otherwise → mixed, need deeper case analysis

### Step 8: Produce Structured Forecast

Output a forecast with:
1. **Prediction**: YES (removal) or NO (continued tenure)
2. **Confidence**: Low (<60%), Medium (60-80%), High (>80%)
3. **Pathway analysis**: Which removal pathway would be most likely if YES occurred
4. **Key uncertainty**: What single factor, if it changed, would flip the prediction

## Example: Milei 2025 (Correct NO)

| Step | Assessment |
|------|-----------|
| Step 1: Leader | Milei, Argentina, term ends Dec 2027, first-term radical reformer |
| Step 2: Pathways | Impeachment: opposition lacks 2/3. Resignation: approval never below 32%. Coup: none since 1976. Health: no known issues. |
| Step 3: Economy | Inflation 300%→1.5% monthly, GDP recovering, wages rising, poverty declining — ALL protective |
| Step 4: Opposition | Fragmented across 3 Peronist successor parties + FIT-U. No unified alternative. |
| Step 5: International | Strong: IMF $20B loan, US Treasury support, Trump ally. |
| Step 6: Resilience | Democratic continuity since 1983, no successful impeachment EVER, military civilianized. |
| Step 7: Reference | 7/8 first-term radical reformers survive. Castillo is the only failure — lacks the protective factors Milei had. |
| **Synthesis** | 5/6 factors favor survival. NO with >90% confidence. |
