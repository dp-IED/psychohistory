# Far-Left Marginalization Under Polarization

## Definition

A structural forecasting pattern in which doctrinaire far-left or Marxist-Leninist parties hit a **hard ideological ceiling at ~3-8%** of the national vote, regardless of economic conditions or anti-incumbent sentiment, because polarization consolidates the left vote behind a moderate social-democratic frontrunner rather than fragmenting it toward the radical left. Unlike the regional third-way squeeze (where geography is the binding constraint), the far-left marginalization operates through **ideological disqualification**: the far-left brand is toxic to median voters in polarized two-round systems.

## Structural Mechanics

### The Ceiling Effect
In a polarized left-right contest, the moderate left candidate (~center-left social democrat) consolidates 80-95% of the left-of-center vote. The far-left candidate cannot break through because:

1. **Median voter logic**: In a high-stakes election where the alternative is a right-wing populist, left-leaning voters flock to the electable moderate rather than "wasting" their vote on a purist candidate who cannot govern.

2. **Coalition imperative**: Far-left parties that refuse to join a united left front (e.g., FIT-U in Argentina) are punished electorally, while those that subordinate to the moderate left (e.g., Pacto Histórico in Colombia) gain influence but lose distinct identity.

3. **Historical trauma**: In countries with traumatic experiences with left-wing radicalism (Argentina's 1970s guerrillas, Chile's Allende→Pinochet sequence, Colombia's M-19/FARC legacy), the far-left label triggers an automatic ceiling among older voters even when economic conditions favor the left.

4. **Two-round system amplification**: In runoffs, far-left candidates almost never advance past the first round, and their voters disproportionately migrate to the moderate-left candidate in the second round — further incentivizing consolidation in the first round.

### Key Insight: The Ceiling Is Structural, Not Cyclical

Unlike the regional third-way squeeze (which operates over 1-2 cycles), the far-left ceiling is **persistent across decades** in most Latin American countries. The 2.70% for FIT-U in 2023 Argentina was not a function of Milei's specific appeal — it was consistent with far-left vote shares in Argentina across the 2000s and 2010s (3-5% range). Even when the incumbent is disastrous (100%+ inflation), the far-left cannot capture more than a small protest vote.

## Forecasting Application

When a forecast question asks about a far-left party's electoral prospects:

1. **Identify the candidate's ideological position**: Are they a doctrinaire Marxist-Leninist, Trotskyist, or communist? If yes, the ceiling is ~3-8%. If they are a moderate social democrat, the ceiling is the normal left-wing maximum (~45-55%).

2. **Check the two-round structure**: In a system with runoffs, the far-left almost certainly cannot advance past the first round, and their voters will migrate to the moderate left in the runoff (adding ~2-5pp to the moderate left's total).

3. **Check for left-wing unity**: If the moderate left has formed a broad front (Pacto Histórico, FA, UP), the far-left either joins (and loses distinct electoral identity) or is marginalized to protest-vote status.

4. **Estimate the ceiling**: Historical base rates for doctrinaire far-left candidates in Latin America: ~3-8% in presidential races, ~5-12% in proportional legislative races (where list PR allows more ideological diversity).

5. **Apply the structural improbability check**: A far-left candidate winning a national presidency is a textbook structurally-improbable outcome (p < 0.01) — no candidate further left than Allende has won via free elections in Latin America since 1970.

The regional squeeze operates through geography (a regional base that cannot scale); far-left marginalization operates through ideology (a brand that cannot expand). They can compound: a far-left party with a regional base (FIT-U in Argentina's industrial belt) has both ideological AND geographic ceilings.

- **[[domains/latin-america/concepts/latin-american-left-turn/_concept]]**: The left-turn concept tracks the broad trend of left-wing electoral success in Latin America since 1998. The far-left marginalization pattern explains which left-wing candidates succeed (moderate social democrats) and which fail (doctrinaire Marxists) — a necessary refinement of the left-turn thesis.
