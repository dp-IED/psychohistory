# Regional Third-Way Squeeze Under Polarization

## Definition

A dynamic in which a centrist or "third way" party with a regional power base is squeezed to irrelevance as the national electorate polarizes into two dominant blocs. The regional third-way party cannot scale its local strength to national competitiveness because polarization drives voters away from ambiguous centrist positions toward clearer ideological poles.

Key characteristics:
- The third-way party has a **strong regional base** (typically a province or autonomous community) but <10% national vote share
- The national political landscape is **bipolar** between a right-wing populist and a left/center-left successor faction
- Voters who lean toward the third-way's **ideological pole** defect to the dominant party on that side rather than "waste" their vote on a minor centrist alternative
- The squeeze is **asymmetric**: the third-way loses more voters to the populist right (if the third-way is center-right) or to the populist left (if center-left), depending on which pole is closer to their ideological position
- The squeeze **accelerates** over successive elections as the dominant blocs consolidate

## Canonical Examples

### Primero País / HNP in Argentina 2023-2025 (Primary Example)
Juan Schiaretti's Hacemos por Nuestro País (rebranded as Primero País for 2025) was a Peronist dissident coalition with a strong base in Córdoba province, where Schiaretti had been governor for 8 years. In the 2023 presidential race, Schiaretti won 6.73% nationally. In the 2025 legislative election, Primero País won 7.73% and 8 seats — a 67% decline from the 24 seats the broader Peronist-aligned coalition had held previously.

The squeeze mechanism: voters who wanted "Peronism but not Kirchnerism" faced a choice between Schiaretti's weak regional party and Milei's ascendant libertarian movement. Most defected to Milei because: (1) Milei was the only viable alternative to Kicillof's Peronism, and (2) voting for Schiaretti was seen as a wasted vote in a polarized contest. ### Spain 2015-2023 (Strong Parallel)
Ciudadanos (Citizens, Cs) was a centrist liberal party that emerged from Catalonia with a reformist, anti-independence platform. In 2015 it won 14% nationally and 40 seats — a credible centrist alternative. But as Spain polarized between PSOE (center-left) and PP/Vox (center-right), Cs was squeezed to 6.8% in 2019 and then collapsed to 0 seats in 2023. Voters who wanted a moderate anti-independence party defected to PP as the only viable right-of-center option, while former Cs voters who leaned left migrated to PSOE.

### France 2017-2024 (Weaker Partial Example)
En Marche!/Renaissance (Macron's party) is itself a "third way" — but it was created from the presidency downward, not from a regional base upward. The closer parallel is the centrist MoDem or smaller regional parties. However, Macron's success demonstrates that **presidential third ways** can survive polarization (by occupying the presidency and using executive power) even as **legislative third ways** are squeezed.

### Brazil 2018-2026 (Emerging Pattern)
The "Centrão" (a loose bloc of centrist parties traditionally used as coalition partners) has been squeezed as Brazil polarizes between Lula's PT and Bolsonaro's PL. Centrist parties like MDB, PSDB, and União Brasil have seen their vote shares decline in presidential elections even as they remain relevant in Congress through coalition bargaining — a variation where the squeeze is weaker due to Brazil's open-list proportional representation system and the Centrão's institutional entrenchment.

## Pattern Archetype

### Structural Dynamics

1. **Regional entrenchment, national weakness**: The party has deep local roots (governors, mayors, patronage networks) but cannot translate these into national legislative dominance. The regional base is a ceiling, not a floor.

2. **Polarization as voting heuristic**: When voters perceive a high-stakes choice between two incompatible blocs, they abandon "wasted vote" centrist options. The third-way party becomes a protest vote that doesn't matter.

3. **Asymmetric defection**: The third-way loses more votes to whichever pole is ideologically adjacent. A center-right third way (Schiaretti, Ciudadanos) loses more to the right-wing populist than to the left bloc. A center-left third way loses more to the mainstream left.

4. **Irreversibility**: Once squeezed, the third way cannot easily re-establish itself. The defected voters have realigned their partisan identity to the dominant bloc. The third way either collapses entirely or survives as a purely regional force with no national ambition.

### Indicators to Watch For

1. **Regional vote share vs national vote share**: A ratio >3:1 (e.g., 30% in home region vs 8% nationally) signals a regional party that cannot scale.
2. **Bipolar support gap**: When the combined support of the two dominant blocs exceeds 70% and is growing, the third-way squeeze is accelerating.
3. **Effective number of parties declining**: A trend toward two-party competition (measured by the effective number of electoral parties) strongly predicts third-way attrition.
4. **Wasted-vote narrative**: Media coverage that says "a vote for [third party] is a vote for [opposite pole]" is both a symptom and a cause of the squeeze.
5. **Ideological proximity**: The closer the third-way is ideologically to one of the dominant blocs, the faster the defection.

### When the Pattern Fails

- When the third-way party **occupies the presidency** (executive power sustains visibility and relevance)
- When the **electoral system is highly proportional** (MDB/PSDB in Brazil survive as legislative forces despite presidential irrelevance)
- When the third-way party has a **constitutional/territorial lock** (Basque or Catalan parties in Spain survive because they serve a distinct nationalist constituency, not an ideological one)
- When the **dominant blocs collapse simultaneously** (rare — requires an economic catastrophe that discredits both sides)

## Timing Calibration

The regional third-way squeeze typically manifests over **1-2 election cycles** after polarization consolidates. In Argentina, it took one cycle (2023→2025). In Spain, Ciudadanos survived from 2015→2019→2023 through three cycles, but the squeeze was visible by the second.

**Key rule**: If a third-way regional party won >10% nationally in the previous election but the national vote is now polarized between two blocs, forecast a 40-60% decline in their seat share in the next election.

## Forecasting Application

When a forecast question asks whether a regional/devolved/third-way party can win a national plurality:

1. **Check the party's national vote share history**: If it has never exceeded 15% nationally, a plurality is structurally impossible without a prior collapse of both dominant blocs.
2. **Check the polarization trend**: Is the effective number of parties declining? Are the two leading blocs consolidating?
3. **Check the party's ideological position**: Is it between the two blocs (worst position), aligned with one bloc (moderate squeeze), or representing a territorial identity rather than an ideological position (best survival chance)?
4. **Apply the squeeze rule**: A centrist third-way party in a bipolarizing system will lose 30-60% of its national vote share over 1-2 cycles and cannot reach >20% nationally under polarization.
5. **For "most seats" questions**: The third-way cannot be the largest bloc unless its vote share plus the squeeze defectors to each side would still exceed 25% of seats — an extremely rare scenario.

## Relationship to Other Concepts
