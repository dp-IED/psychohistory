# Diosdado Cabello

## Summary

Diosdado Cabello Rondón is a Venezuelan politician and former military officer who is the leader of the **hardliner faction** within the [[psuv|PSUV]] — the faction that favors repression, electoral fraud, and military enforcement over negotiated agreements with the opposition. He has served as President of the PSUV (since 2021), Minister of Interior, Justice, and Peace, and previously as President of the National Assembly (2012-2016). A former army lieutenant, Cabello was a close associate of Hugo Chávez and has been widely reported to be the most powerful figure in the regime after Maduro — with direct control over the security apparatus and state intelligence.

Cabello represents the structural obstacle to any democratic opening in Venezuela. His faction benefits from the status quo of sanctions, isolation, and military control. Negotiated transitions (like the Barbados Agreement) threaten his power base. His influence rises when the regime needs security force loyalty — meaning his ascendance is usually a negative signal for opposition prospects.

## Significance for Forecasting

- Cabello's public prominence is a **negative signal** for opposition prospects. When Cabello is highly visible, the regime is in hardline mode — fraud, repression, and military enforcement are the preferred tools. When Cabello is less visible (or publicly sidelined), the negotiator faction has room to maneuver.
- As the PSUV's formal party president, Cabello controls party discipline, candidate selection, and the internal party machinery that enforces loyalty. His power is independent of Maduro's — he commands loyalty from the party's base and the military's mid-level officer corps.
- Cabello's past military service (and rumored connections to organized crime, drug trafficking investigations by US authorities) gives him leverage over military commanders that Maduro cannot easily override.
- His relationship with Maduro is best described as a **power-sharing arrangement** rather than subordination. Cabello controls the party and security forces; Maduro controls the state budget, international diplomacy, and the formal chain of command. Forecasting regime behavior requires assessing which of these two power centers is ascendant.

## Timeline

- **1990s**: Military officer under Chávez's 1992 coup attempt; imprisoned alongside Chávez.
- **2002**: Briefly serves as interim president after Chávez's brief ouster during the April 2002 coup attempt — claims he helped restore Chávez to power.
- **2004-2008**: Minister of Infrastructure and Housing.
- **2012-2016**: President of the National Assembly.
- **2016-2021**: Minister of Interior; authorizes security force crackdowns on 2014 and 2017 protests.
- **2021-present**: President of the PSUV (formal party leader); oversees candidate selection and party discipline.
- **2023-2024**: Opposes Barbados Agreement from within the party; leads hardliner pushback after election-day fraud; directs post-election crackdown and arrest warrants.
- **2025**: Leads PSUV campaign for May 2025 parliamentary election (opposition boycotts); PSUV consolidates institutional control.
