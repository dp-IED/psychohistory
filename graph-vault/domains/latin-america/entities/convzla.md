# ConVzla (Parallel Vote Tabulation)

## Summary

ConVzla (a portmanteau of "Con" — with — and "Vzla" — Venezuela) was the parallel vote tabulation (PVT) operation of the Venezuelan opposition coalition [[plataforma-unitaria|PUD]] for the 2024 presidential election. It was the single most critical piece of opposition infrastructure that enabled the opposition to document its electoral victory despite the regime-controlled CNE's refusal to release official tallies.

ConVzla collected official tally sheets (actas) from polling stations across Venezuela on election day (July 28, 2024) and published a detailed, station-by-station tabulation showing [[edmundo-gonzalez|Edmundo González]] won with approximately 67% of the vote to [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]]'s 30%. The operation covered 81% of polling centers — a coverage rate that made the results statistically irrefutable.

## Organizational Structure

ConVzla was organized by the PUD's technical electoral team under the direction of the opposition campaign. It deployed approximately 90,000 witnesses to polling stations across the country to collect copies of the tally sheets that the CNE's automated voting system produces at each station at the end of voting. These tally sheets are physically printed and signed by party witnesses — they are the authentic, verifiable record of the vote. The CNE's central aggregation system (which produced the fabricated result) could be manipulated, but the individual station tally sheets were genuine documents that the opposition could collect and aggregate independently.

## Significance for Forecasting

- ConVzla is the canonical case of opposition PVT infrastructure as a forecasting variable in authoritarian elections. The existence or absence of such infrastructure is the single strongest predictor of whether the opposition can prove its victory if it wins the vote.
- For any future authoritarian election question, the first check should be: does the opposition have a PVT operation comparable to ConVzla? If yes, the opposition can document its victory regardless of official results. If no, the regime's official count faces no credible contradiction.
- ConVzla's 81% coverage rate meant the opposition's count was statistically robust — sampling error was negligible. PVT operations with lower coverage rates (e.g., <50%) are weaker evidence because the regime can claim incomplete data.

## Key Data

- **Coverage**: 81% of polling centers (approximately 30,000+ of 37,000 actas collected)
- **Result per ConVzla**: González 67%, Maduro 30%, other candidates 3%
- **Official CNE result**: Maduro 51.2%, González 44.2% (no tally sheets ever released)
- **International verification**: Carter Center, UN panel, and OAS all rejected CNE results and recognized ConVzla as credible
- **Statistical signature**: CNE figures (51.20000%, 44.20000%) were widely noted as fabricated due to implausible precision
