# María Corina Machado

## Summary

María Corina Machado is a Venezuelan opposition politician and former National Assembly deputy who emerged as the leading opposition figure in the lead-up to the 2024 presidential election. A pro-market, anti-Chavista candidate, Machado won a landslide victory in the opposition's October 2023 primary with over 92% of the vote (2+ million participants).

In June 2023, the Maduro-controlled Comptroller General's office barred Machado from holding public office for 15 years — a move widely condemned internationally. Despite the ban, Machado maintained overwhelming support and continued campaigning, ultimately endorsing [[edmundo-gonzalez|Edmundo González]] as her replacement candidate after the PUD selected him in April 2024.

Machado's role was crucial: her endorsement transferred her grassroots support to González, enabling a low-name-recognition diplomat to become a credible challenger. She campaigned actively for González despite being unable to appear on the ballot. After the election, she went into hiding in Caracas to avoid arrest.

## Significance for Forecasting

- Machado's case illustrates the "two-track opposition" dynamic in authoritarian elections: a barred but popular leader endorses a consensus candidate, transferring electoral support without requiring the candidate to build their own name recognition.
- Her decision to stay in Venezuela after the election rather than exile prevented the opposition from losing its public face.
- The barring strategy backfired — it made Machado a symbol of resistance and motivated opposition turnout.

## Timeline

- **2023 June**: Barred from holding office by Comptroller General.
- **2023 October**: Wins opposition primary with 92%.
- **2024 April**: Endorses González after PUD selection.
- **2024 July 28**: Election; Machado votes and urges supporters to defend the vote.
- **2024 September**: Goes into hiding; arrest warrant rumored.
