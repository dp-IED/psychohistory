# Supreme Tribunal of Justice of Venezuela (TSJ)

## Summary

The Supreme Tribunal of Justice (Spanish: Tribunal Supremo de Justicia, TSJ) is the highest judicial body in Venezuela. Under the Maduro regime, the TSJ has been captured by the executive and serves as a judicial ratification mechanism for the regime's political actions, including validating disputed election results, stripping the opposition-controlled National Assembly of its powers, and issuing arrest warrants against opposition figures.

The TSJ played a critical role in the 2024 electoral fraud: on August 22, 2024, it "validated" the CNE's fabricated results showing Maduro's victory — a decision widely anticipated given the TSJ's composition (packed with Maduro loyalists after the 2015 opposition National Assembly victory). The TSJ also ordered the CNE to produce tally sheets within 3 days (which the CNE never did), but validated the results anyway despite the absence of supporting documentation.

The TSJ issued an arrest warrant for Edmundo Gonzalez on September 2, 2024, on charges of usurpation of functions, falsification of documents, conspiracy, and criminal association — effectively forcing Gonzalez into exile.

## Significance for Forecasting

- The TSJ exemplifies the "judicial ratification" mechanism in the [[concepts/authoritarian-electoral-facade]] pattern — a captured supreme court that provides legal cover for the regime's political decisions.
- For forecasting authoritarian election dynamics: the existence of a captured supreme court means the regime has a judicial escape hatch for any electoral outcome. The regime can lose the vote but still "win" legally through judicial validation.
- The TSJ's pre-announced validation of the 2024 results (widely expected before it happened) demonstrates that judicial ratification in authoritarian contexts is a performative act rather than a genuine legal review.

## Timeline

- **1999**: TSJ established under the new Bolivarian Constitution.
- **2004-2015**: Gradual packing of TSJ with Chavez/Maduro loyalists through judicial reforms that expand the court's size and change appointment procedures.
- **2015-2017**: After the opposition wins a 2/3 supermajority in the National Assembly, the TSJ strips the Assembly of its powers, declaring it "in contempt" and ruling its legislation void.
- **2017**: TSJ attempts to dissolve the opposition-controlled National Assembly and assume its legislative powers (reversed after international outcry).
- **2018**: TSJ validates Maduro's re-election in the boycoted election.
- **2024 August 22**: TSJ validates the CNE's disputed 2024 election results, as anticipated by independent observers.
- **2024 September 2**: TSJ issues arrest warrant for Edmundo Gonzalez, forcing him into exile.
