# Carter Center

The Carter Center is a non-governmental organization founded by former US President Jimmy Carter in 1982, focused on peace and health initiatives worldwide. In the context of Venezuelan election forecasting, the Carter Center's significance lies in its role as a credible independent election observer — it deployed a mission to the 2024 Venezuelan presidential election and its rejection of the official CNE results was a critical part of the international consensus that [[edmundo-gonzalez|Edmundo González]] actually won the vote.

## Role in Venezuela 2024 Election

The Carter Center was one of the independent observer organizations invited under the [[barbados-agreement-2023|Barbados Agreement]] to monitor the July 28, 2024 presidential election. Alongside the UN and the OAS, it deployed election observers to Venezuela.

On July 29-30, 2024, the Carter Center publicly rejected the [[cne-venezuela|CNE]]'s official results (which claimed Maduro won 51.2%) and stated that the election had been "fundamentally undemocratic" and that the CNE had failed to provide any credible tally sheets to support its announced results. The Carter Center's statement was particularly significant because:

1. It had been one of the few international organizations trusted by both the regime and the opposition.
2. Its technical observers confirmed that the opposition's ConVzla parallel count — showing González with ~67% — was methodologically sound.
3. Its rejection of the official results contributed to the broad international non-recognition of Maduro's claim.

## Forecasting Significance

- The presence or absence of credible international election observers is a material variable for forecasting authoritarian election outcomes. Observers can validate opposition claims of victory and delegitimize regime fraud.
- However, observers' ability to operate effectively depends on regime cooperation. The regime invited observers under the Barbados Agreement but restricted their access.
- The Carter Center's role in Venezuela 2024 demonstrates that even limited observation missions can provide authoritative validation of opposition evidence — but only when the opposition has its own independent documentation (ConVzla) for the observers to verify.
- For future forecasts involving authoritarian elections: check whether credible observer organizations (Carter Center, EU, OAS, UN) have been invited and have deployed. If observer reports align with opposition PVT data, the evidence of fraud is substantially stronger.
