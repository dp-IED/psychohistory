# National Action Party (PAN)

## Summary
The National Action Party (PAN) is a center-right conservative political party in Mexico, one of the three main parties of the pre-MORENA era. It held the presidency from 2000-2012 (Vicente Fox, Felipe Calderón) — the first non-PRI presidents in 71 years. By 2024, PAN had been reduced to the second-largest opposition party behind MORENA.

## Significance for Forecasting
PAN's decline is a leading indicator of Mexico's dominant-party realignment under MORENA. PAN's brand is historically pro-business and socially conservative, but it has been unable to compete with MORENA's populist welfare agenda. PAN formed the "Va por México" (later "Fuerza y Corazón por México") coalition with the PRI and PRD for the 2024 election — an ideologically incoherent alliance that failed to generate a compelling alternative.

## Key Data
- Presidential vote share trajectory: 53% (2000 Fox) → 48% (2006 Calderón) → 38% (2012 Vázquez Mota) → 22% (2018 Anaya) → coalition candidate Gálvez ~28% (2024)
- Party identification: Declined from ~25% (2012) to ~15% (2024)
