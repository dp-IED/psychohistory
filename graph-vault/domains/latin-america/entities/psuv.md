# United Socialist Party of Venezuela (PSUV)

## Summary

The United Socialist Party of Venezuela (Spanish: Partido Socialista Unido de Venezuela, PSUV) is the ruling political party of Venezuela, founded in 2007 by Hugo Chávez through a merger of Chávez's Fifth Republic Movement (MVR) and several smaller left-wing parties. As the dominant political organization in Venezuela under Chávez (2007-2013) and Maduro (2013-present), the PSUV controls the executive, the National Assembly (since 2020), the judiciary (TSJ), the electoral authority (CNE), and the military.

The PSUV operates as a classic dominant party in the PRI (Mexico) / KMT (Taiwan-historical) / FSLN (Nicaragua) mold — it controls the state apparatus, distributes patronage through social programs (Misiones), and uses institutional capture to maintain power. Unlike PRI in its heyday, the PSUV has never faced a genuine alternation in power; its control over the CNE and TSJ means it can fabricate electoral outcomes when the opposition threatens to win.

## Internal Structure

- **Leader**: [[nicolas-maduro|Nicolás Maduro]] (First Vice President of the party, effectively the leader since Chávez's death)
- **Founder**: Hugo Chávez (2007-2013)
- **Key factions**:
  - *Hardliners/Duros*: Military-aligned faction favoring repression over negotiation ([[diosdado-cabello|Diosdado Cabello]], Vladimir Padrino López)
  - *Negotiators/Pragmatists*: Faction favoring tactical negotiations with the opposition to manage international pressure ([[jorge-rodriguez|Jorge Rodríguez]], [[delcy-rodriguez|Delcy Rodríguez]])
- **Party organization**: Vertical command structure with Chavista collective leadership, disciplined legislative bloc, and penetration of state institutions

## Significance for Forecasting

- The PSUV's institutional capture (CNE, TSJ, military) is the primary mechanism enabling Maduro's survival despite economic collapse and electoral defeat. Forecasting Venezuelan political outcomes requires understanding PSUV's institutional control depth.
- The hardliner-negotiator factional divide within PSUV is material for forecasting regime survival and negotiation windows. When negotiators are ascendant (as during the [[barbados-agreement-2023|Barbados Agreement]] process), international pressure can produce tactical openings. When hardliners are ascendant, repression and fraud intensify.
- Succession dynamics within PSUV (who follows Maduro) is an active forecasting domain with high financial and geopolitical consequences.

## Timeline

- **2007**: PSUV founded by Chávez via merger of MVR and allied left-wing parties.
- **2010**: PSUV wins parliamentary election (98 of 165 seats).
- **2012**: Chávez re-elected; PSUV controls all branches of government.
- **2013**: Chávez dies; Maduro wins snap election as PSUV candidate by 1.5% margin.
- **2015**: PSUV loses National Assembly to opposition (55 of 167 seats). TSJ strips Assembly of powers.
- **2017**: PSUV-controlled Constituent Assembly bypasses National Assembly entirely.
- **2018**: Maduro "re-elected" in boycotted election; PSUV consolidates one-party control.
- **2020**: PSUV and allies win parliamentary election boycotted by opposition (256 of 277 seats).
- **2024**: PSUV candidate Maduro claims victory in presidential election; opposition parallel count shows González won. PSUV-controlled TSJ validates fabricated results.
- **2025**: PSUV wins parliamentary election boycotted by opposition, consolidating institutional control.
