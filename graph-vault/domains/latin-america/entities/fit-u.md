# Frente de Izquierda y de los Trabajadores - Unidad (FIT-U)

## Summary

FIT-U (Frente de Izquierda y de los Trabajadores - Unidad) is a Trotskyist-left coalition in Argentina, formed in 2011 as the FIT (Frente de Izquierda y de los Trabajadores) and later adding "Unidad" to its name. It is a coalition of the Workers' Party (Partido Obrero), the Socialist Workers' Party (PTS), and the Socialist Left (IS), with Nicolás del Caño (Partido Obrero) as its leading figure and presidential candidate.

FIT-U is structurally incapable of winning national legislative pluralities in Argentina. Its historical vote share ranges from 2-5% nationally, with isolated provincial peaks of 8-10% in its strongest districts (Mendoza, Salta, Jujuy). This ceiling is structural: Argentina's political landscape is dominated by the Peronist-anti-Peronist cleavage, and the far left has no path to expand beyond its activist and union base.

## Historical Electoral Performance

- **2013 legislative**: ~3.7% nationally, 3 seats
- **2015 presidential**: Nicolás del Caño, 1.8%
- **2017 legislative**: ~4.0% nationally, 2 seats (split from original FIT)
- **2019 presidential**: Nicolás del Caño, 2.8%
- **2021 legislative**: ~4.5% nationally, 4 seats (as FIT-U)
- **2023 presidential**: No credible run; focused on legislative races
- **2025 legislative**: 3.90% nationally, 3 seats (-1). Squeezed by polarization between LLA (Milei) and Fuerza Patria (Kicillof).

## Structural Analysis

FIT-U's 3-5% ceiling has remained stable across a decade of wildly different political environments (Mauricio Macri's center-right government, Alberto Fernández's Peronist government, Javier Milei's libertarian government). This suggests the ceiling is not a function of any specific government's performance but of Argentina's two-bloc party system:

1. **Trotskyist ideology limits coalition partners**: FIT-U's commitment to revolutionary socialism prevents it from forming electoral alliances with center-left or Peronist forces, which limits its scaling potential.
2. **Union base is shrinking**: Argentina's formal labor sector has declined from ~50% of employment to ~33%, eroding the traditional far-left's main constituency.
3. **Populist competition**: Both Milei (right-populist) and Peronist factions (left-populist) compete for anti-establishment voters more effectively than a doctrinaire Trotskyist party can.
4. **No regional stronghold**: Unlike HNP/Primero País (Córdoba) or provincial parties, FIT-U has no territorial base from which to build. Its support is diffuse and activist-driven.

## Relationship to Broader Patterns

FIT-U's 3.9% in 2025 is a textbook example of [[far-left-marginalization-polarization]]: when a right-wing populist and a moderate-left successor faction polarize the electorate, the far left is squeezed to single digits. In the 2025 contest, Milei voters who might otherwise support a left-wing anti-establishment party had no reason to split their vote, and Kicillof voters who wanted a more social-democratic policy saw FIT-U as unelectable.
