# Cuba

Republic of Cuba — Caribbean island nation, one-party communist state under the Cuban Communist Party (PCC). Single-party system since 1959 revolution.

## Political System

- **Current leader**: Miguel Díaz-Canel (President since 2018, First Secretary of PCC since 2021)
- **Predecessor**: Raúl Castro (President 2008-2018, First Secretary 2011-2021)
- **Fidel Castro**: Revolutionary leader, Prime Minister/President 1959-2008, died 2016
- **Raúl Castro**: Born June 3, 1931 (age 95 in 2026). Retired from formal leadership in 2021 but retains influence as elder statesman of the revolution
- **System**: Single-party communist state, PCC is the only legal political party
- **National Assembly**: 605-member unicameral legislature, rubber-stamp body

## US-Cuba Relations

- **Embargo**: US trade embargo in place since 1962, codified by Helms-Burton Act (1996)
- **Diplomatic thaw**: Obama restored diplomatic relations (2014-2016), removed Cuba from SST list
- **Re-freeze**: Trump first term (2017-2021) reimposed travel restrictions, re-added Cuba to SST list, Title III Helms-Burton lawsuits permitted
- **Biden**: Limited rollback — some remittance/flight easing, but kept Cuba on SST list; no ambassador appointed
- **Trump second term (2025-present)**: No major Cuba policy shifts documented. Focus has been on Middle East peace brokerage, NATO burden-sharing, tariff policy. No Cuba-specific executive orders announced.
- **Cuban Adjustment Act (1966)**: Grants special immigration status to Cuban nationals — any Cuban who reaches US soil is eligible for permanent residency after 1 year

## Regime Stability Assessment (June 2026)

The Cuban regime has survived 67 years (since 1959) through: collapse of the Soviet Union (1991, loss of $4-6B annual subsidies), the Special Period (1990s economic depression), multiple US administrations attempting to isolate it, Fidel Castro's death (2016), and the COVID-19 pandemic. The PCC's monopoly on power is enforced through state security (MININT), the Committees for the Defense of the Revolution (CDRs, neighborhood-level surveillance), and control over all legal economic activity.

### Current Pressure Points
- **Economic crisis**: Severe shortages of food, medicine, fuel since 2021, exacerbated by post-COVID tourism collapse and reduced Venezuelan oil subsidies (PDVSA production decline limiting Cuba support)
- **Emigration wave**: Largest exodus since Mariel boatlift (1980), ~500K+ Cubans leaving since 2022, primarily through Nicaragua and US border — acts as pressure valve reducing domestic revolutionary pressure
- **Aging leadership**: Díaz-Canel (born 1960) is first non-Castro leader since 1959 but lacks revolutionary legitimacy of Castro brothers
- **July 2021 protests**: Largest since 1994 (50+ cities); regime responded with 1,000+ arrests, internet blackouts, show trials — demonstrating willingness to suppress
- **March 2024 protests**: Smaller but persistent

### Why Regime Survival Remains Likely for 2026
1. **No organized opposition**: PCC is the only legal party; dissidents systematically monitored, imprisoned, or exiled
2. **Security apparatus loyalty**: MININT and FAR remain under PCC control with no history of factional splits
3. **No external intervention scenario**: US focused on Iran peace implementation, Colombia election, midterms — no appetite for Cuba intervention
4. **Regime-change base rates**: Soviet Union 74 years, China 75+, North Korea 75+, Vietnam 48+ — Cuba at 67 is within durable range
5. **Emigration pressure valve**: Discontented Cubans leave rather than organize domestically

## Key Facts for Raúl Castro Custody Question

- Raúl Castro is 95 years old (born 1931), retired from all formal positions in 2021
- He is not a fugitive — no US warrant, no Interpol notice, no extradition request
- The US does not have an extradition treaty with Cuba
- Any "custody" scenario would require one of:
  1. Raúl leaving Cuba voluntarily and traveling to a third country that would extradite him to the US (extremely unlikely — no US charges, and he has no reason to leave Cuba at 95)
  2. US military/naval action extracting him from Cuba (would be an act of war; no precedent for extracting a retired 95-year-old former leader)
  3. Regime change in Cuba resulting in his arrest and transfer to US custody (no active regime-change scenario)
  4. Voluntary surrender to US authorities for unknown reasons (no incentive)
