# Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI)

## Summary
The Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI) governed Mexico continuously for 71 years (1929-2000) under various names, making it one of the longest-ruling parties in modern history. After losing the presidency in 2000, the PRI returned under Enrique Peña Nieto (2012-2018) but collapsed to a historic low of 14% in 2018. By 2024, the PRI was a junior partner in the PAN-led opposition coalition, polling at ~10% party identification.

## Significance for Forecasting
The PRI's collapse and inability to recover is the clearest indicator of Mexico's realignment from a two-party (PRI-PAN) to a dominant-party (MORENA) system. The PRI brand carries the legacy of 71 years of one-party rule, but also the Peña Nieto corruption scandals, making it a liability for any coalition it joins. The PRI in coalition with PAN (historically its fiercest rival) signals the opposition's desperation and organizational weakness.

## Key Data
- Presidential vote share: 48% (2012 Peña Nieto) → 14% (2018 Meade) → coalition (2024)
- Party identification: Declined from ~30% (2012) to ~10% (2024)
- State governorships: Collapsed from 22 (2012) to 2 (2024)
