# Barbados Agreement (2023)

## Summary

The Barbados Agreement (also known as the Electoral Roadmap Agreement) was signed on October 17, 2023, between the [[nicolas-maduro|Maduro]] regime and the [[plataforma-unitaria|Unitary Democratic Platform (PUD)]] opposition coalition, with facilitation by Norway and witnessed by the United States, the Netherlands, and other international parties. It established the framework for the 2024 Venezuelan presidential election.

The agreement was a classic mediated electoral framework: the regime agreed to allow international observation, reduce barriers to opposition candidate registration, and respect the election outcome; in exchange, the US agreed to ease oil and gas sanctions on Venezuela. The agreement was the result of months of negotiations in Barbados (hence the name), following earlier talks that had collapsed in 2021-2022.

## Key Provisions

1. **Mutual recognition**: Both parties agreed to compete in the 2024 presidential election under the existing legal framework.
2. **Candidate eligibility**: The regime agreed to allow all candidates to register and compete, including those previously barred — a provision the regime immediately violated by maintaining [[maria-corina-machado|María Corina Machado]]'s disqualification.
3. **International observation**: The regime agreed to invite international observers (EU, UN, Carter Center) — a provision the regime partially violated by delaying invitations and restricting observers.
4. **Vote count respect**: Both parties agreed to respect the election outcome — the regime violated this on July 29, 2024, by fabricating results.
5. **Sanctions relief**: The US issued a 6-month general license (October 18, 2023) temporarily lifting oil/gas sanctions on Venezuela — the regime's primary incentive for signing.

## Collapse

The agreement began unraveling almost immediately. The regime's TSJ upheld Machado's disqualification in January 2024, violating the candidate eligibility provision. The US reinstated sanctions in April 2024 after Maduro failed to lift the ban. However, the opposition continued to participate in the election under the agreement's framework, using the legal cover of the "roadmap" to register González and collect parallel tallies. The agreement failed to produce a democratic power transition but succeeded in providing the opposition sufficient legal space to contest the election and document its victory.

## Significance for Forecasting

- The Barbados Agreement is a canonical case of a **partial compliance** international-brokered electoral framework. The regime signed to get sanctions relief and then violated key provisions once relief was granted. The opposition participated anyway because the electoral framework — however flawed — was preferable to a boycott that would cede the field entirely.
- For forecasting authoritarian election dynamics: when a regime signs an electoral framework agreement, assess enforcement mechanisms. If the agreement is backed by sanctions relief (carrot) but has no strong response mechanism for violations (stick), the regime will likely violate provisions it considers existential threats (e.g., allowing a popular opposition candidate to run).
- The agreement demonstrated that even a violated electoral framework can provide the opposition sufficient space to campaign and build PVT infrastructure — the most important outcome was that the PUD could organize [[convzla]] under the agreement's legal cover.

## Timeline

- **2023 October**: Agreement signed in Barbados; Norway facilitates; US issues 6-month sanctions relief license.
- **2023 November-December**: Opposition holds primary (October 22); Machado wins 92% of vote.
- **2024 January**: TSJ upholds Machado's disqualification — first major violation.
- **2024 March**: PUD registers Edmundo González as consensus replacement candidate after Machado blocked.
- **2024 April**: US reinstates oil sanctions, citing regime violations of Barbados Agreement.
- **2024 July 28**: Election held; regime fabricates results on July 29 — terminal violation.
- **2024 August**: TSJ "validates" fabricated results despite absence of supporting tally sheets.
