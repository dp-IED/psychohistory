# National Liberation Army (ELN)

Ejército de Liberación Nacional (ELN) — Colombia's largest remaining left-wing guerrilla group. Founded in 1964 by radicalized Catholic students and intellectuals. Marxist-Leninist ideology with strong Catholic Liberation Theology roots.

## Operations

- **Estimated strength**: 3,000-5,000 combatants across Colombia and Venezuela border regions
- **Economic base**: Drug trafficking (cocaine), illegal mining, extortion, kidnapping for ransom, oil pipeline attacks
- **Areas of control**: Catatumbo region (Norte de Santander), Arauca, Chocó, Pacific coast. Significant presence in Venezuelan border states (Apure, Zulia, Táchira) with Maduro regime tolerance.

## Peace Process

- **Petro administration**: Active peace negotiations since 2022. Bilateral ceasefire agreed (Aug 2023-Apr 2024). Negotiations continued but stalled after ceasefire collapsed.
- **Cuba talks**: Peace talks relocated to Cuba after Colombia-Venezuela border issues complicated in-country negotiations.
- **Dissident risk**: Moderates within ELN favor negotiated settlement; hardliners prefer continued insurgency. Any peace deal risks ELN fracturing like FARC did in 2016.

## Forecasting Relevance

- **US strike scenario**: ELN operates in remote areas and has rarely targeted US diplomatic or military personnel (unlike FARC in the 1990s). Lower strike trigger probability than Clan del Golfo.
- **Colombia post-2026**: The incoming government's approach to ELN peace talks will be a key policy differentiator. Cepeda (Pacto Histórico) would continue negotiations. De la Espriella (populist right) would take a hardline military approach.
- **Venezuela border dynamics**: ELN's safe haven in Venezuela creates a transnational dimension that could draw US attention if ELN attacks Colombian infrastructure with cross-border logistical support.
