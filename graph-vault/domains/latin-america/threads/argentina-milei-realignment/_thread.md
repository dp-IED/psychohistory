# Argentina Milei Realignment

## Overview

Javier Milei, a libertarian economist and former TV pundit, won the Argentine presidency in November 2023 with 56% of the vote, defeating Peronist Sergio Massa. His victory represented a dramatic realignment of Argentine politics — a country historically dominated by Peronism (center-left) and radical civic unionism (center-right). Milei's La Libertad Avanza (LLA) coalition went from a minor force to the dominant party in just a few years.

The 2025 legislative election (October 26, 2025) for 127 of 257 Chamber of Deputies seats served as a midterm verdict on Milei's governance. LLA won 40.66% of the vote and 64 seats (+54 relative to the previous election), establishing it as the largest bloc. The Peronist successor Fuerza Patria (Axel Kicillof) won 33.70% and 47 seats. The far-left FIT-U coalition won 3.90% and 3 seats — far from contention.

## Key Dynamics

1. **Populist coattail effect**: Milei's presidential victory created a political realignment that took 2 years to fully manifest in legislative terms. His party went from 10 seats (pre-2023) to 64 in 2025 — a +54 seat gain driven entirely by presidential coattails, not local organization.

2. **Peronist fragmentation**: The traditional Peronist vote fragmented. Fuerza Patria (Kicillof) won 47 seats, down only 1 seat, but this masked the broader collapse of the Peronist coalition. Primero País (Schiaretti) lost 16 seats, reflecting the disintegration of the broader Peronist umbrella.

|3. **Far-left irrelevance**: FIT-U's 3.90% and 3 seats (-1) demonstrate that far-left Trotskyist politics have no path to power in Argentina's current realignment. In a two-way contest between Milei's libertarianism and a reorganized Peronism, the far left is squeezed to single digits. This pattern matches the [[far-left-marginalization-polarization]] concept observed across multiple polarized democracies.

### Regional Peronist Collapse: HNP/Primero País

|4. **Regional Peronist collapse (HNP/Primero País)**: Juan Schiaretti's Hacemos por Nuestro País/Primero País coalition fell from 24 seats (2021-2023) to 8 seats (2025) — a 67% decline. This was not survivable for a Peronist dissident whose entire organizational structure relied on one province (Córdoba). In a polarized national contest between Milei and a Kirchnerist successor, regional Peronist alternatives cannot scale up. Voters who want "Peronism but not Kirchnerism" defect to Milei rather than to a weak third party. This pattern — **regional dissident squeezed by polarization** — has implications for other countries with fragmented party systems (Spain, France, Brazil).

HNP/PPA was founded in 2023, contested the presidential race (Schiaretti, 6.73%), rebranded as Primero País for the 2025 legislative election (7.73%, 8 seats), and dissolved on 26 October 2025 after the election. The coalition illustrates a structurally-limited political strategy: a regional power base cannot become a national "third way" alternative when the electorate is polarized between two dominant forces. See [[hacemos-por-nuestro-pais]] for the full entity stub.

|5. **International alignment**: Milei's foreign alignment with Trump (inauguration attendance, shared anti-establishment positioning) and his pro-Israel/pro-US stance positioned Argentina firmly in the Western conservative camp — a dramatic reversal from the Peronist era's non-aligned posture.

## Forecasting Significance

The Milei realignment demonstrates several patterns that may recur elsewhere:

1. **Populist outsider → legislative majority in 2 elections**: An anti-system president who enters office with a weak legislative position can, through a combination of governance wins (inflation reduction), attrition of traditional parties, and presidential coattails, achieve legislative dominance in the next election cycle.

2. **Far-left irrelevance under populist polarization**: When a right-wing populist and a moderate-left successor faction polarize the electorate, the far left gets squeezed to marginal vote shares (3-4%). This pattern was observed in the US (DSA/left fringe under Trump-Biden polarization) and now Argentina.

3. **Populist durability through austerity**: Milei's ability to survive politically despite implementing severe austerity (50% devaluation, spending cuts) suggests that voters who elect a radical reformer have higher tolerance for short-term pain than traditional parties' voters would.

## Presidential Survival Assessment (2025)

This thread is also relevant to questions about Milei's tenure security — whether he would leave office before his term ends in 2027. The question "Milei out as President of Argentina in 2025?" was correctly forecast NO, and the vault's coverage supported this assessment through the following reasoning chain:

### Why Milei Survived 2025 Despite Scandals

1. **No viable removal pathway reached its threshold**:
   - **Impeachment**: Requires 2/3 of both chambers. LLA had only 10/257 seats (pre-Oct) and 64/257 (post-Oct). Even combined with allied blocs, no opposition supermajority existed in either the Chamber or Senate. The $LIBRA impeachment calls died in the Impeachment Commission.
   - **Resignation**: Milei's approval never fell below 32% (Sep 2025 low). Historically, presidents resign when approval drops below ~20% AND protests are sustained AND elite allies defect. None of these conditions were met simultaneously.
   - **Coup**: Argentina has had zero successful coups since 1976; the military is thoroughly civilianized.
   - **Death/incapacity**: No known health crisis.

2. **Economic trajectory was strongly positive**: Inflation fell from ~300% to ~1.5% monthly. Poverty declined from 57% to 31.6%. GDP grew 5.5% (projected 2025). Wages rose above inflation. These are politically protective — voters credited Milei with the improvement.

3. **International support provided a backstop**: The IMF $20B loan and US Treasury support meant any removal would have triggered severe financial consequences for Argentina, creating incentives for all domestic actors to avoid extra-constitutional action.

4. **Opposition was fragmented**: The Peronist opposition split into Fuerza Patria (Kicillof), Primero País (Schiaretti), and FIT-U. No single opposition figure or party had national majority support. This fragmentation prevented the coordinated action needed for impeachment.

5. **Reference class favors radical reformers**: Of the modern first-term radical reformers reference class, only Castillo (Peru) was removed — because he lacked a visible quick win (inflation reduction), had no party organization, and presided over economic deterioration. Milei had all the protective factors Castillo lacked.

### What Would Have Made Milei Leave Office

A YES resolution would have required one of:
- A unified opposition achieving 2/3+ in both chambers for impeachment
- A sustained protest movement with military/police defections
- A health crisis (assassination, stroke, etc.)
- A catastrophic economic reversal (return to hyperinflation)

None of these materialized in 2025. The risk was never above ~10-15% at any point in the year.

### Forecasting Implications for Argentina (2026-2027)

- Milei is likely to serve his full term unless he faces a severe health crisis or the opposition unifies and wins a legislative supermajority.
- The 2027 presidential election will be the next major test of Milei's realignment durability.
- Midterm victory does not guarantee presidential victory; see Macri 2017 (midterm win) → 2019 (reelection loss).
- The Peronist opposition needs to unify behind a single candidate and develop a policy platform that doesn't simply promise to undo Milei's reforms — this is structurally difficult.

### Peronist Reconstitution Pathways for 2027

The 2025 midterm completed Phase 4 of the [[peronist-fragmentation-reconstitution]] cycle: the weaker Peronist faction (HNP/Primero País) was squeezed to irrelevance. Peronism is now in Phase 5 (leadership consolidation) with Axel Kicillof's [[fuerza-patria]] as the surviving faction.

**Scenario Tree for Peronist Reconstitution by 2027:**

1. **Kicillof-led unified ticket (~40% probability)** — The default scenario. Kicillof absorbs the HNP remnants and runs as the single Peronist candidate. This follows the canonical pattern (Menem 1989, Kirchner 2003, Fernández 2019) but faces two structural obstacles:
   - HNP voters defected to Milei in 2025, not to Fuerza Patria. Reconstitution requires winning them back from the incumbent — harder than absorbing them while in opposition.
   - Kicillof's Kirchnerist identity limits his appeal to conservative Peronists who preferred Schiaretti's developmentalism over CFK's left populism.
   - Mitigating factor: Peronist voters have historically rallied behind whoever emerges as the single candidate, even if their preferred faction lost.

2. **Fragmented Peronism, no single candidate (~25% probability)** — Kicillof runs as the Kirchnerist candidate but a conservative Peronist (possibly a new figure or a revived Massa/Schiaretti vehicle) runs separately. This splits the Peronist vote and guarantees Milei's re-election or a non-Peronist successor. This scenario would break the canonical reconstitution pattern and signal a structural change in Argentine politics.

3. **Internal primary produces a non-Kicillof candidate (~20% probability)** — An open internal contest (interna) selects a compromise figure — possibly a provincial governor from a non-Kirchnerist Peronist faction, or a labor union leader with cross-faction appeal. This would be the healthiest reconstitution but requires Kicillof to step aside, which is unlikely before 2027.

4. **Organizational collapse, no reconstitution (~15% probability)** — Peronism's labor-union base and provincial patronage networks deteriorate under Milei's reforms (labor flexibilization, spending cuts, elimination of provincial transfers). The movement permanently fragments into regional fiefdoms with no national candidate. This would be a 1-in-100-year structural break for Argentine politics.

**Leading indicators to watch:**
- Kicillof's approval trajectory within Peronist identifiers (polling)
- HNP/PPA voters' second-choice preference (return to Kicillof vs. stay with Milei)
- Union leadership alignment (CGT internal elections, political positioning)
- Internal primary scheduling (early primary = healthy reconstitution; no primary = Kicillof imposes candidacy)
- Emergence of a consensus figure from the conservative Peronist wing

**Forecasting heuristic:** If the 2027 election is competitive (Milei approval < 45% in early 2027), the structural pressure to reconstitute increases. If Milei is cruising to re-election, Peronist fragmentation is more likely to persist.
