# Mexican Politics — MORENA Dominant-Party Era

## Overview
This thread tracks the consolidation of MORENA as Mexico's dominant party following the 2018 election of [[domains/latin-america/entities/andres-manuel-lopez-obrador]] and the subsequent 2024 election of [[domains/latin-america/entities/claudia-sheinbaum]]. Mexico's political system has transitioned from a competitive two-party system (PAN vs PRI) through a transition period (2000-2018) to a dominant-party system under MORENA (2018-present).

Key dynamics to track:
- MORENA's electoral dominance and whether it consolidates or erodes over time
- The opposition's ability to reorganize after the 2024 defeat
- Sheinbaum's governance style — continuation of AMLO's agenda or independent course
- Constitutional reform push (judicial reform, electoral reform, security forces)
- Economic and security outcomes under MORENA rule

## Key Events

### 2018
- **July 1**: AMLO wins presidency with 53% — first outright majority in decades
- MORENA coalition wins congressional majorities

### 2021
- **June 6**: Midterm elections — MORENA coalition retains lower-house majority with 46%, loses some seats but confirms durability

### 2023
- **June**: AMLO announces he will not seek re-election (term-limited)
- **September 6**: Sheinbaum wins MORENA presidential primary with 39% of party-member vote
- Opposition coalition "Fuerza y Corazón por México" (PAN-PRI-PRD) nominates Xóchitl Gálvez

### 2024
- **June 2**: Sheinbaum wins presidency with ~60% — the largest vote share since 1982
- MORENA coalition wins supermajorities in both chambers, enabling constitutional amendments
- **October 1**: Sheinbaum inaugurated as Mexico's first female president
- **Late 2024**: Sheinbaum administration begins — early indicators of governance direction:
  - **Continuity signal**: Maintained AMLO's key programs and cabinet continuity (many holdovers)
  - **Differentiation signal**: Initial emphasis on security strategy reform (more intelligence-based vs military) and energy transition (moving beyond Pemex nationalism)
  - **Constitutional reform push**: Judicial reform (elected judiciary) and electoral reform (INE restructuring) proposed — key tests of whether the constitutional supermajority enables consolidation of party-state integration
  - **Fiscal strain**: Continued high social spending (~15% of GDP) combined with Pemex debts and deficit concerns

## Key Dynamics

### Dominant-Party Consolidation
MORENA's trajectory mirrors the early consolidation of the PRI (1929-1940s) — a new party winning successive elections, expanding state-level control, and achieving the constitutional majority needed to reshape institutions. Key indicators to watch:
- **Electoral dominance durability**: Will MORENA maintain its ~50%+ vote share in 2027 midterms and 2030 presidential?
- **Party institutionalization**: Is MORENA a personalist vehicle for AMLO (which would weaken after his departure) or a genuine mass party?
- **Constitutional entrenchment**: Judicial reform and electoral reform (2024-2025) may lock in MORENA's institutional advantages

### Social Program Entrenchment as Approval Floor
The core structural mechanism sustaining MORENA's dominance is **social program expansion** — not commodity-funded populism (see [[domains/latin-america/concepts/social-program-approval-sustainability/_concept]] for full framework):

- **Programs**: AMLO expanded the universal pension for seniors (doubling the payment), created Jóvenes Construyendo Futuro (youth apprenticeships with stipends), Sembrando Vida (reforestation stipends), and universalized fertilizer distribution for small farmers.
- **Coverage**: Approximately 60% of Mexican households received at least one direct government transfer by 2023 — creating a durable pro-government voting bloc independent of economic growth.
- **Approval decoupling**: AMLO maintained 55-65% approval despite GDP growth averaging ~1% annually, because 60% of households evaluated him on program continuity rather than macroeconomic indicators.
- **Successor inheritance**: Sheinbaum inherited this social-program approval floor, which is why her ~60% vote share matched AMLO's approval even though she had far less personal charisma. The floor is party-anchored, not person-anchored.
- **Fiscal sustainability risk**: Social spending rose from ~10% to ~15% of GDP (2018-2024), financed partly by debt and partly by squeezing Pemex and other state enterprises. Fiscal sustainability is the primary vulnerability — a fiscal crisis could force benefit cuts that erode the approval floor.

### Economic Perception Decoupling (The Peso Paradox)
Mexico's economic performance under AMLO presents a forecasting-relevant puzzle:

- **GDP growth**: ~1% annual average (2020-2023) — weakest recovery among major Latin American economies
- **Peso**: The "superpeso" — strengthened dramatically against the USD, driven by high interest rates (Banxico kept rates at 11% through most of 2023), nearshoring inflows, and record remittances
- **Inflation**: Peaked at ~8% in 2022, declined to ~4.5% by early 2024
- **Voter perception**: Majority of Mexicans rated AMLO's economy positively, despite weak GDP growth

**Key insight for forecasting**: Voters do not evaluate the economy primarily through GDP growth. Exchange rate strength is widely discussed and visible (Mexicans living near the border or with US-based family see the peso's purchasing power). Social program beneficiaries evaluate the economy through their personal transfer receipt. Traditional "economy → vote share" models systematically underestimate dominant-party successor performance in social-program-entrenched systems. For forecasting Mexican politics, track three metrics: (1) real value of social program transfers, (2) peso exchange rate trend, (3) inflation relative to benefit growth — not GDP.

### Dedazo (Dedazo revival)
AMLO's succession of Sheinbaum represents a conscious revival of the PRI-era dedazo tradition (see [[domains/latin-america/concepts/incumbent-successor-dominant-party/_concept]] for full framework):

- **Historical parallel**: Every PRI president from 1929-2000 personally chose their successor through dedazo. AMLO, an anti-establishment populist who built MORENA by attacking the PRI, paradoxically adopted the PRI's most characteristic institutional mechanism.
- **Mechanism**: AMLO structured the 2023 MORENA primary to eliminate Marcelo Ebrard (foreign minister, potential rival) by signaling his preference for Sheinbaum early and using his personal popularity to make the primary essentially an anointment.
- **Forecasting value**: Dedazo produces cleaner approval transfer than competitive primaries because there is no factional wound to heal. However, it also means the successor has weaker independent political identity — Sheinbaum entered office with an "AMLO 2.0" label rather than her own mandate.
- **Risk**: If Sheinbaum's government encounters crisis (security, fiscal, or economic), she lacks the independent political capital to differentiate from AMLO without alienating his base. The dedazo creates a path-dependency that constrains the successor's options.

### Opposition System Destruction
The story of Mexican politics 2018-2024 is not just MORENA's rise but the **destruction of the traditional opposition**:

- **PAN**: Vote share collapsed from 53% (2000) to ~15% party identification (2024). The center-right brand is caught between MORENA's welfare populism on the left and MC's centrist reformism on the center.
- **PRI**: Reduced from 71-year ruling party (1929-2000) to ~10% party identification and 2 state governorships. The corruption brand (Peña Nieto, videoscandals, Odebrecht/Lava Jato links) makes the PRI toxic to the median voter.
- **PRD**: Approaching extinction — polling below the 3% registration threshold. AMLO's departure (2011) hollowed out the party's base; his victory (2018) completed its destruction.
- **MC (Citizens' Movement)**: The only opposition party with a stable trajectory — holds 5-15% vote share and 2 governorships. But MC's centrist positioning means it cannot directly challenge MORENA for national leadership; it serves as a spoiler that splits the anti-MORENA vote.
- **Opposition coalition failures**: The PAN-PRI-PRD "Fuerza y Corazón por México" coalition was ideologically incoherent (center-right + centrist/corrupt + center-left) and could not generate a positive governing vision. Future opposition coalitions face the same structural problem: the three traditional parties have incompatible ideologies and negative brand associations.
- **Forecasting implication**: Any forecast that assumes opposition "recovery" or "reorganization" in the next 2-3 election cycles must account for the structural unlikelihood of this happening. The traditional parties are not currently reorganizing; they are fragmenting further.

### Security and Violence
Mexico's homicide rate remained high throughout AMLO's term (~25-30 per 100,000). Sheinbaum inherits this crisis. The election was notably violent — 37 candidates were killed during the 2023-2024 campaign cycle, the deadliest in modern Mexican history.

## Forecasting Significance
Mexican politics is a recurring prediction market domain for:
- Presidential elections (every 6 years with midterms at year 3)
- Constitutional reform questions (judicial reform, electoral reform, military/National Guard)
- Security policy changes
- Energy policy (AMLO's Pemex nationalism vs market-friendly alternatives)
- Trade relations (USMCA review 2026)
