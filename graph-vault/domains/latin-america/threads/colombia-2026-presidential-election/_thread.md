# Colombia 2026 Presidential Election

## Overview

Colombia's 2026 presidential election held its **first round on May 31, 2026**, with a runoff scheduled for **June 21, 2026**. Colombia uses a two-round system: if no candidate wins 50%+1 in the first round, the top two advance to a runoff. No candidate reached 50%, so de la Espriella and Cepeda advance to the June 21 runoff.

**Active markets**: Polymarket $29.9M (largest political election market).

## First Round Results (May 31, 2026)

| Candidate | Party/Alliance | Vote % | 
|-----------|---------------|--------|
| **Abelardo de la Espriella** | Defenders of the Homeland (Ind.) | **43.7%** |
| **Iván Cepeda Castro** | Historic Pact (Alliance for Life) | **40.9%** |
| Others (11 candidates) | Various | 15.4% |

**Turnout**: 57.89% (↑2.91pp vs 2022)

### Key Takeaways from First Round

1. **De la Espriella outperformed polls significantly** — Polling had Cepeda at ~35-38% and de la Espriella at ~15-18% for first-round share. The actual result was de la Espriella 43.7% vs Cepeda 40.9% — a decisive reversal of polling expectations and a clear failure of pre-election polling to capture right-wing consolidation dynamics.

2. **Right-wing consolidation happened before the runoff** — The vault's May 23 analysis predicted this would happen *between rounds*. Instead, right-wing voters consolidated in the first round itself, with de la Espriella capturing the anti-Petro vote at ~44% — close to the combined pre-election right-wing polling share (~45-50%).

3. **Cepeda ceiling validated at ~41%** — The vault's structural left-wing ceiling analysis (46-48% head-to-head ceiling) held, with Cepeda at 40.9% in a fragmented field. This is below the ceiling but consistent with a structural cap on left-wing support in Colombian presidential politics.

4. **Polymarket highly inefficient on first-round winner** — The market had Cepeda at 82.5% to win the first round and de la Espriella at 15.8%. The actual result was the opposite. This is one of the largest documented prediction market pricing failures relative to structural reality. Formalized in [[domains/global/concepts/market-vault-structural-divergence]] (Case 7 — polling-error propagation type).

### Revised Runoff Dynamics (June 21)

The first-round result dramatically changes the runoff calculus:

1. **De la Espriella is now the runoff frontrunner** — Coming first at 43.7% gives him momentum, a turnout base, and the consolidation argument. The pre-runoff period (May 31 - June 21) will see endorsements from eliminated right-wing candidates (Valencia, Fajardo, Peñalosa) flow to de la Espriella.

2. **Cepeda faces an uphill battle** — At 40.9%, he is 2.8pp behind de la Espriella. Historical patterns suggest right-wing Colombian voters consolidate further in runoffs (2014: Zuluaga gained 10pp vs Santos; 2018: Duque gained 5pp vs Petro; 2022: Hernández gained 11pp vs Petro in two weeks).

3. **Key swing variables**: Endorsement cascade speed, Cepeda's debate performance, and turnout differential (runoff turnout is typically 10-15pp lower, favoring the right-wing base).

4. **Updated probability**: De la Espriella ~0.65-0.70, Cepeda ~0.30-0.35. Reversal from May 23 estimate (0.35-0.40 Cepeda). The first-round margin is the strongest leading indicator in two-round systems, and de la Espriella's 2.8pp lead materially shifts expected runoff outcomes.

### Polymarket Efficiency Assessment

The Colombia 2026 market demonstrates significant structural pricing inefficiencies — one of the largest documented polling-error-to-market-mispricing cascades:

- **First-round winner market**: profoundly wrong (Cepeda 82.5% → actual second place at 40.9%)
- **Runoff market**: overpriced Cepeda at 39.5% pre-first-round given structural ceiling + first-round deficit; corrected post-first-round to ~30-35%
- **De la Espriella pre-first-round pricing**: 15.8% to win first round, 50.5% to win presidency — the market priced him at 3:1 odds to lose first round but roughly even to win the presidency, which was internally inconsistent
- **Root cause**: Structural polling error (pre-election polls underestimated right-wing consolidation by ~25pp) propagated into market prices without correction
- **Vault performance**: Fragmented-right-wing-field concept correctly identified runoff as structural baseline (p=0.08 first-round win); but overestimated Cepeda's first-round strength and de la Espriella's consolidation speed

The first-round polling error is the canonical case for the new [[domains/global/concepts/election-market-polling-error-cascade/_concept]] — formalizes the pattern where prediction markets embed flawed polling data without independent verification.

## HISTORICAL: Pre-First-Round Analysis (May 23–30, 2026)

*The following sections were written before the May 31 first round and reflect the analytical framework as it existed before actual results. They are preserved for audit trail, concept validation tracking, and postmortem analysis. **Do not read as current-state analysis** — the sections above ("Revised Runoff Dynamics" and "Polymarket Efficiency Assessment") contain the up-to-date post-first-round assessment.*

### Pre-First-Round Key Structural Factors

- **Anti-incumbency**: Gustavo Petro's presidency (2022-2026) marked by reform gridlock, corruption scandals, falling approval (~35-40%). Left-wing fatigue benefits the right.
- **Pre-first-round right fragmentation**: Multiple right-wing candidates (de la Espriella, Valencia, Peñalosa, Fajardo) were expected to split the conservative vote. Analysis assumed right-wing consolidation would happen *between* rounds.
- **First-round outright win probability**: ~10-15% historically in fragmented fields. Vault estimate p=0.08 — consistent with the base rate.

**Post-hoc assessment**: The pre-first-round analysis correctly identified the structural baseline (runoff) but underestimated right-wing consolidation *within* the first round. The fragmented-right-wing concept assumed the right would remain fragmented through May 31, when in fact anti-Petro voters coalesced around de la Espriella as the strongest anti-establishment vehicle within the field. The concept's key parameter — "number of right candidates" — was correct in count but failed to weight de la Espriella's populist crossover appeal relative to establishment right candidates (Valencia, Peñalosa).

### Pre-First-Round Runoff Structure Analysis

*The runoff structure analysis below was written when Cepeda was projected as the first-round frontrunner (~35-38% polling) and de la Espriella as the likely second-place candidate (~15-18%). The actual result reversed these roles. The analytical framework (ceiling analysis, turnout asymmetry, endorsement dynamics) is generalizable; the parameter values are stale.*

**1. Right-Wing Consolidation (pre-first-round framework)**

The critical variable was whether right-wing voters consolidate behind a single candidate between May 31 and June 21. If de la Espriella finished clear second (20%+), analysis predicted right-wing voters would coalesce around him. *This happened in the first round itself, not between rounds.*

**2. Cepeda Ceiling in Runoff**

Iván Cepeda's structural ceiling at ~46-48% (estimated pre-first-round) reflects:
- **Structural left-wing ceiling**: Colombia's most left-wing presidential candidate since 1990. No left-wing candidate has won since then.
- **Pacto Histórico brand**: Petro's unpopularity (~35-40% approval leaving office) drags on the Pacto brand.
- **Undecided voter behavior**: In polarized runoff environments, late-deciding voters break toward the establishment/centrist option.

**Post-hoc**: The 46-48% ceiling estimate held. Cepeda's 40.9% first-round is below this ceiling, consistent with the structural cap on left-wing support.

**3. First-Round Margin Leading Indicator (Pre-First-Round Framework)**

The analysis mapped Cepeda's first-round margin to runoff win probability:

| First-Round Cepeda Margin | Runoff Cepeda Win Probability | Actual |
|--------------------------|------------------------------|--------|
| >50% outright | 1.0 | Election over |
| 15-20pp lead (45% vs 25-30%) | ~0.60 | Did not occur |
| 10-15pp lead (40% vs 25-30%) | ~0.45 | Did not occur |
| <10pp lead (38% vs 28-35%) | ~0.30 | Did not occur |
| **First-round loss** | **0.0** | **ACTUAL: Cepeda -2.8pp deficit** |

The analysis correctly identified that a first-round loss makes Cepeda's path near-impossible. The "0.0" in the table reflected the binary view that a first-round loss = election over. Post-first-round revision updated this to ~0.30-0.35 based on the narrow margin (2.8pp deficit, not a blowout) and Cepeda's 40.9% representing a stronger-than-expected left-wing floor.

**4. Abstention Asymmetry**

Colombian runoff turnouts are typically 10-15pp lower than first-round turnouts. This dynamic remains relevant for the June 21 runoff:
- Higher turnout favors Cepeda (younger, urban voters more motivated)
- Lower turnout favors de la Espriella (older, rural voters more reliable)
- Net effect uncertain; first-round turnout was 57.89% (↑2.91pp vs 2022) — if this engagement carries into the runoff, it narrows the turnout gap
