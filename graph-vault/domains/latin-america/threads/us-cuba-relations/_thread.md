# US-Cuba Relations

## Thread Arc

US-Cuba relations have been frozen in hostility since the 1959 Cuban Revolution, with periodic thaw-freeze cycles. This thread tracks bilateral dynamics relevant to Cuban stability forecasting, regime-change probability, and regional geopolitics.

## Structural Context

### Helms-Burton Framework
The Cuban Liberty and Democratic Solidarity Act (Helms-Burton, 1996) codified the embargo (embargo) into law, requiring Congressional action to fully lift it. Key provisions:
- Title III: Lawsuits against entities trafficking in confiscated US properties (waived by every president until Trump activated it in 2019)
- Title IV: Visa bans on executives of companies using confiscated property
- Conditions for embargo lifting: Free elections, legal opposition, free press, market economy — effectively requiring regime change

### Obama Thaw (2014-2016)
- December 2014: Normalization announcement
- July 2015: Embassies reopened
- March 2016: Obama visit to Havana
- Key achievements: Remigration, telecom/civil aviation agreements, cruise ship travel
- Did NOT lift embargo (Congressional requirement)

### Trump Reversal (2017-2020)
- June 2017: Cuba policy speech in Miami — reversed Obama-era openings
- Reimposed travel restrictions, remittance caps
- Activated Title III lawsuits
- Maximum pressure campaign reinforced

### Biden Status Quo (2021-2025)
- Maintained Trump-era sanctions framework
- Modest humanitarian adjustments (family remittances, consular services)
- No major policy shift — Cuba was low priority amid Ukraine, China, domestic agenda

### Trump Second Term (2025-present)
- Continuation of maximum pressure — no sign of engagement
- Cuba not a foreign policy priority (focus on Iran, Ukraine, trade war, immigration)
- Current speculation: Trump may seek humanitarian/human rights reputation by offering selective easing in exchange for migration cooperation

## Key Indicators for Regime Stability

### Economic Pressure Points
- GDP decline: ~1-2% annual contraction since 2019
- Severe shortages of food, medicine, fuel
- Tourism collapse: Post-COVID slow recovery, further hit by US travel restrictions
- Remittance decline: Estimated $3B in 2025, down from $4B+ pre-COVID
- Reduced Venezuelan oil subsidies: PDVSA production decline limits Cuba support
- Emigration wave: 500K+ Cubans entered US via Nicaragua route since 2022 — acts as pressure valve

### Political Pressure Points
- July 2021 protests: Largest anti-government demonstrations since 1994 (in 50+ cities). Regime responded with 1,000+ arrests, internet blackouts, show trials
- March 2024 protests: Smaller but persistent
- No organized opposition — PCC controls all legal political space
- Dissidents monitored, imprisoned, or exiled
- Military/security loyalty intact — MININT and FAR remain under PCC control

### Migration Diplomacy
- Migration is the primary US-Cuba bilateral negotiating lever
- US benefits from continued emigration (relieves internal pressure on Cuba, provides labor)
- Cuba benefits from remittances (second-largest hard currency source after tourism)
- Both sides have incentives to maintain orderly migration — reduces bilateral crisis risk
