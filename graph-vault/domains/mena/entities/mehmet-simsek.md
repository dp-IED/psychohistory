# Mehmet Simsek

## Overview

Mehmet Simsek is the Minister of Treasury and Finance of Turkey, appointed after Erdogan's May 2023 election victory. He is widely regarded as the architect of Turkey's post-2023 economic policy normalization — the reversal of the unorthodox low-interest-rate policies that characterized the 2018-2023 period. His return to government was the single most important signal that Erdogan was willing to tolerate orthodox economic policy.

## Background

- Previously served as Deputy Prime Minister (2015-2018) and Finance Minister (2009-2015) under Erdogan and Prime Minister Ahmet Davutoglu
- Held senior roles at Merrill Lynch and UBS before entering politics
- Educated at Oxford and Exeter University (UK)
- Widely respected by international investors — his appointment triggered a rally in Turkish assets and a CDS compression
- English-fluent, comfortable communicating with Western investors (unlike his predecessor)

## Role in the Normalization

Simsek's role is often described as "the adult in the room" — he provides political cover for TCMB Governors Erkan and Karahan to pursue orthodox monetary tightening against the President's stated preference for low rates. His political relationship with Erdogan is the key variable sustaining the normalization:

- **Credibility transmission**: Simsek's international reputation reassures foreign investors that Turkey has returned to orthodox policy. Capital inflows during the normalization are partly a "Simsek premium."
- **Political buffer**: He absorbs pressure from Erdogan's circle, allowing the TCMB to operate with de facto independence even without de jure independence.
- **Exit risk**: If Simsek resigns or is fired, the normalization collapses. Markets would interpret his departure as a return to unorthodoxy regardless of the TCMB governor's public statements.

## Forecasting Significance

- Any question about Turkish interest rates, lira stability, or economic policy must assess Simsek's status
- Key leading indicators: (1) Has Simsek met privately with Erdogan recently? (2) Has he given interviews signaling frustration? (3) Are foreign investor meetings still on his schedule?
- If Simsek leaves, forecast: immediate lira depreciation 10-20%, rate hike emergency meeting, eventual reversion to unorthodox policy within 6 months
