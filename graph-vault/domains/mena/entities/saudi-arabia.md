# Saudi Arabia

## Summary

Saudi Arabia is the dominant Sunni Arab power in the Middle East and Iran's primary regional rival for influence across the Muslim world. Under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman (MBS), Saudi Arabia has pursued an aggressive economic diversification agenda (Vision 2030), normalized relations with Israel (pre-Oct 7 trajectory), and positioned itself as a mediator between great powers (US-China-Russia triangulation). The kingdom is the world's largest crude oil exporter (~10M bpd) and the de facto leader of OPEC+ alongside Russia.

## Significance for Current Forecasting

### Iran Peace Deal & Regional Realignment

The June 14, 2026 US-Iran peace deal fundamentally reshapes Saudi Arabia's strategic environment:

- **Security**: Saudi Arabia was the primary beneficiary of Iran's military degradation during the Twelve-Day War (June 2025). A peaceful Iran reduces the immediate ballistic missile threat to Saudi cities and oil infrastructure (Abqaiq-style attacks, reference [[events/us-iran-peace-deal-june-2026]]).
- **Nuclear concern**: Saudi Arabia has repeatedly stated it will pursue nuclear enrichment capabilities if Iran does. The Iran peace deal's enrichment provisions directly determine Saudi nonproliferation posture. If Iran retains enrichment rights, Saudi Arabia will demand equivalent treatment — potentially triggering a regional proliferation cascade.
- **Yemen**: Iran's Houthi proxy threat to Saudi Arabia is calibrated to Iran's strategic posture. Post-peace-deal, Houthi attacks on Saudi targets (which largely stopped after the 2022 truce) are unlikely to resume absent an Iranian signal.

### Strait of Hormuz & Oil Markets

Saudi Arabia is the single most affected stakeholder in the Strait of Hormuz traffic normalization question:

- **Export dependency**: ~90% of Saudi crude exports transit Hormuz. The US naval blockade (imposed June 2025) forced Saudi to reroute via the Red Sea (Yanbu pipeline, which carries ~5M bpd). Blockade lifting and traffic normalization are existential for Saudi oil revenue (see [[runs/20260618-hormuz-traffic-normal]], vault p_yes=0.58).
- **Kharg Island dynamics**: Iran's main oil export terminal at Kharg Island was targeted during the war. Saudi Arabia has spare production capacity (~2M bpd) that could compensate for disrupted Iranian supply, but OPEC+ quota dynamics constrain this.
- **Oil market strategy**: Saudi-Russia OPEC+ coordination faces a new variable — Iranian oil re-entry. Saudi preference is to manage total supply to maintain ~$70-80/bbl, balancing budget needs (Saudi fiscal breakeven ~$85/bbl) against market share.

### US-Saudi Relations

- **Trump alignment**: Saudi-US relations under the second Trump term are strong — Saudi was the first foreign leader Trump spoke to after his 2025 inauguration. The transactional "oil-for-security" bargain deepens.
- **Normalization track**: Pre-Oct 7 Saudi-Israeli normalization was paused. The Gaza ceasefire (Jan 2025) and Iran peace deal (Jun 2026) may reopen the Palestinian state component. Saudi demands a credible Palestinian state pathway — this is the single hardest remaining diplomatic issue.
- **China triangulation**: Saudi maintains independent ties with China (largest oil customer, BRICS member), creating a multi-alignment posture that complicates US-Saudi relations but gives Saudi leverage on both sides.

### Israel Normalization & Palestinian Question

The Saudi-Israeli normalization trajectory was the most consequential Middle Eastern diplomatic trend before Oct 7, 2023. Post-Gaza-war and post-Iran-peace-deal, the conditions for normalization are arguably better than ever:

- Iran is no longer a shared existential threat demanding unified front
- The Gaza ceasefire (Jan 2025) removes the immediate humanitarian obstacle
- MBS views normalization as the capstone of his modernization legacy
- Remaining barrier: Saudi demand for a Palestinian state pathway, which Israel's current coalition cannot accept

## Key Entities & Relationships

- **MBS (Mohammed bin Salman)**: Crown Prince and de facto ruler since 2017. Controls security, oil policy, and foreign relations. Architect of Vision 2030. Key decision-maker on normalization, OPEC+ policy, and regional posture.
- **King Salman**: Monarch since 2015, age ~90. Ceremonial role; MBS holds executive power.
- **Prince Faisal bin Farhan**: Foreign Minister, key diplomat for US and Iran engagement.
- **OPEC+**: Saudi-Russia led producer alliance controlling ~40% of global oil supply. Saudi-Russian coordination is the central axis.
- **Vision 2030**: Mega-projects (NEOM, Red Sea tourism), economic diversification, social liberalization. Energy transition hedging.
