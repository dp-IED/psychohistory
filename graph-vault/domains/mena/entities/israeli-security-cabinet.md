# Israeli Security Cabinet

## Summary

The Israeli Security Cabinet (HaKabinet HaBitchoni) is a statutorily-authorized subset of the full Israeli cabinet, empowered to make decisions on military operations and ceasefire agreements without requiring a full cabinet vote. It is the body that must **ratify** a ceasefire agreement before it becomes official Israeli policy, making its decision-making process a critical variable for ceasefire timing forecasts.

## Composition (Netanyahu 2025 government)

- **Prime Minister**: Benjamin Netanyahu (chair, with casting vote)
- **Defense Minister**: Israel Katz (2024-)
- **Foreign Minister**: Gideon Sa'ar
- **Finance Minister**: Bezalel Smotrich
- **National Security Minister**: Itamar Ben-Gvir
- **Strategic Affairs Minister**: Ron Dermer
- **Justice Minister**: Yariv Levin
- **Other security-portfolio ministers**: As appointed

The far-right faction (Ben-Gvir, Smotrich) holds veto power on security decisions critical to coalition stability.

## Ceasefire Approval Process

### Standard Procedure (Type A — Diplomatic)
1. Negotiation team reaches framework with mediator(s)
2. Security cabinet convenes for vote
3. Simple majority required; prime minister has casting vote
4. Approval typically takes 1-2 days from framework agreement to vote
5. Far-right ministers can delay but rarely block if PM is committed
6. Full cabinet then ratifies (pro forma), adding 0-1 days

### Crisis Procedure (Type B — War-Termination)
1. War ongoing; superpower enters combat
2. PM convenes security cabinet in emergency session (can be done remotely)
3. Vote happens same day (urgency compresses timeline)
4. Full cabinet informed after vote, not consulted in advance
5. **Approval within hours, not days**

**Validation (June 2025):** The war-termination ceasefire was announced and ratified on the same day (June 23). The security cabinet met after Trump's announcement, ratified unanimously, and the full cabinet was informed concurrently — not sequentially. This crisis-accelerated procedure compressed the standard 1-2 day gap to 0 days.

## Forecasting Significance

1. **The security cabinet is the bottleneck for Israeli ceasefire ratification** — not the Knesset, not the full cabinet. The time between "framework agreed" and "ceasefire official" is determined by when the security cabinet can convene and vote.

2. **Crisis conditions compress the security cabinet timeline to 0-4 hours.** Under active war, the cabinet can convene remotely, vote by phone or secure video, and the PM can announce results immediately. The standard "1 day gap" applies only to non-crisis conditions.

3. **Far-right members (Ben-Gvir, Smotrich) can delay but cannot indefinitely block.** They can walk out, boycott votes, or threaten coalition exit, but the PM can proceed without them on security matters. Their leverage is political (coalition stability), not procedural.

4. **Track cabinet composition changes** — leadership changes (new PM, new defense minister) reset the security cabinet's composition and may change the vote dynamics.
