# Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (TCMB)

## Overview

The Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey (TCMB) is the institution responsible for monetary policy in Turkey. It sets the policy rate (one-week repo auction rate) through its Monetary Policy Committee (PPK). The TCMB has been at the center of Turkey's macroeconomic instability since 2018, when President Erdogan's unorthodox view that high interest rates cause high inflation led to sustained political pressure on the central bank to keep rates low despite surging inflation.

## Historical Monetary Policy Regimes

### Pre-2018: Orthodox Era
- The TCMB operated with relative independence, targeting inflation through conventional interest rate policy
- Policy rates responded to inflation as expected: rates rose when inflation rose

### 2018-2023: Unorthodox Era ("Erdoganomics")
- Starting in 2018, Erdogan's influence over the TCMB intensified. Governors who raised rates were fired:
  - Murat Cetinkaya (2016-2019): Fired for not cutting rates fast enough
  - Murat Uysal (2019-2020): Fired after lira collapsed
  - Naci Agbal (2020-2021): Fired after raising rates (served only 4 months)
  - Sahap Kavcioglu (2021-2023): Compliant governor who cut rates from 19% to 8.5% despite inflation peaking above 85%
- Policy rate was cut from 19% (Nov 2020) to 8.5% (Feb 2023) while CPI inflation exceeded 85% (Oct 2022)
- The lira depreciated catastrophically: from ~5.5/USD in 2018 to ~23/USD in mid-2023
- Foreign exchange reserves were depleted through unsustainable FX intervention

### June 2023-Present: Normalization Era
- After Erdogan's May 2023 re-election, a new economic team was appointed:
  - Mehmet Simsek (Finance Minister) — former deputy PM, credible orthodox economist
  - Hafize Gaye Erkan (Governor, June 2023-Feb 2024) — former Goldman Sachs, first female governor
  - Fatih Karahan (Governor, Feb 2024-present) — former Amazon/NYU economist
- The TCMB raised the policy rate from 8.5% to 50% over 14 months
- Multiple tightening measures introduced: reserve requirements, credit growth limits, macroprudential simplification
- The policy reversal signaled a commitment to orthodox monetary policy, but credibility remained fragile

## Forecasting Considerations

- **Forward guidance is less structured than the Fed's**: The TCMB does not use dot plots or have a Chair press conference tradition comparable to the Fed. Its primary communication tools are the policy rate decision announcement and the quarterly Inflation Report.
- **Political interference risk is always present**: Despite the normalization, Erdogan has not changed his view on interest rates. A new governor could be appointed at any time, reverting to unorthodox policy.
- **Inflation trajectory is the dominant variable**: TCMB rate decisions are driven by the inflation outlook. When inflation is declining toward target but remains elevated, the TCMB typically holds rather than cuts, protecting credibility.
- **Lira stability is a secondary constraint**: Sudden lira depreciation forces the TCMB's hand — it may need to hike to defend the currency even if inflation data alone wouldn't warrant it.
- **Reserve adequacy affects policy room**: The TCMB's net FX reserve position determines how much it can smooth lira volatility without needing rate action. Improved reserve buffers (from foreign capital inflows post-normalization) give more policy space.
