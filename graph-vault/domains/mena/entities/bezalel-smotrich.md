# Bezalel Smotrich

## Type
Far-right Israeli politician; Finance Minister (2022–present). Leader of the Religious Zionism party. Also holds ministerial authority over civilian affairs in the West Bank (via a 2022 coalition agreement delegation from the Defense Ministry).

## Significance for Forecasting

Smotrich is, alongside Ben-Gvir, the second pillar of the far-right bloc in Netanyahu's coalition. As Finance Minister with control over the budget and West Bank settlement policy, his leverage is both political and functional:

1. **Coalition exit threat**: His Religious Zionism party (7 seats) withdrawing would collapse the government
2. **Budget authority**: As Finance Minister, can defund or delay any ceasefire-related reconstruction aid to Gaza
3. **West Bank portfolio**: Controls settlement expansion, land registration, and Palestinian civilian affairs — can escalate or de-escalate the West Bank front independently of the Gaza decision

## Key Dynamics

### Ceasefire Position
- Smotrich is ideologically opposed to any ceasefire that leaves Hamas with governance capability in Gaza
- His stated position: "We must complete the mission — destroy Hamas entirely"
- More strategically calculating than Ben-Gvir: Smotrich is more likely to accept a ceasefire that (a) achieves permanent Israeli security control of Gaza, (b) enables West Bank annexation as compensation, or (c) comes with US guarantees for settlement expansion
- **Key difference from Ben-Gvir**: Smotrich cares more about West Bank annexation as a priority than about Gaza specifically. A Gaza ceasefire paired with annexation progress is a viable trade-off for him.

### Coalition Dynamics
- Smotrich and Ben-Gvir act as a coordinated bloc but with distinct red lines
- Smotrich is more pragmatic on process — he will negotiate and make demands, Ben-Gvir tends to simply veto
- Netanyahu can sometimes split the pair by offering Smotrich West Bank concessions in exchange for Gaza ceasefire tolerance
- **Threshold test**: If Smotrich can be bought off with annexation movement, Ben-Gvir becomes isolated and Netanyahu can proceed

### Post-March 18 Collapse
- Like Ben-Gvir, Smotrich was satisfied by the resumption of combat — his preferred policy (no ceasefire, military victory) was in effect
- Both far-right ministers being satisfied meant the political cost of a ceasefire by July 15 was high: Netanyahu would need to either lose them from the coalition or offer major concessions (West Bank annexation) as a trade

## Key Events
- 2022: Religious Zionism party wins 7 seats; Smotrich becomes Finance Minister
- 2023-2024: Uses West Bank portfolio to accelerate settlement expansion during the war
- Jan 2025: Votes against ceasefire; stays in coalition
- Mar 18, 2025: Supports resumption of combat, calls for permanent Israeli security control of Gaza
- 2025: Continues to condition coalition support on advancing West Bank annexation
