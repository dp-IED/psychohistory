# Itamar Ben-Gvir

## Type
Far-right Israeli politician; National Security Minister (2022–present). Leader of Otzma Yehudit (Jewish Power) party.

## Significance for Forecasting

Ben-Gvir is the most hardline member of Netanyahu's coalition on ceasefire issues. As National Security Minister with a seat on the security cabinet, he can:

1. **Vote against ceasefire frameworks** in the security cabinet (though outvoted)
2. **Threaten coalition collapse** if a ceasefire is approved — his departure would leave Netanyahu without a Knesset majority
3. **Mobilize public opposition** through his constituency of religious-Zionist and settler activists

His baseline position: opposes ANY ceasefire with Hamas that does not involve the complete destruction of the organization. He opposed the November 2023 pause, the January 2025 ceasefire, and called for resumption of combat after the March 18 collapse.

## Key Dynamics

### Ceasefire Veto Leverage
- Ben-Gvir's leverage is **political, not procedural** — he cannot block a ceasefire in the security cabinet (he is one of ~12 members), but he can withdraw from the coalition, triggering early elections
- Netanyahu manages this by: (a) framing ceasefires as "temporary" or "phased" to give Ben-Gvir cover for staying, and (b) ensuring the coalition has votes from other parties before Ben-Gvir's departure
- **Threshold test**: If the Knesset opposition signals willingness to join a unity government to pass a ceasefire, Ben-Gvir's leverage is nullified (as happened in Jan 2025 — opposition leader Lapid offered a safety net)

### Ben-Gvir's Decision Rule
- **Opposes any ceasefire that leaves Hamas standing** — this is non-negotiable
- **Tolerates temporary pauses** (humanitarian, hostage-exchange) if framed as military necessity or tactical, not diplomatic
- **Can be outflanked** by a determined PM who (a) secures opposition support for the coalition, (b) frames the ceasefire as temporary, or (c) presents the ceasefire as forced by US pressure

### Post-March 18 Collapse
- After the March 2025 resumption of combat, Ben-Gvir was satisfied — his preferred policy (no ceasefire, full military operation) was in effect
- This meant the political cost for Netanyahu to reach ANY ceasefire by July 15 was very high: it would require either losing Ben-Gvir from the coalition or confronting a full coalition crisis
- **This is a structural variable**: when the far-right is satisfied, the status quo is a high-barrier environment for ceasefire diplomacy

## Key Events
- Oct 7, 2023: Hamas attack; Ben-Gvir calls for massive retaliation
- Nov 2023: Opposes humanitarian pause/pause deal
- Jan 2025: Opposes ceasefire framework; threatens coalition exit but stays after Netanyahu frames as temporary
- Mar 18, 2025: Welcomes resumption of combat; calls for permanent reoccupation of Gaza
- Active through 2025: Continues to oppose any diplomatic settlement that leaves Hamas with governance capability
