# Litani River Line / UN Resolution 1701

## Summary

The Litani River in southern Lebanon serves as the demarcation line for UN Security Council Resolution 1701 (2006), which ended the 2006 Lebanon War. The resolution called for: (1) cessation of hostilities, (2) Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon, (3) deployment of Lebanese Army and UNIFIL to the area south of the Litani River, (4) disarmament of all armed groups in Lebanon (i.e., Hezbollah). The Litani River runs approximately 30 km north of the Israel-Lebanon border, creating a buffer zone in southern Lebanon.

## Current Status (June 2026)

- **Israeli ground presence**: Israeli forces entered southern Lebanon during the post-Twelve-Day War escalation cycle (June 2025) and expanded operations during the Feb-March 2026 US-Iran war. The stated mission was degrading Hezbollah's military infrastructure south of the Litani.
- **UNIFIL**: The UN Interim Force in Lebanon remains deployed but has limited enforcement capacity. UNIFIL's mandate has been renewed but its monitoring capability is contested by Israeli operations.
- **Lebanese Army**: Under President Joseph Aoun (elected January 2025) and PM Nawaf Salam, Lebanon has sought to reassert sovereignty in the south but capacity is limited by the economic crisis.
- **Hezbollah position**: Post-Khamenei proxy autonomy increases Hezbollah's operational independence. Without IRGC command constraint, Hezbollah's posture south of the Litani is less predictable.

## Historical Precedent

- **2006 War**: Israel withdrew from southern Lebanon after 33 days of conflict, with UNIFIL deployment as the face-saving mechanism. Withdrawal was announced after a political decision, not a military victory.
- **2000 Withdrawal**: Israel unilaterally withdrew from southern Lebanon after 18 years of occupation (1982-2000). The withdrawal was verified by the UN as complete to the "Blue Line."
