# GCC / Gulf Politics

## Thread Arc

The Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC), founded May 25, 1981, comprises six monarchies: Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, and Bahrain. It is one of the world's most durable regional blocs, with zero member exits in 43 years. This thread tracks intra-GCC dynamics, structural tensions, and forecasting-relevant developments.

## Key Structural Dynamics

### Institutional Durability
- **100% membership retention** (1981-2026): Survived the 2017-2021 Qatar blockade, Iran-Iraq War (1980-88), Gulf War (1990-91), Iraq War (2003), Arab Spring (2011), Yemen War (2015-present), and US-Iran war (2026)
- **Collective security**: Peninsula Shield Force headquartered in Saudi Arabia; shared THAAD/Patriot missile defense architecture; intelligence cooperation against Iran/Houthi threats
- **Economic integration**: GCC common market (2008), customs union (2015), coordinated currency pegs to USD (except Kuwait), GCC-wide VAT implementation (2018)

### Intra-GCC Rivalries
- **Saudi-UAE competition**: OPEC+ quota disputes (2021, 2023-24), economic rivalry (NEOM vs Dubai, Vision 2030 vs UAE diversification), foreign policy divergence (Yemen withdrawal, Israel normalization track, Iran engagement)
- **Qatar blockcade (2017-2021)**: Saudi/UAE/Bahrain/Egypt severed ties with Qatar over alleged terrorism support and Iran alignment. AlUla Declaration (Jan 2021) restored relations. Qatar emerged stronger, with independent foreign policy (Taliban office, Hamas ties, Turkey alliance) and LNG-driven wealth
- **Oman as neutral broker**: Muscat's traditional mediation role (US-Iran backchannel, Yemen peace talks, GCC internal disputes) gives it outsized diplomatic influence
- **Kuwaiti consensus-building**: Kuwait often mediates GCC internal disputes; Emir plays traditional conciliatory role

### External Pressure Points
- **Iran normalization**: Post-June 14 peace deal creates new Gulf security architecture. GCC members diverge on engagement pace
- **US security guarantee uncertainty**: Trump administration's transactional approach to alliance commitments creates hedging incentives
- **China economic penetration**: Belt and Road investments, trade dependence, non-dollar settlement experiments (Saudi China oil trade)
- **Israel normalization**: Abraham Accords (2020) created de facto GCC-Israel alignment against Iran. Saudi normalization paused after Oct 7, 2023 but resumed in 2025-26 context
