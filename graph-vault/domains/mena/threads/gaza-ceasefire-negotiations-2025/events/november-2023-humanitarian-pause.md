## Summary

A 4-day humanitarian ceasefire (humanitarian pause) between Israel and Hamas, announced on November 22, 2023 and taking effect November 24. The pause was later extended to 7 days. Under the deal, 105 Israeli hostages were released in exchange for 240 Palestinian prisoners, and humanitarian aid was allowed into Gaza. Fighting resumed on November 30 when the pause expired.

This was the first mutually agreed halt in military engagement between Israel and Hamas since the October 7 attack. It qualifies as a "ceasefire" under standard Polymarket resolution criteria ("publicly announced and mutually agreed halt in military engagement") despite being temporary — the subsequent resumption of fighting did not retroactively invalidate it.

## Mediation Team

- Qatar — primary mediator, hosted negotiations
- Egypt — coordinated via Rafah crossing and security channels
- United States — applied diplomatic pressure on Israel

## Key Terms

- 4-day initial ceasefire (extended to 7 days)
- 50 women and children hostages released by Hamas (ultimately 105)
- 3 Palestinian prisoners released per each hostage (240 total)
- Humanitarian aid, fuel, and medical supplies allowed into Gaza
- Israeli military to pause operations in agreed zones

## Forecasting Significance

This event is the canonical example of the [[concepts/temporary-vs-enduring-ceasefire]] concept. A forecaster who interprets "ceasefire" as "enduring end to hostilities" would predict NO on a question about a 2023 ceasefire, missing the fact that the temporary humanitarian pause satisfies the resolution criteria. The event demonstrates:

1. Temporary pauses with specific exchange mechanisms count as ceasefires
2. A ceasefire is not invalidated by subsequent resumption of hostilities
3. Media and official descriptions as "humanitarian ceasefire" are strong evidence for resolution
4. Qatari/Egyptian mediation is the consistent structural pattern for Israel-Hamas ceasefire agreements, regardless of duration
