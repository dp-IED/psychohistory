## Summary

On March 18, 2025, Israel launched widespread aerial bombardments on Gaza, killing at least 591 people and effectively ending the January 2025 ceasefire that had held for approximately two months. The collapse was driven by Israel's assessment that Hamas had not been sufficiently degraded militarily. This reset the diplomatic clock and required 6+ months of accumulated pressure to rebuild a ceasefire framework — eventually achieved October 8, 2025 (see [[events/gaza-october-ceasefire-2025]]).

## Aftermath

Between March 18 and the October 8 ceasefire:
- April-June 2025: Diplomatic attention consumed by the Iran-Israel Twelve-Day War
- July 21 2025: 28-nation joint statement calling for end to war
- August 22 2025: Famine confirmed in Gaza City (IPC Phase 5)
- September 2025: Trump 20-point peace proposal; Israel strikes Hamas in Doha
- September 16 2025: UN genocide finding against Israel
- October 8 2025: Final ceasefire announced

## Forecasting Significance

The collapse demonstrates that ceasefires in asymmetric conflicts driven by incompatible war aims are fragile absent structural resolution of the underlying conflict drivers. The [[concepts/diplomatic-pressure-tipping-point]] pattern shows that a collapsed ceasefire requires rebuilding diplomatic infrastructure from scratch — a process that took 6+ months in this case.
