## Overview

For decades, Iran and Israel fought through proxies (Hezbollah, Hamas, Houthis) and covert operations (assassinations of Iranian nuclear scientists, cyberattacks). This changed in April 2024 when they struck each other's territory directly for the first time. The escalation cycle accelerated through October 2024 and culminated in the June 2025 Twelve-Day War — a full-scale conventional conflict that ended with a US-brokered ceasefire 12 days after it began. The arc demonstrates a pattern of progressive escalation from covert conflict through direct exchange to full war terminated by superpower mediation.

## Timeline / Key Events

### 2024-Q2: First Direct Exchange
- **April 1, 2024**: Israel attacked the Iranian consulate in Damascus, killing 16 including two Iranian generals. [[2024-Q2]]
- **April 13, 2024**: Iran launched retaliatory drones and missiles at Israel — first direct Iranian attack on Israeli territory. Most intercepted by Israeli, US, UK, and Jordanian defenses. [[2024-Q2]]
- **April 19, 2024**: Israel conducted calibrated airstrikes near Isfahan, Iran — designed to re-establish deterrence without triggering full war. [[2024-Q2]]

### 2024-Q3: Assassination in Tehran
- **July 31, 2024**: Ismail Haniyeh (Hamas political leader) assassinated at his residence in Tehran hours after Iran's presidential inauguration. Israel did not officially claim responsibility. [[2024-Q3]]
- The killing on Iranian soil, of a guest at a state function, represented a significant escalation of covert operations within Iran.

### 2024-Q4: Second Direct Exchange
- **October 1, 2024**: Iran launched ballistic missiles at Israel in response to Israeli assassinations of Nasrallah and Haniyeh. [[2024-Q4]]
- **October 26, 2024**: Israel launched airstrikes against Iran in retaliation. [[2024-Q4]]
- The 2024-Q4 summary noted the "fall of Assad in December reshaped the regional order, weakening Iran's 'Axis of Resistance'" — a structural shift that left Iran strategically exposed.

### 2025-Q1: Nuclear Diplomacy Fails
- **January 15, 2025**: Israel-Hamas ceasefire (later collapsed March 18). [[2025-Q1]]
- **March 2025**: Trump administration gave Iran two months to reach a nuclear deal, with the deadline expiring the day before Israel's June strikes.
- **May 31, 2025**: IAEA reported Iran had enough enriched uranium for nine warheads.
- **June 12, 2025**: IAEA Board of Governors passed a resolution declaring Iran non-compliant with nuclear obligations — first such finding since September 2005.

### 2025-Q2: The Twelve-Day War
- **June 13, 2025**: Israel launched Operation Rising Lion — large-scale airstrikes on Iranian nuclear facilities, military bases, and command infrastructure. IRGC commander Hossein Salami and multiple nuclear scientists killed. Iran retaliated with ballistic missiles and drones (Operation True Promise III). [[2025-Q2]]
- **June 14-21, 2025**: Daily exchanges of strikes. Israel targeted nuclear facilities, missile launchers, IRGC command centers. Iran struck Israeli cities including Tel Aviv, Haifa, Bat Yam, Beersheba.
- **June 22, 2025**: **US direct entry** — B-2 Spirit bombers struck three Iranian nuclear sites (Fordow, Natanz, Isfahan). Iran retaliated by striking US bases in Qatar and Iraq.
- **June 23, 2025**: Trump announced ceasefire agreement. Israeli security cabinet ratified same day. War duration: 12 days.
- **June 24, 2025**: Ceasefire took effect. Last missile exchanges occurred during the morning.

> **THREE-DATE TRACKING**: Announcement date = June 23 (Trump's announcement + Israel PMO confirmation). Ratification date = June 23 (Israeli security cabinet, same day — crisis-accelerated). Effective date = June 24 (halt in military engagement). Unlike the Gaza ceasefire process which had a 1-day gap between announcement and cabinet ratification, the Iran-Israel ceasefire had announcement and ratification on the same day due to the urgency of US direct entry terminating an active 12-day war. See [[concepts/ceasefire-announcement-ratification-gap]] for the general pattern: the announcement-ratification gap is compressed to 0 days under crisis conditions (active war, US entry) and widens to 1-2 days under standard negotiation conditions.

## Key Dynamics

### 1. Progressive Escalation Ladder
The conflict moved through clear thresholds:
1. Proxy warfare (pre-2024)
2. Covert operations inside Iran (nuclear scientist assassinations, cyber)
3. Attack on diplomatic facility (Damascus consulate, April 2024)
4. Direct state-on-state retaliation (April 2024)
5. Assassination on Iranian soil of a foreign leader (Haniyeh, July 2024)
6. Ballistic missile exchanges (October 2024)
7. Full-scale conventional war with nuclear site strikes (June 2025)
8. Superpower direct entry (US strikes, June 22)
9. Ceasefire mediated by the entering superpower (June 23-24)

Each step normalized behavior that was previously considered escalatory.

### 2. Superpower Entry as Ceasefire Catalyst
US direct entry (step 8) was both the peak escalation AND the trigger for termination. When US forces entered combat, three dynamics converged:
- **Credible escalation threat**: Iran now faced the world's largest military
- **Mediation leverage**: The US could offer Iran de-escalation terms
- **Political imperative**: Trump needed to control escalation to avoid a protracted Middle East war

This pattern — superpower entry creating the off-ramp — is analogous to the [[great-power-multilateral-intervention]] concept but with a distinct termination mechanism.

### 3. Weakened Deterrence Preceding War
Iran's Axis of Resistance was severely degraded before the June war: Hezbollah weakened in Lebanon, Hamas degraded in Gaza, Assad regime fallen in Syria, and Iran's economy under sanctions. This strategic weakening may have made war more rather than less likely — a "use it or lose it" dynamic where the nuclear program became the last remaining lever of Iranian power.

### 4. IAEA as Casus Belli Precondition
The IAEA's non-compliance resolution on June 12 (the day before the war) provided legal cover for Israel's strike. The diplomatic pathway was exhausted just before the military one began — a consistent pattern in pre-war periods.

## Forecasting Significance

1. **Watch for "escalation ladder" patterns** in state-on-state conflicts: each threshold-crossing event increases the probability of the next. After April 2024, the question wasn't whether Israel and Iran would fight directly again, but when and at what scale.

2. **Superpower patron entry does not always mean prolonged war**: The US entering the Twelve-Day War terminated it within 48 hours. This contradicts the assumption that superpower involvement lengthens conflicts. Key conditions: the superpower must have both escalation dominance AND mediation leverage with both sides.

3. **Nuclear latency as a war trigger**: The period when a state approaches but has not yet acquired nuclear weapons is particularly dangerous ("nuclear threshold crisis"). The IAEA finding of non-compliance was the trigger for preemptive action.

4. **The "use it or lose it" dynamic in degraded proxies**: When a state's proxy network is dismantled, it may be more willing to escalate directly rather than more constrained — the opposite of conventional deterrence theory.

## Cross-Domain Implications: Post-War Iran and Ceasefire Architecture

The Twelve-Day War's aftermath continued to shape Middle East dynamics through 2026, creating a new interaction between Iran's weakened posture and the Gaza ceasefire implementation:

1. **Khamenei succession uncertainty**: Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei (born 1939, age 86-87) was largely absent from public view following the war. His health — and the succession dynamics within the IRGC-civilian leadership — became a structural uncertainty for the entire ceasefire architecture. The IRGC's degraded command capacity meant regional proxies (Hezbollah in Lebanon, Houthis in Yemen, Iraqi Shia militias) operated with increased autonomy, creating risks of ceasefire-violating actions that Tehran could not control or disavow.

2. **Degraded nuclear bargaining position**: The destruction of Fordow, Natanz, and Isfahan enrichment facilities eliminated Iran's near-term nuclear breakout capability. This weakened Iran's negotiating position in any future nuclear framework talks, but also removed the "nuclear threshold crisis" trigger for renewed Israeli strikes — a paradoxical stability-inducing effect.

3. **Proxy autonomy as ceasefire risk**: With IRGC command-and-control degraded (Salami killed, nuclear sites destroyed, leadership focused on internal survival), Hezbollah and Houthi actions became less constrained by Tehran. A Hezbollah rocket attack on northern Israel or a Houthi Red Sea ship strike during the Gaza ceasefire period could trigger Israeli retaliation that escalates beyond what Iran would have authorized pre-war. This **proxy autonomy risk** is a distinct dynamic from the pre-war period where Tehran actively constrained proxies.

4. **Gulf state recalibration**: The war weakened Iran's conventional deterrent and its nuclear threat simultaneously. Gulf states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Bahrain) adjusted their regional positioning — some accelerated normalization with Israel (seeing Iran as structurally weakened), others hedged by maintaining diplomatic channels with Tehran (fearing long-term instability if Iran collapses into succession crisis). This created an unusually fragmented Gulf alignment that affected reconstruction funding for Gaza.

5. **Attention bandwidth competition**: The Iran succession question competed with Gaza ceasefire implementation, French elections, and US midterm campaigning for diplomatic and media attention — a classic [[domains/global/concepts/attention-scarcity-diplomatic-bandwidth]] pattern where multiple concurrent crises reduce the bandwidth available for any single one.

**Forecasting implication**: Any Gaza ceasefire stability question with post-2025 cutoff must model Iran's weakened proxy control as an independent risk factor — not because Iran is more aggressive, but because it has less control over its proxies than before the war. Proxy autonomy is the opposite of proxy escalation: it is harder to detect and harder to de-escalate through the normal Tehran channel.
