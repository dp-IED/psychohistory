# Israel-Hamas War and Ceasefire (Oct 2023 – Mar 2025)

## Overview

The most intense military conflict between Israel and Hamas since Israel's
founding. Triggered by the Hamas-led attack on October 7, 2023 (~1,200 Israelis
killed, ~250 taken hostage). Israel responded with a sustained air and ground
campaign in Gaza that lasted over 15 months. The war concluded with a phased
ceasefire announced January 15–17, 2025, taking effect January 19, and
ultimately collapsing on March 18, 2025 when Israel resumed airstrikes.

## Timeline

### Phase 1: October 7 Attack and Initial Response (Oct–Dec 2023)

- **Oct 7, 2023**: Hamas-led attack on Israel. Largest single-day massacre of
  Jews since the Holocaust. ~1,200 killed, ~250 taken hostage.
- **Oct 8**: Israel formally declares war. IDF begins mobilization.
- **Oct 27**: Israeli ground invasion of Gaza begins.
- **Nov 24–30**: First temporary ceasefire. Hostage/prisoner exchange: 105
  hostages released for 240 Palestinian prisoners. Mediated by Qatar, Egypt,
  US.
- **Dec 1**: Fighting resumes after talks break down.

### Phase 2: Stalemate and Escalation (Jan–Aug 2024)

- **Jan–Mar 2024**: Ceasefire negotiations continue in Doha and Cairo. Hamas
  demands permanent ceasefire; Netanyahu refuses.
- **Mar 18**: First direct Israel-Hamas talks since Dec 2023.
- **Apr 1**: Israeli strike kills 7 World Central Kitchen aid workers. Biden
  warns Netanyahu to seek immediate ceasefire.
- **May 5**: Hamas accepts a three-phase ceasefire proposal drafted by US,
  Egypt, and Qatar.
- **May–Jun**: Netanyahu rejects permanent ceasefire. UNSC Resolution 2735
  endorses the proposal (June 10).
- **Jul–Aug**: Negotiations stall. Sinwar becomes more intransigent.

### Phase 3: Regional Escalation and Leadership Decapitation (Sep–Nov 2024)

- **Sep 2024**: Israel escalates against Hezbollah (pager attacks, Nasrallah
  assassination, ground invasion of southern Lebanon).
- **Oct 1**: Iran launches ~180 ballistic missiles at Israel.
- **Oct 16**: Israel kills Yahya Sinwar, Hamas leader in Gaza — the mastermind
  of Oct 7. This removes the main obstacle to a ceasefire.
- **Nov 5**: Donald Trump wins the 2024 US presidential election.
- **Nov 27**: Israel-Hezbollah ceasefire takes effect, removing the northern
  front as a complicating factor.

### Phase 4: Transition-Window Diplomacy (Nov 2024 – Jan 2025)

- **Nov 2024**: Trump (president-elect) signals he wants a hostage deal BEFORE
  his inauguration on Jan 20, 2025. Trump envoy publicly threatens Hamas with
  unspecified retaliation if hostages not released.
- **Dec 2024**: Netanyahu decides to travel to Cairo to advance ceasefire
  talks. Negotiating gaps narrow. Both Biden and Trump administrations apply
  simultaneous pressure on Israel — a rare bipartisan alignment.
- **Dec–Jan**: Israel achieves key military objectives in Gaza — Hamas
  brigades degraded, Sinwar dead, Rafah captured. War exhaustion sets in.
- **Jan 13, 2025**: Reports that parties are close to agreement.
- **Jan 15, 2025**: Israel and Hamas accept a three-phase ceasefire proposal —
  a variation of the May 2024 framework.
- **Jan 17, 2025**: Israel's Security Cabinet and full Cabinet approve and sign
  the deal.
- **Jan 19, 2025**: Ceasefire takes effect. First phase: six-week halt,
  33 hostages released, prisoner exchanges, humanitarian aid, phased Israeli
  withdrawal.

### Phase 5: Fragile Implementation and Collapse (Jan–Mar 2025)

- **Feb 2025**: First phase implemented with eight rounds of exchanges.
  Ceasefire violations claimed by both sides.
- **Mar 1**: Hamas rejects Israel's proposal to extend Phase 1, insists on
  Phase 2 (permanent ceasefire).
- **Mar 2**: Israel halts aid entry to Gaza.
- **Mar 9**: Israel halts electricity supply to Gaza.
- **Mar 18**: Israel launches surprise airstrikes on Gaza, breaking the
  ceasefire. Netanyahu declares "resumed combat in full force."

## Causal Analysis: Why the Ceasefire Happened in Jan 2025

### Driving factors for YES (ceasefire announcement by Jan 17):

1. **US presidential transition created a forcing function**: Trump's
   inauguration (Jan 20) deadline concentrated minds. Trump wanted the hostage
   issue resolved before taking office. This created the narrowest possible
   window for agreement.
2. **Biden-Trump bipartisan alignment**: Both administrations simultaneously
   pressured Israel. This removed Netanyahu's ability to delay by betting on
   a more favorable US administration after Jan 20.
3. **Sinwar's death (Oct 16, 2024)**: The most intransigent actor on the Hamas
   side was killed. This removed the primary obstacle to a deal within Hamas.
4. **Hezbollah ceasefire (Nov 27, 2024)**: Israel's northern front was
   neutralized, allowing Israel to negotiate from a position of strength rather
   than needing a Gaza ceasefire to avoid a two-front war.
5. **Military objectives largely achieved**: By Jan 2025, Israel had degraded
   Hamas's military capability, killed its leadership, captured Rafah, and
   established a buffer zone. Further military action had diminishing returns.
6. **War exhaustion**: After 15 months, Israeli domestic support for continuing
   the war was declining. Hostage families were a vocal political force.
7. **Netanyahu's political incentives**: A deal let Netanyahu claim victory
   (hostage return, Hamas degraded) and pivot to his coalition's preferred
   priorities (West Bank annexation, judicial reform).

### Countervailing factors (could have prevented ceasefire):

1. **Netanyahu's long-standing opposition to a permanent ceasefire**: He had
   repeatedly rejected permanent ceasefire terms throughout 2024. The Jan deal
   was a variation of the same May 2024 framework he'd previously rejected.
2. **Coalition fragility**: Far-right coalition partners (Ben-Gvir, Smotrich)
   opposed any ceasefire. Netanyahu risked coalition collapse.
3. **Hamas leadership fractures**: Post-Sinwar leadership was untested. It
   could have fragmented instead of unifying around a deal.

### Why YES was the right forecast:

The transition-window dynamic was the decisive factor that the countervailing
factors could not overcome. Netanyahu had rejected the same deal for 8 months;
what changed was the US election outcome. The incoming Trump administration's
deadline and dual-pressure (Biden + Trump) created conditions so uniquely
favorable that even Netanyahu's coalition constraints were navigable. The
question's narrow window (Jan 16–17) was well-calibrated to capture the
cabinet approval phase after the Jan 15 acceptance.
