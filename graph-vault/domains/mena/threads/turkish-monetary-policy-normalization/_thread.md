# Turkish Monetary Policy Normalization (2023-present)

## Overview

This thread tracks Turkey's transition from the unorthodox monetary policy regime of 2018-2023 (characterized by rate cuts despite hyperinflation) to an orthodox tightening cycle beginning in June 2023. This normalization is the single most important macroeconomic development in Turkey and directly determines the outcome of prediction market questions about TCMB rate decisions, lira depreciation, and Turkish inflation.

## Thread Status

**Status: active** — The normalization is ongoing. Key forecasting questions include: (1) Will the TCMB continue raising/holding rates at upcoming meetings? (2) When will the first rate cut occur? (3) Will political pressure re-emerge?

## The Unorthodox Era (2018-May 2023)

### Cause Structure
- President Erdogan holds an unorthodox view that high interest rates cause high inflation (contrary to conventional economics)
- Over 2019-2023, Erdogan fired three central bank governors for either refusing to cut rates or cutting too slowly
- The TCMB's credibility collapsed as policy rate (8.5%) was set far below inflation (85%+ in Oct 2022)
- Turkish lira depreciated from ~5.5/USD (2018) to ~23/USD (mid-2023)
- Foreign investors fled Turkish markets; domestic savers fled lira for gold and FX

### Key Lesson for Forecasting
An unorthodox central bank can defy economic gravity for years through capital controls, FX intervention, and regulatory pressure on banks. But the cost is cumulative: reserves deplete, inflation expectations de-anchor, and eventually the regime becomes unsustainable even for its political sponsor. The trigger for policy change was not economic collapse but electoral timing — Erdogan needed Simsek's credibility to attract capital after barely winning the May 2023 election.

## The Normalization (June 2023-present)

### Phase 1: Initial Tightening — Erkan Governorship (June 2023-Feb 2024)
- **Rate path**: Policy rate raised from 8.5% to 45% over 8 meetings
- **Key dynamics**: 
  - Mehmet Simsek as Finance Minister provided political cover
  - Hafize Gaye Erkan as first female governor, credible technocrat
  - Market welcomed the pivot: CDS compressed from ~700bps to ~300bps
  - Foreign capital returned slowly, partially reversing years of outflows
  - Rate hikes were accompanied by macroprudential simplification (exit from complex FX-protected deposit scheme)

### Phase 2: Continuity — Karahan Governorship (Feb 2024-present)
- **Governor transition**: Erkan resigned amid nepotism allegations; Karahan (her deputy) appointed, signaling continuity
- **Continued tightening**: Rates raised to 50% in 2024 as inflation persistence proved sticky
- **Key dynamics**:
  - Karahan maintained the hawkish stance; market barely reacted to the governor change
  - Inflation peaked around 75% in mid-2024, then began a gradual decline
  - Disinflation was slower than initial projections due to sticky services inflation, wage indexation, and lira passthrough
  - The policy rate exceeded inflation in real terms (positive real rates) for the first time in years by late 2024
  - Net FX reserves recovered from deeply negative (swap-adjusted) to positive territory
  - Foreign portfolio inflows continued, though at a measured pace

### Phase 3: Sticky Disinflation and Watchful Holding (2025)
- With inflation declining toward 25-30% range but still well above target (5%), the TCMB faced the question of when to start cutting
- Each monthly meeting brought the cumulative-hike-or-hold decision
- The TCMB's communication emphasized data dependence, with a bias toward maintaining tight conditions until inflation expectations converged toward targets
- The key risk: premature easing would trigger lira depreciation, re-anchor expectations upward, and destroy the credibility built over 18 months

## Causal Framework for Forecasting TCMB Rate Decisions

### Primary drivers of rate decisions (ordered by importance):

1. **Inflation trajectory**: The TCMB's reaction function is dominated by the inflation outlook. When monthly inflation prints exceed expectations, the probability of a hike at the next meeting rises sharply. When inflation declines on trend and expectations converge, the probability of a cut rises.

2. **Inflation expectations**: The TCMB surveys market participants and households monthly. If 12-month-ahead inflation expectations are declining toward the policy rate, the TCMB can hold. If they are rising or sticky, further tightening is needed.

3. **Lira pressure**: Sudden lira depreciation forces the TCMB to act. A 5%+ monthly depreciation typically triggers a rate hike or liquidity tightening at the next meeting, even if inflation data alone wouldn't warrant it.

4. **Political context**: The status of [[mehmet-simsek]] as Finance Minister is the political bellwether. If Simsek is sidelined, resigns, or signals frustration, the normalization is at risk and rate decisions become unpredictable.

5. **Reserve adequacy**: The TCMB's net FX reserve position determines how much it can defend the lira without rate action. Reserves recovered from -$50B (swap-adjusted, mid-2023) to positive territory by late 2024, giving more policy space.

6. **External environment**: EM capital flows, Fed policy, and risk appetite affect the lira and capital flows, indirectly influencing TCMB policy room.

### Key leading indicators:
- CPI inflation (monthly, released ~3rd of each month)
- TCMB Survey of Expectations (15th of each month) — inflation expectations, policy rate expectation
- Net FX reserves (weekly)
- USD/TRY exchange rate (daily)
- 5-year CDS spread
- Simsek interview frequency and tone
- Parliament calendar — potential no-confidence or by-election dynamics affect Erdogan's willingness to sustain orthodoxy

## Forecasting Pattern for This Thread

When a question asks "Will the Turkish Central Bank raise interest rates at its [Month] meeting?":

1. **Check if the TCMB is in a tightening, holding, or cutting phase.** The TCMB cycles through extended phases of hiking (2023-early 2024), holding (mid-2024 through disinflation), and eventually cutting. Determine which phase you're in from the most recent meeting's decision and statement.

2. **Read the most recent TCMB policy statement.** The TCMB statement includes a paragraph on the inflation outlook and a forward-looking assessment. Key phrases:
   - "Decisive tightening" / "further monetary tightening" → more hikes likely
   - "Maintain tight monetary stance" → hold likely
   - "Careful stance" / "monitoring" → hold, potential pivot later
   - "Limited room" / "data dependent" → approaching a pivot

3. **Check the most recent Inflation Report.** Karahan's quarterly press conference is the closest thing to Fed-style forward guidance. His inflation forecast trajectory signals the rate path.

4. **Verify Simsek is still in his position.** If Simsek is no longer Finance Minister, the normalization is on borrowed time and rate decisions become politically driven.

5. **Assess the CPI print for the most recent month.** If inflation printed above expectations, a hike is more likely. If below expectations, a hold or cut is more likely.

6. **Check lira movement.** A 5%+ depreciation since the last meeting significantly increases hike probability.

7. **Apply the central bank rate decision procedure:**
   - If inflation is above target and declining OR stable, the TCMB holds
   - If inflation is above target and accelerating, the TCMB hikes
   - If inflation is converging to target and expectations are anchored, the TCMB cuts
   - The TCMB never surprises directionally — it telegraphs through its monthly statements and quarterly reports

## Key Differences from Fed Forward Guidance

| Feature | Federal Reserve | TCMB |
|---------|---------------|------|
| Meeting frequency | 8x/year | 12x/year (monthly) |
| Forward guidance | Dot plot, press conference, minutes | Statement, quarterly Inflation Report |
| Communication style | Explicit, structured, pre-telegraphs | More opaque, less detailed |
| Political independence | High (de facto and de jure) | Low (instrument independence only) |
| Primary constraint | Dual mandate (price stability + max employment) | Price stability + lira stability |
| Surprise risk | Near-zero for direction | Moderate — political override possible |
| Governor turnover risk | Low (Powell served full term) | High (3 governors fired 2019-2023) |
