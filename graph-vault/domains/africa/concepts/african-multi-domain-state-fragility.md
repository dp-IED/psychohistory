# African Multi-Domain State Fragility

## Core Insight
African political forecasting requires a multi-domain framework that integrates three distinct fragility dimensions — electoral, conflict, and governance — that interact non-linearly. Unlike Western democracies where a single dimension (e.g., economic policy or candidate quality) dominates election outcomes, African political risk is often driven by simultaneous pressures across all three domains.

## Three-Dimension Model

### 1. Electoral Fragility
- Post-election violence risk (Kenya 2007/2017, Ethiopia 2005, Côte d'Ivoire 2010)
- Incumbent electoral manipulation (Uganda, Rwanda, Chad, Cameroon)
- Opposition boycott dynamics (Ethiopia 2021, Senegal 2024 delay)
- Courts as political battleground (Kenya 2017 nullification, Malawi 2020 annulment)

### 2. Conflict Fragility
- Civil war / insurgency (Sudan, DRC, Sahel, Ethiopia Tigray, Mozambique Cabo Delgado)
- Coup contagion (Mali 2020, Burkina Faso 2022, Niger 2023, Gabon 2023)
- Ethnic militia / communal violence (Nigeria Middle Belt, Ethiopia Oromia/Amhara, Sudan Darfur)
- Separatist movements (Cameroon Ambazonia, Nigeria IPOB, Ethiopia TPLF)

### 3. Governance Fragility
- Debt crisis (Ghana, Zambia, Ethiopia defaulted 2022-2024)
- Currency collapse (Nigeria, Egypt, Ethiopia)
- Service delivery failure (Eskom in SA, fuel in Nigeria)
- Succession uncertainty (long-serving leaders: Biya 42yrs, Kagame 24yrs, Isaias 31yrs, Obiang 44yrs)

## Interaction Effects (Key Forecasting Insight)
The most dangerous scenarios involve **two or three dimensions activating simultaneously**:
- **Electoral + Conflict overlap**: Pre-election violence escalating into insurgency (Kenya 2007 → ICC cases, Côte d'Ivoire 2010 → civil war)
- **Conflict + Governance overlap**: Civil war + debt default (Sudan, Ethiopia) creating a vicious cycle where neither resolution is possible without the other
- **All three**: State collapse scenario (Somalia 1991-2012, Libya 2011, Sudan 2023-present)

## Forecasting Application
When forecasting an African political event, check all three dimensions:

1. Is there an election within 12 months? → Check electoral fragility indicators (vote manipulation risk, security force politicization, opposition coalescence)
2. Is there an active insurgency or coup risk? → Check conflict fragility indicators (military budget increases, extra-judicial killings, ethnic polarization)
3. Is there a debt/currency crisis? → Check governance fragility indicators (IMF program status, reserve depletion, subsidy reform backlash)

The **highest-signal indicator** for near-term political crisis in Africa is when two dimensions are simultaneously deteriorating: e.g., election approaching AND currency reserves falling → high probability of post-election violence.

## Known Exceptions
- Botswana, Ghana, Senegal, Namibia, and South Africa have stronger institutional buffers (independent electoral commissions, professional militaries, free press) that reduce fragility despite occasional governance stress.
- North Africa (Egypt, Tunisia, Morocco, Algeria) has different dynamics shaped by Arab Spring aftermath, military-state fusion, and Mediterranean geopolitics — better covered by MENA domain.
