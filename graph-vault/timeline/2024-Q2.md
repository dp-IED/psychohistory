# 2024-Q2 Summary (April 1, 2024 - June 30, 2024)

## Geopolitics & International Relations

### Middle East

- **April 1** -- Israel attacked the Iranian consulate in Damascus, killing 16 people, including two Iranian generals. This marked a significant escalation in Israel's campaign against Iranian military presence in Syria (Syrian Observatory for Human Rights, April 3, 2024).

- **April 13** -- Iran launched retaliatory strikes against Israel with drones and missiles, marking the first direct Iranian military attack on Israeli territory. Most were intercepted by Israeli, US, UK, and Jordanian defenses (New York Post, April 13, 2024).

- **April 19** -- Israel conducted airstrikes against Iran near Isfahan in response to Iran's April 13 attack, in a calibrated strike that appeared designed to avoid full-scale war while re-establishing deterrence (CNN, April 19, 2024).

- **May 20** -- ICC Chief Prosecutor Karim Khan sought arrest warrants for Israeli PM Benjamin Netanyahu, Defense Minister Yoav Gallant, and Hamas leaders Yahya Sinwar, Ismail Haniyeh, and Mohammed Deif for alleged war crimes and crimes against humanity related to the October 7 attacks and the subsequent Gaza war (BBC News, May 20, 2024).

- **May 24** -- The International Court of Justice ruled that Israel must halt its military offensive in Rafah, southern Gaza (BBC News, May 24, 2024).

- **May 28** -- Spain, Ireland, and Norway formally recognized the State of Palestine, a significant diplomatic step that brought the total number of UN member states recognizing Palestine to over 140 (BBC News, May 28, 2024).

- **June 12** -- A UN Commission of Inquiry found that both Israel and Hamas committed war crimes since October 7, the first such UN inquiry into the conflict (The Guardian, June 12, 2024).

### Iran

- **May 19** -- Iranian President Ebrahim Raisi, Foreign Minister Hossein Amir-Abdollahian, and seven others were killed in a helicopter crash near the Azerbaijan-Iran border in the Varzaqan region. The crash occurred in foggy conditions (BBC News, May 20, 2024).

### Americas

- **April 5** -- Ecuadorian police raided the Mexican embassy in Quito to arrest former Vice President Jorge Glas, who had been granted political asylum by Mexico. Mexico and Nicaragua broke diplomatic relations with Ecuador over the violation of the Vienna Convention (Primicias, April 6, 2024).

- **April 25** -- Haiti's Transitional Presidential Council took power as the new head of state following the resignation of acting PM Ariel Henry, amid ongoing gang violence and instability (Le Nouvelliste, April 25, 2024).

- **May 5** -- José Raúl Mulino was elected President of Panama (Reuters, May 5, 2024).

- **May 19** -- Incumbent President Luis Abinader was re-elected for a second term in the Dominican Republic (BBC, May 20, 2024).

- **June 2** -- Claudia Sheinbaum was elected President of Mexico, becoming the first woman to lead the country (The New York Times, June 2, 2024).

- **June 26** -- A failed coup attempt in La Paz, Bolivia, was led by former General Commander Juan Jose Zuniga. President Luis Arce called on the population to mobilize in defense of democracy, and the attempt was quickly suppressed (CNN, June 26, 2024).

### Europe

- **April 6** -- Peter Pellegrini was elected President of Slovakia in the second round of the presidential election (Politico, April 6, 2024).

- **April 9** -- Simon Harris became Ireland's youngest Taoiseach (prime minister) after Leo Varadkar's resignation, following a Dail vote of 88-69 (RTÉ News, April 9, 2024).

- **June 6-9** -- European Parliament elections: The center-right EPP remained the largest group, but far-right parties made significant gains, dealing defeats to French President Macron's alliance and German Chancellor Scholz's SPD (AP News, June 9, 2024).

### Ukraine War

The US aid breakthrough in April reshaped the war's trajectory. However, the six-month aid gap (Oct 2023-Apr 2024) had lasting effects and Russia maintained tactical initiative.

- **Apr 20**: US House passes $61B Ukraine aid package after months of delay. The 6-month gap (Oct 2023-Apr 2024) had direct battlefield consequences: enabled Avdiivka's fall, depleted air defense interceptors, and signaled to Moscow that Western support was fragile.
- **May 10**: Russia opens new front in northern Kharkiv Oblast, advancing toward Vovchansk. The offensive forces Ukraine to redeploy troops from other sectors, demonstrating Russia's advantage in force generation.
- **May-Jun**: Russia makes slow but steady gains in Donetsk Oblast — Chasiv Yar, Ocheretyne, and other settlements fall to grinding Russian assaults.
- **Jun 25**: EU formally opens accession negotiations with Ukraine and Moldova.
- **Jun**: Russia demands withdrawal of Ukrainian troops from Donetsk and Luhansk as precondition for peace talks. Ukraine rejects the demand.

**FORECASTING SIGNIFICANCE**: Q2 2024 demonstrated the central paradox of 2024 ceasefire forecasting: the US aid passage prevented Ukrainian collapse but did not restore Ukrainian offensive capability. The war remained in positional attrition — Russia could not achieve a decisive breakthrough, Ukraine could not launch a counteroffensive. No diplomatic framework existed. The structural barriers to ceasefire remained intact.

## Economics & Monetary Policy

### [[federal-reserve-system]]: Late Plateau Deepens — Hold Continues, Dot Plot Hawkshifts

**May 1** — The [[federal-open-market-committee]] holds the federal funds rate at 5.25-5.50% at its May meeting, maintaining the cycle peak for the 6th consecutive meeting. Chair [[jerome-powell]] acknowledges that progress on inflation has stalled, stating the Fed needs "greater confidence" that inflation is moving sustainably toward 2% before cutting. The statement explicitly notes the lack of further progress on inflation in recent months — a darker tone than the March statement. Powell rules out the next rate move being a hike, stating the current policy rate is "sufficiently restrictive" to bring inflation down. (Source: Federal Reserve Press Release, May 1, 2024)

**CPI trajectory**: April CPI (May 15 release) reads 3.4% YoY, down from 3.5% but above the 3.4% consensus. Core CPI falls to 3.6% — the lowest since April 2021 — but shelter costs remain stubbornly high at 5.5%. May CPI (June 12 release) falls to 3.3% YoY, with core dropping to 3.4%. The month-over-month reading for May core CPI is 0.2% — the smallest since 2021 — suggesting disinflation is resuming. (Source: Bureau of Labor Statistics, May-June 2024)

**June 12** — The June FOMC meeting produces the most consequential signal of the quarter: the dot plot (June SEP) shifts from a median of 3 cuts in 2024 (March SEP) to **1 cut in 2024**. Four participants project no cuts at all in 2024. The median fed funds rate projection for end-2024 moves from 4.50-4.75% to 5.00-5.25%. Powell's press conference emphasizes the "modest further progress" on inflation and repeats that the Committee needs to see "more good data" before cutting. The rate is held at 5.25-5.50% (7-2 vote, Barkin and Kashkari opposing the hold in favor of a hike? — No, actual unanimous 12-0, though internal SEP projections were split). (Source: Federal Reserve Press Release, June 12, 2024; SEP June 2024)

**FORECASTING SIGNIFICANCE**: The June SEP shift from 3 cuts to 1 cut was the critical signal that established the monetary policy baseline for the next 6+ months. After this meeting:
- **A July hike was structurally impossible**: The Fed had spent 11 months at 5.25-5.50%. A hike would require initiating a new tightening cycle — a multi-meeting process of new forward guidance that had zero foundation in any FOMC communication. The SEP showed the median member projecting 1 cut in 2024 (not 0, not a hike). The probability of a hike at the July FOMC meeting was effectively zero.
- **A September cut became the live meeting**: Markets priced the July meeting as a guaranteed hold and September as the first possible cut. The only uncertainty was magnitude (25bp vs 50bp).
- This baseline correctly predicts the July hold and the September 50bp cut (which was larger than the 25bp guided, but directionally correct). See [[domains/economics/concepts/monetary-policy-cycle-phases#Phase 3: Late Plateau (6+ months at peak, or plateau + declining inflation signal)]].

### Other Central Banks

- **April 11** — [[domains/europe/entities/european-central-bank]] holds rates at 4.50%, but signals a June cut is increasingly likely as eurozone inflation falls toward 2.4%. The ECB's measured communication stands in contrast to the Fed's caution. (Source: ECB Press Release, April 11, 2024)
- **June 6** — ECB cuts rates by 25bp to 4.25% — the first cut since September 2019 and the first cut in the cycle among major advanced-economy central banks. President [[domains/europe/entities/christine-lagarde]] states the decision is based on the inflation outlook and "lacks commitment to a particular rate path." (Source: ECB Press Release, June 6, 2024)
- **June 20** — Bank of England holds rates at 5.25%, with a 7-2 vote split (2 members favoring a cut) as UK inflation falls to 2.0% — the BoE's target. The MPC signals the policy stance is restrictive enough to bring inflation sustainably to target. (Source: BoE Press Release, June 20, 2024)

**Global rate cycle divergence**: Q2 2024 saw the first major divergence in the global monetary cycle: the ECB cut while the Fed held and the BoJ was considering a hike. This divergence — structurally driven by different inflation compositions (eurozone inflation driven more by energy, US inflation more by services and shelter) — would shape FX markets and cross-border capital flows through H2 2024. The ECB cut ahead of the Fed for the first time since 2016.
