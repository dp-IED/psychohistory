### Reasoning

**Two conditions must BOTH hold — each is structurally improbable:**

**Condition 1: A single right-wing candidate must consolidate above ~45%.** Brazil's right wing is deeply fragmented: Tarcísio de Freitas (São Paulo governor, center-right, ~20-25%), various Bolsonaro loyalists (Renan Santos at ~3-10%, others), Centrão placeholder candidates (Tereza Cristina at ~0.5%), and evangelical-aligned candidates. The [[fragmented-right-wing-field]] concept — originally developed for Colombia where it correctly predicted right-wing consolidation advantage in the runoff but fragmentation in round 1 — documents why fragmentation persists: personalist factions, state-level machines that resist national consolidation, and Bolsonaro's unwillingness to anoint a single successor. In Brazil's open-list PR system, candidates have strong incentives to stay in through round 1 to build name recognition for legislative races. Historical probability of full right-wing consolidation by October: ~15-20%.

**Condition 2: Lula must bleed below his ~40% structural floor.** Lula's first-round vote share history:
- 2006: 48.6% (won outright in round 1)
- 2022: 48.4% (led round 1, advanced to runoff)
Lula has NEVER finished below 40% in a first round he contested. His PT base is structural — Northeast regional stronghold, Bolsa Família beneficiaries (~21M families), organized labor, and low-income voters. While his approval rating has moderated (~45-50%), approval ≠ vote share — many disapprovers still vote PT as the lesser evil. Anti-incumbency sentiment in Brazil historically penalizes the runoff more than the first round (voters use round 1 to signal preferences, round 2 to decide). Probability of Lula falling below 40% even with right-wing consolidation: ~15-25%.

**Joint probability**: P(consolidation) × P(Lula collapse | consolidation) ≈ 0.175 × 0.20 = 0.035. This is consistent with the 3.4% market price.

**Colombia analogue reinforces**: The Colombia 2026 first round (May 31) showed right-wing fragmentation persisting — de la Espriella led with 43.7% while multiple right-wing candidates polled below 6%. Brazil's fragmentation is structurally deeper (more candidates, larger country, state-level machine politics). The analogue confirms the fragmentation persistence mechanism.
