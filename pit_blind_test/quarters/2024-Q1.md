# 2024-Q1 Summary: January 1 - March 31, 2024

## GEOPOLITICS & INTERNATIONAL RELATIONS

### Middle East

**Gaza War intensifies.** The conflict that began in October 2023 continued through Q1 with devastating humanitarian toll. On January 26, the International Court of Justice (ICJ) ruled that Israel must take all measures to prevent genocidal acts in Gaza, but stopped short of ordering an immediate halt to operations (Wikipedia, January 26). On February 29, Israeli Defense Forces opened fire on a crowd of civilians in Gaza City, killing more than 100 people, as Palestinian casualties exceeded 30,000 (Wikipedia, February 29). On March 14, the United Nations estimated that at least 56% of Palestinian civilians killed in the war were women and children (Wikipedia, March 14). On March 25, the UN Security Council passed a resolution calling for an "immediate ceasefire" and demanding the immediate and unconditional release of all hostages (Wikipedia, March 25).

**Red Sea Crisis.** On January 12, a U.S.-led coalition launched airstrikes at Houthi militant locations in Yemen in retaliation for Houthi attacks on ships in the Red Sea, under Operation Prosperity Guardian (Wikipedia, January 12). The Houthi campaign against commercial shipping continued to disrupt global trade routes throughout the quarter.

**Iran-Pakistan tit-for-tat strikes.** On January 16, Iran carried out missile and drone strikes within Balochistan, Pakistan, targeting the Iranian Baloch militant group Jaish ul-Adl. On January 18, Pakistan conducted retaliatory airstrikes on Iran's Sistan and Baluchestan province, raising fears of broader escalation before both sides de-escalated (Wikipedia, January 16-18).

**Iran: Kerman bombings.** On January 3, an Islamic State double bombing killed 94 people during a memorial event commemorating the assassination of Qasem Soleimani in Kerman, Iran. The bombing was carried out using two briefcase bombs placed at the entrance that were detonated remotely (Wikipedia, January 3).

**Israel: Syria strike.** On April 1 (the very end of the quarter's frame), sixteen people were killed in an Israeli attack in Damascus (Wikipedia, March 31 - note: this event is listed under the March section but occurred April 1).

### Europe & NATO

**Sweden joins NATO.** On March 7, Sweden officially joined NATO as its 32nd member, becoming the final Nordic country to join the alliance, following Finland a year earlier (Wikipedia, March 7; NATO press release, March 7, 2024).

**Russia: Presidential election.** On March 15-17, incumbent Vladimir Putin was re-elected for a fifth term in an election widely criticized internationally as neither free nor fair, with opposition candidates Alexei Navalny deceased and others barred from the ballot (Wikipedia, March 15-17).

**Navalny dies in custody.** On February 16, Russian opposition leader Alexei Navalny died under mysterious circumstances at the age of 47 in a Russian prison colony. His death sparked international condemnation and protests outside Russian embassies globally (Wikipedia, February 16).

**Crocus City Hall attack.** On March 22, Islamic State-affiliated gunmen attacked concertgoers at Crocus City Hall in Krasnogorsk, Russia, killing at least 145 people and injuring 551 — the deadliest terrorist attack in Russia since the Beslan school siege in 2004 (Wikipedia, March 22).

**EU AI Act passed.** On March 13, the European Union passed the Artificial Intelligence Act, the world's first comprehensive legal and regulatory framework for artificial intelligence (Wikipedia, March 13).

**Schengen expansion.** On March 31, Bulgaria and Romania became members of the Schengen Area through sea and air routes (Wikipedia, March 31).

**Portugal election.** On March 10, the Democratic Alliance won a plurality of seats in the Portuguese legislative election, forming a minority government amid major gains by the right-wing Chega party (Wikipedia, March 10).

**Danish abdication.** On January 14, Queen Margrethe II formally abdicated as Queen of Denmark on the 52nd anniversary of her accession, with her eldest son Frederik succeeding her as King Frederik X (Wikipedia, January 14).

**Finland elects president.** On February 11, Alexander Stubb was elected president of Finland in the closest presidential election in Finnish history, in the second round (Wikipedia, February 11).

**Russia: Il-76 crash.** On January 24, a Russian Ilyushin Il-76 military transport plane carrying (according to Russia) 65 Ukrainian prisoners of war, 6 crew members, and 3 guards crashed in Russia's Korochansky District near the Ukrainian border, killing everyone on board (Wikipedia, January 24).

**US airstrikes in Iraq and Syria.** On February 2, the US launched airstrikes on 85 targets in Iraq and Syria in response to a deadly drone attack on a US military base in Jordan that killed three American soldiers on January 28 (Wikipedia, February 2; BBC News, February 2, 2024).

### Asia-Pacific

**Japan: Noto Peninsula earthquake.** On January 1, a 7.5 Mww earthquake struck the western coast of Japan (Noto Peninsula), killing at least 504 people and injuring 1,389 others. A further 5 were killed the next day when a Coast Guard aircraft carrying humanitarian aid collided with a Japan Airlines Airbus A350-900 at Haneda Airport; all 379 people aboard the passenger jet were evacuated safely (Wikipedia, January 1).

**Japan: SLIM moon landing.** On January 19, Japan became the 5th country to achieve a soft landing on the Moon with its SLIM (Smart Lander for Investigating Moon) mission (Wikipedia, January 19; JAXA press release).

**Taiwan presidential election.** On January 13, Lai Ching-te of the ruling Democratic Progressive Party won the Taiwanese presidential election with 40% of the vote (Wikipedia, January 13).

**Bangladesh election.** On January 7, the Awami League, led by incumbent Sheikh Hasina, won a 4th consecutive term in the Bangladeshi general election amid protests by opposition parties and a large drop in voter turnout (Wikipedia, January 7).

**Pakistan election.** On February 8, independent politicians, most of whom were members of the banned political party Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI), won a plurality of seats in the National Assembly (Wikipedia, February 8).

**Indonesia election.** On February 14, Prabowo Subianto won the Indonesian presidential election, and the Democratic Party of Struggle won the most votes in the legislative election (Wikipedia, February 14).

**Azerbaijan reclaims Nagorno-Karabakh.** On January 1, the Republic of Artsakh was formally dissolved following the recapture of the Nagorno-Karabakh region by Azerbaijan in September 2023 (Wikipedia, January 1).

**Ethiopia-Somaliland deal.** On January 1, Ethiopia announced an agreement with Somaliland to use the port of Berbera. Ethiopia also said it would eventually recognize Somaliland's independence, becoming the first country to do so (Wikipedia, January 1).

**BRICS expansion.** On January 1, Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, and the United Arab Emirates became BRICS members (Wikipedia, January 1).

**Papua New Guinea riots.** On January 11, riots broke out throughout Papua New Guinea after an alleged rounding error caused pay cuts for police officers and soldiers (Wikipedia, January 11).

### Americas

**Ecuador: State of emergency.** On January 8, Ecuadorian President Daniel Noboa declared a state of emergency following the escape of Los Choneros drug cartel leader Jose Adolfo Macias Villamar from prison. The military was deployed onto the streets and into prisons, while setting a national nighttime curfew (Wikipedia, January 8).

**Guatemala: Arevalo inaugurated.** On January 15, Bernardo Arevalo was inaugurated as the 52nd President of Guatemala following a brief political crisis after the 2023 elections (Wikipedia, January 15).

**El Salvador: Bukele re-elected.** On February 4, incumbent President Nayib Bukele won the Salvadoran presidential election with over 80% of the vote, becoming the first president to be re-elected in El Salvador since 1944 (Wikipedia, February 4).

**Namibia: President dies.** On February 4, President of Namibia Hage Geingob died at the age of 82; Vice-President Nangolo Mbumba succeeded him (Wikipedia, February 4; The Namibian, February 4, 2024).

**Chile: Pinera dies.** On February 6, former President of Chile Sebastian Pinera died in a helicopter crash at the age of 74 (Wikipedia, February 6).

**Haiti crisis escalates.** On February 28, a state of emergency was declared by the Haitian government after gangs stormed two prisons and demanded the resignation of acting Prime Minister Ariel Henry. On March 11, Henry announced his pending resignation amid the ongoing crisis marked by gang warfare (Wikipedia, February 28 - March 11). The US deployed Marines to secure its embassy on March 13.

**Senegal: Faye elected.** On March 24, Bassirou Diomaye Faye was elected President of Senegal after his party and its former candidate Ousmane Sonko were disqualified from running (Wikipedia, March 24).

## UNITED STATES POLITICS & DOMESTIC AFFAIRS

**2024 Presidential campaign.** On January 15, Vivek Ramaswamy ended his presidential campaign after Iowa caucuses. Ron DeSantis suspended his campaign on January 21 after poor Iowa showing. Nikki Haley suspended her campaign on March 6 after Super Tuesday losses, leaving Trump as the presumptive Republican nominee. On the Democratic side, Marianne Williamson ended her campaign on February 7, and Dean Phillips on March 6 after poor primary showings (Wikipedia, US section).

**Trump legal battles.** On January 26, a jury awarded E. Jean Carroll $83.3 million in a defamation case against Donald Trump. On February 16, a New York judge ordered Trump to pay $355 million ($464 million after interest) in a civil fraud case; Trump filed an appeal ten days later (Wikipedia, US section).

**Supreme Court on ballot access.** On March 4, in Trump v. Anderson, the US Supreme Court unanimously ruled that states cannot keep presidential candidates off the ballot, overturning the Colorado Supreme Court's decision (Wikipedia, US section, March 4).

**Border standoff.** On January 24, the standoff between Texas and the federal government at Eagle Pass intensified after the Supreme Court ruled against Texas for placing barbed wire at the border. Texas Governor Greg Abbott announced the state would not follow the court orders. Twenty-three states announced allegiance to Texas (Wikipedia, US section, January 24; PBS NewsHour, January 25, 2024). The Supreme Court on March 19 allowed Texas to begin enforcing Senate Bill 4, a controversial immigration law allowing state officials to arrest and deport migrants (Wikipedia, US section, March 19).

**Mayorkas impeachment.** On February 13, Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas was impeached by the House of Representatives (Wikipedia, US section, February 13).

**McConnell steps down.** On February 28, Mitch McConnell announced he would step down as Senate Republican leader in January 2025, a position he held since 2007 (Wikipedia, US section, February 28).

**Havard president resigns.** On January 2, Harvard University President Claudine Gay resigned following a congressional hearing on antisemitism and plagiarism allegations (Wikipedia, US section, January 2).

**Alaska Airlines 737 MAX incident.** On January 5, Alaska Airlines Flight 1282 suffered an uncontrolled decompression after an emergency door plug on a Boeing 737 MAX 9 blew out, resulting in an emergency landing in Portland. The FAA grounded all 737 MAX 9 aircraft (Wikipedia, US section, January 5).

**Nitrogen hypoxia execution.** On January 25, convicted murderer Kenneth Smith was executed in Alabama using nitrogen hypoxia, the first death row inmate to die via this method (Wikipedia, US section, January 25).

**Student debt cancellation.** On February 21, Biden cancelled another $1.2 billion of student loan debts for nearly 153,000 people (Wikipedia, US section, February 21; Reuters, February 21, 2024).

**Capital One-Discover merger.** On February 21, Capital One announced an agreement to acquire Discover Financial for nearly $35 billion (Wikipedia, US section, February 21; CNBC, February 21, 2024).

**Alabama frozen embryo ruling.** On February 21, the Alabama Supreme Court ruled that frozen embryos can be considered children under state law, raising concerns about fertility treatments (Wikipedia, US section, February 21).

**Change Healthcare ransomware.** On February 21, a ransomware attack crippled Change Healthcare's payment system, disrupting insurance claims and prescription medicine access nationwide (Wikipedia, US section, February 21; New York Times, March 5, 2024).

**Baltimore Key Bridge collapse.** On March 26, the container ship MV Dali collided with the Francis Scott Key Bridge in Baltimore, causing a total collapse of the bridge and the deaths of six people (Wikipedia, March 26; BBC News, March 26, 2024).

**Sam Bankman-Fried sentenced.** On March 28, Sam Bankman-Fried was sentenced to 25 years in prison for wire fraud and money laundering after scamming $8 billion from investors via FTX (Wikipedia, US section, March 28; BBC News, March 28, 2024).

**Biden State of the Union.** On March 7, President Biden delivered his third State of the Union Address (Wikipedia, US section, March 7).

**SpaceX Starship test.** On March 14, SpaceX successfully launched the first Starship rocket on its integrated flight test 3, the most powerful rocket ever built, but the vehicle was lost during reentry (Wikipedia, US section, March 14; CNN, March 14, 2024).

**EPA emissions rules.** On March 20, the Biden administration announced new vehicle emission standards for 2027-2032, pushing for wider adoption of hybrid and electric vehicles (Wikipedia, US section, March 20; EPA press release, March 20, 2024).

**Pig kidney transplant.** On March 21, a man received a kidney from a genetically engineered pig at Massachusetts General Hospital in Boston, a medical breakthrough (Wikipedia, US section, March 21; New York Post, March 21, 2024).

**Other notable events:** The 66th Grammy Awards (Feb 4, Taylor Swift won Album of the Year); the 96th Academy Awards (March 10, Oppenheimer won Best Picture); Super Bowl LVIII (Feb 11, Kansas City Chiefs defeated San Francisco 49ers 25-22 in overtime); massive AT&T outage (Feb 22, ~71,000 customers affected); the Smokehouse Creek Fire in the Texas panhandle (Feb 27, burned ~200,000 acres); Bluesky social network opened to public (Feb 6); Jo-Ann Stores filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy (March 18); and the Pentagon documents leaker Jack Teixeira pleaded guilty (March 4).

## ECONOMY

**Monetary Policy.** The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady at 5.25-5.50% throughout Q1 2024, maintaining the highest level in 23 years as inflation remained above the 2% target. Markets priced in rate cuts later in the year. The European Central Bank and Bank of England similarly held rates.

**Stock Markets.** Global equity markets rallied in Q1 2024. The S&P 500 hit multiple record highs, driven by enthusiasm around artificial intelligence and expectations of rate cuts. The Nasdaq Composite also reached new highs, led by technology megacap stocks (the "Magnificent Seven"). Japan's Nikkei 225 surged past its 1989 record in February, reaching an all-time high.

**Cryptocurrency.** Bitcoin surged in Q1 2024, driven by the January 10 approval of spot Bitcoin ETFs by the SEC (approved in late Q1 cycle/carrying into the quarter). Bitcoin reached new highs above $70,000 in March 2024.

**Corporate Activity.** Capital One announced its $35 billion acquisition of Discover Financial. Toyota announced a $1.3 billion investment in a Kentucky EV factory. AWS announced a $10 billion data center investment in Mississippi. Scout Motors started construction on a $2 billion EV plant in South Carolina.

**Corporate Bankruptcies.** Audacy (largest US radio operator) filed for Chapter 11 on January 7. Jo-Ann Stores filed for Chapter 11 on March 18 after accumulating over $1 billion in debt.

**Inflation.** US CPI readings continued to moderate but remained above the Fed's 2% target. The January CPI reading came in at 3.1% year-over-year. Core inflation remained sticky. This delayed market expectations for the timing of rate cuts.

## TECHNOLOGY & SCIENCE

**Artificial Intelligence.**

- **EU AI Act passed.** On March 13, the European Parliament passed the world's first comprehensive AI regulatory framework, categorizing AI applications by risk level (Wikipedia, March 13).
- **OpenAI Sora demonstrated.** In February 2024, OpenAI publicly demonstrated Sora, a text-to-video AI model that could generate realistic videos from text prompts, though it was not released to the public (OpenAI blog, February 15, 2024).
- **xAI open-sources Grok.** In March, Elon Musk's xAI open-sourced Grok, its large language model (The Verge, March 18, 2024).
- **AI and fingerprint study.** An AI-based study published January 12 showed for the first time that fingerprints from different fingers of the same person share detectable similarities (CNN, January 12, 2024).
- **GPT-4 persuasiveness research.** A preprint trial suggested large language models could be more persuasive than humans when using personal information (Nature Human Behaviour, March 2024).

**Space.**

- **Japan's SLIM moon landing.** January 19 — Japan became the 5th country to achieve a soft lunar landing (JAXA/Wikipedia).
- **Intuitive Machines' Nova-C.** February 22 — American company Intuitive Machines' Nova-C lander became the first commercial vehicle to land on the Moon (Wikipedia, February 22; NASA/Intuitive Machines).
- **SpaceX Starship IFT-3.** March 14 — Starship launched successfully but was lost during reentry (SpaceX/Wikipedia).
- **US missile-detecting satellites.** February 15 — The US launched six missile-detecting satellites into orbit in response to concerns about Russia developing nuclear capabilities in space (Fox News, February 15, 2024).

**Computing.**

- **Petabit optical disk.** Researchers described an approach for an optical disk with petabit capacity (Nature, February 2024).
- **Open-source voice assistant.** LAION released a first version of BUD-E, a fully open-source voice assistant (TechCrunch, February 15, 2024).
- **AI waste sorting.** An AI-powered automatic waste sorting system that can distinguish between over 500 waste categories was released.
- **John Walker dies.** February 2 — John Walker, co-founder of Autodesk and creator of AutoCAD, died at 74 (New York Times, March 6, 2024).

**Medical.**

- **Pig kidney transplant.** March 21 — First successful transplant of a kidney from a genetically engineered pig into a human patient at Massachusetts General Hospital.

## KEY THEMES & EMERGING PATTERNS

1. **Incumbent weakness globally.** Multiple elections in Q1 saw incumbents facing significant opposition or winning amid boycotts and low turnout, previewing a global trend that would intensify through the year.

2. **AI regulation takes shape.** The EU AI Act marked the first major regulatory framework for AI, setting a precedent as governments worldwide grappled with rapid AI advancement.

3. **Red Sea disruption.** Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping and the US-led military response created ongoing supply chain disruptions and demonstrated the fragility of global trade routes.

4. **Gaza humanitarian crisis.** The conflict's civilian toll continued to mount, with international legal pressure (ICJ ruling) and diplomatic efforts (UNSC ceasefire resolution) failing to halt the fighting.

5. **Space renaissance.** Commercial moon landings (Intuitive Machines), new lunar missions (Japan SLIM), and SpaceX Starship test flights marked accelerating space capabilities.

6. **NATO expansion completes.** With Sweden's accession, NATO absorbed Finland and Sweden — two historically neutral countries — in direct response to Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

7. **Democracy under strain.** The US presidential campaign featured unprecedented legal proceedings against a major candidate; Russia held elections without genuine opposition; Haiti spiraled into gang-led collapse.

8. **Economic resilience.** Despite high interest rates, equity markets rallied, corporate investment continued (especially in EV and AI infrastructure), and the US economy showed surprising strength.
