# Q3 2022 (July -- September) -- Point-in-Time Summary

**Compiled from sources available as of 2022-09-30. No information from after this date is included.**

---

## 1. Geopolitics & Conflict

### 1.1 The Russo-Ukrainian War

The third quarter of 2022 saw dramatic reversals in the trajectory of Russia's invasion of Ukraine, which had begun on 24 February 2022.

**July -- Russian advances stall; siege warfare continues.** After capturing the entirety of Luhansk Oblast in early July (the fall of Lysychansk on 3 July gave Russia full control of Luhansk), Russian forces shifted to artillery-heavy bombardment in Donetsk. On 1 July, a Russian missile strike on a residential building and recreation centre in Serhiivka, Odesa Oblast, killed at least 21 civilians (The Guardian, 1 July 2022). The Donbas grinding campaign continued through the month, with Ukrainian forces conducting a fighting withdrawal.

**The Black Sea Grain Initiative.** On 22 July, with Turkey and the United Nations acting as brokers, Russia and Ukraine signed the Black Sea Grain Initiative, a landmark agreement to facilitate the safe export of grain from Ukrainian Black Sea ports -- critical for alleviating rising global food prices (UN News, 22 July 2022). The first grain ship departed Odesa for Lebanon on 1 August.

**August -- A grinding war of attrition.** The front lines shifted little in August, but the war's character changed as Ukraine prepared for counter-offensives. Russian strikes continued to hit civilian infrastructure. The Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant -- Europe's largest -- became a recurring flashpoint, with Russia and Ukraine trading blame for shelling near the facility. On 5 August, Ukraine's nuclear agency Energoatom said Russian rockets had damaged a nitrogen-oxygen unit and a high-voltage power line at the plant (BBC News, 5 August 2022). International concern over nuclear safety grew.

**September -- The Kharkiv counteroffensive and Russian collapse.** In early September, Ukrainian forces launched a lightning offensive in Kharkiv Oblast, catching Russian forces off-guard. Within days, Ukraine recaptured thousands of square kilometres of territory, including the key logistical hub of Izium (The Guardian, 10 September 2022). The rout was the most significant shift in the war's front lines since March. A simultaneous southern counteroffensive in Kherson Oblast made slower but steady progress, recapturing the town of Vysokopillia on 4 September (Newsweek, 4 September 2022).

**Putin's response: partial mobilisation and nuclear threats.** Humiliated by the battlefield reverses, Russian President Vladimir Putin announced a "partial mobilisation" of 300,000 reservists on 21 September, Russia's first such mobilisation since World War II. In a televised address, Putin threatened nuclear retaliation, saying "this is not a bluff" (BBC News, 21 September 2022). The mobilisation triggered an exodus of military-age men: around 98,000 Russians fled to Kazakhstan alone (HuffPost, 27 September 2022), while Finland announced it would deny entry to Russian tourists, cutting the last direct road link between Russia and the EU (Reuters, 29 September 2022). Mass anti-war protests broke out across Russia, particularly in Dagestan, with over 2,000 demonstrators detained (BBC News, 25 September 2022).

**Sham referendums and illegal annexation.** Between 23-27 September, Russian occupation authorities held widely denounced referendums in Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, and Kherson oblasts, claiming over 87% support in each. International observers dismissed the votes as a sham (AP, 27 September 2022). On 30 September, Putin signed treaties formally annexing the four regions -- a move condemned as a breach of international law by the UN General Assembly, the G7, and most nations (BBC News, 30 September 2022).

**Wagner Group founder admits role.** On 26 September, Russian oligarch Yevgeny Prigozhin, a close Putin ally, publicly admitted for the first time that he had founded the Wagner Group private military company, after years of denials (The Guardian, 26 September 2022).

**Nord Stream sabotage.** On 26-27 September, three (later four) gas leaks were detected on the Nord Stream 1 and 2 pipelines in the Baltic Sea near Bornholm, Denmark. Seismologists recorded underwater explosions. European leaders described the events as sabotage, though the perpetrator remained unknown as of the end of Q3 (Euronews, 27 September 2022; Sky News, 29 September 2022). The leaks rendered both pipelines inoperable, dealing a severe blow to Europe's energy relationship with Russia.

### 1.2 China-Taiwan Tensions

The quarter's most significant flashpoint in the Asia-Pacific came from US House Speaker Nancy Pelosi's visit to Taiwan on 2-3 August, the highest-level US official visit since 1997. China responded furiously: the People's Liberation Army was placed on high alert and, on 4 August, launched its largest-ever military exercises around Taiwan, including ballistic missiles that landed in the Taiwan Strait (BBC News, 4 August 2022). China warned airlines to avoid areas near Taiwan. Cross-strait tensions remained elevated for the rest of the quarter, with Taiwanese forces shooting down an unidentified civilian drone near the Shi Islet on 1 September (Reuters, 1 September 2022).

### 1.3 Iran

**Death of Mahsa Amini and protests.** On 16 September, 22-year-old Mahsa Amini died in the custody of Iran's "morality police" after being arrested for allegedly wearing her hijab improperly. Her death sparked the largest protests in Iran since the 2009 Green Movement, with women at the forefront burning hijabs and cutting their hair in defiance. By late September, protests had spread to over a dozen cities. The government launched a violent crackdown, with security personnel firing live ammunition. On 24 September, the US State and Treasury Departments announced measures to increase internet freedom in Iran, and Elon Musk confirmed that Starlink would be enabled in Iranian cities (Reuters, 24 September 2022). The regime blamed Kurdish separatist groups for fomenting unrest and launched missile and drone attacks on Kurdish opposition bases in Iraq on 28 September, killing 13 (Al Jazeera, 28 September 2022).

**Nuclear talks stall.** Indirect US-Iran negotiations on reviving the 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) remained stalled throughout Q3, with the US saying Iran's latest proposals were "not constructive."

### 1.4 South Asia

**Sri Lanka's collapse and political transition.** Sri Lanka's catastrophic economic crisis -- its worst since independence -- continued. Inflation reached 54.6% in June (CNA, 1 July 2022) and nearly 60% by July (Bloomberg, 5 July 2022). President Gotabaya Rajapaksa fled the country on 13 July after protesters stormed the presidential palace, and resigned from Singapore. Prime Minister Ranil Wickremesinghe was elected president by Parliament on 21 July (Reuters, 21 July 2022). Former president Rajapaksa returned from exile on 2 September (AP, 2 September 2022). The IMF provisionally approved a US$2.9 billion bailout on 1 September (Reuters, 1 September 2022).

**Pakistan floods.** From mid-June through August, catastrophic monsoon flooding submerged up to one-third of Pakistan's land area. The death toll exceeded 1,000 by 28 August. On 28 August, the government declared a "climate catastrophe" and appealed for international assistance. The UN and several nations pledged millions in aid (Reuters/BBC, August 2022). The floods were described as the world's deadliest since 2017.

**India.** On 18 July, Droupadi Murmu was elected President of India, the first tribal person and the youngest to hold the office (BBC News, 18 July 2022). BYD Auto overtook Tesla as the world's largest electric vehicle producer by sales in H1 2022 (South China Morning Post, 5 July 2022).

### 1.5 East Africa

**Kenyan election.** On 9 August, Deputy President William Ruto was elected Kenya's fifth president, defeating longtime opposition leader Raila Odinga. The Supreme Court certified the result on 5 September after dismissing legal challenges (Leadership, 5 September 2022). Protests by Odinga supporters accompanied the certification.

**Ethiopia and Tigray.** The Tigray War flared up again after a five-month truce collapsed. On 1 September, the Tigray People's Liberation Front claimed Ethiopia and Eritrea had launched a joint offensive (Reuters, 1 September 2022).

### 1.6 Europe

**UK political crisis: three prime ministers in one quarter.** Boris Johnson's government collapsed in July after the Chris Pincher scandal triggered mass resignations, including Chancellor Rishi Sunak and Health Secretary Sajid Javid on 5 July (BBC News, 5 July 2022). Johnson announced his resignation on 7 July, triggering a Conservative leadership contest. Foreign Secretary Liz Truss defeated former Chancellor Rishi Sunak and was elected Conservative leader on 5 September, becoming Prime Minister on 6 September (Sky News, 6 September 2022).

**Death of Queen Elizabeth II.** On 8 September, Queen Elizabeth II -- the UK's longest-reigning monarch at 70 years -- died at Balmoral Castle, Scotland, aged 96. Her son acceded as King Charles III. The Queen's state funeral on 19 September at Westminster Abbey was attended by hundreds of world leaders and was speculated to be the most-watched television event in history (BBC News, 8-19 September 2022). Her death marked a profound cultural moment for the UK and the Commonwealth.

**UK economic crisis: the "mini-budget" disaster.** On 23 September, Chancellor Kwasi Kwarteng delivered a mini-budget that announced £45 billion in unfunded tax cuts, the largest such package in 50 years. Financial markets revolted: the pound sterling fell to a record low of $1.035 against the US dollar on 26 September (BBC News, 26 September 2022), and UK government bond yields soared, forcing the Bank of England to launch an emergency £65 billion bond-buying programme on 28 September to stabilise the economy. The crisis severely damaged Truss's political standing within weeks of her taking office.

**Italy shifts right.** The 25 September Italian general election resulted in a decisive victory for the centre-right coalition led by Giorgia Meloni's Brothers of Italy party, which observers described as the most right-wing government since 1945 (BBC News, 25 September 2022).

**Sweden shifts right.** The 11 September Swedish general election saw Prime Minister Magdalena Andersson's centre-left bloc narrowly lose to a right-wing bloc, 176 seats to 173, with the far-right Sweden Democrats becoming the second-largest party (BBC News, 11 September 2022).

**Armenia-Azerbaijan clashes.** On 12-13 September, Azerbaijan attacked Armenian positions along their shared border, resulting in over 100 Armenian and 71 Azerbaijani soldiers killed -- the worst fighting between the two since the 2020 Nagorno-Karabakh war (Reuters/The Guardian, 13 September 2022).

**Kyrgyzstan-Tajikistan clashes.** Sporadic fighting along the disputed border from 14-20 September killed dozens on both sides (BBC News, September 2022).

**Turkey-Israel rapprochement.** On 17 August, Turkey and Israel agreed to restore full diplomatic relations after a period of tensions (Reuters, 17 August 2022).

### 1.7 Americas

**Colombia turns left.** Gustavo Petro, a former M-19 guerrilla, was inaugurated as Colombia's first left-wing president on 7 August (The Globe and Mail, 7 August 2022).

**Peru.** Prime Minister Anibal Torres resigned on 4 August amid multiple criminal investigations targeting President Pedro Castillo (Reuters, 3 August 2022).

**Chile rejects new constitution.** On 4 September, Chileans voted by a margin of 62% to 38% to reject a proposed new constitution drafted by the Constitutional Convention, a major setback for the progressive rewrite process (The Guardian, 4 September 2022).

**Cuba referendum.** On 25 September, Cubans voted to legalise same-sex marriage and adoption in a referendum on the Family Code (Time, 26 September 2022).

### 1.8 Africa

**Burkina Faso coup within a coup.** The military junta that had seized power in January was itself overthrown on 30 September by Army Captain Ibrahim Traore, citing the junta's failure to contain Islamist insurgents (AP, 30 September 2022).

### 1.9 Assassinations and Terror

**Shinzo Abe assassinated.** On 8 July, former Japanese Prime Minister Shinzo Abe was assassinated while giving a campaign speech in Nara, Japan -- a shocking killing of a former head of government in one of the world's safest countries. The suspect, Tetsuya Yamagami, was arrested at the scene (BBC News, 8 July 2022). A state funeral was held on 27 September at the Budokan in Tokyo (NPR, 27 September 2022).

**Ayman al-Zawahiri killed.** On 31 July, a US drone strike in Kabul killed Ayman al-Zawahiri, the Egyptian terrorist who succeeded Osama bin Laden as leader of al-Qaeda. It was the first US military action in Afghanistan since the Taliban takeover (The Washington Post, 1 August 2022).

**Salman Rushdie stabbed.** On 12 August, Sir Salman Rushdie was stabbed multiple times as he was about to give a lecture at the Chautauqua Institution in New York. He suffered severe injuries including loss of sight in one eye (AP/BBC News, 12 August 2022). The suspect, Hadi Matar, was arrested at the scene.

## 2. Economics & Finance

### 2.1 Inflation and Central Banks

Global inflation remained the dominant macroeconomic story. The **US Federal Reserve** raised interest rates by 75 basis points in both July and September, bringing the federal funds rate to 3.00-3.25% by the end of Q3.

The **European Central Bank** raised its key interest rate for the first time in 11 years on 21 July, from -0.50% to 0.00%, ending the era of negative rates (ECB press release, 21 July 2022). Further increases followed in September.

The **Bank of England** raised rates from 1.25% to 1.75% on 4 August, its largest single hike since 1995 (Reuters, 4 August 2022).

Turkey reported inflation near 80%, its highest in 24 years (The Guardian, 4 July 2022).

### 2.2 Energy Crisis

Europe's energy crisis, triggered by Russia's weaponisation of gas supplies, deepened dramatically. On 2 September, Gazprom announced that the Nord Stream 1 pipeline would not resume flows after maintenance indefinitely, blaming an "oil leak" in a turbine (Financial Times, 5 September 2022). The subsequent sabotage of both Nord Stream pipelines on 26-27 September eliminated the possibility of Russian gas reaching Germany via the Baltic Sea for the foreseeable future.

The G7 agreed on a price cap on Russian oil on 2 September (Reuters, 2 September 2022). Russia vowed to halt sales to countries imposing it.

On 27 September, Poland, Norway, and Denmark opened the Baltic Pipe, a new natural gas pipeline that would transport gas from the Norwegian shelf to Poland, part of Europe's diversification away from Russian energy.

Czech energy protests: An estimated 70,000 people marched in Prague on 3 September demanding action on soaring energy prices and opposing the country's role in supporting Ukraine against Russia (Reuters, 3 September 2022).

### 2.3 US Fiscal Policy

**Inflation Reduction Act.** On 16 August, President Biden signed the Inflation Reduction Act of 2022 into law, a landmark piece of legislation that represented the largest investment in addressing climate change in US history. The bill raised $738 billion through tax reform and prescription drug reform, authorised $783 billion in energy and climate spending, and expanded IRS enforcement. It passed the Senate 51-50 on 7 August (with Vice President Harris breaking the tie) and the House 220-207 on 12 August (Congress.gov, August 2022).

### 2.4 Global Food Crisis

The Black Sea Grain Initiative (22 July) partially alleviated food price pressures, allowing Ukrainian grain exports to resume. However, global food prices remained elevated, and the Pakistan floods destroyed vast amounts of agricultural land.

## 3. Technology & Science

### 3.1 James Webb Space Telescope

On 11 July, NASA released the first operational image from the James Webb Space Telescope -- the deepest and sharpest infrared image of the distant universe ever taken, showing the galaxy cluster SMACS 0723 (NASA, 11 July 2022). Subsequent images of the Carina Nebula, Southern Ring Nebula, and Stephan's Quintet were released in the same week, ushering in a new era of astronomy.

### 3.2 DART Mission

On 26 September, NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) spacecraft successfully impacted the asteroid Dimorphos at 23:16 UTC, the first-ever test of planetary defence technology (CNN, 26 September 2022). The mission aimed to determine whether kinetic impact could alter an asteroid's trajectory.

### 3.3 Artemis I Delayed

NASA's Artemis I mission -- the first launch of the Space Launch System rocket and Orion capsule -- was scrubbed twice due to technical issues. A second launch attempt on 3 September was aborted after a fuel leak (MSN, 3 September 2022). A further delay was announced on 24 September due to an approaching storm.

### 3.4 Fields Medal

The International Mathematical Union awarded the Fields Medal on 5 July to Hugo Duminil-Copin (France), June Huh (USA), James Maynard (UK), and Maryna Viazovska (Ukraine), the latter becoming only the second woman ever to win the prize (The New York Times, 5 July 2022).

### 3.5 AI and Open Source

On 6 September, an AI art model was released as open source -- a model that had cost only $6,000 to train, democratising access to generative AI capabilities and presaging the explosion of open-weight models (Wikipedia, September 2022).

### 3.6 Monkeypox

The WHO declared the monkeypox outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on 23 July, when cases had exceeded 17,000 in 75 countries (WHO, 23 July 2022). The US declared it a public health emergency on 4 August (MSN, 4 August 2022).

## 4. Environment & Climate

### 4.1 European Heatwaves

A series of extreme heatwaves struck Europe from July to August, causing at least 53,000 deaths and setting record temperatures in multiple countries. The UK recorded its first-ever 40°C (104°F) temperature on 19 July. Major wildfires broke out in France, Spain, Portugal, and Greece. The heatwaves caused widespread travel disruption, including buckling railway tracks and airport runway closures.

### 4.2 Pakistan Floods

Monsoon floods from June through August submerged vast areas of Pakistan, killing over 1,000 and affecting more than 33 million people. Climate Minister Sherry Rehman called it "a climate catastrophe." The disaster was attributed to unprecedented monsoon rainfall, compounded by glacial melt.

### 4.3 Hurricanes

**Hurricane Fiona** (14-25 September) caused severe damage across Guadeloupe, Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic, Bermuda, and Eastern Canada.

**Hurricane Ian** (27-30 September) was the quarter's most devastating storm. It made landfall in western Cuba as a Category 3 hurricane on 27 September, knocking out power for the entire island. It then intensified to a high-end Category 4 hurricane and struck southwest Florida near Fort Myers on 28 September, causing catastrophic storm surge flooding. The Sanibel Causeway collapsed, and Port Charlotte suffered extreme damage. By the end of September, at least 17 deaths had been confirmed, with hundreds more feared (CNN, 29 September 2022). The full death toll would not be known for weeks.

### 4.4 Earthquakes

- **27 July:** A magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck Luzon, Philippines, killing 11 and injuring over 600.
- **5 September:** A magnitude 6.8 earthquake hit Luding County, Sichuan, China, killing at least 66 and injuring 250+ (initially 117 reported dead by month end) (Reuters, 5 September 2022).
- **September:** Two earthquakes in eastern Afghanistan killed 16 people.

### 4.5 Other Disasters

- **3 July:** A serac collapse on Marmolada, Italy's highest peak, killed 11 hikers.
- **5 August:** A nightclub fire in Sattahip, Thailand killed 14.
- **6 September:** A karaoke bar fire in Binh Duong, Vietnam killed 32.
- **26 September:** A school shooting in Izhevsk, Russia killed 17.

## 5. Society & Law

### 5.1 Abortion Rights in the US

In the first major electoral test of abortion rights since the Supreme Court overturned Roe v. Wade in June, Kansas voters on 2 August overwhelmingly rejected a constitutional amendment that would have removed state-level abortion protections (ABC News, 2 August 2022). The result was seen as a bellwether of voter sentiment. The Department of Justice also filed its first post-Roe lawsuit against Idaho's near-total abortion ban.

### 5.2 Alex Jones Verdict

On 5 August, a Texas jury ordered conspiracy theorist Alex Jones to pay $45.2 million in punitive damages to the parents of a 6-year-old boy killed in the 2012 Sandy Hook massacre, after Jones falsely claimed the shooting was a hoax (Reuters, 5 August 2022).

### 5.3 Saskatchewan Stabbings

On 4 September, a mass stabbing on the James Smith Cree Nation and in the village of Weldon, Saskatchewan, Canada, killed 10 people and injured 15. One suspect was found dead on 5 September; the other was later arrested (CBC News, 4-5 September 2022).

### 5.4 Brittney Griner Sentenced

On 4 August, American basketball star Brittney Griner was sentenced to nine years in a Russian penal colony after being convicted of drug possession with intent to supply. The US government denounced the sentence as politically motivated (Reuters, 4 August 2022).

### 5.5 Hong Kong Chief Executive

John Lee was sworn in as the fifth Chief Executive of Hong Kong on 1 July, replacing Carrie Lam (RTHK, 1 July 2022).

### 5.6 Myanmar

Aung San Suu Kyi was sentenced to three more years in prison for election fraud on 2 September, bringing her total sentence to 20 years. The deposed leader faced further charges under the state secrets act, receiving another three years on 29 September (Al Jazeera/AP, September 2022).

## 6. Sports & Culture

### 6.1 Football

**UEFA Women's Euro 2022.** Hosted by England from 6-31 July, the tournament was won by England, who defeated Germany 2-1 after extra time in the final at Wembley Stadium. It was England's first major international football trophy since the men's 1966 World Cup.

### 6.2 Commonwealth Games

The 2022 Commonwealth Games were held in Birmingham, England from 28 July to 8 August.

### 6.3 Tennis and Other Sports

- Carlos Sainz Jr. won his first Formula 1 race at the British Grand Prix on 3 July.
- Ian Nepomniachtchi won the Candidates Chess Tournament on 3 July, qualifying for the 2023 World Chess Championship.

- Sri Lanka won the 2022 Asia Cup on 11 September.

## 8. Patterns and Emerging Themes

1. **The energy war.** Russia's weaponisation of natural gas exports, culminating in the Nord Stream sabotage, transformed Europe's energy landscape. The Baltic Pipe opening and frantic LNG procurement marked a structural break from decades of Russian energy dependence.

2. **Ukraine shifts the narrative.** The Kharkiv counteroffensive proved that Russian forces could be defeated in open battle, changing Western perceptions of the war's trajectory and setting conditions for sustained Western military aid. Putin's mobilisation and nuclear threats signalled desperation but also raised escalation risks.

3. **Democratic resilience tested.** The US midterm environment (Kansas abortion vote, the Inflation Reduction Act), Chile's constitutional rejection, and Kenya's peaceful election outcome contrasted with rising authoritarian drift in Russia, Myanmar, and Burkina Faso.

4. **Extreme weather as the new normal.** Three catastrophic events -- the European heatwave, Pakistan floods, and Hurricane Ian -- in a single quarter underscored the accelerating impacts of climate change on human systems.

5. **Monetary tightening accelerates.** Central banks across the developed world raised rates aggressively to combat inflation, navigating the post-pandemic recovery through the energy crisis and war-driven commodity inflation.

6. **The UK's fragility exposed.** The combination of the Truss government's fiscal recklessness and the bond market's punitive response revealed the UK's vulnerability to investor confidence, even as a G7 economy.

7. **Iran's regime faces its greatest domestic challenge in a generation.** The Mahsa Amini protests, led by women, posed an existential challenge to the Islamic Republic's authority, with slogans directly targeting the Supreme Leader.
