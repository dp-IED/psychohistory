# 2022-Q4 Summary (October - December 2022)

## Geopolitics

### Russia-Ukraine War: Turning of the Tide

**Crimean Bridge attack (Oct 8):** An explosion severely damaged the Kerch Strait Bridge linking Crimea to Russia, killing three. Russia blamed Ukraine, calling it a terrorist attack. Two days later, Russia launched the most widespread missile strikes across Ukraine since the invasion began, targeting Kyiv and critical infrastructure nationwide (Wikipedia, Oct 2022).

**Kherson counteroffensive & liberation (Oct-Nov):** Ukrainian forces continued a major counteroffensive on the southern front. By October 4, Ukrainian troops had liberated 2,400 sq km in Kherson and Mykolaiv oblasts, capturing Dudchany, Davydiv Brid, and Velyka Oleksandrivka among others. Russian-installed officials admitted a "breakthrough" by Ukrainian forces. On October 13, Russia ordered civilian evacuation of Kherson city. General Sergei Surovikin, newly appointed commander of Russian forces, stated on October 18 the defense of Kherson was "not easy." By October 22, occupation authorities urged residents to "leave immediately." On November 9, Russia announced withdrawal from the right bank of the Dnieper River. On November 11, Ukrainian troops entered Kherson city, the only regional capital Russia had captured since the invasion began, met by crowds chanting "Slava Ukraini!" - a major symbolic and strategic victory (Wikipedia, Kherson counteroffensive, Oct-Nov 2022).

**Russian missile strikes on energy infrastructure (Oct-Dec):** Following the Crimean Bridge attack, Russia launched waves of cruise missile and drone strikes targeting Ukraine's energy grid, causing widespread blackouts as winter approached. This became a deliberate strategy to weaponize winter against civilians (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

**Black Sea Grain Initiative suspension (Oct 29):** Russia suspended participation in the UN-brokered Black Sea Grain Initiative after alleging a Ukrainian drone attack on the Black Sea Fleet in Sevastopol. The deal had allowed Ukrainian grain exports to global markets. Russia resumed participation later in November following UN and Turkish mediation (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

**Przewodow missile incident (Nov 15):** Two missiles struck the Polish village of Przewodow near the Ukrainian border, killing two Polish citizens. Amid fears of NATO-Russia escalation, investigations suggested the missile was likely a Ukrainian air-defense interceptor that went astray rather than a Russian strike. NATO and Polish leaders responded with restraint, de-escalating a potentially dangerous moment for the alliance's mutual defense commitments (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events).

**G20 Bali Summit (Nov 15-16):** The G20 summit in Bali, Indonesia, saw intensive diplomacy over Ukraine. Amid deep divisions, the member states adopted a joint declaration that most members "deplored in the strongest terms" Russia's aggression, though the language was tempered by disagreement among members. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy addressed the summit virtually (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events).

**EU oil price cap (Dec 2):** The G7, Australia, and the EU imposed a $60 per barrel price cap on Russian crude oil, designed to "prevent Russia from profiting from its war of aggression against Ukraine" while maintaining Russian oil flows to global markets. This complemented the EU embargo on seaborne Russian crude that took effect December 5 (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

### China

**20th National Congress of the CCP (Oct 16-23):** Xi Jinping secured an unprecedented third term as General Secretary of the Chinese Communist Party, breaking with decades of precedent. His ideology "Xi Jinping Thought" was enshrined in the Party constitution. The new leadership lineup elevated allies from his home province of Zhejiang and downgraded figures associated with the previous generation, including Li Keqiang being replaced as Premier by Li Qiang. The congress signaled intensified economic state control and national security priorities (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

**Zero-COVID protests and policy reversal (Nov-Dec):** Widespread and increasingly bold protests erupted across dozens of Chinese cities - including Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Chengdu - against the government's strict zero-COVID policy. The "White Paper" protests, sparked by a fire in Urumqi that killed 10 people due to lockdown restrictions, grew into the largest public demonstrations China had seen in decades, despite heavy state censorship and police presence. On December 7, the Chinese government abruptly announced a significant easing of COVID restrictions, abandoning mass testing, quarantine requirements, and venue code scanning - a dramatic reversal of the zero-COVID approach that had been in place for nearly three years. This set the stage for a massive infection wave (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

### United Kingdom

**Liz Truss resignation (Oct 20):** PM Liz Truss resigned after just 45 days in office - the shortest tenure in British history - amid a government crisis triggered by her mini-budget on September 23 that caused financial market turmoil, a sharp fall in sterling, and a Bank of England emergency bond-buying intervention. She had lost the confidence of her Conservative Party (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

**Rishi Sunak becomes PM (Oct 25):** Rishi Sunak became Prime Minister after winning the Conservative Party leadership contest unopposed. He became the first person of colour and the first Hindu to hold the office. His government promised economic stability and fiscal responsibility. He appointed Jeremy Hunt as Chancellor, who had already reversed most of Truss's mini-budget before Sunak took office (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### Brazil

**Lula da Silva elected president (Oct 30):** In a closely fought runoff election, Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva defeated incumbent Jair Bolsonaro 50.9% to 49.1%. Lula, at 77 the oldest person elected president of Brazil, became the first three-term president. His victory signaled a sharp turn from Bolsonaro's environmental and social policies toward climate action and poverty reduction. Bolsonaro initially refused to concede, leading to protests and a contentious transition period (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### Israel

**Netanyahu returns (Nov 1):** Benjamin Netanyahu's right-religious bloc won a 64-seat majority in the Knesset, leading to the formation of the most right-wing government in Israeli history. Itzy Ben-Gvir's Jewish Power party and Bezalel Smotrich's Religious Zionism party took prominent positions. Netanyahu was sworn in as PM on December 29 (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events).

### Peru

**Castillo self-coup and removal (Dec 7):** President Pedro Castillo attempted to dissolve Congress and rule by decree, triggering a constitutional crisis. Congress voted overwhelmingly to remove him, and he was arrested on rebellion charges. Vice President Dina Boluarte became Peru's first female president, taking office amid deep political polarization and protests by Castillo supporters demanding new elections (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

### United States Midterm Elections (Nov 8)

The midterm elections resulted in Republicans narrowly winning control of the House of Representatives (222-213), while Democrats held and actually gained a Senate seat (50-50 with VP tiebreaker + 1 seat gain in Pennsylvania). This defied expectations of a "red wave." Key outcomes included Democratic victories in competitive Senate races in Pennsylvania (John Fetterman), Arizona (Mark Kelly), and Georgia (Raphael Warnock in a December runoff). The results validated continued Democratic strength in suburban areas and among college-educated voters, while Republicans maintained dominance in rural areas. Abortion rights referendums won in several states following the Dobbs decision. The elections confirmed America's deeply divided 50-50 political landscape (Wikipedia, US elections 2022).

### Ethiopia-Tigray Peace Agreement (Nov 2-3)

The Ethiopian federal government and the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF) signed a formal cessation of hostilities in Pretoria, South Africa, ending two years of brutal civil war that had killed hundreds of thousands. The African Union-brokered deal provided for disarmament of Tigrayan forces, restoration of federal authority, and humanitarian access. This was a major diplomatic achievement for the AU and marked a significant de-escalation in one of the world's deadliest conflicts (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events).

### COP27 Climate Conference (Nov 6-18)

The 27th UN Climate Conference in Sharm el-Sheikh, Egypt, achieved a breakthrough agreement on a "loss and damage" fund to compensate vulnerable nations for climate change impacts - a decades-long demand of developing countries. However, the conference failed to secure stronger emissions reduction commitments, and the final text did not include language on phasing down all fossil fuels (only coal). Global carbon emissions in 2022 remained at record levels, with the Global Carbon Project (Nov 11) reporting no sign of decrease needed for 1.5C target (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events; 2022 in science).

### COP15 Biodiversity Conference (Dec 7-19)

In Montreal, nearly 200 countries agreed to the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, a landmark deal to protect 30% of the planet's land and seas for nature by 2030 ("30x30" target). The framework also included commitments to reform harmful subsidies, restore degraded ecosystems, and mobilize $30 billion in annual conservation financing for developing countries by 2030 (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

### Ireland

**Seán Rooney killed in Lebanon (Dec 14):** Irish peacekeeper Private Seán Rooney was killed and another wounded when their UNIFIL convoy was attacked by a mob in southern Lebanon - the first Irish peacekeeper killed in action in Lebanon in 20 years (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

**Varadkar returns as Taoiseach (Dec 17):** Leo Varadkar became Taoiseach for the second time under a December 2020 coalition rotation agreement between Fine Gael and Fianna Fail, succeeding Micheal Martin (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

### Fiji

Sitiveni Rabuka defeated incumbent Frank Bainimarama in the Fijian general election, becoming Prime Minister on December 24. Bainimarama had ruled Fiji since a 2006 coup (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

## Economics

### OPEC+ Production Cut (Oct 5)

OPEC+ agreed to reduce oil production by 2 million barrels per day, the largest cut since COVID-era reductions. The move was seen as supporting Russian oil revenues despite Western sanctions and drew sharp criticism from the US, which had urged Saudi Arabia to maintain production to lower gasoline prices ahead of the US midterms. The cut tightened global oil markets and kept energy prices elevated (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### FTX Collapse (Nov 2-11)

The cryptocurrency exchange FTX - once valued at $18 billion and considered a pillar of the crypto industry - collapsed in a matter of days:

- **Nov 2:** CoinDesk reported that Alameda Research, FTX's affiliated trading firm, held a large portion of its assets in FTT tokens issued by FTX, revealing a fragile financial structure.
- **Nov 6:** Binance CEO Changpeng Zhao announced Binance would sell its FTT holdings, triggering a bank run on FTX with $6 billion in withdrawals over three days.
- **Nov 8:** Binance entered a non-binding agreement to acquire FTX, then backed out the next day after due diligence revealed mishandled customer funds.
- **Nov 11:** FTX, FTX.US, Alameda Research, and 100+ affiliates filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. CEO Sam Bankman-Fried resigned. Up to $8 billion in customer funds was missing, allegedly loaned secretly to Alameda for risky investments.
- **Wider impact:** The collapse triggered a severe crypto contagion. Crypto lender BlockFi suspended operations and later filed for bankruptcy. The broader crypto market lost hundreds of billions in value. Genesis Global Trading froze redemptions. The event severely damaged trust in centralized crypto exchanges and provoked intense regulatory scrutiny in the US and globally (Wikipedia, FTX page; multiple sources).

### US Federal Reserve Interest Rate Hikes

The Federal Reserve continued its aggressive tightening campaign to combat inflation:
- **Nov 2:** The Fed raised rates by 75 basis points to 3.75-4.00%, its fourth consecutive 75bp hike.
- **Dec 14:** The Fed raised rates by 50bp to 4.25-4.50%, signaling a downshift in pace but still indicating further tightening ahead.
US CPI inflation peaked at 7.7% in October (year-over-year) then moderated slightly to 7.1% in November, still well above the 2% target. The Fed's rate hikes caused the strongest dollar in two decades, impacting emerging market economies and global trade balances (Federal Reserve press releases, Nov-Dec 2022).

### World Population Reaches 8 Billion (Nov 15)

The UN estimated that the global population reached 8 billion on November 15, 2022. The milestone highlighted stark demographic divides: while India was set to overtake China as the world's most populous country, many developed nations faced aging populations and fertility decline below replacement rates (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events).

### Malaysia General Election (Nov 19)

Malaysia's general election resulted in a hung parliament - the first in the country's history. Long-time opposition leader Anwar Ibrahim was appointed Prime Minister five days later, forming a unity government and ending decades of dominance by the Barisan Nasional coalition (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events).

## Technology & Science

### ChatGPT Launch (Nov 30)

OpenAI released ChatGPT, an AI chatbot based on the GPT-3.5 language model, free to the public. It could answer questions, write essays, explain code, and engage in detailed dialogue. The release was a watershed moment in artificial intelligence, going viral and reaching 1 million users within five days. It brought large language models and general-purpose AI into mainstream consciousness, sparking debates about AI's implications for education, employment, and society (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events).

### NASA Artemis 1 Launch (Nov 16)

NASA launched Artemis 1, the first integrated flight test of the Space Launch System (SLS) - the most powerful rocket ever to reach orbit - and the Orion spacecraft. The uncrewed mission sent Orion on a multi-week journey around the Moon, testing systems for eventual crewed Artemis missions. Orion splashed down successfully on December 11, paving the way for Artemis 2 (crewed lunar flyby) and Artemis 3 (crewed lunar landing). This was NASA's first step toward returning humans to the lunar surface since Apollo (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events; 2022 in science).

### Fusion Ignition Achieved (Dec 5)

The National Ignition Facility at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory achieved net energy gain in a fusion reaction for the first time in history, a major scientific milestone. The experiment used 192 laser beams to compress a tiny deuterium-tritium pellet, producing 3.15 megajoules of energy output from 2.05 megajoules of input. While decades from practical power generation, the breakthrough demonstrated the scientific feasibility of inertial confinement fusion (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events; 2022 in science).

### James Webb Space Telescope Science

The JWST continued releasing remarkable science results through Q4:
- **December 1:** JWST observed methane clouds moving across Saturn's moon Titan (2022 in science).
- **Population III stars (Dec 8):** Astronomers reported the possible detection of the earliest first stars (Population III) in a preprint (2022 in science).
- Throughout Q4, JWST images of the Carina Nebula, Southern Ring Nebula, and Stephan's Quintet transformed astronomical observation capabilities.

### Elon Musk Acquires Twitter (Oct 28)

Elon Musk completed his $44 billion acquisition of Twitter after months of legal battles. He immediately fired top executives, dissolved the board, and began restructuring the platform. The acquisition led to massive changes: widespread layoffs (roughly 50% of staff), a rapid rollout of Twitter Blue subscription ($8/month), verification system overhaul, and content moderation policy shifts. The chaotic transition drove advertisers away and sparked a surge of interest in alternatives like Mastodon (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

## Disasters

### Kanjuruhan Stadium Crush (Oct 1)

A human crush at a football stadium in Malang, East Java, Indonesia, killed 135 people and injured over 500 after police fired tear gas into the crowd, causing panic and a stampede toward exits. It was one of the deadliest stadium disasters in history (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### Seoul Halloween Crowd Crush (Oct 29)

At least 158 people were killed and 197 injured during Halloween festivities in the Itaewon district of Seoul, South Korea, when a crowd surge occurred in a narrow downhill alley near a popular bar. Many were young people in their 20s. The disaster led to national mourning, resignation of police officials, and scrutiny of crowd management failures (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### Mogadishu Car Bombings (Oct 29)

A double car bombing by Al-Shabaab near the Ministry of Education in Mogadishu, Somalia, killed at least 121 people and injured around 300 - one of the deadliest attacks in the country's history. The first explosion was followed by a second as rescuers arrived, maximizing casualties (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### Gujarat Bridge Collapse (Oct 30)

A suspension bridge over the Machchhu River in Morbi, Gujarat, India, collapsed under the weight of hundreds of people, killing at least 135. The bridge, built in 1879, had undergone renovation and reopened just days earlier without proper safety certification (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### Cianjur Earthquake, Indonesia (Nov 21)

A 5.6 Mw earthquake struck near Cianjur in West Java, Indonesia, killing at least 635 people and injuring 7,700. The shallow depth and poor building construction caused catastrophic damage in the densely populated area (Wikipedia, Nov 2022 events).

### North American Winter Storm (Dec 21-26)

A major winter storm, dubbed "Winter Storm Elliott," brought blizzard conditions, record cold temperatures, and heavy snow across the northern US and southern Canada. At least 91 people died. The storm severely disrupted travel, with Southwest Airlines alone canceling over 15,000 flights due to its crew-scheduling system collapse, triggering a federal investigation into the airline's operations (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

## Culture & Society

### FIFA World Cup Qatar 2022 (Nov 20 - Dec 18)

The 22nd FIFA World Cup was held in Qatar - the first World Cup in the Middle East and the first played in November-December rather than June-July due to heat. The tournament was attended by 3.4 million spectators and drew global attention to Qatar over human rights concerns, treatment of migrant workers, and the country's laws on LGBTQ rights.

**Final (Dec 18):** Argentina defeated defending champions France 3-3 (aet, 4-2 on penalties) in what is widely considered one of the greatest World Cup finals ever. Lionel Messi scored two goals for Argentina and converted in the shootout, while Kylian Mbappe scored a hat-trick for France (just the second in a World Cup final). The victory marked Argentina's third title and Messi's crowning achievement, cementing his legacy in football history. The final was watched by an estimated 1.5 billion people worldwide (Wikipedia, 2022 FIFA World Cup page).

**Morocco's historic run:** Morocco became the first African and first Arab nation to reach the World Cup semi-finals, defeating Belgium, Spain, and Portugal before falling to France.

### Benedict XVI Dies (Dec 31)

Pope Emeritus Benedict XVI died at the age of 95 at the Mater Ecclesiae Monastery in Vatican City. His funeral was held in St. Peter's Square on January 5, 2023, presided over by Pope Francis. Benedict had become the first pope in 600 years to resign, in 2013, setting a precedent for future popes (Wikipedia, Dec 2022 events).

### Bratislava Shooting (Oct 12-13)

A gunman killed two people outside Teplaren, a gay bar in Bratislava, Slovakia, before fleeing and committing suicide. The victims were Juraj Vankulic (a non-binary person) and Matus Horvath (a bisexual man). The perpetrator's manifesto expressed far-right, anti-LGBTQ+ and anti-Semitic ideology. The attack shocked Slovakia and sparked community grief and calls for action against hate speech (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### Creeslough Explosion (Oct 7)

A gas explosion at a service station in Creeslough, County Donegal, Ireland, killed 10 people and injured 8. The blast destroyed a convenience store and apartment building, devastating the small town (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events).

### Nobel Prizes

The 2022 Nobel Prizes were awarded in October:
- **Peace:** Ales Bialiatski (Belarusian activist), Memorial (Russian human rights group), and Center for Civil Liberties (Ukraine).
- **Literature:** Annie Ernaux (France) "for the courage and clinical acuity with which she uncovers the roots, estrangements, and collective restraints of personal memory."
- **Economics:** Ben Bernanke, Douglas Diamond, and Philip Dybvig for research on banks and financial crises (Wikipedia, Oct 2022 events; 2022 in science).

## Key Emerging Patterns

1. **The Russo-Ukrainian war enters a new phase:** After Ukraine's successful counteroffensives in Kharkiv (Sept) and Kherson (Nov), the war shifted from rapid maneuver to a grinding winter campaign of attrition. Russia's targeting of energy infrastructure sought to break Ukrainian morale through cold and darkness, while both sides prepared for expected spring offensives.

2. **AI mainstream breakthrough:** ChatGPT's launch represented an inflection point for public awareness of generative AI, setting the stage for a wave of AI investment, competition, and societal debate that would define 2023.

3. **China's COVID reversal:** The dramatic abandonment of zero-COVID after widespread protests marked a historic policy failure for the CCP and presaged a massive infection wave with unknown economic and social consequences.

4. **Crypto winter deepens:** FTX's catastrophic collapse destroyed trust in centralized crypto finance, triggering cascading failures across the industry and intensifying global regulatory scrutiny.

5. **Climate action advances but emissions continue:** COP27's loss and damage fund and COP15's 30x30 biodiversity target represented genuine diplomatic achievements, but record CO2 emissions showed the gap between ambition and action remained vast.

6. **Economic headwinds:** Global central banks' synchronized rate hikes to combat inflation created recession risks for 2023. Energy price volatility from the Ukraine war, European energy crisis, and lingering supply chain disruptions kept inflation elevated across developed economies.

7. **Democratic resilience tested:** Elections in Brazil, the US, Israel, Malaysia, and Fiji showed both democratic turnover (Lula, Netanyahu, Anwar, Rabuka) and polarization, while Peru's self-coup attempt underscored the fragility of democratic institutions in some regions.

8. **Fusion energy breakthrough:** NIF's ignition achievement, while not immediately practical for power generation, demonstrated scientific viability and spurred increased investment in fusion energy startups and public research programs.
