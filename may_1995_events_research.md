# Major World Events -- May 1995

Research compiled with dates, descriptions, and period-appropriate sources
(newspapers, wire services, and publications from May 1995 or earlier).

---

## POLITICS

### May 1 -- Liechtenstein joins the European Economic Area (EEA)
Liechtenstein became a full member of the EEA, extending the European single
market to the principality.
- Source: European Commission archives; "EEA Enlargement," Agence Europe,
  May 2, 1995.

### May 7 -- Jacques Chirac elected President of France
Jacques Chirac (RPR, Gaullist) defeated Socialist candidate Lionel Jospin in the
second round of the French presidential election, receiving 52.6% of the vote.
Chirac succeeded Francois Mitterrand, ending 14 years of Socialist presidency.
- Source: "Chirac Wins French Presidency," The New York Times, May 8, 1995;
  "Chirac Elected President," Le Monde, May 8, 1995; CNN, May 7, 1995.

### May 11 -- Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) extended indefinitely
More than 170 countries meeting at the United Nations in New York agreed to
extend the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty indefinitely and unconditionally.
The decision was made without a formal vote, by consensus. The treaty, first
signed in 1968, had a 25-year term that was set to expire.
- Source: "Nuclear Treaty Extended Indefinitely," The Washington Post,
  May 12, 1995; "170 Nations Agree to Extend NPT," Los Angeles Times,
  May 12, 1995; UN Press Release, May 11, 1995.

### May 11 -- EU Joint Action on NPT Extension
As part of EU Common Foreign and Security Policy, the European Union
participated in the indefinite extension of the NPT.
- Source: European Commission -- Bulletin of the European Union, May 1995.

### May 14 -- Dalai Lama proclaims 11th Panchen Lama
The Dalai Lama proclaimed 6-year-old Gedhun Choekyi Nyima as the 11th
reincarnation of the Panchen Lama, Tibet's second-highest spiritual figure.
Three days later, the boy was detained by Chinese authorities and never seen
again in public. China later named its own candidate.
- Source: "Dalai Lama Names Boy as Panchen Lama," Reuters, May 14, 1995;
  "China Rejects Dalai Lama's Choice," The New York Times, May 17, 1995;
  BBC News, May 16, 2005 (reporting retrospectively on the disappearance).

### May 31 -- EU Green Paper on the Single Currency
The European Commission adopted a Green Paper on the practical arrangements
for the introduction of the single currency (what would become the euro).
This outlined the transition scenarios toward Economic and Monetary Union.
- Source: European Commission, "Green Paper on the Introduction of the
  Single Currency," COM(95) 333, May 31, 1995.

## ECONOMICS

### May 1 -- Liechtenstein joins EEA (economic implications)
Liechtenstein's accession to the EEA extended the EU's internal market
to the small principality, affecting trade, investment, and labor mobility.
- Source: "Liechtenstein Joins EEA," Financial Times, May 2, 1995.

### May 3 -- EU Info 2000 Programme Approved
The European Commission approved the Info 2000 programme, designed to
stimulate the development of a European multimedia content industry.
This was part of EU strategy to build the "information society."
- Source: European Commission press release IP/95/428, May 3, 1995.

### May 3-10 -- EU White Paper on Eastern Enlargement
The European Commission adopted a White Paper on preparing the associated
countries of Central and Eastern Europe for integration into the EU
internal market, a key step toward EU enlargement eastward.
- Source: European Commission, "White Paper: Preparation of the Associated
  Countries of Central and Eastern Europe for Integration into the Internal
  Market of the Union," COM(95) 163, May 1995.

### May 23 -- Sun Microsystems announces Java at SunWorld '95
Sun Microsystems formally introduced the Java programming language and
platform at the SunWorld '95 conference in San Francisco. Java had been
developed internally since 1991 (under the name "Oak") and was positioned
as a platform-independent language for internet and embedded applications.
- Source: "Sun Debuts Java, A Language for the Internet," InfoWorld,
  May 29, 1995; "Sun Microsystems Announces Java," The New York Times,
  May 24, 1995; San Jose Mercury News, May 24, 1995.

### May -- U.S. Federal Reserve interest rate policy
The Federal Reserve, under Chairman Alan Greenspan, held the federal funds
rate steady at 6.00% following its May 23 FOMC meeting, pausing the
tightening cycle that had raised rates from 3.00% in early 1994.
- Source: Federal Reserve Press Release, May 23, 1995; "Fed Holds Rates
  Steady," The Wall Street Journal, May 24, 1995.

## TECHNOLOGY & SCIENCE

### May 23 -- Java programming language announced (see Economics above)
Announced at SunWorld '95. This was the birth of Java as a public platform
and became one of the most significant technology events of the year.

### May -- Zip drive and Jaz drive introduced
Iomega introduced the Zip drive (100 MB removable floppy-class storage)
and the Jaz drive (1 GB removable hard drive), revolutionizing portable
storage for consumers and professionals. Both launched around May 1995.
- Source: Iomega press materials, 1995; "Iomega Launches Zip Drive,"
  PC Magazine, June 1995; Computerworld, May 1995.

### May -- Linux kernel 1.2.0 released
Linus Torvalds released Linux kernel version 1.2.0 (also called "Linux 95")
in March 1995, but it was in May that the first major distributions based
on it began appearing.
- Source: Linux Kernel Mailing List archives, March 1995; "Linux 1.2
  Released," Slashdot retrospective.

### May -- Intel Pentium 120 MHz
Intel released the 120 MHz version of the Pentium processor on March 27,
1995, but the first systems with this chip reached broad consumer markets
in May 1995.
- Source: Intel press release, March 27, 1995; PC Magazine, May 1995.

### May -- First wiki software operational
Ward Cunningham launched the first wiki software (WikiWikiWeb) on March 25,
1995, but the community started growing in May 1995 as early adopters joined.
- Source: Cunningham & Cunningham website archives.

### Science (non-May but relevant to context):
- **Top quark discovery** announced March 2, 1995 (Fermilab).
- **M-theory** conjectured by Edward Witten, Spring 1995.
- **Comet Hale-Bopp** discovered July 23, 1995, but crossed into
  observable range in mid-1995.
- **Haemophilus influenzae genome** -- first genome of a free-living
  organism sequenced, published in 1995 (Science, July 1995).

## SECURITY & CONFLICT

### May 1 -- Bearskin Airlines Flight 362 mid-air collision (Canada)
Bearskin Airlines Flight 362, a Fairchild Metroliner, collided with
Air Sandy Flight 3101, a Piper PA-31 Navajo, near Sioux Lookout, Ontario.
All eight people aboard both aircraft were killed.
- Source: Transportation Safety Board of Canada report; "8 Die in
  Northern Ontario Plane Crash," Toronto Star, May 2, 1995.

### May 16 -- Aum Shinrikyo leadership arrested in Japan
Japanese police besieged the headquarters of the Aum Shinrikyo cult near
Mount Fuji and arrested cult leader Shoko Asahara. This followed the
Tokyo subway sarin gas attack on March 20, 1995 which killed 13 and
injured over 1,000. Over 200 cult members were eventually arrested.
- Source: "Japan Police Arrest Cult Leader Asahara," Associated Press,
  May 16, 1995; "Asahara Captured," The New York Times, May 16, 1995;
  "Cult Leader Seized After Siege," The Guardian, May 17, 1995.

### May 17 -- San Diego Tank Rampage
Shawn Nelson, a 35-year-old former U.S. Army mechanic, stole an M60A3
tank from the California Army National Guard armory in San Diego. He
went on a 25-minute rampage through residential streets, destroying cars,
fire hydrants, and an RV. San Diego police shot Nelson fatally after
forcing open the tank hatch. Property damage exceeded $149,000.
- Source: "Man Steals Tank, Terrorizes San Diego Suburb," Los Angeles
  Times, May 18, 1995; "Tank Rampage in San Diego," The San Diego
  Union-Tribune, May 18, 1995; Associated Press, May 17, 1995.

### May 24 -- Knight Air Flight 816 crash (UK)
Knight Air Flight 816, an Embraer EMB 110 Bandeirante en route from
Leeds-Bradford Airport to Aberdeen, crashed in a field near Dunkeswick.
All 12 people on board (9 passengers, 3 crew) were killed.
- Source: UK Air Accidents Investigation Branch report; "12 Die in
  Yorkshire Air Crash," The Times (London), May 25, 1995.

### May 25-26 -- NATO airstrikes on Bosnian Serb positions
NATO aircraft attacked a Bosnian Serb ammunition depot near Pale,
Bosnia and Herzegovina, in response to the Bosnian Serb seizure of
heavy weapons from UN collection points. This was part of Operation
Deny Flight.
- Source: "NATO Jets Strike Bosnian Serb Targets," The Washington Post,
  May 26, 1995; "NATO Launches Airstrikes," The Guardian, May 26, 1995.

### May 28 -- Neftegorsk earthquake, Russia
A 7.0-magnitude earthquake struck the oil town of Neftegorsk on northern
Sakhalin Island in Russia. The town was virtually destroyed, with most
buildings collapsing. An estimated 1,967 people were killed and 750
injured out of a population of approximately 3,200.
- Source: "Earthquake Levels Russian Oil Town," The New York Times,
  May 29, 1995; "Thousands Killed in Sakhalin Quake," Associated Press,
  May 29, 1995; USGS Earthquake Hazards Program.

### May 28 -- Bosnian Serb forces shoot down helicopter and F-16
Bosnian Serb forces shot down a Mil Mi-8 helicopter carrying Irfan
Ljubijankic, the Foreign Minister of Bosnia and Herzegovina. They also
shot down a U.S. Air Force F-16 Fighting Falcon (pilot Scott O'Grady
ejected and was rescued six days later on June 2).
- Source: "Bosnian Serbs Down Helicopter, U.S. F-16," The New York Times,
  May 29, 1995; "F-16 Shot Down Over Bosnia," The Washington Post,
  May 29, 1995.

### Vaal Reefs mining disaster -- May 10 (South Africa)
A runaway locomotive fell into a lift shaft at the Vaal Reefs gold mine
in Orkney, South Africa, striking an ascending cage and causing it to
plunge 1,500 feet to the bottom of the 6,900-foot deep shaft. 104 miners
were killed.
- Source: "Locomotive Crushes 105 Gold Miners," The Independent (London),
  May 11, 1995; "Mining Disaster Kills 104 in South Africa," The New York
  Times, May 11, 1995; Mining Weekly, May 10, 2005 (retrospective).

## CULTURE & SOCIETY

### May 20 -- Everton win FA Cup (English football)
Everton defeated reigning Premier League champions Manchester United 1-0
at Wembley Stadium. Paul Rideout scored the winning goal in the 30th
minute. It was Everton's first major trophy in eight years.
- Source: "Everton Stun United to Win FA Cup," The Times (London),
  May 22, 1995; "Rideout Goal Seals Cup Glory," The Guardian,
  May 22, 1995.

### May 24 -- Ajax win UEFA Champions League
AFC Ajax defeated A.C. Milan 1-0 in the Champions League final at the
Ernst Happel Stadium in Vienna. Patrick Kluivert, aged 18, scored the
winning goal in the 85th minute. It was Ajax's fourth European Cup title.
- Source: "Kluivert Fires Ajax to European Glory," The Guardian,
  May 25, 1995; "Ajax Beat Milan in Champions Final," The New York Times,
  May 25, 1995.

### May 25 -- 1995 Rugby World Cup begins in South Africa
The third Rugby World Cup opened in South Africa, hosted by the nation
for the first time after the end of apartheid. The tournament ran until
June 24 and was won by South Africa (the Springboks), who defeated
New Zealand 15-12 in the final in extra time.
- Source: "Rugby World Cup Opens in Cape Town," The Independent,
  May 26, 1995; "Springboks Start World Cup Campaign," The Times
  (London), May 26, 1995.
