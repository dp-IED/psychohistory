# Class: junta-run constitutional referendum

- Instantiations: P-gw-const-ref (30 Aug 2026 Guinea-Bissau yes/no on a National Transitional Council constitution expanding presidential power; held that Sunday; CNE provisional 1 Sep 70% Sim / 30% Não of valid votes, turnout ~62.6–63%; CNE homologated those figures Thursday 3 Sep after no appeals — Bissau voted Não ~52% while the national line was Sim: https://www.africanews.com/2026/09/02/guinea-bissau-votes-yes-in-referendum-for-new-constitution/ ; https://www.ecofinagency.com/news/0209-58564-guinea-bissau-approves-new-constitution-with-70-of-valid-votes ; https://ang.gw/cne-reconfirma-em-comunicado-vitoria-do-sim-sobre-o-nao-do-referendo-de-domingo/ ; https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/30/vote-count-starts-in-guinea-bissau-referendum-on-strengthening-presidency ; https://libreinfo.net/guinee-bissau-les-resultats-du-referendum-attendus-sous-trois-a-sept-jours/); Mali junta constitutional referendum 18 Jun 2023 — AIGE provisional 23 Jun 97% Oui / 39.4% turnout, Constitutional Court 21 Jul 96.91% Oui / 38.23% turnout (https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/mali-approves-constitutional-amendments-referendum-2023-06-23/ ; https://www.france24.com/en/africa/20230623-mali-voters-overwhelmingly-approve-constitutional-amendments-says-election-body ; http://news.abamako.com/h/284739.html); Chad junta constitutional referendum 17 Dec 2023 — Conorec provisional 24 Dec ~86% Yes, Supreme Court 28 Dec 85.90% Yes / 62.86% turnout (https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/chad-votes-yes-new-constitution-backed-by-junta-2023-12-24/ ; https://www.africanews.com/2023/12/29/chad-supreme-court-approves-yes-referendum-vote/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Chadian_constitutional_referendum); Guinea junta constitutional referendum 21 Sep 2025 — MATD/DGE ~89.38% Yes, turnout ~86–88%, Supreme Court 26 Sep (https://apnews.com/article/guinea-referendum-consitution-doumbouya-junta-fd2738e59a0e4bf399edf2792c97411c ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Guinean_constitutional_referendum).
- Mechanism: A military transition owns the draft, the campaign, the electoral body, and the count. The ballot is a short-clock yes/no on a text that already expands the presidency (appoint/dismiss PM, dissolve parliament) and is framed as a step toward later civilian elections. Opposition and civil society can boycott, demand delay, or circulate empty-polling-station photos; they do not control the ballot once the decree date holds. Two clock phases. **Held:** rainy-season turnout, boycott copy, and mid-day empty stations are compatible with proceeding, not with postponement, unless a new suspend/postpone instrument issues. **Official result:** polling day is not the answering date. Electoral commissions typically name a later provisional day (Mali +5; Chad +7; Guinea-Bissau Tuesday after Sunday). That CNE/AIGE/Conorec provisional is the official-Yes clock. An empty-appeal homologation a few days later, or press copy that a court “will” speak in coming days, is not a further answering date unless a new calendar day is actually named. The modal published line, once the vote is held, is official Yes at lopsided national margins with turnout that can be modest (Mali ~38%) or CNE-claimed majority (Guinea-Bissau ~63%). Capital/regional No (Bissau ~52% Não) can sit under a national Yes. Freeze the ledger on polling day and the Yes/No phase drops off predict the same way a tariff pause dropped the post-pause clock.
- Base rate: **Held phase:** when a junta/transition decree still dates the vote and campaign is live, the modal is that the poll is held, not postponed. **Official-Yes phase:** among West/Central African junta-run constitution votes that were actually held, the published result is Yes by a wide national margin (Mali ~97; Chad ~86; Guinea 2025 ~89; Guinea-Bissau 2026 70). Unconditional “boycott photos ⇒ delay or No” and “the capital voted No ⇒ official No” are the wrong priors. Official No, or a cancelled clock after campaign start, is the rare side.
- Typical openings: Split held from certified Yes. Set **resolution day** to the CNE/court proclamation date the public instrument names, not only polling Sunday. After the vote is held, start from official Yes unless a listed falsifier has fired; do not wait for a last-day turnout scrape, and do not wait for an unnamed later court window once the commission has already proclaimed. Empty stations, opposition boycott, CNE-vs-RFI turnout arguments, and a capital No under a national Yes are the usual noise around that later Yes. If the commission names a later count window after polling day, slide Resolution later on this problem (same heading). Slide only to a named calendar day.
- Disanalogy: A competitive civilian constitution vote with an independent count and a real No path (Chile 2022). A postponed poll after a new legal instrument (Guinea Condé 1 Mar → 22 Mar 2020). Binding membership/EU talks in an EEA fisheries state (different card). The claim is only whether a street protest occurs, not the gazette text.
- Falsifiers: **Held:** a published postpone/cancel instrument before polling day; no vote that day. **Official Yes:** certified national No; the electoral body or court publishes a failed measure; not CNE-vs-opposition turnout split, boycott photos, rainy-season queues, capital/regional No under a national Yes, “results expected Tuesday” while the count is still open, or an unused Supreme Court window after CNE has already proclaimed. Not a falsifier of held: extra-parliamentary delay demand that does not move the decree.
