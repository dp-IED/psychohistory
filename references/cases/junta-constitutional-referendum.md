# Class: junta-run constitutional referendum

- Instantiations: P-gw-const-ref (30 Aug 2026 Guinea-Bissau yes/no on a National Transitional Council constitution expanding presidential power; held that day, CNE turnout “exceeded” 55%, first provisional results named Tuesday 1 Sep and official results 3–7 days after the poll — no certified Yes/No as of 31 Aug 2026: https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/30/vote-count-starts-in-guinea-bissau-referendum-on-strengthening-presidency ; https://libreinfo.net/guinee-bissau-les-resultats-du-referendum-attendus-sous-trois-a-sept-jours/ ; https://www.rfi.fr/fr/afrique/20260830-guin%C3%A9e-bissau-participation-timide-%C3%A0-la-mi-journ%C3%A9e-lors-du-vote-du-r%C3%A9f%C3%A9rendum-constitutionnel ; https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/guinea-bissau-vote-new-constitution-that-would-expand-presidential-powers-2026-08-25/); Mali junta constitutional referendum 18 Jun 2023 — AIGE provisional 23 Jun 97% Oui / 39.4% turnout, Constitutional Court 21 Jul 96.91% Oui / 38.23% turnout (https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/mali-approves-constitutional-amendments-referendum-2023-06-23/ ; https://www.france24.com/en/africa/20230623-mali-voters-overwhelmingly-approve-constitutional-amendments-says-election-body ; http://news.abamako.com/h/284739.html); Chad junta constitutional referendum 17 Dec 2023 — Conorec provisional 24 Dec ~86% Yes, Supreme Court 28 Dec 85.90% Yes / 62.86% turnout (https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/chad-votes-yes-new-constitution-backed-by-junta-2023-12-24/ ; https://www.africanews.com/2023/12/29/chad-supreme-court-approves-yes-referendum-vote/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Chadian_constitutional_referendum); Guinea junta constitutional referendum 21 Sep 2025 — MATD/DGE ~89.38% Yes, turnout ~86–88%, Supreme Court 26 Sep (https://apnews.com/article/guinea-referendum-consitution-doumbouya-junta-fd2738e59a0e4bf399edf2792c97411c ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Guinean_constitutional_referendum).
- Mechanism: A military transition owns the draft, the campaign, the electoral body, and the count. The ballot is a short-clock yes/no on a text that already expands the presidency (appoint/dismiss PM, dissolve parliament) and is framed as a step toward later civilian elections. Opposition and civil society can boycott, demand delay, or circulate empty-polling-station photos; they do not control the ballot once the decree date holds. Two clock phases. **Held:** rainy-season turnout, boycott copy, and mid-day empty stations are compatible with proceeding, not with postponement, unless a new suspend/postpone instrument issues. **Official result:** polling day is not the answering date. Electoral commissions typically name a later provisional day (Mali +5; Chad +7; Guinea-Bissau Tuesday after Sunday, or 3–7 days). The modal published line, once the vote is held, is official Yes at lopsided margins with turnout that can be modest (Mali ~38%) or CNE-claimed majority. Freeze the ledger on polling day and the Yes/No phase drops off predict the same way a tariff pause dropped the post-pause clock.
- Base rate: **Held phase:** when a junta/transition decree still dates the vote and campaign is live, the modal is that the poll is held, not postponed. **Official-Yes phase:** among West/Central African junta-run constitution votes that were actually held, the published result is Yes by a wide margin (Mali ~97; Chad ~86; Guinea 2025 ~89). Unconditional “boycott photos ⇒ delay or No” is the wrong prior. Official No, or a cancelled clock after campaign start, is the rare side.
- Typical openings: Split held from certified Yes. Set **resolution day** to the CNE/court proclamation date the public instrument names, not only polling Sunday. After the vote is held, start from official Yes unless a listed falsifier has fired; do not wait for a last-day turnout scrape. Empty stations, opposition boycott, and CNE-vs-RFI turnout arguments are the usual noise around a later Yes. If the commission names a later count window after polling day, slide Resolution later on this problem (same heading).
- Disanalogy: A competitive civilian constitution vote with an independent count and a real No path (Chile 2022). A postponed poll after a new legal instrument (Guinea Condé 1 Mar → 22 Mar 2020). Binding membership/EU talks in an EEA fisheries state (different card). The claim is only whether a street protest occurs, not the gazette text.
- Falsifiers: **Held:** a published postpone/cancel instrument before polling day; no vote that day. **Official Yes:** certified No; the electoral body or court publishes a failed measure; not CNE-vs-opposition turnout split, boycott photos, rainy-season queues, or “results expected Tuesday” while the count is still open. Not a falsifier of held: extra-parliamentary delay demand that does not move the decree.
