# Class: tariff-proclamation deadline delay

- Instantiations: P-usca-338; Section 232 steel/aluminum ally exemptions delayed hours before 1 May 2018 then allowed to expire 1 Jun 2018 (https://www.reuters.com/article/world/trump-delays-metal-tariffs-on-canada-eu-mexico-exempts-some-others-idUSKBN1I1164/ ; https://apnews.com/article/6ff92f0f76124685b18b8ed87e4763a0); China 10→25% hike delayed past 1 Mar 2019 after weekend talks without a signed text (https://apnews.com/article/0104a56ab495461f86cf4883b8d69ee7 ; https://www.reuters.com/article/business/trump-delays-tariff-hike-on-chinese-goods-citing-trade-talk-progress-idUSKCN1QD0KZ/); Section 232 auto decision delayed 180 days on 17 May 2019 instead of tariffs that Saturday (https://www.npr.org/2019/05/17/724271067/trump-delays-auto-tariffs-for-6-months ; https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/presidential-actions/adjusting-imports-automobiles-automobile-parts-united-states/).
- Mechanism: The US side owns an administrative clock (proclamation effective date, exemption expiry, or statutory decision day) that it can amend unilaterally. Talks run into the last hours. Remaining gaps on the real bargain (autos, dairy, IP, quotas) stay large and are consistent with a pause, not evidence the duty fires. The cheap instrument is a short suspend/amend of that clock, often announced as a “DEAL” subject to finalizing documents, not a signed comprehensive agreement and not silence. A last-day source-split (one capital expecting delay, the other calling it a coin flip) is the usual noise, not a falsifier. The counterpart may confirm only the postpone. Leverage is the next clock, not the last one.
- Base rate: While negotiators are still in the capital and neither side has declared talks over, the modal near-deadline outcome is a delay or pause of days to weeks, not a finished treaty and not an on-time take-effect. That prior holds against last-day skepticism unless a listed falsifier has fired (`references/analog-prior.md`). Take-effect happens after an already-extended clock when the administration says progress is insufficient (Canada/EU/Mexico metals, 1 Jun 2018). Cancellation of the threatened duty is rarer than a new date.
- Typical openings: Treat remaining sectoral gaps as compatible with a last-hour pause, not as proof the duty takes effect. Watch the administrative instrument (annex date, exemption expiry), not the press noun “deal” versus “pause.” A short delay keeps leverage; do not treat it as a settlement or as take-effect. Last-day “coin flip” quotes are not a reason to drop the analog.
- Disanalogy: Both sides have publicly ended talks; the clock is statutory with no amend power; the claim requires a signed treaty text rather than a pause; markets and officials price take-effect after a prior “final” extension.
- Falsifiers: Consecutive official statements that negotiations ended; no legal instrument to move the date; a prior extension already labeled final and then honored; the cheap instrument (short delay) is absent from both sides’ public language in the last day. Not falsifiers: remaining bargain gaps; anonymous source-split; “deal” language wrapping a proclamation amend.
