# Class: tariff-proclamation deadline delay

- Instantiations: P-usca-338 (19 Aug 2026 three-day pause, then 22 Aug 2026 take-effect after talks collapsed: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/08/temporary-suspension-of-additional-duties-to-offset-canadian-discrimination-against-the-commerce-of-the-united-states-with-respect-to-alcoholic-beverages-dairy-and-motor-vehicles/ ; https://apnews.com/article/tariffs-trump-canada-usmca-trade-aae597c22617bec7a99f670a2c787ef9 ; https://apnews.com/article/canada-us-trade-tariffs-trump-857ef76b20a766e370d70176135b678e); Section 232 steel/aluminum ally exemptions delayed hours before 1 May 2018 then allowed to expire 1 Jun 2018 (https://www.reuters.com/article/world/trump-delays-metal-tariffs-on-canada-eu-mexico-exempts-some-others-idUSKBN1I1164/ ; https://apnews.com/article/6ff92f0f76124685b18b8ed87e4763a0); China 10→25% hike delayed past 1 Mar 2019 after weekend talks without a signed text (https://apnews.com/article/0104a56ab495461f86cf4883b8d69ee7 ; https://www.reuters.com/article/business/trump-delays-tariff-hike-on-chinese-goods-citing-trade-talk-progress-idUSKCN1QD0KZ/); Section 232 auto decision delayed 180 days on 17 May 2019 instead of tariffs that Saturday (https://www.npr.org/2019/05/17/724271067/trump-delays-auto-tariffs-for-6-months ; https://trumpwhitehouse.archives.gov/presidential-actions/adjusting-imports-automobiles-automobile-parts-united-states/).
- Mechanism: The US side owns an administrative clock (proclamation effective date, exemption expiry, or statutory decision day) that it can amend unilaterally. Two phases. On the **first** approaching deadline, talks run into the last hours; remaining gaps on the real bargain (autos, dairy, IP, quotas) stay large and are consistent with a pause, not evidence the duty fires. The cheap instrument is a short suspend/amend of that clock, often announced as a “DEAL” subject to finalizing documents, not a signed comprehensive agreement and not silence. A last-day source-split is the usual noise, not a falsifier. The counterpart may confirm only the postpone. Leverage is the next clock. On an **already-extended / post-pause** clock, the same live talks, last-hour optimism, and unfinished documents are consistent with take-effect: the pause already spent the cheap instrument; CBP often files the new date the day before. One side saying it will not finalize, or sending negotiators home, is the usual close, not a surprise that restarts delay.
- Base rate: **First clock:** while negotiators are still in the capital and neither side has declared talks over, the modal near-deadline outcome is a delay or pause of days to weeks, not a finished treaty and not an on-time take-effect. **Already-extended clock:** the modal outcome is take-effect at the new date (P-usca-338 on 22 Aug 2026; Canada/EU/Mexico metals on 1 Jun 2018). Stacked delays need a *new* suspend/amend this forecast day. Cancellation of the threatened duty is rarer than a new date on the first clock and rarer still after a short “finalization” pause.
- Typical openings: On the first clock, treat remaining sectoral gaps as compatible with a last-hour pause, not as proof the duty takes effect. Watch the administrative instrument (annex date, exemption expiry), not the press noun “deal” versus “pause.” A short delay keeps leverage; do not treat it as a settlement *or* as take-effect. After that pause, do not restart the delay prior because talks are still live or because someone says the deal is “moving along.” Last-day “coin flip” quotes are not a reason to drop the matching phase.
- Disanalogy: Both sides have publicly ended talks *before* the first deadline (then delay is less likely); the clock is statutory with no amend power; the claim requires a signed treaty text rather than a pause; a new proclamation moving the already-extended date has already issued today.
- Falsifiers: **First clock:** consecutive official statements that negotiations ended; no legal instrument to move the date; the cheap instrument (short delay) is absent from both sides’ public language in the last day. **Already-extended clock:** a new suspend/amend or signed text before the new date; not last-hour optimism, remaining gaps, or CBP implementing the published next date. Not falsifiers of either phase: remaining bargain gaps; anonymous source-split; “deal” language wrapping a proclamation amend.
