# Class: fixed-date list-PR plurality with a nationwide threshold

- Instantiations: P-se-riksdag-26 (13 Sep 2026 Swedish Riksdag: scored line is which single party wins the most of 349 seats, not which bloc forms the government; preliminary count still has Social Democrats largest at about 28.1% vs Moderates 19.9% and Sweden Democrats 17.6% while the blocs sit 176–173; Valmyndigheten’s uppsamlingsräkning of late advance/overseas ballots slipped from Wednesday to Thursday 17 Sep and is still not a later Resolution: https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/swedish-centre-left-opposition-pole-position-take-power-after-tight-election-2026-09-14/ ; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/sweden-election-results-far-right-down-left-up/ ; https://eutoday.net/sweden-opposition-three-seat-lead-election/ ; https://www.di.se/nyheter/nu-raknas-de-sista-rosterna/); Sweden 11 Sep 2022 — Social Democrats 30.33% / 107 seats still the plurality while losing the government to the Tidö right (Sweden Democrats 20.54% / 73; Moderates 19.10% / 68): https://val.se/english/election-results/elections-to-the-riksdag-and-regional-and-municipal-councils/election-results-2022 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Results_of_the_2022_Swedish_general_election ; Norway 13 Sep 2021 Storting — 4% national threshold for leveling seats, modified Sainte-Laguë; Labour 26.3% / 48 of 169 still the plurality over Conservatives 20.4% / 36: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Norwegian_parliamentary_election ; https://www.csis.org/programs/europe-russia-and-eurasia-program/projects/european-election-watch/2021-elections/norway. Sweden 9 Sep 2018 is the same finish family (Social Democrats 28.26% / 100 still largest after a messy bloc night): https://val.se/english/election-results/elections-to-the-riksdag-and-regional-and-municipal-councils/election-results-2018.
- Mechanism: One fixed election night. Nationwide list PR with a ~4% hurdle (Sweden; Norway’s 4% is for compensatory seats). The scored object is the **largest party**, which is vote share among lists that clear the floor, not the coalition that later elects a speaker or prime minister. A long-run plurality party that still leads consecutive public polls by about 8–10 points three-plus weeks out usually remains that plurality even when the two *blocs* are a few seats apart, a small party hovers on the threshold, or the previous election already showed the same party first while losing office. Campaign-week “slide toward last time” copy and a coin-flip government are the usual press. They are a different clock.
- Base rate: **Long-run plurality party, consecutive late polls still ~8–10pp ahead of the next list:** the modal is that party (Sweden 2018 28.26/100; Sweden 2022 30.33/107; Norway 2021 Labour 26.3/48; Sweden 2026 S still first on the preliminary). Unconditional “the far-right is second in Europe ⇒ they overtake the Social Democrats on seats” is the wrong prior once the table is still S by a wide gap. Tight bloc arithmetic is not a plurality flip. A later overseas/final count that cannot close an 8pp party gap is not a new Resolution (same family as a disputed-ballot sitting after the count is already public).
- Typical openings: Name list PR and the nationwide threshold, then whether the scored line is largest party or government. If consecutive polls still show one list well ahead of the next and no listed falsifier has fired, follow that list. Do not import wartime Knesset fragmentation, FPTP, or a two-round presidential. Do not slide Resolution because the election authority will certify later in the week while the plurality is already public and not close.
- Disanalogy: First-past-the-post or two-round majority. Israel’s 3.25% wartime Knesset (Motivation on P-il-knesset-26). Consecutive late polls already have another party ≥ the named plurality party. The clock is “who becomes PM,” not “who has the most seats.”
- Falsifiers: Consecutive public polls with another party clearly ≥ the named plurality party; certified seat table with a different largest party. Not falsifiers: bloc lead of a few seats; a 4% party dropping in or out; campaign-week copy that the plurality party is “sliding toward last time”; overseas/final count still open while the party gap remains wide; government-formation talks after election night.
