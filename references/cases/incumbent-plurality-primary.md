# Class: incumbent plurality primary vs named intra-party challenger

- Instantiations: P-ma-sen-d-prim (1 Sep 2026 Massachusetts Democratic Senate primary: sitting Sen. Ed Markey vs Rep. Seth Moulton; AP called Markey ~8:30 p.m.; unofficial DDHQ 2 Sep 7:48 a.m. ET Markey 556,865 / 64.88% vs Moulton 301,372 / 35.12%; Moulton conceded election night; AdImpact ads Moulton/allies $7.1M vs Markey/allies $3.9M: https://apnews.com/article/massachusetts-senate-primary-election-markey-moulton-7968135279320c758b0fd4750d864db9 ; https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/ed-markey-wins-massachusetts-democratic-senate-primary-seth-moulton-rcna594717 ; https://votes.decisiondeskhq.com/races/2026-09-01/massachusetts-us-senate-democratic-primary ; https://www.wgbh.org/news/politics/2026-09-01/markey-wins-primary-for-us-senate-seat-moulton-concedes-race); Massachusetts Democratic Senate 1 Sep 2020, United States — Markey vs Rep. Joe Kennedy III (same generational-change ask; Markey 782,694 / 55.35% vs Kennedy 629,359 / 44.51%: https://ballotpedia.org/United_States_Senate_election_in_Massachusetts,_2020_(September_1_Democratic_primary) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_Senate_election_in_Massachusetts); Utah GOP Senate 26 Jun 2012, United States — Sen. Orrin Hatch vs former state Sen. Dan Liljenquist after Hatch missed the 60% convention cutoff (59.2%); Hatch 146,394 / 66.5% vs Liljenquist 73,668 / 33.5% (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Senate_election_in_Utah ; https://www.deseret.com/2012/6/27/20421150/sen-orrin-hatch-easily-wins-primary-election-against-former-state-sen-dan-liljenquist/).
- Mechanism: One clock, not a delayed majority. A sitting senator faces a named same-party challenger on a first-past-the-post intra-party ballot (Massachusetts, Utah after a convention). The first-round electorate *is* the nomination. Late double-digit public-poll leads plus the incumbent still owning the party’s activist or ideological lane usually stick: age/generational copy, a last debate, a newspaper endorsement of the challenger, and challenger ad spend are the trailer’s usual play. Markey 2020 and 2026 are the same seat with inverted ideology (Kennedy from the left; Moulton as a moderate plus age) and the same finish family. A same-cycle House wave of younger challengers beating House incumbents is a different electorate and a different ballot, not a reason to flip a Senate plurality with stable double-digit polls.
- Base rate: **Stable double-digit incumbent lead, party lane still owned:** the modal is the incumbent (Markey 2020 ~55–45; Hatch 2012 ~66–34; Markey 2026 ~65–35 after Suffolk/Globe 52–38 and UMass/WCVB 60–32). Unconditional “House members are losing to younger challengers this year ⇒ the senator loses” is the wrong prior. The rare side is when late polls have already flipped *and* the incumbent has lost the party lane (Lugar 2012: Howey/DePauw 30 Apr–1 May Mourdock 48–38 before a 60.5–39.5 certified loss: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Senate_election_in_Indiana ; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/richard-lugar-loses-primary-to-tea-party-challenger-mourdock/). Lieberman 2006 (Lamont 51.75–48.25) is the same rare side on Iraq, not age copy plus a still-double-digit incumbent poll (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_United_States_Senate_election_in_Connecticut).
- Typical openings: Name plurality (no delayed second round) and whether the sitting officeholder is on the ballot. If late public polls still show a double-digit incumbent lead and no listed falsifier has fired, follow the incumbent over age copy, challenger cash, and a last debate. Do not import a House anti-incumbent headline from the same Tuesday. An open-seat plurality (New Hampshire GOP 8 Sep 2026) is a different class. A delayed majority runoff is `references/cases/majority-primary-runoff.md`.
- Disanalogy: The seat is open (no incumbent on the ballot). The state delays a majority runoff. The clock is a top-two/jungle or ranked-choice general. The race is a House district, not a statewide Senate primary. Consecutive late polls already have the challenger ahead (Lugar). The incumbent has been stripped of the party’s activist lane on the live issue (Lieberman/Iraq), not merely called old.
- Falsifiers: Consecutive public polls with the named challenger clearly ahead; certified challenger win; incumbent dropout or party-lane walk-back that shows up in those polls. Not falsifiers: challenger outspending; a last debate; newspaper endorsement of the challenger; generational/age copy while polls remain double-digit for the incumbent; other incumbents losing House primaries the same cycle.
