# Class: two-time defending slam champion vs a remaining same-surface slam winner

- Instantiations: P-uso-ws-26 (12 Sep 2026 US Open women’s singles: two-time defending champion Aryna Sabalenka lost the final to Elena Rybakina 6–4 5–7 6–2; Rybakina already owned the 2026 Australian Open on hard courts and 2022 Wimbledon: https://www.reuters.com/sports/tennis/rybakina-beats-sabalenka-first-us-open-title-2026-09-12/ ; https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/articles/clym434j01xo ; https://www.wtatennis.com/tournaments/905/us-open/2026/scores/LS73992564); Wimbledon men’s singles 6 Jul 2008, United Kingdom — five-time defending champion Roger Federer lost the final to Rafael Nadal, already the 2008 French Open champion and a multi-slam winner on the same calendar (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_singles ; https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2008/jul/06/wimbledon.nadal); US Open women’s singles 7 Sep 2014, United States — Serena Williams completed a three-peat 6–3 6–3 over Caroline Wozniacki, who still had zero majors (https://www.espn.com/tennis/usopen14/story/_/id/11484243/2014-us-open-serena-williams-beats-caroline-wozniacki-win-3rd-straight ; https://www.tennis.com/news/articles/u-s-open-s-williams-d-wozniacki).
- Mechanism: One 128-draw slam, seven wins, same surface as the two titles already in hand. The defending champion is the default *name* in August copy and often does reach the final. The cheap error is treating that path as the title. When another player still in the draw already won a slam this year on this surface (or is a proven same-surface major winner with a winning slam-final H2H), the three-peat is the rare side. Ranking #1, “her house,” and a split rest-of-field do not cancel that rival. Revising only after the defending champion is *eliminated* is too late: the claim is who lifts the trophy, not who makes the final.
- Base rate: **Two-time (or longer) defending champion with a remaining same-surface slam winner in the draw:** the modal is that the three-peat fails (Sabalenka 2026; Federer 2008). The three-peat holds more often when the other finalist is not already a major winner (Serena 2014 vs Wozniacki). Unconditional “defending champs are still the modal name vs a split field” is the wrong prior once that rival is alive.
- Typical openings: Name the surface and whether another slam winner from this cycle (or a proven same-surface major winner) is still in the draw. If yes, do not follow the defending champion as champion. Reaching the final is not the scored line. A men’s or women’s draw is the same class; best-of-three vs best-of-five is not a different prior. An inaugural invite-only track final is not this card.
- Disanalogy: The other slam winners are already out. The defending champion has withdrawn. The event is not a major (WTA/ATP 1000). The remaining rival has never won a major (Serena–Wozniacki 2014 phase). The clock is a team title (Davis/BJK Cup, Super Bowl).
- Falsifiers: The same-surface slam winner is eliminated before the defending champion; the defending champion withdraws; certified three-peat. Not falsifiers: ranking #1; two-time defending copy; Sunshine Double or home-slam win-streak; a split 2026 slam field among *other* names; pre-tournament ankle/fitness noise on the rival that does not become a retirement; last-week markets still pricing the defending champion first.
