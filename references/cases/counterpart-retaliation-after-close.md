# Class: counterpart named-date retaliation after talks closed

- Instantiations: P-ca-retal-08 (8 Sep 2026 Canadian dollar-for-dollar counter-tariffs after US Section 338 took effect 22 Aug and talks were declared over; Carney named 8 Sep on 22 Aug; duties in force 12:01 a.m. 8 Sep on ~C$27.6bn / ~US$20bn of US goods, 15–50%, steel/dairy/appliances/agricultural equipment/pulp and paper/electronics; seafood later dropped from the list, not the date: https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/canadas-retaliatory-tariffs-take-effect-us-trade-talks-stall-2026-09-08/ ; https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/08/list-of-products-from-the-united-states-subject-to-counter-tariffs-effective-september-8-2026.html ; https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/22/carney-canada-will-enact-retaliatory-us-tariffs-starting-september-8); Canada 1 Jul 2018 United States Surtax Order after US Section 232 steel/aluminum hit Canada 1 Jun 2018 — Ottawa named 1 Jul on 31 May / early June and the surtaxes took effect that morning on C$16.6bn, not a further delay (https://canadagazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2018/2018-07-11/html/sor-dors152-eng.html ; https://www.canada.ca/en/global-affairs/news/2018/06/canada-stands-up-for-our-steel-and-aluminum-workers-and-industry.html ; https://www.usitc.gov/research_and_analysis/trade_shifts_2018/special_topic.htm); EU rebalancing 22 Jun 2018 after the same 1 Jun 2018 US 232 hit — Commission named Friday 22 Jun and the first-tranche duties on €2.8bn of US goods took effect that day (https://www.bbc.com/news/business-44567636 ; https://www.npr.org/2018/06/22/622488352/eu-tariffs-take-effect-retaliating-for-trumps-taxes-on-imported-steel-and-alumin ; https://www.gov.uk/government/news/eu-measures-in-response-to-us-steel-and-aluminium-tariffs).
- Mechanism: The *counterpart* owns this administrative clock (Gazette/OIC, Commission regulation), not the US proclamation that already fired. Two different US phases live on `references/cases/tariff-proclamation-deadline-delay.md`. This card starts only after talks are publicly over *and* the first side’s duty is already in force, and the counterpart has published a later retal date. That named morning is take-effect. Thinning the product list after industry comments (seafood 2026) is compatible with the date holding. US officials threatening a counter-round, later US import bans (29 Sep 2026 alcohol/dairy), or “details in coming days” on the list are not a new Canadian delay instrument.
- Base rate: **Named counterpart date after the first duty already fired and talks are declared over:** the modal is take-effect on that morning (Canada 8 Sep 2026; Canada 1 Jul 2018; EU 22 Jun 2018). The US first-clock delay prior (talks still live in the capital ⇒ pause) is the wrong prior here: negotiators already went home. Cancellation or a further dated delay needs a *new* counterpart suspend/Gazette this forecast day. List edits that drop a sector without moving the effective date are not delay.
- Typical openings: Check that the US (or first-side) duty already took effect and that talks were declared over before treating this as a delay clock. Follow the published retal date unless a listed falsifier has fired. Do not reuse `references/cases/tariff-proclamation-deadline-delay.md` first-clock delay because remaining US–Canada gaps once existed. A US counter-threat dated after this morning is a different problem. Recapping “Carney said 8 Sep” is not the same as scoring whether the duties are in force that morning — but once that date is Resolution, take-effect is the modal, not another pause.
- Disanalogy: Talks are still live and the first-side duty has not yet fired (then the US/first-clock delay card may apply). The claim is a signed comprehensive deal rather than whether the named retal list is in force. The counterpart has already published a new later date today. The clock is a US proclamation the US can amend unilaterally.
- Falsifiers: A Canadian Gazette/OIC (or EU/Commission legal instrument) that delays or cancels the named morning; a new US–counterpart suspend that the counterpart treats as standing down the retal before that morning; no duties on the listed US goods that morning. Not falsifiers: US USTR threatening a counter-round; dropping seafood or otherwise thinning the list while the date holds; “details in the coming days” after the date is already named; later US bans that start after this resolution day.
