# Class: stacked no-nation-cap championship 100m vs world-champion label

- Instantiations: P-wauc-m100-26 (12 Sep 2026 World Athletics Ultimate Championship men’s 100m, Budapest: Kenny Bednarek 9.85 SB over world champion Oblique Seville 9.90 and Ronnie Baker 9.93; inaugural invite field, no nation cap, same-night semi/final: https://worldathletics.org/competitions/world-athletics-ultimate-championship/2026/news/report/report-men-100m-world-ultimate-championship-budapest-26 ; https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/09/12/seville-beaten-ultimate-100m-final/ ; https://www.nbcsports.com/olympics/news/melissa-jefferson-wooden-kenny-bednarek-100-meters-world-athletics-ultimate-championship); World Athletics Championships Tokyo 25 men’s 100m, 14 Sep 2025, Japan — world leader Kishane Thompson (9.75 SB) took silver in 9.82; Oblique Seville, not the seasonal leader, won in 9.77 (https://worldathletics.org/competitions/world-athletics-championships/world-athletics-championships-tokyo-2025-7190593/news/report/world-championships-tokyo-25-report-mens-100m ; https://www.nbcsports.com/olympics/news/oblique-seville-noah-lyles-100-meters-world-championships-track-and-field-2025); Olympic Games Paris 2024 men’s 100m, 4 Aug 2024, France — Noah Lyles 9.79 (.784) over Kishane Thompson 9.79 (.789); the seasonal/favorite label was not the gold by itself (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2024_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men%27s_100_metres ; https://www.olympics.com/en/news/noah-lyles-wins-mens-100m-gold-paris-2024).
- Mechanism: Eight men in a global 100m final, several already sub-9.90, often two from the same country when the meet has no nation cap (Ultimate / some invitationals). The reigning world champion or WA ranking #1 is the press label and often wins *a heat*. A same-night semi→final, prize money, and a field that reconvenes the last two globals’ silvers is a different clock from “the Tokyo champion therefore wins Budapest.” Perennial global silvers (Bednarek: Olympic and world 200m silvers) are live once they are in the same race with a season’s-best near the leader. Championship-round copy that “title form beats a world lead” is parametric memory, not this prior — Tokyo 2025 already showed the world lead losing, and Ultimate 2026 showed the world title losing.
- Base rate: **Stacked elite 100m final (multiple global medallists / sub-9.90 men):** do not treat reigning world champion or ranking #1 as the modal name. The label from the last global is a heat prior. The scored line is whoever actually wins this final; historically the labeled favorite is often second (Thompson Tokyo 2025; Seville Budapest 2026). Naming the label because “recent golds cluster on the world champ” is the method miss.
- Typical openings: Count how many global 100/200 medallists and sub-9.90 men are in the same final and whether a nation cap is off. If the field is stacked, do not follow ranking #1 / reigning world champ as champion. A slam tennis three-peat is a different class. Prefer the athlete who has repeatedly taken global silver in 100/200 *and* holds a season SB within ~0.05 of the leader when that person is in the race (Bednarek 2026), not because silvers “deserve” a title but because that profile is live and the label is not a default.
- Disanalogy: A national championship or Diamond League with a thin field (the label *is* the race). A 4×100 relay. The world champion has withdrawn. The meet still uses a hard one-per-nation cap *and* the labeled favorite is also the clear seasonal world lead by a tenth — still not a free default, but closer to a championship-peak phase.
- Falsifiers: The labeled world champion/ranking #1 is the only sub-9.90 athlete in the final; that athlete withdraws; certified win by that labeled athlete after the others DNS. Not falsifiers: current WA ranking #1; last year’s world title; a seasonal world lead held by someone else in the same race; same-night semi/final; $150k purse; “clash of titans” preview copy.
