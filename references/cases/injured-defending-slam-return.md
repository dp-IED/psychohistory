# Class: defending slam champion returning from a multi-month injury layoff

- Instantiations: P-uso-ms-26 (13 Sep 2026 US Open men’s singles: ranking #1 Jannik Sinner WD 21 Aug with a right knee; defending champion Carlos Alcaraz had not played since April with a right-wrist layoff and was still named the remaining modal on 22 Aug; Ben Shelton beat him in the QF; Alexander Zverev, already the 2026 French Open champion and the remaining #1 seed, beat Shelton 6–3 7–6(7–2) 5–7 6–2: https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/articles/clyrkeyn5pgo ; https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/13/sport/us-open-mens-final-2026 ; https://www.cbsnews.com/news/alexander-zverev-wins-u-s-open-ben-shelton/); US Open women’s singles 3 Sep 2021, United States — defending champion Naomi Osaka, after skipping the clay and grass slams, lost in the third round to Leylah Fernandez (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women%27s_singles ; https://www.nytimes.com/2021/09/03/sports/tennis/osaka-us-open-fernandez.html); Australian Open men’s singles 18 Jan 2023, Australia — defending champion Rafael Nadal, already arriving short of matches, lost 6–4 6–4 7–5 to Mackenzie McDonald after a hip injury in the second round (https://www.bbc.com/sport/tennis/64314237 ; https://ausopen.com/articles/news/mcdonald-overcomes-injured-nadal-ao-2023).
- Mechanism: One 128-draw slam, seven wins. Ranking #1 and “defending champion” are August copy. A WD of the ranking favorite does **not** mint the remaining household name as champion when that name has not played a tour match in months. The cheap error is treating Open-era clustering on top hard-court names as a named modal once the healthy co-favorite is gone. A remaining same-year slam winner (Zverev RG 2026) is live; that is not the women’s three-peat card (two-time defending vs a *same-surface* slam winner still in the draw). Revising only after the injured defending champion is eliminated is too late for early skill.
- Base rate: **Defending champion returning from a multi-month layoff (or a season already stripped of the other slams):** do not start from that name as champion. Title defense fails more often than August markets imply (Alcaraz 2026 QF; Osaka 2021 R3; Nadal 2023 R2). Unconditional “the remaining top hard-court name is the modal after a WD” is the wrong prior. The 9 Sep Zverev row matched because Alcaraz was already out — news-now after a listed QF falsifier, not the 22 Aug analog.
- Typical openings: Name whether the defending champion has a recent match on the clock. If the last match is months back, or the ranking favorite has already WD, do not follow the layoff return as champion. A two-time three-peat with a remaining same-surface slam winner is `references/cases/defending-slam-three-peat.md`. Ranking #1 WD is a falsifier of *that* name, not a reason to paste the next seed.
- Disanalogy: The defending champion has a normal run-in (warm-up titles, no multi-month hole). The event is not a major. The layoff name has withdrawn. The remaining rival has never won a major and the defending champion is healthy (closer to Serena–Wozniacki 2014 on the other card).
- Falsifiers: The layoff defending champion has already played a full warm-up slam or 1000 and holds a current winning streak into this draw; certified title by that defending champion. Not falsifiers: remaining ATP/WTA ranking; “his house” / Flushing copy; markets still pricing the return first; the ranking favorite’s WD; a remaining same-year slam winner on another surface.
