### Forecast
**Prediction:** NO
**Confidence:** 0.99
**Reasoning:**

The 1.1% market price is generous. Demeke Mekonnen's path to PM requires overcoming three structural barriers:

**1. Abiy Ahmed's parliamentary lock**: The Prosperity Party holds an overwhelming majority in the House of Peoples' Representatives. The PM is elected by parliamentary majority — Abiy controls that majority. For Mekonnen to become PM, either: (a) Abiy's party must defect from him en masse, (b) an opposition coalition must win a majority (unlikely in Ethiopia's dominant-party system), or (c) Abiy must step down and anoint Mekonnen as successor.

None of these are remotely plausible. Abiy has spent 2018-2026 systematically consolidating power: purging TPLF figures, centralizing authority, building patronage networks. The Prosperity Party is an Abiy vehicle.

**2. Mekonnen's diminished position**: Removed as Deputy PM in February 2024. Replaced by Temesgen Tiruneh (a trusted Abiy loyalist and former intelligence chief). This demotion signals Abiy's distrust — Mekonnen was useful during the Tigray war (as an ethnic Tigrayan legitimizing the federal government) but expendable afterward. His current political position is weak: no party base, no government role, no patronage network.

**3. Ethnic federalism constraints**: Ethiopia's system allocates power along ethnic lines. As a Tigrayan, Mekonnen faces structural headwinds: the TPLF dominated Ethiopian politics from 1991-2018, and the Amhara/Oromo backlash against Tigrayan dominance is still active. Abiy (Oromo) and the Amhara elite would resist a Tigrayan returning to the premiership. The post-Tigray-war settlement has marginalized Tigrayans in federal politics.

**Tail risk (the 1.1%)**: If Abiy dies suddenly or is removed in a coup, Ethiopia enters a succession crisis. In that scenario, Mekonnen could emerge as a compromise candidate: acceptable to Tigrayans (as a Tigrayan), acceptable to international partners (as a known diplomat), and not threatening to Amhara/Oromo elites (weak personal base). But this requires: (a) Abiy's removal (~1-2% annual probability in Ethiopia's coup-prone politics), AND (b) Mekonnen specifically being the compromise choice (~10% conditional on crisis) → combined ~0.1-0.2%. The 1.1% market is an order of magnitude too high.
